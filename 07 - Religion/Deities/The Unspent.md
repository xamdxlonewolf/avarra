# The Unspent

> **People call it the Poured God.** Some [[Long-Lived]] keep a cup and say they made a god by giving it their memory, one year at a time. A member is asked once to join, and the circle asks five years of a member in all. For each year a member gives, that year's papers go onto the long-house shelf, and the circle counts the member one year younger ([[The Pourers]]). They do it so they can keep living without carrying everything. Neighbours call the cup a memorial with airs, the Motherfaith calls it a private devotion, and nobody sets the cup up in a public square.

This note is about the presence in the cup. The group that keeps the cup is [[The Pourers]]. Members say they give their memory of their own accord, one year at a time, each year on its own night, with its own minutes, and a member may stop before the next year begins. They read what the cup is storing by means of blood, and they believe they created the god they are giving the memory to. A member may leave, and leaving costs that member their friends and their seat.

## Overview

[[Long-Lived]] people outlive the families they started with. The ordinary Tithe is the need to keep learning, and the heavier cost is the funerals. Over the years, some of them began to give away the memories they could no longer carry, pouring those memories into a cup, and they gave the presence in the cup a name they had chosen themselves.

They do not agree on the name. Among themselves they say **the Cup We Made**. On a quay, people say **the Poured God**, and they say it with the smile they keep for a rich uncle's hobby. No town Tree is involved in the practice, no leaf falls for the cup, and no warden Speaks a colour over it.

What a neighbour is able to see is ordinary enough: a cup, or a small bowl, usually made of common ware; one drop of the giver's blood in that cup, because the Long-Lived already know how to read a memory out of blood; and a person who has given years away and is still recognizably the same person. They will tell you that they invented whatever is on the other side of the cup. That claim is the centre of the practice.

## Worship and what people see

There is no temple for it, only a shelf, and sometimes a second cup sitting beside the first. What it costs a member to leave the circle is written on [[The Pourers]]. The years a member has already given stay in the cup, and the papers are the thing that comes back.

There are no priests. The oldest member of a circle keeps the minutes, and that person is a member with a book, not a Hand and not a warden of a Tree.

Each circle describes the god in its own picture. One circle says a librarian, another a quiet guest, another a child who never left home, and the picture changes when you go from circle to circle. The rule does not change with the picture. Members give years, one year at a time, the cup keeps what they gave, and the cup cannot Give a Condition or save anyone from death.

The other faiths have already said what they think it is. The Motherfaith calls it a Long-Lived Tithe, that need to keep learning, dressed up so that it looks like a rite, and the Watching calls it one more cost, and not the Mother. The Fair Hand calls it a contract with nobody standing on the other side to stamp it. The Old Ways say it is not a sacred place and not a door, and the Open Table calls it a need the mainland has and the hill does not share.

## What this is not

The cup cannot make anyone Long-Lived. The practice is not a cult of the Tree, and it is not one of the five faiths people live by.

## GM Notes

- **What it is.** The Unspent is a small household presence that stores the memories it is given, and it does not make plans. It is not one of the Five Hands. It cannot Give a Condition, and it cannot Strike someone into one. It cannot make anyone [[Long-Lived]], because only the Leaf-Mother Gives that life, and it cannot save anyone who has reached a threshold, and it cannot Give at a Tree. If a circle asks the cup for more years, or for a second Condition, or for a rescue from death, the cup only keeps what it already holds. Asking it for a Gift gets nothing back. The structure of that limit is on [[The Other Hands]].
- **It is older than the name.** The Pourers say they made it. They did not. This presence has always taken in the memories that deathless people could no longer carry. It had no public face. The Long-Lived gave it a name, gave memory into that name, and decided that the naming was what had made it. It lets them say so, and it does not correct them. Do not play it as a spirit the circle managed to trap, and do not play it as the place vampires come from.
- **What it takes in.** It takes memory, and only memory. The papers from the years a member gives go onto the shelf in the long-house, not into the cup, and the circle's count of a member's years is a custom of the long-house. Neither the papers nor that count has anything to do with this presence. When the circle reads the cup, they are looking something up. They are not feeding it. The presence does not feed on people, and it does not need to be worshipped in order to go on existing.
- **The taking.** The presence stores whatever a person puts into a cup, and it does not know or care whether that person chose to give it. When the inner circle of [[The Pourers]] forces a Long-Lived to give, the memory goes into the second cup, and the founder reads what was given. The founder is a Long-Lived person who started the Pourers. The founder is not this presence. Do not put the founder's name into the player-facing part of this note. The way the forcing is done is written on [[The Pourers#The taking]].
- **The founder's belief.** The founder believes that the knowledge stored in the second cup will carry them across into godhood. The founder also believes the attempt could ruin the very things they want to keep safe, and the founder has accepted that risk. These notes record the belief. They do not say that the crossing can happen, and they do not say that the world could end. Text a player can read does not call the founder a god, a forgotten deity, or a threat that will end the world. What a player is allowed to read is the circle's claim that the circle made the presence.
- **What it is not, by name.** It is not a sixth Turning. It is not [[Returned|Threnvaeth]]'s record of the unfinished work of the dead. It is not [[Bound|Orledd]]'s bargain, because nobody turns to it and asks for help in a desperate moment. The player-facing body of the note leaves the names of the Hands out.
- **What it will not do.** It will not take over a person's will by itself, sort the Kept, confirm the Leaf-Mother, name the Other Hands, take Phoenix fire, or give a gift back in the form of a Condition. A member who leaves is given their papers back, and members call that returned sheaf a thinner copy. The years that were already given stay in the cup.
- **Tone.** Keep the fright at about five percent. A deathless person giving up a year of memory at a time, and putting that year's papers on a shelf, is sad, and a little vain, and it is not a blood cult. The part that is actually frightening is the inner circle, and that part stays hidden under ordinary courtesy and under scholarship that is real.
- **Reveal tag.** `the-unspent`. The player-facing section describes the practice and the claim the circle makes. The fact that the presence was already there before the Pourers gave it a name stays in these GM Notes.
- **Keep the groups apart.** This presence is not [[The Protectors]], and it is not [[The Walled Book]]. The Pourers picture it as a god. It does not become a Hand.
- **[[Reimaethe]] is a seed.** They want to stop before the fifth year. The group they would be leaving is [[The Pourers]]. Do not build the circle around Reimaethe on this page.
- **Links (GM).** [[The Other Hands]] is the structure behind this presence, and the presence is not a sixth Hand. [[Is the Leaf-Mother Real]] is the keystone, and the cup must not be used to confirm the Leaf-Mother. [[11 - Secrets]].
