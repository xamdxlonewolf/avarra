# Maieth

> **The name a devout square says.** [[The Leaf-Mother]] is the faith: the practice, the schism, the clergy, and the texts. This note is the presence those faithful mean when they say the name Maieth. It is what they hold to be there. A skeptic can live a whole life in the same square and never grant that anyone is listening.

## Overview

They say she is a mother, and quiet: that she gives, that she does not thunder, and that she does not come when she is called. A gift, they say, has a weight, and tending that weight is how a life is spent. A hug, they say, is also hers. Whether a person who changed at a drowning, or at an oath, or at a death, was still met by her is the faith's argument, and that argument is still live. It is kept on [[The Leaf-Mother]], and no picture on this page settles it.

The faithful hear a mind in the way a fall fits, in the way a leaf seems to have come to the particular person it landed on. Skeptics hear a tree doing the thing this tree does. Either of them can bring a child to the square, and either of them can be a good member of the faith, because the faith counts the doing and not the theory you carried in with the child.

They give her no throne, and they keep no sentence that will answer merely because someone demanded an answer. If they meet her, they meet her at the wood, in the turn a life takes, and in the fit of a thing that arrives. For them the quiet is a mother letting her children live their lives. The same quiet is why a skeptic can remain in the faith and remain sincere.

## Worship & Iconography

The houses do not share one face for her, and what they share is the wood. A pressed leaf kept in a chest is a common keepsake, and it is only a leaf, and it does not speak, and some hearths keep no image of her at all.

The Motherfaith does not picture her standing at a drowning, or at an oath, or out in the deep wild, because the faith has not agreed that she is in those places, and that argument stays on the faith's own note. The cup some deathless people keep is [[The Unspent]], and a warden does not set that cup on the sill.

What a year feels like when people are actually doing the things of the year, the week and the burial and the guest and the hour a person changes, is written on [[What the Year Feels Like]]. The blessing that is spoken over a child stays on [[The Leaf-Mother]], and it is not copied here.
