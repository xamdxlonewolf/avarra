# When the Town Buries

> **The hour a street has a body to carry.** What it is like to be married to someone you will outlive, and the work the Returned already do among the dead, both live on [[Daily Life]]. This page is the walk, the meal that follows it, and the door of the house.

## The walk

The body is washed in the house, often by a person who is Returned, because that labour is already their street, and the family still chooses the cloth. Then the body is carried. In a Tree-town the carry goes **past** the canopy, along the street the person used when they were alive, and the body is not stood under the boughs, and nobody asks the Tree for a colour. A warden may walk with the house. If the house wants a name said, the warden says Maieth, the name the devout use, and says it the way a neighbour would say it. If the house has asked for silence, the warden walks without speaking. Both of those walks are the same office.

Neighbours bring food to the door, and the door stays open through the meal. The person who is going to sleep in the house is the one who shuts it, or else the person the house has asked to do the shutting.

A Long-Lived who loved the dead waits on the step until someone says to come in. That waiting is already how a household treats a deathless life, a life long enough that the funerals in it have to be measured, and those funerals are written on [[Daily Life]]. This hour is one of them, and it is still a door and a loaf.

## In the room

A mixed house is the usual house. A sibling who keeps the Motherfaith says Maieth over their own bowl, and by the name they mean the life that has ended. A Watcher sibling talks about what the dead were like at the stall in Gift-month, the coat and the argument and the way they counted change, and does not thank any giver for the life. If the dead kept a book, a Settler closes that book on the table, where the room can see the last line that was written, and the Settler does not write a new line. A kin-speaker sets a little of the meal at the threshold. If the dead kept the Old Ways, the rest of that food is taken to the door once the street has finished eating. Nobody holds the argument about which name ought to come first while the bowls are still full. The argument can have the time after the dishes.

Nobody Speaks a fall. Speaking a fall would mean naming a colour over the dead, as if the Tree were being asked to answer, and the room does not do it. A neighbour who tries is walked outside by whoever is standing nearest the pot.

## On a dock

If the dead person worked the water at [[Orentel]], the gang carries the box along the quay. The box does not go under Tallo, the quay's name for its Tree, to be read. Some of the gang say *fair wind, fair term* over the box, because that is the sentence they have for a leaving. Some of them will not say it, because they keep that sentence for a hull that is still going out. Both of those things happen in the same hour, and the gang does not stop the carry in order to settle which is the right one.

If there is a book, it is closed where the gang can see that it has been closed. The salt on the roots is not part of this hour ([[Orentel]]).

## In a waiting town

[[Eolvaeth]] does not send off to a Tree-town to borrow a warden it does not have. If the warden is already in the vale, that warden may walk with the house, and if the town sent its children somewhere else to Turn during that summer, the town buries its dead with the people who stayed. The pot that feeds the mourners is the gift-hall's pot, and the food goes to the door the dead person kept. The wet leaf on the spring is the spring's business, and it is not a funeral token ([[Eolvaeth]]).

## At the hill

At [[Ndenjoo]] the body is carried above the tide, and the hall does not lay a second empty plate for the dead. The dead person's own cup is set in the place that was already laid, it sits there for one night, and then it goes back onto the shelf. Whether anyone speaks a name is the argument the valley already knows how to have, and that argument waits until the dishes are done. The shore does not come up the hill to invent a rite on the sand.

## GM Notes
*(Stripped on player-safe export.)*

- **Keep it to the one hour.** Do not invent a funeral liturgy, a judgement passed on the dead, or a second afterlife. The Returned already tend the dead. The kin who stay on the hill stay on [[The Open Table]] and [[Ndenjoo]], and they should not be folded into some other rite.
- **~5%.** The weight of the hour is a door held open, a book closed where the room can see it, and a step that is not taken until someone speaks. There is no procession arranged to prove a cosmology.
- **Do not** coin a blessing for the walk, put a date on the Tree, or explain the four habits. The world book stays untouched.
