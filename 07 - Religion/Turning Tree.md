# Turning Tree

> **What a Turning Tree is.** A Turning Tree stands at the centre of a town. At a child's tenth-year solstice the town gathers, and one of two things happens. A coloured leaf falls on the child and **Gives a Condition**, the change that child will grow up carrying, or the leaves close round the child in a hug with no colour and the child becomes one of **the Kept**, wanted as they are and not handed a Condition. This note says what a Tree is, where the Trees come from, and how the Leaf-Fall, that solstice ceremony, is held. The faith built around the Tree is [[The Leaf-Mother]]. Which Conditions a leaf can Give, and how many people carry each of them, is on [[Conditions]].

## What a Turning Tree is

A Turning Tree is a broad, slow, long-lived hardwood, with a canopy like a great oak or a great beech, though the tree is its own kind. It stands at the ceremonial heart of a town, usually in a square, on a green, or in a walled yard the town keeps for it. Out of season it looks like an unusually grand old tree, and the town treats it as one: children climb the low boughs, courts and markets meet in the shade, and lovers carve nothing into the bark, because cutting a mark there is taboo. **In the tenth-year solstice week, the midsummer people call Turning-Week, the tree turns.** The canopy shifts through colours no ordinary tree wears, and a leaf falls for a child, or the leaves close around them.

The everyday name is **Turning Tree**, and it is named for what the tree does at the solstice. It turns, and it turns children into what they will become. The devout call each one **a Hand of the Mother**, which [[The Leaf-Mother]] takes up as the faith's own name for that wood, and the reverent word for the species in the liturgy is **motherwood**. In the old liturgical [[The Old Tongue|root tongue]], the Awakening Tree's own name is **Thaeloren** *(THAEL-or-en)*. **thael** means Tree and **oren** means to witness, so the name says *"the Tree that witnesses"*. People wear that long name down, in everyday speech, to *the Thallow*.

## One Tree, many hands — the graft

There is **one origin Tree, the [[#The Awakening Tree|Awakening Tree]]**, the great first Tree and the place the world's pilgrimage is aimed at. **Every town Turning Tree is a living graft of that one tree.** When a town takes root, and especially when the town is so far off that a family could never reach the Awakening Tree, someone carries out a sapling grafted from the Awakening Tree and plants it in the new town's square. A scion is not a seedling that grew from a seed the Tree dropped. It is cut tissue of the one Tree, kept alive on the road, and planted into the new ground so the same wood grows on.

A town gets two things from that arrangement.

- **A Tree nearby.** Ordinary families walk to their own town's Tree for the Leaf-Fall, so the rite is local and the distance of the road does not exile anyone from it. This is the ordinary way in the Hands era, the years when a town's own Tree is what people expect. It was not always so. [[The Ages of the Turning]] tells the earlier shape.
- **A pilgrimage, for the people who are devout enough to want one.** They travel to the **Awakening Tree** itself, the first Hand, either to have a child Turned under the origin or simply to stand there. On [[Heskoren]], and in any place where a graft has failed, that walk is still a necessity. It is not only a devotion.

### The Awakening Tree

This is the origin. It is old past any dating the faith can rely on, and it is vast, and it is the one Tree that nobody grafted out of another tree, which is why every other Turning Tree descends from it. The liturgy calls it **the First Hand**. People really do make the pilgrimage. Devout families travel so that a child can be Turned under the origin, and some of those families settle in the country around it. The faith's central clergy sit in the Tree's shadow. They are the **First Seat**, a college of [[The Tree-Wardens]], and they are not a throne. The Tree stands on [[Maiethorn]], in the Sacred Core.

## Where scions come from — grafting, and what can go wrong

- **A graft is a rite, and it is also the horticulture.** The faith's tree-wardens are the ones who cut a scion from the Awakening Tree, or, in ordinary practice, from a healthy town Tree that is cutting on the origin's behalf. The cutting travels in living earth, and it is planted with ceremony. The story of how that rite was captured, first as a heresy and then as a paper the college could hold, is [[The First Cut]].
- **A young Tree cannot Turn anyone yet.** A scion that has just been planted needs years, a generation by the reckoning people actually use, before it is mature enough to hold a Leaf-Fall. While it grows, the new town leans on a neighbour's Tree, and the children travel to the nearest town that can Turn them. That borrowing is where a lot of the small texture of the world comes from: rivalries between towns, customs that belong to the road, and the sentence people still say, "we Turned at Harrow's Tree before we had our own."
- **A Tree can sicken, and a Tree can die.** The wood is mortal. It is not a thing that cannot be destroyed. A Tree that is blighted or dying is a real crisis for the town, because the town may lose the power to Turn its own children and have to fall back on a pilgrimage or on a neighbour. A Tree that has died is replaced only by a fresh graft, and then by the long wait while the new wood matures. A mature Tree is hard to move once it is in the ground. The practice is to graft a new scion, rather than to dig the old tree up and carry it.
- **A town with no Tree** is what the frontier usually looks like. The place is too new, or too remote, or it has lost the Tree it had. Towns in that condition send their ten-year-olds onto the road to be Turned, and the journey is one of the ways the map marks its own edge.

## The Leaf-Fall (the ceremony)

The Leaf-Fall is the rite the rest of the world is built around. It is held in the **tenth-year solstice week**, the High Solstice of midsummer, which people keep as **[[The Reckoning of the Year|Turning-Week]]**.

**Who comes.** Every child who has turned ten since the last solstice is brought by their family. The witness-circle, the people gathered to see the child, comes together, and a **tree-warden** of [[The Tree-Wardens]] officiates. That warden is the town's own hearth. The First Seat is present only in a week when people have come on pilgrimage. The hour is a sacred rite and, at the same time, the largest festival the settlement holds all year, something like a christening and a graduation and a fair held in the same afternoon. In a village the witness-circle simply is the town. In a city the circle is a hearth, a group of people who already know the child, and the square is still full. See [Turning-Week in a city](#Turning-Week%20in%20a%20city).

**How the children are brought up.** They come to the Tree one at a time, or in the small cohort of children who share that year, and they stand where the canopy can reach them, and they wait. The Tree turns, and it lets fall whatever it is going to let fall. The rest of the town watches from the edge of the boughs.

**The two things that can happen:**
- **A colour falls, and a Condition is Given.** One leaf, or more than one, in a colour that means something, drifts down and settles on the child. **The colour is the name of the Condition** (see *Reading the colours*, below). This is the outcome people expect, the one they call proper, and the one they celebrate. The child grows up already knowing what they will become, and the town trains them and guilds them toward that life.
- **The leaves hug the child, and there is no colour, and the child is Kept.** Instead of a coloured fall, the ordinary leaves gather and wrap the child, a green embrace with no colour that names anything. That hug marks one of **the Kept**: kept whole, and wanted for their own sake. The faith holds the hug to be a kindness the Tree chose. The schism argues over that reading, and the argument is on [[The Leaf-Mother]].

**What the hour feels like.** The Leaf-Fall is warm, and it has weight, and it belongs to the whole town: a breath that the circle holds, every eye on one child, a parent who was Given this same colour years ago weeping when it falls again, or a parent who had quietly hoped the leaves would hug. The Tithe, which is the upkeep a Condition later asks of the person who carries it, is a matter for the years after this afternoon. The shadow that belongs to the rite itself is a human one, a wrong word spoken over a fall, or a week that was missed. See [[The Wrong Green]], and the questions below.

## Turning-Week in a city

A Gift can still be given in that **one week** and not in some other week, and the Tree is still the centre. A city of tens of thousands does not grow a second rite to get through the number of children. It divides the witnesses into hearths, groups of people who already know the child they have brought.

- **The stands run by hearth, across the week.** In the morning, the afternoon, and the eve, a quay-gang or a courtyard or a guild-stair or a lot takes its turn: eighty to two hundred people who already know the child. Hundreds of ten-year-olds are not stood up together as one crowd that nobody could read.
- **The warden Speaks.** To Speak is to say aloud whether the Tree gave a colour or a hug, and to name the colour when there is a colour. The warden does not write those gifts onto a roll. The week-slate, the board kept for the week, only records which hearth stands at which bell. It is a festival schedule, the same kind of board that says who has the square for the fair.
- **A crowd does not witness a child. A hearth does.** That is the legal fact ([[Law and Citizenship#Turning in a city — the hearth stood]]). When a desk far from the city asks who saw the standing, one pillar of the hearth carries it. The pillar is a person who already carries one of three Conditions, a Long-Lived, a Bound, or a Far-Voiced, and who stood in that hearth.
- **Threnmaieth writes the stand down.** No other place does. A clerk who copies the colours off a week-slate has begun a register of Conditions, and a register is a thing the hearths do not keep.

A village still watches the rite as one circle.

## Reading the colours (colour → Condition)

The colour of the leaf that falls is how the town knows which Condition has been Given. The way that reading is known is also what gives the faith its institutions.

- **Ordinary people know the common colours, and the clergy hold the reading that counts.** The core meanings are folk knowledge. A person in an ordinary town can read the obvious leaves, because everyone knows what the deep-red leaf of the Long-Lived looks like and what the storm-grey fall of the Two-Bodied looks like. The **tree-wardens**, who are the clergy, keep the reading that has authority. They confirm it out loud at the ceremony, and they are the ones who handle a fall that is rare, or subtle, or mixed: a colour almost nobody present has seen, or a fall that could honestly be read two ways. That work is why the faith has a civic job and not only a doctrine. It is a reason the town organizes itself around the Tree, rather than only praying under it.
- **The Conditions that can only be Given** have colours that are clear and widely known, because those colours are only ever seen at a Tree. **The Conditions that can also be Struck**, the paths a person can enter either way, which are [[Far-Voiced]], [[The Stilled]], and [[The Taken-In]], can fall as colours too. That is why a Far-Voiced who was Given and a Far-Voiced who was Struck share a nature and do not share a story. See [[Conditions]].
- **The colours, and who is trusted to read them:**

| Leaf-colour | Condition | Who can read it |
|---|---|---|
| **deep red / wine** | [[Long-Lived]] | folk-known, and already a proverb |
| **storm-grey** | [[Two-Bodied]] | folk-known, and already a proverb |
| **lamp-amber / brass** | [[The Answered]] | folk-known |
| **sea-blue** | [[Far-Voiced]] | folk-known. A child Given the blue leaf is trained toward it, and a Far-Voiced who was Struck has no leaf at all |
| **pale stone / marble-white** | [[The Stilled]] | folk-known, and feared, so the warden says the name carefully |
| **copper-green / verdigris** | [[The Taken-In]] | folk-known, once you know not to confuse it with the hug. This green is the wrong green for a hug: metallic, and not the Tree's own green |
| **white-fire / opal** | [[Phoenix]] | **clergy-known, from the texts.** Almost no town now alive has seen it fall. A warden who names it at a ceremony has just made history, and has also made a problem |

**The Tree never Strikes**, so [[Returned]] and [[Bound]] have **no colour**, and [[The Unbound]] has none either, because the Unbound chains off Bound instead of falling as a leaf of its own. The hug that marks the Kept is ordinary summer-green, and it carries *no naming colour*. That is why the leaf of the Taken-In must not be that same ordinary green.

Falls that are rare, or mixed, or argued over, two colours at once, or a colour nobody in the square has seen, or a reading of Phoenix that someone in the square wants to be wrong, are the reason the tree-wardens are a civic office and not only a devout one. The orders they keep are on [[The Tree-Wardens]].

> **A Tree never Strikes.** The Tree only **Gives**, or it **Keeps**. People who are **Struck** received a Condition through a later threshold, a drowning, an oath, or a death they survived, and that change happens away from the Tree, with no leaf and with no ceremony. Whether the faith still counts the Struck as the Mother's own doing is the schism that is still live, and it is set out on [[The Leaf-Mother]]. This page does not settle it.

## Questions a warden gets asked

These are the questions families actually bring to a warden, and the answers are the custom of the town. The door is still **one week**, the High Solstice week of the year a child turns ten. [[The Walking Years]] tells why the door is that narrow, and the towns of the Hands era did not grow a second chance beside it. A healthy Tree, with a child stood under it, still does one of two things: it gives a colour, or it gives a hug.

| Question | What the town does | What that does to a life |
|---|---|---|
| **We missed the week.** The child had a fever, or the pass was shut, or the boat did not come, so the child was ten and was not under the boughs. | They are **unTurned**, and the door has shut, so next summer they may visit the square and they do not stand for a Gift. The doors by which a person can be [[Conditions|Struck]] still open later, somewhere else. The expensive mercy is still the old one: winter a *nine*-year-old near a Tree that is sound, so that this particular week cannot be missed. | The child has no colour and no hug, the neighbours remember the miss, and folk will call the child Kept, though the law will not, because Kept is a hug the town was there to see. |
| **We will not stand.** The family refuses the Tree, from the Old Ways, or from the Open Table, or from fear, or from a quarrel. | The Tree does not chase them, and the legal result is the same result as a miss: the child is **unTurned**, and the door is shut. A warden may ask one time, and the warden does not put a rope on a child in order to produce a Gift. | [[The Old Ways]] already treat a refusal as a different door, and not as a failure. [[The Open Table]] will not walk a child of the table to a Tree, and the quarrel over that can use up the week. See [[The Open Table#Two suppers, or a Turning-witness]]. |
| **Who brings an orphan?** There is no living parent, or there is no parent who will come. | The town acts as the family. Kin are asked first, then the household that feeds the child, then the **warden-hearth**. An orphan who stands is witnessed the way anyone else is witnessed. An orphan that nobody brings is a miss. | Origin-orphans are older than the grafts. They are a Given child whose only crowd was a continent away, or a child of a road-house with nobody to walk them home ([[The Walking Years]]). What they need is someone to vouch for them. They do not need a second leaf. |
| **An adult who never Turned.** A road-child, a refuse-child, a child of a storm-year, a child raised on the isle, a held-over child who missed the week even so. | They remain **unTurned**, and they do not stand for a Gift at thirty. The town can vouch for them as the person the town raised, and they can still be Struck later. Some hearths file such a person as Kept in order to be kind, and that kindness is sloppy law. | The life is a playable backstory, and it is not a late appointment the Tree will keep. The mercy left is a later door, or an ordinary life with no Tithe, the upkeep a Condition would have asked of them, and with a thinner witness. |
| **Can they stand again?** The family wanted a different colour, or they regret the hug, or a parent is sure the Tree "missed." | **No.** A standing that was completed, whether it ended in a colour or in a hug, under a Tree the warden called sound, happens once. The blessing already says that the Hand does not reach twice by chance. A warden who offers a second Leaf-Fall is not being generous. That warden is inventing a rite the wood has never answered. | A town may correct a **Speaking**, the word the warden said aloud, and a town may not run the fall a second time. [[The Wrong Green]] is the year people cite when they need the precedent. |
| **Our Tree is sick this week.** The canopy is blighted, or it is dying, or the scion is still too young. | Do not stand the children under wood you already know is unsound. Borrow a neighbour's canopy, and borrow it in this same week. A warden who lets a cohort stand under a Tree they know cannot Turn has spent the week, and those children are unTurned. | The next year does not repair a week that has already been spent. |
| **The child is already Struck.** They drowned at nine, or they died and [[Returned]] in the spring, or a contract that no ten-year-old should have been able to reach did reach them. | Bring the child to be **acknowledged**, and do not bring them for a Gift. The Tree does not Strike, and it does not lay a second Condition on top of a first one. Some canopies hug such a child anyway, and the town reads the hug as a welcome. A colour falling on a child who is already Struck is a problem for the Seat, and it is not a second life. | The child is Struck, the town vouches for them again, and the child is guilded if the door they came through is a hazardous one. The tenth year is how the square takes them in, and it is not a chance to do the year over. |
| **Twins, or two colours, or a fall that nobody will name.** | Two children mean two standings and two outcomes. A fall that is mixed, or that people are already arguing, is the reason the **Speaking** exists. Colours the square already knows, the folk can read for themselves, and the warden's word is what makes that reading a civic fact. The texts that cover the rare falls are kept at the First Seat. | Two practices in one square is a common arrangement. A Watcher may describe the same fall alongside the warden, and not over the warden ([[The Watchers]]). Two Speakers who both claim the last word in one square is a fight. |
| **The warden named it wrong.** | The Gift does not move when the word was wrong, and the town is what moves. Someone Speaks a second word, and the child is not stood again. The town trains the child toward the life the leaf actually gave, un-trains the life it named by mistake, and apologises to the neighbour whose week it used. Cite [[The Wrong Green]], which is Harrow's, in Cut-year 248: a hug that was Spoken aloud as copper-green, and two summers the child spent in a garden that had no way to teach them. | The square goes on citing that year. What it has is a civic name that wasted years of a life, and the Tree is not haunted. |
| **White-fire fell, or someone wants to say that it did.** | A warden of a town does not guess at [[Phoenix]], and sends a runner to the First Seat. To name white-fire is to make history, and it is also to make a problem, which the colour table already records. A Speaking of Phoenix that turns out to be false is the loud cousin of the Wrong Green. | Almost no town now living has seen white-fire fall. If the Seat agrees that this child received it, the child cannot hide what they are. The weight of that belongs to the Phoenix Condition, and not to a ruling invented in the square. |
| **A child of the Open Table, or a Yumboe, under our boughs.** | If the child stands during the week of their tenth year, the Tree does what the Tree does for anyone who stands, and if the host will not bring the child, the week can end without them. There is no mission sent to Kumbaan, and there is no warden of the moon. When the fight over the child happens, it is already happening in the port. | [[The Open Table]] holds that the child was never lacking anything. A warden from the mainland holds that a tenth year is a tenth year. Neither of those people is the villain of the week, and the door does not wait while they finish the argument. |
| **The city cannot know every child.** The port holds tens of thousands of people, and hundreds of ten-year-olds come due in the one week. | The children stand **by hearth**, in the slots the week has named, and the square is full of people. What gets remembered is the street that brought the child, together with one pillar who can carry that memory later, and the colours are not written down. | "I am of the third quay" is the sentence by which a person of the city says where they belong. The procedure is written under [Turning-Week in a city](#Turning-Week%20in%20a%20city), and the law of it is on [[Law and Citizenship]]. |

**UnTurned is not the same thing as Kept.** Kept means the leaves hugged the child and the town watched it happen, and UnTurned means the child never stood. From the street the two lives can look alike, because neither one carries a Tithe and neither one carries a guild, and only the Kept life was witnessed at the Tree. Citizenship still depends on who saw you. A neighbour who is unTurned is vouched for as *this child we raised*, and not as a colour, and not as a hug. The fuller statement of the law is on [[Law and Citizenship]].

### When the Leaf-Fall fails

A failure in this rite means that a standing was not completed, and that the Tree has not done some third thing.

- When the Tree is healthy, the child is under the boughs, and the town is watching, a colour falls or the leaves close in a hug. The wardens do not write a third outcome into the book.
- When the week was missed, or the family refused it, or the week was spent under wood that could not Turn, there is no Given. The later doors, the ways a person can still be Struck, are still there. That is the whole of the mercy.
- When the rite is interrupted, by a fire in the square, or a bough coming down, or the child being pulled away before the fall, and the week still has days left and the Tree is sound, the standing is finished. If the week has already gone, the town treats the child as a miss.
- When the Speaking fails, the precedent is [[The Wrong Green]]. The town corrects the word that was said. It does not bargain with the wood for a different life.

A parent who asks *what if the Tree simply does nothing* is asking a question the hearths do not keep a form for. A skeptic will say that is because it is just a tree, and trees that Turn, Turn. A believer will say the Hand does not come to the square to shrug. The working clergy side with the book they have: two outcomes, or the child was not there.

## GM Notes

- **Names.** *Thaeloren* and *the Thallow* are working forms, one liturgical and one worn down, from the naming pass of 2026-08-21. They are safe to polish. Do not add a second everyday name beside them.
- **The continuity of the graft is doctrine in the player-facing body, and it is literal on this side of the wall.** Because every scion is continuous tissue of the one Tree, the sentence "the Turning Trees are her hands" is literally true, and it is not a metaphor. When any town's Tree chooses, the one power is choosing through a grafted hand. See [[Is the Leaf-Mother Real]]. Present the body of this note as doctrine the faithful hold, and never as a cosmology you have demonstrated in front of the players. Skeptics can grant the grafting, because the horticulture is something a person can see, and they deny that the wood has a mind. The note carries `reveals: [keystone-adjacent]`. The player-facing text does not confirm her. This wall does.
- **The hug.** Some of the Kept were chosen so that they would remain whole, and some of them were past her reach this turning. The player-facing body must not say which child is which, and she does not say it either. Do not let a sentence the players can read call the hug a kindness that has been locked, as if the schism were already lost.
- **What is frightening is not the wood.** Resist the urge to make the Tree eerie, or to make the ceremony sinister. That reading is off the tone, and it is off the canon of the keystone, whose floor is sound. See [[Is the Leaf-Mother Real]]. Most of the dread still lives in the Tithes. The one shadow that sits inside the rite is human, and it already has a citation, [[The Wrong Green]]. Do not add a third outcome that people can see, do not add a fall that is cursed, and do not add a leaf that lets someone retry the rite in order to "fix" a hug.
- **The pin that fires a confirmation.** A confirmation can fire. The artifact that carries it is [[The Spent Leaf]], and the desk that keeps the artifact is [[The Remainder]]. The early rungs on [[Is the Leaf-Mother Real]] have to stay deniable. Do not use the Tree as a relic that talks.
- **Once is settled, and settling it does not change the engine.** The door through which a Condition is Given remains the tenth-year week of the High Solstice. A standing that was completed happens once. A miss is not a standing, and a miss does not unlock the next year. Some of the Kept were chosen for wholeness, and some were past her reach this turning. She does not tell those two apart, and no later week is offered as a way to sort them. That unsorted ache is the point. Do not quietly invent a year in which a child gets a second chance. [[The Walking Years]] already said that the door of one week is where the engine has teeth.
- **UnTurned has to stay distinct from Kept.** The custom that players will hear already wants to fold the two together. Let the NPCs be sloppy about the difference. Do not let the note on the law be sloppy about it.
- **The size of a city does not change the engine (Story R.6).** The door for a Gift is still one week. A standing that was completed is still once. UnTurned is still not Kept. A city nests its witnesses into hearths. It does not grow a register, it does not grow a second-chance year, and it does not grow a third thing the Tree can do. Threnmaieth writing the stand down on paper is still the aberration.
- **The offices of the clergy** are built on [[The Tree-Wardens]], and the personal names there are still 🟡. The questions that sit at the edge of the rite live in the table above. The palette that runs from a colour to a Condition stays locked. The history of how the grafts spread is [[The Ages of the Turning]]. The quarrel with the Open Table that can spend a whole week is [[The Open Table]].
- **The dial, and the rules for a city.** The Leaf-Fall stays warm, and it stays communal, and the Tithes carry most of what is frightening in the setting. Do not narrate every settlement as if it were a city. Do not invent a register for a city in order to get around Dunbar's number.
- **Links (GM).** [[The Premise]] holds the engine that is locked, the division into Given, Struck, and Kept, the math of the population, and the Tithe. [[Is the Leaf-Mother Real]] is the keystone this note is pointing at. [[The Spent Leaf]] and [[The Remainder]] are how a campaign can confirm the wood without preaching a sermon.
