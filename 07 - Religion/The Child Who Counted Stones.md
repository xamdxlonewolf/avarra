# The Child Who Counted Stones

> **A road-song with missing numbers.** It is sung, not recited. No two miles preserve the same omissions. The count carries distance and graves in one breath without stopping to explain which is which.

## The counting song

The leader sings a number. The walking line answers with what that number bought.

> **One** for water.<br>
> **Two** for shade.<br>
> **Three** for the woman who knew the ford.<br>
> **Four** for bread.<br>
> **Six** for the boy whose ribbon stayed.

Children object to the missing five the first time. The line answers: **Keep your breath.**

The song continues. A number may buy a dry bed, a ferryman's lie, a shoe stitched twice, a name no one at the next stone knows. Some verses are useful. Some are names. Most do not announce the difference.

At a mile-shrine the leader skips the number cut into the stone. At an unmarked grave they sing one twice. If challenged, the leader says **Keep your breath** and walks on.

The origin has no number in the song. Neither does the Leaf-Fall. The line arrives, stands, and begins again on the home road:

> **One** for water.<br>
> **Two** for shade.<br>
> **Four** for bread.<br>
> **Five** for the ribbon we left on the way.

Children object that five was missing before.

This time the line answers: **Then carry it.**

## How the song is used

- A practical leader uses water and shelter verses to pace the road.
- A bereaved household inserts a name once. If the verse survives three walks, the name has become geography.
- Quay crews count tides instead of stones and leave long silences where the inland line expects a number.
- Hands-town children use it for skipping games. Walkers dislike this until they hear which numbers the children omit.

## GM Notes (the buried truth)

*(Stripped on player-safe export. Keyed to the keystone via `reveals: [keystone-adjacent]`.)*

- **The paradox sits in the omissions.** The road needs a reliable count; mourning breaks and repairs it. A name can become navigation without ceasing to be a name.
- **No official ribbon-colour.** A copper-green rag is not a Taken-In. If a warden "reads" mile-shrine ribbons as leaf-colours, they are doing Hands-era overlay on Walking-era rag. Useful as a wrong NPC; do not make it doctrine.
- **Use it under movement.** Change a verse when the party passes a marked stone. Let an NPC refuse to explain a missing number until camp. This song should cost breath.
