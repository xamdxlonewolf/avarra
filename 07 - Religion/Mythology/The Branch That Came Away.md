# The Branch That Came Away

> **The teaching-story of the Cut,** the first time living wood was taken from a Tree so another town could plant a Tree of its own. It is told about [[The First Cut]], and people still tell it in a town that has a Tree when the neighbour does not. In the telling, a Hand is that planted wood, the town's living copy, and sharing a Hand requires wounding a Hand, because the branch has to be cut from a tree that already stands. The first copy invented both the local mercy and the wait: wood that has taken where people can stand under it, and the question of who is allowed the next branch. The bones of the story stay the same from town to town. The last line will not choose whose hand.

## The story (as told)

A branch came away from the Tree.

A person carried it. They watered it. They put it in new ground.

It died.

They carried another. Or they carried the same one, and the tellings do not agree, and it does not change the ground.

It took.

The origin said: *that wood is ours.*

The new ground said: *that wood is here.*

Both were right, which is why there had to be a paper.

The paper said who may take the next branch. The Tree was not asked. The Tree kept choosing children under both canopies.

A child asked which Tree was the real one.

The mother said: *count the Hands.*

The child counted two, then twenty, then the towns that were not in the count yet.

The child asked who was next.

The mother said: *the soil is thin there.*

The child could see the neighbour's canopy from the ford, and could not say, later, whether they had been counting Hands or counting waits.

A Long-Lived who planted the wood, a person whose life runs long enough to still be here from that planting, will tell you the child's name, and will not tell you which summer, and still will not write who cut.

## The last lines (as told aloud)

The bones above are common, and what a teller adds last is the stance they are willing to be heard taking:

- **Folk (Near Mile):** *The mother was the one who cut. The paper came after, like a fine.*
- **Clergy:** *The warden cut with a leave the college had not invented yet. The paper is how we kept the next one alive.*
- **Devout:** *The branch came away. No one stole a Hand. She gave one.*
- **[[The Watching]]:** *We have always cut living wood. They named one cut the First. The count is optional.*
- **[[The Old Ways]]:** *The ground received a guest. The paper is a mainland word for a meal they did not stay for.*
- **[[The Fair Hand]]:** *The second carrying was a berth. The first was an unlicensed one. That is the whole hymn.*
- **Hands-era children (a Tree-town):** *We Turn here. The neighbour walks. The story is why.*
- **Hands-era children (still walking):** *We can see the canopy. The story does not plant anything.*

Skeptics of every house tell a short ending: *There was not a Hand that learned to walk. There was wood, and a pot, and a college that got to the knife late.*

## Variants

- On [[Maiethorn]] the story is told at Turning-Week, the week the town gathers at its Tree, both in the Motherwood and in kitchens that keep a knife on a ribbon. The Motherwood version starts at *came away*, and the kitchen version starts at *took*.
- On [[Strandoren]] the quay-version never finds a Tree until the hold is opened. The joke is the bill. The bill is not a joke inland.
- On [[Heskoren]] some tellers omit the taking, and the child is still asking who is next when the story stops. Tellers who read the story as a guest-meal start at the meal. Tellers who read it as a first claiming start at the claiming.
- A Seat telling ends on the paper rather than at the ford, and the Speakers who give that telling think it is the real version.
- [[The Sundering Isle|Kumbaan]] does not keep this story. A mainland guest who tells it is offered more supper, which is an answer.

## GM Notes (the buried truth)

*(Stripped on player-safe export. Keyed to the keystone via `reveals: [keystone-adjacent]`.)*

- **The paradox is the point.** You cannot share a Hand without wounding a Hand, and you cannot name a First without inventing a Second that then has to wait. If you lift the paradox out, what remains is a theft-ballad or a sermon, so keep the taking and the queue inside the same count.
- **Do not add "and then she told them to cut."** That line would close the keystone inside a children's stick. If a confirming warmth comes, it does not come from this story. The branch is her tissue, and the story must not say so.
- **Do not pick a last line in the GM's voice.** Which ending an NPC offers is a faster read than any speech about Eoloren. A Road-hand who tells the clergy ending, and then sleeps in the town that was not chosen, has already told you what that office is for.
- **Use it as a tell.** A folk last line travels with a ribboned knife. A Watcher last line travels with pears. An Old Ways last line travels with a bowl. A Fair Hand last line travels with a hold that smells of earth.
