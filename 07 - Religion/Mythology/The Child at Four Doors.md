# The Child at Four Doors

> **A teaching-story told in every house.** The bones of it stay the same, and the last line does not, so the story carries the argument of [[Faiths of the Turning]] in a shape that will not resolve. A Turning, in this telling, is the year a child comes to the one door the town said would be there. Children hear the ending that belongs to their own house first. Travellers hear the other endings, and they do not sleep well.

## The story (as told)

A child came to the year of their Turning and found **four doors** where the town said there was one.

The first door was a Tree, and a leaf hung over it, coloured, waiting.
The second door was a book, open, a line already drawn, a space for a name.
The third door was a river-stone, wet, older than the road.
The fourth door was a table with a place set, and no one sitting in it.

The child asked which door was theirs.

No one would say. Each keeper only said: *this one opens.*

The child sat down in the middle and waited to be chosen, or seen, or signed, or fed.

They waited a long time.

When they looked up, all four doors were open, and the child was still in the middle, and the year had gone on without a colour, or with one — the tellings do not agree — and the child had to stand, and pick, and did, and the story ends before you see which.

## The last lines (by house)

The bones above are common, and what a house adds, once the bones are done, is the faith that house keeps:

- **[[The Leaf-Mother|Motherfaith]] (Tender):** *The Mother had opened the Tree before the child sat down. The waiting was the hug. They were wanted either way.*
- **Motherfaith (Wanting):** *The Mother opened one door and the child was looking at the other three. That is how a person is passed over: by having too many doors.*
- **[[The Watching]]:** *The Tree had been looking the whole time. The other three doors were people talking. The child was seen. That was the Turning. The leaf was optional.*
- **[[The Fair Hand]]:** *The child signed by sitting. Waiting is a term. Someone should have told them the price of a year in the middle.*
- **[[The Old Ways]]:** *The stone was wet before the Tree was planted. The child was already the land's. The waiting was weather.*
- **[[The Open Table]]:** *The place was set. The child was the guest, then the household. There was never a test. The other doors are how hungry people decorate a supper.*

Skeptics of every faith tell a seventh ending, which is not kind and is very short: *There were not four doors. There was a child, and a year, and a town that needed a story.*

## Variants

- On [[Maiethorn]] the four doors are carved on the underside of old door-beams, and the wardens say the carving is decoration.
- On [[Strandoren]] the story is a quay-song, and it has a chorus the Bound like to sing: *name the term or get up.*
- On [[Heskoren]] the tellers put the river-stone door first and the Tree third, and some of them leave the book out entirely.
- On [[The Sundering Isle|Kumbaan]] the only door in the story is the table. The other doors are "mainland verses," and people tell them as a curiosity after the dishes, if a guest insists on hearing them.
- A school-version told in Threnmaieth adds a clerk who writes all four doors down on a roll. The children are not meant to laugh. They do.

## GM Notes (the buried truth)

*(Stripped on player-safe export. Keyed to the keystone via `reveals: [keystone-adjacent]`.)*

- **The paradox is the point.** The child cannot choose a door without already having been claimed, and the child cannot be claimed without choosing. Each faith makes the middle bearable by deciding that *its* door was the one that had been open the whole time. None of the last lines is the locked cosmology. The locked cosmology is that a mother reached, and the reaching cost her, and she did not reach everyone in the same way. The fable will not say that. If it did, it would stop being useful.
- **Use the ending as a tell.** The last line an NPC chooses will show you their stance faster than a speech about doctrine. A household that keeps more than one faith will argue the last line at the table, and that argument is cheap, true, and playable.
- **Do not write an official fifth door.** The Open Table already refuses the frame of four doors. A GM who adds "and then the Mother sat down with them" has closed the keystone inside a children's story. If a confirming warmth is ever going to arrive, save it for that beat, and do not spend it here.
