# The Open Table

> **The isolate faith of [[The Sundering Isle|Kumbaan]].** It grew with no [[Turning Tree|Tree]] and no Mother-name. The [[Yumboe|good people]] keep the night, set a place for hands that have no bodies, and hold that a stranger at the table is the holiest thing in the world, holier than a colour, a graft, or a bargain.

On the mainland the everyday name is **the Open Table**, and sailors say **the Moon-Feast**. Their own name is **Ndeyaan** (ndey-AHN), the night-keeping. In full courtesy the name is **Bakhna Ndeya**, "the good night." The opening of every meal that matters is already their creed: *"No one is a stranger twice."*

## Shape of the faith (at a glance)

Ndeyaan is a hospitality-religion, and the founding fact is the table. To feed and to be fed is how the living sit with the staying-kin, the hands that remained, and with anyone the storm-wall did not keep out. There is no Leaf-Fall, because, as they say, there was nothing missing to give.

The practice is the meal: Ndeyaan has almost no clergy and no chapel, because the rite *is* supper under the moon, a place set for hands you can see and for a body you cannot, with daylight reserved for sleep. A Yumboe who keeps this on the mainland is still of the Open Table. A mainlander who sits once is a guest, and a mainlander who sits twice is kin.

What they hold as divine is the hill, the moon, and the ones who stayed, and it is not a mother and not a ledger. The dead do not leave; they stay as hands. Whether those hands *are* the dead, which is the Staying, or the hill's own courtesy, which is the Serving, is the house-argument, and neither reading makes the good people into ghosts. The Yumboe are a living Kind, the idiom is ancestral, and the bodies are warm.

You can know a house of the faith by an extra place laid, even when the household is poor. The unseen hands are what make that place possible. That is not a metaphor they use; it is the furniture.

## Cosmology & doctrine (as believed)

Wholeness is the default: the mainland invented a hunger and called it a gift, and on Kumbaan you are born Yumboe and that is enough. If the good people become anything else, they do not become it at a Tree, because there is none. The how of any other becoming is not a doctrine they owe strangers. It is a family matter, and they are not in a hurry.

The dead stay to serve: folklore on the mainland calls Yumboes spirits of the dead, and the Yumboe find this rude and, in a sideways way, nearly true, because the *hands* are the staying-kin: the living host, and the staying pour. A feast that outshines its means is a theological statement, because the household is larger than the bodies you can count.

The moon wakes both, so night is the shared day of the living and the staying, and daylight is a Sabbath of sleep, not a fallen hour. Mainland noon-faiths are *loud* the way a guest who will not sit is loud.

A stranger is the sacrament, and the storm-wall keeps the world out, and anyone who crosses it has already been through drowning-water, so you do not test them with a colour; you feed them, and the second supper is the rite of belonging. This is why a lone Yumboe on [[Strandoren]] becomes a host wherever they land, still keeping Ndeyaan with whatever table they can make.

The Mother, as they hear her, is a story about a quiet table, told too loud. They do not hate her, having never met her, and they do not understand why a people would need to be *chosen* in order to be wanted.

## Practice

**The moon-feast** is the ordinary high rite: night, a hill-hall or open turf, the extra place, and the unseen hands fetching and pouring. There are songs, and there is no colour-reading and no terms recited.

**The place set** is laid even in a poor house. To omit it is the one real impiety, not because the staying starve (they do not) but because you have counted the household too small.

**No one is a stranger twice** is spoken at the second supper. The first night you are a guest, and a guest is already sacred; the second night you are of the table. Mainlanders who hear this as a proverb have missed that it is a *legal* sentence in all but parchment.

**Daylight sleep** is rest, and the glare is not holy and not cursed. It is when the good people and the staying-kin rest. A Yumboe abroad will still take their long rest when the moon is down if they can, and they will not make a scene about it.

## Clergy & institutions

The working house is [[The Table-Keepers]].

**Table-keepers**, also called **hill-hosts**, are almost not clergy. The office is the person whose hall the valley comes to on the bright nights, and it is a reputation rather than a titled post. They have one job, which is to keep the table, and the full faction is [[The Table-Keepers]].

**Shore-sitters**, also called **wall-waiters**, are the other isle job: they sit the storm-beach and wait, they do not swim out, and the living go uphill to a table. This is flavor rather than a mainland order, and the full faction is [[The Shore-Sitters]].

A Yumboe abroad is a table-keeper by necessity. One person can keep Ndeyaan in a Lestrand attic, and that is how the faith travels, when it travels at all. It recruits by faith, by who will lay the extra place, and not by Kind. There is no mission sent out from Kumbaan.

## The live schism (the faith arguing with itself)

1. **The Staying.** The unseen hands *are* the dead: grandparents, the drowned, the ones the hill kept. The reading is ancestral and literal, older in the tellings, and still common in the deep-hill halls. It is not a ghost-cult, because the dead are *family who took the serving-work*.

2. **The Serving.** The hands are the hill's own courtesy, the good people's oldest wonder, and not a roster of names. This reading is gentler, it is the majority in the surface-facing halls, and it is easier to explain to a mainland guest who has gone pale.

Both branches set the extra place. A Serving host who will not say the dead's names and a Staying host who will not stop saying them will still pass the same cup. The argument is for after the dishes.

## Two suppers, or a Turning-witness

On Kumbaan, *no one is a stranger twice* is how you belong. On the mainland, you belong because a town watched you Turn, while a host of this faith counts a guest as family after two suppers and a town clerk wants to know where the person was Turned.

This is the faith's edge on the mainland, and it does not need a mission or a chapel on [[Strandoren]]. It needs a room, a tenth year, and a clerk who counts belonging a different way.

### Two suppers, one week

**The everyday collision is citizenship.** A guest sits a second night in a Lestrand attic, the host says the sentence and means it, and a quay clerk, a Book-hand, or a warden passing through wants a Turning-witness, someone who saw the person Turn, before they will treat that person as *of a place*. Inside the room the guest is kin, and outside the door the same person is still a guest. Neither office is cruel, and the sentence does not mean the same thing outside the room.

**The week that can close is the Given-door.** A child of the table, whether isle-raised, convert-raised, or a sailor's tenth summer in a port, reaches Turning-Week. The host will not walk them to a [[Turning Tree]], because they were never lacking, and a neighbour or a town-warden will. The Given-door is **one week** ([[Turning Tree#Questions a warden gets asked]]). If the argument outlasts Eolthael, the child is unTurned, and although Struck doors still open later, that is not a comfort the host asked for, and not a victory the warden can file as kindness.

This is not a Kumbaan crusade, because the child is already on the mainland and the storm-wall stayed where it was.

When a tenth year is in the house, the extra place is set slightly back, and the host says the sentence or refuses to take the child down to the Tree in the morning. A warden, a clerk, or a neighbour is in the street with a list of this year's children. In Hale-month the same list is manners, and in Eolthael it is a child's life.

**Mbela** *(MBEH-lah)* keeps an attic off a Lestrand salt-stair and will not spend a child's week on a colour. **Sorel** of the quay will not file a second supper as belonging, and will walk a tenth-year neighbour to the Tree if the host will not. They are not enemies; they count belonging differently, and they share a stair.

Romanticizing the Moon-Feast is itself a slight, and printing the sentence on a luck-card is how Lestrand files the whole faith. The woman in the attic still sets the place.

House detail, who lays the plate and what they refuse, is [[The Table-Keepers]].

## In-world text

What follows is a place-setting blessing, said to the empty plate before the hands arrive, taught to children, and heard once, badly copied, by a Strandor sailor who did come back.

> *We have counted you. Sit. If you have a name we have not learned it yet; if you have a colour we have no tree to read it. The moon is enough light. Eat, and be the household.*

It reads as hospitality, almost twee, and it also says that being wanted never depended on a colour. The sailor who copied it thought it was a grace, and what was copied is a claim that *wanted* was never a gift.

## GM Notes
*(Stripped on player-safe export. Keyed to the keystone via `reveals: [keystone-adjacent, the-other-hands]`.)*

The name Ndeyaan, the Open Table, is locked (2026-08-23), and the texture is still open. What is fixed is an isolate cosmology, an ancestral flavour reinvented as a living faith rather than as literal ghosts, and a faith that owes nothing to the Leaf-Mother or her household.

Read the place-setting blessing underneath the manners. It was authored per `oblique-worldbuilding`, and on its face it is hospitality, while underneath it is a whole cosmology that never needed the Leaf-Fall, the sharpest keystone experiment in the world, spoken as table-manners.

This is the cleanest control in the set, a cosmology with zero Motherfaith contamination, and, per [[The Other Hands]], the staying-kin are **not of her household**. Do not fold the staying-kin into Threnvaeth or anyone else. Yumboes have no Gifts on the isle, and Struck are possible but rare, as [[Yumboe]] has it. Their doctrine, *we were never lacking*, is still a late beat.

**Links (GM).** [[Is the Leaf-Mother Real]] · [[The Other Hands]].

They are not ghosts. The source myth calls Yumboes ancestral spirits, and the setting reinvented them as a playable Kind. Ndeyaan keeps the ancestral *idiom* and parks it in the hands, not in the people. The Returned already own literal undeath. If a table leans Staying, play it as family-who-serve, and not as a haunt.

Hospitality is the ~5% dial at its gentlest, until a tenth year is in the room. A Yumboe host is wonder, and not a threat. Do not turn the Open Table into a trap-cult, or into an "eat and you belong forever" fairy-bargain, because that is the Fair Hand's grammar leaking in. "No one is a stranger twice" is courtesy with legal weight, and it is not a geas. The scare, when you need one, is the week closing while two good offices argue.

Play the clash between the sentence and witnessed citizenship, and play the host who will not walk a child to a Tree. Do not invent a mission-chapter to make the faith reachable, and treat Mbela and Sorel as scene-faces rather than as pivots.

The calendar is already a tell. Kumbaan keeps the moon, and not the High Solstice ([[The Reckoning of the Year]]). That is a keystone tell, a people outside her ordinary hand keeping a different sky. Ndeyaan is the faith-face of that fact, so don't over-explain it: let a mainland priest notice that their holiest week is, here, just weather.

You can start it at the table, and you do not need a council, only one supper and one square. At night the extra place is set slightly back, a second supper is underway or about to be, and the host says the sentence or will not take a ten-year-old to the Tree. In the morning a warden, a clerk, or a neighbour is in the street with a list, a piece of gossip, or a kind offer. Then ask whether the week has days left, because Hale-month plays as manners and Eolthael plays as a life.
