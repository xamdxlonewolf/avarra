# The Leaf-Mother

> **The faith built around the [[Turning Tree]].** Some hold that the **Leaf-Mother** blessed the world with the Conditions, the gifts of a becoming beyond the life a person was born into, and that the Turning Trees are her hands reaching down to *choose*, so that each Leaf-Fall, the town's gathering at a child's tenth-year solstice, is an act of a mother's love. This is **a belief held by many, not confirmed cosmology**: skeptics are sincere and full members of society, and the faith itself is built to make room for them. What follows is the doctrine, the practice, the clergy, and the live schism. Who they mean by the name, as a presence, is [[Maieth]]. The rite's object is the [[Turning Tree]], the Conditions it gives are on [[Conditions]], and the felt week and the hours beside it are [[What the Year Feels Like]].

## Shape of the faith (at a glance)

This is a gift-religion, and it is not a salvation-religion, so no one is being *rescued* from a fallen state. The founding fact is that the world received *gifts*, the Conditions, and goes on receiving them each solstice. The faith is a long answer to one question: how do you live worthy of a gift?

What makes you of the Motherfaith is what you **do**. You bring your children to the Tree, you tend your Tithe honestly, and you keep the festival, and that practice counts for far more than what you privately believe about *who* does the choosing. This is the structural reason a devout believer and a quiet skeptic can stand at the same Leaf-Fall and both belong. **Right practice binds; right belief is argued about.**

The divine character is a mother: benevolent, attentive, generous, and *quiet*. She does not thunder, command, or demand worship; she gives, and (the faithful say) she watches. The maternal frame is the whole tone: you don't fear the Mother, you try not to waste what she hands you.

The Motherfaith is one faith among several: it is the dominant faith of the Turning lands, and it is **not the only religion**. The others, and the ways they coexist, compete, and syncretize, live in [[Faiths of the Turning]]. The Leaf-Mother is not written here as the universe's sole power; she is *this* people's Mother.

## Cosmology & doctrine (as believed)

*What follows is presented as the faithful hold it, and nobody in the world has proved it.*

In the beginning, the faithful say, the world was only ever *born into*. People were their Kind, the people they were by birth, and nothing more. The Leaf-Mother's gift was **becoming**: the capacity to be given something beyond your birth, a Condition the world would need. They call that first gift the Giving. The first Tree, the [[Turning Tree#The Awakening Tree|Awakening Tree]], is where she first reached down, and every town Tree is a grafted **Hand of the Mother**, a living cut of that first Tree, so that no child is too far from her reach.

Doctrine holds that she chooses, and that she chooses well. The colour a child receives is *chosen*, looked-at, intended, and fitted to the child and to the town's need. Believers point to gifts that "fit too well" to be accident, and skeptics call that hindsight. The schism below is that argument.

The Tithe is the weight of a gift, and it is not a punishment. Every Condition costs upkeep, as [[Conditions]] sets out. The faith reads the cost as *honest weight*: a real gift has a real charge to carry, and tending it well is the ordinary devotion of a lifetime. **"Tend what you're given"** is the closest thing the faith has to a first commandment.

To be Kept, hugged by the leaves and given no colour, as [[Turning Tree]] describes it, is doctrinally a *gift of being left whole*, and not an oversight. This is where the schism bites hardest, in the argument over what that hug meant.

She has no throne to visit and no voice that answers on demand. A person meets her at the Tree, in the turning of a life, and in the fittingness of things, and never by summoning her. The faithful hold that this quietness is *intended*, because a mother lets her children live, and that same quiet is exactly what leaves room for honest doubt.

## Practice (what the faithful actually do)

Religion here is lived, and not only believed. The Motherfaith is dense with practice, which is what lets skeptics belong by *doing*.

**The Leaf-Fall** is the central rite, a child's tenth-year solstice Turning, and it is at once sacrament, coming-of-age, and the town's great festival. The full staging, and the questions a warden actually gets, a missed week, a refusal, orphans, and whether anyone stands twice, are in [[Turning Tree]]. The rite's cited shadow, the mis-speaking the faith still tells, is [[The Wrong Green]].

**The Tending** is the ongoing, private devotion of *paying your Tithe well*. The religious frame is simple: to tend your Condition honestly is to honour the gift. A Two-Bodied who runs their other self, and a Long-Lived who keeps their vigils, are doing what the faith calls worship-by-living, and not just upkeep.

**The Turning-week festival** is the solstice week around the Leaf-Falls: markets, feasts, the reunion of families, and the naming of the year's new Given and new Kept. It is the emotional high point of the calendar, the week [[The Reckoning of the Year]] calls the High Solstice, and also Turning-Week.

**Threshold rites for the Struck** grew up because the Struck receive a Condition *away* from the Tree, at a drowning survived, an oath sworn, or a death crossed, as [[Conditions]] has it. The faith brings a Struck person to the Tree *after* the fact, not to be Turned, because the Tree doesn't Strike, but to be **acknowledged**, folded into the community of the Given rather than left as an anomaly. How warmly a town does this is itself contested, and that contest is the schism below.

**Tree-tending** is the civic and sacred care of the town's Turning Tree: watering rites, warding against blight, and the graft-rites when a new scion, a living cut for planting, is set in the ground. The **tree-wardens**, the clergy described below, handle that care.

## Clergy & institutions

The working house is [[The Tree-Wardens]].

**Tree-wardens** are the working clergy, one order with offices, and not rival chapters. They tend the town Tree, they officiate the Leaf-Fall, and they hold the **authoritative reading of the colours**. As [[Turning Tree]] has it, common colours are folk-known, and the wardens confirm them and rule on the rare, the subtle, and the mixed falls. That gives the clergy a *needed civic function*, and not only a devotional one, which is a concrete reason society organizes around them.

**The First Seat** is a *college*, and not a throne, in the Motherwood about the Awakening Tree. It is a destination of pilgrimage. It authorizes graft-scions, and the cuts may come from the origin or from a healthy town Tree acting on the origin's behalf. It confirms Road-hands and Speakers, and it does not appoint every town-warden.

The economic base is ordinary and local. Towns house the warden-hearth, Turning-Week supplies the festival share, Cutting-leave carries a fee framed as carrying-earth, the cost of carrying the living cut, and pilgrimage keeps the Seat. The wardens are **not** the Tithe-infrastructure sector, which is [[Tithe-Infrastructure]].

## The live schism (the faith arguing with itself)

The faith is **not monolithic**, and its central argument is a genuine, good-faith one that no one can settle, which is exactly why it endures. It turns on three questions the doctrine can't prove.

1. **The Kept — spared, or slighted?** If the Mother blesses everyone, why does the Tree hug so many children and give them no colour at all? The **Tender reading** is the majority, and it is the orthodox one. It says the Kept are *given wholeness*, a real gift chosen for them, and that to be Kept is to be trusted to need nothing added. The **Wanting reading** is the minority. It says the Kept were *passed over*, loved but not chosen, and that the doctrine of "wholeness" is a kindness the faithful tell them. This reading quietly stings, and it shapes how some Kept feel about the festival.

2. **The Struck — still hers, or not?** The Struck receive a Condition the Tree never handed out. Did the Mother reach through the threshold too, or did *something else* give the gift? The **Wide-Hand reading** says she meets people at every door, the drowning, the oath, and the death, and that the Tree is only her *ordinary* hand, so the Struck are fully hers. The **Narrow-Hand reading** says she gives only at the Tree, and that the Struck received their Condition by *some other means*, which is why this reading finds the Struck "a little suspect." That suspicion is the seed of real social friction toward the Struck.

3. **Does a mind choose at all?** This is the believer and skeptic line proper. **Believers** say the fittingness of the gifts, the care of the Kept, and the way the Struck feel *received* add up to a Someone. **Skeptics** say it is just what the Trees do, biology, or a phenomenon so old that it wears a face people gave it. The grafting is observable horticulture, a cut of wood set into new ground, and the *choosing mind* is an inference they decline to make. The faith's own genius is that it is **orthopraxic**. A skeptic who tends their Tithe and brings their children to the Tree is a *good member of the Motherfaith*. Belief in the Mother is honoured, and it is not policed, which keeps the skeptic sincere and un-persecuted.

Every branch above is a *good-faith* position, and no house is a villain-theology. Being a skeptic costs nothing day to day, because right practice binds. The questions stay live because no one in the square can close them.

## Two sayings

The Tender's blessing is the first of them, spoken over each child at the Leaf-Fall:

> *"What falls to you, wear well. What is kept from you, keep. The Hand that reaches is the Mother's own — and the Mother does not reach twice by chance."*

It is comfort, and it carries the "tend your gift" ethic. Believers hear **intent** in it, skeptics hear a proverb about not asking the wood twice, and both can say the words.

A Kept-child's saying, folk, half-consoling and half-barbed, runs:

> *"The Tree that gives to all its neighbours held me instead. I have decided that is not the same as being forgotten."*

It is the Tender reading of the Kept, made personal and a little defiant. *"I have decided"* is a choice made against doubt, and not a certainty.

## GM Notes

Player-facing text on this page is keystone-adjacent. Doctrine taken as true *is* the keystone, so the body must present it as **belief**. Be reverent where the faithful are reverent, and let skeptics keep their full dignity. Never let a player-facing beat confirm the Mother. If a campaign fires the pin, it lands as *warmth with an ache*, which is [[The Spent Leaf]] and [[Is the Leaf-Mother Real#If confirmation lands]].

The schism is two-sided, and not a case of one reading being the whole truth. Believers are right about the big things: she is real, present, and kind. The constraint, that she is bounded and costly, is why skeptics have hold of something real. They have felt the edge, the thinning at the margins, the unmet Struck, the Kept who seem passed over, and they have mislabelled a *limit* as an *absence*. Wide-Hand is right that she reaches the thresholds. Narrow-Hand's unease is earned, because she reaches those thresholds only at the farthest edge of her arm, and even then by [[The Other Hands|household hands]]. The Kept are genuinely *both*, some chosen for wholeness and some past her reach, with no way to tell which. No faction holds the whole shape.

The two texts point, and they do not close: the Tender's blessing is, unknowingly, a true statement of intent. The Kept-child's saying seeds the warm answer, that the embrace was a choice *for some* and that they were not forgotten, without sorting which Kept were which. Do not let either quote close the keystone in a player's ear.

The schism is the reusable engine. The three questions above are the faith's inexhaustible fuel for NPCs, factions, and family drama, a Kept sibling, a Struck spouse, a skeptic parent. Reach for them whenever a scene needs belief-shaped conflict. Per [[The Other Hands]], Narrow-Hand is right that she does not Strike, and Wide-Hand is right that the catch is still hers, her household and her allowance. Do not let a player-facing scene award the win to either branch.

The keyed tags are `keystone-adjacent`, for clue and doctrine with no confirmation in the body, and `the-other-hands`, because this wall names the household.

The clergy orders are built as [[The Tree-Wardens]], and the names are still open. The other four houses are [[The Watchers]], [[The Book-Hands]], [[The Door-Keepers]], and [[The Table-Keepers]]. The sacred calendar is locked in [[The Reckoning of the Year]], the faith's Tithe-economy lives in [[Economy and the Tithe]], and the *other* religions are built in [[Faiths of the Turning]]. What is fixed here is gift-not-salvation, orthopraxy-over-orthodoxy, the maternal and quiet divine character, the practice set, the tree-warden clergy, and the three-branch schism.

Read the two sayings underneath as well as on their face. They were authored per `oblique-worldbuilding`, and each carries doctrine on its face while quietly pointing at the keystone. On its face the Tender's blessing is comfort, and underneath it believers hear intent. Underneath the Kept-child's saying, *"I have decided"* is a choice made against doubt. Point at the keystone only through [[Is the Leaf-Mother Real]]; the player body does not.
