# The Old Ways

> **The ancestor-and-land faith of [[Heskoren]].** It predates the grafts, and it says the Mother's reach is new here. The land, on this account, was already making children of its own, the [[Conditions|Struck]] and the [[The Taken-In|Taken-In]], at rivers, drowning-pools, oath-stones, and groves. The Trees are *guests*, and they are not the source.

Everyday, and in their own common speech, the name is **the Old Ways**. The liturgical name is **Vaeloren** (vay-LOR-en), from *vael* and *oren*. They use **vael** in an older sense: not "a Condition Given," a becoming handed down as a leaf-colour, but **what the land yields**. They say the Motherfaith *narrowed* the word until it meant only a leaf-colour. Pious speech in Vaethorn calls them **the Before-Faith**, and the Taken-In among them say **the Green Kin**.

## Shape of the faith (at a glance)

This is a land-and-kin religion, and the founding fact is that *place* makes people. A river that has drowned someone and returned them, a hill that took someone in, and an oath-stone that heard a vow are **the old doors**. The grafted Tree is a new door, and it is welcome if it behaves.

You keep the Old Ways by walking, not by watching a colour fall. You go to the doors, you feed the kin who stayed, and you stand for the Struck when a town will not, and belief about the Mother is secondary. Many Kin-speakers also bring a child to a healthy graft when they can reach one.

What they hold as divine is many, and the dead stay: not one mother, and not a ledger, but hills, waters, old trees (the wild kin of motherwood, and not the grafts), and the people who went into them. Ancestors are not ghosts to be feared, [[Returned|the Returned]] being a different door, and the ancestors of this faith are *kin who remained in the ground*.

The mark of the faith is a worn token from your door, a river-stone, a grove-twig, or a pinch of hill, carried the way a Settler carries a book. No two tokens look alike, and that difference is the theology.

## Cosmology & doctrine (as believed)

The land was first: before grafts and before pilgrimage, Heskoren's people already became things other than the people they were born. The Struck are not a problem the Motherfaith has failed to place; they are the *oldest* rite, the land doing what the land does, and the Taken-In are the grove's own children rather than a frontier embarrassment.

**Vael** meant yield: grain, game, a child returned from water, a person the hill kept. The Motherfaith's *vael*, a Given Condition, is a *special case* they mistook for the whole word. To call a leaf a *vael* is fine, and to say that only a leaf is a *vael* is a theft of speech.

Grafts are guests, and a healthy Tree is a new kin-house: you may tend it, and you may Turn under it, and you may not pretend it invented becoming, or that a town without one is empty of the sacred. A sickening Tree is a guest who is dying in your grove, and the fight with the tree-wardens is *who is host*.

The Kept are a year the land had no door, and the Old Ways will not call that spared, or slighted, or free labour. Some years a door does not open for you, and you are still the land's, whether the opening comes next year, in the drowning-year, or never. The waiting that Vaethorn sanctifies as pilgrimage is, here, just weather.

The Mother is a late name, either the land wearing a face the newcomers needed, which is the Guest-reading, or a claiming, which is the First-reading. Both readings agree she did not *arrive first*.

## Practice

**Threshold-walking** happens at the year's hinges, the turns of the year, and not only at [[The Reckoning of the Year|High Solstice]]. People walk the old doors of their district. They greet the water, the hill, and the stone, they leave food, and they do not ask for a colour; they ask to be *known*.

**Ancestor-meals** set a place at the board for the ones in the ground. The meal is quiet and domestic, easy to miss as religion if you came from Maiethorn looking for a chapel, and the courtesy is the rite.

**Standing for the Struck** is what they do when a person changes later and alone. The Old Ways do not take that person to a Tree to be *acknowledged as Given*. They take them back to the door that made them, if it can be found, and *name them kin*. The Motherfaith's quiet receiving-rite and this one sometimes happen in the same week, for the same person, and the argument is which name goes first.

**The contested grove** is an ailing graft standing in an old Taken-In green. That is the signature fight: warden against door-keeper, guest against host, the scarcity that sends people on pilgrimage against the sentence "we had ways." It is a quarrel between neighbours who both love the wood.

## Clergy & institutions

The working house is [[The Door-Keepers]].

**Door-keepers**, also called **Kin-speakers**, know which doors still open, speak at ancestor-meals, and stand for the unvouched Struck, the ones no one has yet stood for, when they can. This is not a hierarchy so much as a reputation, and a famous door-keeper is just the one three valleys will walk to, with the two names often belonging to the same person.

The Old Ways have **no seat**, and they cannot have a mother-church, because the land is the seat. Vaethorn's [[The Tree-Wardens|tree-wardens]] find this infuriating and, in a dry year, quietly useful. The door-keepers walk the doors, and they do not carry scions, the young grafted wood a new Tree is planted from.

## The live schism (the faith arguing with itself)

1. **The Guest-reading.** The Mother is the land wearing a face the newcomers needed, the land in a new dress, and the grafts are kin. The reading is syncretic, and it is common in Vaethorn towns that finally got a Tree and did not want to throw their grandparents out of the rite. They will pilgrimage *and* walk the doors.

2. **The First-reading.** The grafts are a claiming, and the old doors were enough. This reading is harder, and it lives in the thinner-reach districts, the ones still waiting on a scion that will not take. They will tend a dying graft as a *guest in their house* and still refuse the Tender's blessing.

Both branches revere the Struck and the Taken-In, and that shared reverence is the load-bearing courtesy. A reading that started pitying them as "unreached" has already become Motherfaith.

## In-world text

Said at a drowning-stone on Heskoren's wet margin, the year after a graft sickened and a child came back from the river Changed:

> *The tree is young. The water is not. If a mother sent the water she sent it before she sent the leaf, and she has not told the wardens. We will feed both. We will not let either pretend it was first.*

It is frontier courtesy, a little barbed, and it is the Old Ways' whole case. They have correctly felt that becoming happens at doors with no healthy Tree.

**Links (GM).** [[Is the Leaf-Mother Real]] · [[The Other Hands]].

They are half-right at the edge. Per [[The Other Hands]] the land-doors are real, Vaelorn, Ornhael, and Eloren among them, and she does not walk those doors herself. She authorizes them. The Old Ways have built a whole religion on the part she does not do in person. Keep their grievance real: the grafts *are* late, and the Motherfaith *did* talk as if becoming were new. Keep their miss intact: the guests came with her leave.

They are the frontier mirror of the Watching. These are two old faiths, on opposite edges of the world, both remembering something the Motherfaith smooths over. The Watching remembers a Before *at the Tree*, and the Old Ways remember a Before *without* one. If a campaign triangulates the keystone, these two plus [[The Open Table]] are the corners. Do not let them agree too easily, because they would not.

Vaethorn's guilt-theology is the enemy, not the warden, and the injustice on this continent is scarcity read as unworthiness. The Old Ways refuse that reading, because a closed door is weather. A Guest-reading Kin-speaker and a Tender-reading warden can share a dying grove in good faith. A Narrow-Hand preacher calling the Struck "not hers" is who the Old Ways will not eat with.

The older *vael* is a scholar-tell. Let someone notice that the frontier still uses the word for a harvest and a returned child, and that the Motherfaith narrowed it. That is memetic depth, and not a pronunciamento.
