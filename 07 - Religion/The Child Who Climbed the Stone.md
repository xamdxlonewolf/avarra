# The Child Who Climbed the Stone

> **A square-game mistaken for a fable.** The learned title is *The Child Who Climbed the Stone*. Children call it **Here and Far**. It has no fixed narrator, no doctrinal endings, and no Long-Lived witness. A town can play it only if an old mile-stone still stands where a local Tree later made a square.

## Here and Far

One child stands on the mile-stone and is **Here**. Everyone else waits at the square's edge and is **Far**.

Far calls: **How many days?**

Here must answer: **None. The Tree is here.**

Far advances one paving-stone and names something still carried from the road:

> A bed kept empty.<br>
> A cup set before dawn.<br>
> A ribbon gone white.<br>
> A child sent to the neighbour.

Here may answer only **Already here.** If Here laughs, repeats a thing already named, or steps down, the nearest child takes the stone.

The game should end when Far reaches the marker. It rarely does. The list grows faster than the square is wide. Children borrow grievances from adults, invent impossible cargo, and argue whether *a grave* and *a name* are one thing or two. Shopkeepers walk through the line. Carts scatter it. The child on the stone keeps shouting **Already here** at people who are plainly going somewhere.

There is one rule adults never teach and every square knows: when a real walking household crosses the game, play stops. Here gets down. The children move the marker aside for the packs.

Afterward they put it back. Sometimes in the same socket.

## What survives outside the square

- A Road-hand calls moving the marker courtesy. A paving clerk calls putting it back vandalism.
- In a town with no waiting neighbours, children run out of true things and begin naming weather.
- Past the ford, children play without a centre stone. **Here** stands where the canopy can be seen. No one agrees whether that is mockery.
- Adults tell the game as a story only after they have forgotten how to lose it.

## GM Notes (the buried truth)

*(Stripped on player-safe export. Keyed to the keystone via `reveals: [keystone-adjacent]`.)*

- **The paradox is enacted, not explained.** The marker is obsolete enough to obstruct a cart and current enough to stop the game when walkers arrive. Moving it and restoring it are both ordinary acts, not rival morals.
- **Use it in a scene.** Let a chase cut through the line, let a PC be assigned Here, or let a walking family arrive while the party is listening. The square supplies voices without an NPC reciting doctrine.
- **No official ending.** The physical interruption is the ending. Do not add a verdict from the Mother, the Seat, or the GM.
