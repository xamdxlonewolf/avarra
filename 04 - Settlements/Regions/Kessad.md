# Kessad

> A rich southern province taken within living memory — the model of *contested gratitude*. Materially better off under the Empire and unable to deny it; also full of people who buried someone in the taking. The recently-pacified frontier, where the campaign's moral tension runs hottest.

## Overview

Kessad is the hot, fertile southern lowland — deltas, canals-older-than-the-Empire, walled river-cities that were sophisticated and self-governing when the Empire arrived a couple of generations ago. That is the friction: Kessad was not a wilderness that the Empire civilized. It was already a civilization, with its own courts, coin, and pride, and it *lost.* Now it is prosperous, orderly, well-fed, fairly judged — and it remembers being its own.

The educated Kessadi make the uncomfortable case out loud: the roads are better, the granaries fuller, the courts fairer than the ones their grandparents ran. And also: their grandparents' courts were *theirs.* Both halves of that sentence are true, and the province lives in the gap between them. This is the province a thoughtful rebellion would recruit from first.

## Geography & Climate

Warm to hot, wet, staggeringly fertile — the best growing ground in the Empire, two crops a year where the basin gets one. The Kessadi delta-cities are ancient and dense, threaded with pre-imperial canals the Empire simply adopted and improved. The new imperial trunk-road to the south is visibly *new* — cut straight through old country in one determined generation.

## Settlements & Sites

- **Old walled river-cities** (proposed capital: **Ish-Karan**) — pre-imperial, proud, still administered largely by Kessadi notables under an imperial governor.
- New garrison-towns along the trunk road — obviously imperial, obviously recent, watched with mixed feelings.
- Shrines and law-courts of the *old* Kessadi order, some preserved as heritage, some quietly still operating in the cracks of imperial law.

> The tell of a recent conquest: two sets of everything. Two calendars in use, two names for every street, two memories of the same war — the imperial one (a *pacification*, bloodless in the telling) and the Kessadi one (a siege, with a body count). The circuit court's job here is often to adjudicate which memory the law will honour. That is not a neutral act, and everyone knows it.

## GM Notes

- The pre-imperial canals and the "two of everything" texture are useful later: Kessad is old, and old places are where the [[Climate & Terrain|land-older-than-the-Empire]] motif can surface without tipping anything.
