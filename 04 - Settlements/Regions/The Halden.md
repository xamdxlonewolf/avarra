# The Halden

> A long-integrated upland province — the model of *content*. Conquered so long ago that the conquest is a schoolroom date, not a wound; proud of its own distinct character and quietly certain the Empire is the best bargain going.

## Overview

The Halden is hard hill-country shouldering the northern basin: pasture, ore, terraced grain, stone villages, and a famously stubborn people who will tell you at length how different they are from the soft folk of the Aurenne — in the Empire's common tongue, over Empire-taxed ale, having just come from an Empire circuit court where they won. Integration here is total *and* affectionate. The Halden keep their own dialect, their hill-gods, their festivals; they also send more clerks and officers into imperial service, per head, than almost anywhere.

This is the province that proves the promise from [[The Shape of the Empire]]: a march becomes a province becomes, near enough, core. The Halden remember being the frontier. They tell the story as a triumph — *theirs*, not the Empire's.

## Geography & Climate

Cool, wet, high. Short growing season, long winters, good stone and better ore. The uplands would be poor and isolated on their own; roped into the network, their wool, ore, and hard grain trade out and the basin's surplus trades in. When the Halden harvest fails — and at this altitude it sometimes does — the grain comes upriver and no one dies. That single lived fact is the root of Halden loyalty.

## Settlements & Sites

- Fortified-looking hill-towns whose walls are now decorative — the fortifications predate the Empire and are kept for pride and festival.
- Mine-towns and drove-roads feeding ore and cattle down to the river network.
- Old shrines to hill-gods the Empire never touched, now cheerfully hosting imperial holidays alongside the local ones.

> A Halden grandmother and a Halden youth will give you two different countries. To the old, the Empire is the thing that *ended the raiding* — living memory of a harder time. To the young, it is simply the air; they are more Halden-proud and less Empire-grateful, because they never saw the before. The grievance the Empire should fear is not here yet, but you can see where it would grow.
