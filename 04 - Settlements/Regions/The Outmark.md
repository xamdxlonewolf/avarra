# The Outmark

> The far frontier, where the network thins to a thread and stops. Cold, thin, and quiet — a line of forts and milestones, and beyond them, not-the-Empire. Player-facing, it is the proud edge of imperial reach. It is also the setting's most patient question.

## Overview

The Outmark is the marginal country at the Empire's rim — high, cold, poor ground that barely repays settling. What holds it is not farmland but **the line**: a chain of garrison-forts and relay-stations strung along the last of the trunk-road, keeping the border legible and the marches safe. Duty here is unglamorous and coveted for exactly that reason — it is *quiet.* Nothing comes over the line. It has not, in generations.

The Outmark is where a settled subject goes to feel the size of the Empire: stand at the last milestone, look at the empty country beyond, and understand that everything behind you — the safe roads, the full granaries, the fair courts — stops *here*, held by these cold forts and the people in them. It makes the comfort of the interior feel, briefly, like something that was *decided* rather than given.

## Geography & Climate

Harsh, thin, high or far-northern; short brutal seasons; sparse population; long supply lines that only the network makes possible at all. The forts are provisioned from the interior — another quiet proof of the granary system: even this far out, no garrison starves.

## Settlements & Sites

- **The line of forts** — regularly spaced, well-built, permanently manned, connected by relay.
- **The last milestones** — where the road's measured count simply ends.
- Small hard settlements clinging to the forts: suppliers, families, veterans who stayed.

## GM Notes

`visibility: gm` — walled off; this note is player-safe.

- **The quiet is the hook.** The Outmark was pacified fast and has been silent since — forts manned, nothing ever coming. To players this is triumph. Later it can become the question *what are they holding the door against, if nothing ever comes?* — a natural, un-forced approach to [[The Cost]] (the "dam against the dark" half). Do **not** confirm anything for a long time. The forts should look like ordinary border security first, for many sessions.
- Keep it deniable: real frontiers are quiet when they're won. The correct reading and the innocent reading must stay indistinguishable until very late.
- Good eventual site for the "gentle euphemism" to surface in cold, bureaucratic form — a garrison ledger that "reconciles" something on a schedule (tie to [[The Shape of the Empire|reconciling the rolls]]).
