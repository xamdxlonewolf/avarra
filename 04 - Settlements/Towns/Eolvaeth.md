# Eolvaeth

> Eolvaeth is the Waiting Lands' seat, a pilgrim-edge town and not a live-front luck, where people walk in order to wait rather than because the canopy is a certainty. The name is said *(EOL-vaeth · [[The Old Tongue|eol]] "hinge" + [[The Old Tongue|vaeth]] "the waiting.")*, and folk-speech shortens it to **Elvaeth** *(EL-vaeth)*.

> *Vaethod* keeps the square. This town is not [[Harrow's Green]], not [[The Three Hamlets Past the Ford]], and not [[Saelvaeth]]'s march, and this Tree does not have Harrow's luck.

## Overview

The town sits in a vale behind [[Heskoren]]'s Strandoren-facing coast, and it is one of the earlier pots on this continent, a graft of the Cut-year 200s that took, spoke a colour in some years, and never grew into a neighbour's certainty. The live front moved on along the wave, and the waiting stayed, and congregation made a country of that staying. [[Polity Archetypes|Vaethorn]] is the gospel of the wait, and **Eolvaeth is the square the gospel stands in.**

It is not a throne. Governance here is almost absent, so what sits in the vale is a Tree, a spring, a gift-hall that is the town's worship, and the camp-streets of people who came for a hinge and did not leave. Custom outruns written law. The town-warden is Hands-folk, which means the office is kept by practice and not by a crown.

[[Saelvaeth]]'s luck is a different weather, further along the wave, and [[Harrow's Green]] is a Hand that grew up, a Tree a neighbour can already count on. The three hamlets can see that canopy from a ford. Eolvaeth cannot see it, and does not want to, because wanting it would be admitting the wait is a queue. The gospel says stay, and horticulture, in some years, says send.

The town's older work is devotion, the wait kept as a pilgrimage you can walk to. Necessity is what visitors from thinner districts are actually doing, and the town will not call it that. Nobody books a thin vale.

## Site

Two tracks meet at a spring in a fold of upland, and the graft was married to that water because living earth wants a drink and a child does. Settlement clustered where the wood took. [[Heskoren]] already says that civilization here is a map of surviving Trees, and then the camps filled the fold, because a thin Tree that *might* speak is still the oldest Hand a long walk can reach without crossing into Saelvaeth's orbit.

The spring is the day's drink, and it is not a mile-shrine stone. Ribbons belong on other squares, and children leave a wet leaf on the lip the year the Tree does not speak. Travellers from the core will try to climb something, and there is nothing here to climb. That absence is what the Long Mile left behind: the road used to pass this vale when the neighbour was a continent, the requirement to walk it ended, and the wait did not.

Food is the vale's gardens, ranging on the slopes, alms from the gift-hall pot, and whatever Hale-month guests bring when the town is full of people from somewhere else. Fuel is the same woods the [[Two-Bodied]] use as wild-leave. There is no resident [[The Stilled|stiller]], and a travelling blessing-surgeon is a decade's luck. The [[The Taken-In|Taken-In]] are the backbone, Mother's green held in common, honoured, and not a nation.

No walls stand around the vale. The boundary is the last camp-street and the ranging, and low governance looks like this: you can walk in, and someone will feed you, and no clerk will write you down.

## The square (not a district grid)

The town grew around the canopy, organic rather than laid on a grid. The oldest houses face the Tree, later houses filled the wedge between the tracks, and the **camp-streets** run up the fold. They are pilgrim-camps that learned to winter, set one against the next until they look like a town and still smell like a season.

- **The Tree at the centre** — the civic and sacred heart, as [[Daily Life]] requires, even when the wood is only a maybe. It is mature enough that grandparents remember a fall, and young enough, for a capital, that a Seat clerk would not call it a First. Some years it Turns. Some years the colour is late, or thin, or **Vaethod** *(VAYTH-od)* will not call a fall that isn't. A missed Eolthael, the week the Tree is supposed to speak, still shuts the Given-door. Struck remains the later mercy, and there is still no second-chance year.
- **The spring** — the vale's reason for being where it is. In a wet-leaf year the leaves are already on the lip, and there is climbing-scuff there anyway, from children who have heard the stone-story.
- **The gift-hall** — worship and venting in the same room, an [[Naming in the Turning|ael-hall]] kept as the congregation. A [[Far-Voiced]] hour here is a hymn whether they meant it to be. There is no counted hall, and no release-house ticket.
- **The camp-streets** — adaptive reuse at the scale of a town, a tent-line that became a house. Hale-month still doubles the people, and Ledger-month is local and guilty about the empty rooms.
- **Mother's green** — commons around the Tree, enough to live from, and not a [[The Greens-Keepers|lot]] with a ticket. They will not follow you home. Home is the vale if they did.
- **The wild-leave** — ranging on the slopes, at the edge of the houses, not a beast-yard. No Kind-flag hangs on the trees.
- **The far shed** — Returned work, set downwind of the spring. It is not an infirmary, and it is not a district.

Homes accommodate becomings the way [[Daily Life]] already said, so the house has room for the change a person is already undergoing. Mixed Kinds live here: a Fox of the Wilds at the ranging is unremarkable, a Tengu on a spine-visit is a guest, and a Selkie who married in from the coast is wet about the vale and kept anyway.

Tithe-provision, what people need if they are going to stay whole, is alms and congregation: real warmth, and real want beside it. You can be too poor to stay whole here, and the gospel will console what it cannot furnish.

## What the town still does

Some Hands-children did Turn at this Tree, so the Long Mile is supposed to be optional and the hinge is supposed to have arrived. The Tree is not a certainty. Camp-streets still fill for Eolthael, and some years a cohort still goes toward Saelvaeth's luck, or further if that luck is full ([[The Watch and the Cohort]]). The gospel says wait, and the Seat's horticulture, on a thin year, says send. Vaethod has done both, and the town remembers which summers.

People walk to Eolvaeth to wait together, and that is how a poor land gets a capital without a crown. It is not [[Orenbren]]'s extra mile, because those people already have a healthy canopy, and it is not the hamlets' necessity, because those kitchens can see Harrow's. It is the wait made into a destination, which is also how scarcity becomes unworthiness if you let the sermon land.

A wet leaf lies on the spring, and pilgrims from the core ask if it is a ribbon. Eolvaeth will not say. In a year when the Tree speaks, there is no leaf, and in a year when it does not, there are too many to count, and no one writes the number down.

## People & Powers

The cast lives on [[People of the Turning]], and [[Vaethod]] is a person now, not only an office.

**Named wants on this square (carried by people):**

- [[Vaethod]] wants [[Saelid]]'s stall to keep feeding its winter guests.
- [[Nedaen]] wants the gift-hall pot to feed a child through Ledger-month.
- [[Heiral]] wants a marriage witnessed without Mother-kept pity.
- [[Daron]] wants a corpse named so a grain-debt dies.

[[Hithaen]] is eating in the gift-hall this Hale-month. Vaethod has not decided which kind of guest that is.

Offices here:

- **Town-warden ([[Vaethod]])** — Hands-folk, practice-first and not a Kind, so the office is not a people you are born into. Vaethod speaks the fall truly enough for a waiting-seat, and will not write a colour that didn't happen, even when a visiting clerk wants the hinge tidy. The year they send a cohort toward Saelvaeth is the year the gospel and the wood disagree. They have not resigned.
- **The square that watches, and the camp-streets that watch instead** — some years the witness is local, and some years the witness has to walk home from a luckier Hand. Vouching-at-a-distance, on a gospel-capital, is the tell that waiting is still a road.
- **Road-hands passing through** — they sleep in the town that was chosen early and stayed thin, on the way to a town that was not chosen this year.
- **Vaethorn-folk custom** — a light state, a hot faith, and alms, and no list of persons for a clerk to keep. [[Polity Archetypes|Threnmaieth]] would like a list. [[Ornled]] would not understand why anyone stays.

The unvouched Struck, people the later mercy took who have nobody to stand witness for them, are the ache this seat was built to meet with faith rather than with machinery.

## What can enter a scene

- **A sent cohort walking back in.** Some of them were Given at a luckier Hand, some were not, and one is unvouched. They come into the gift-hall while Vaethod is still chalking this year's notice, and Saelid's stall feels it by evening.
- **A camp-street fire.** Hale-month has packed the tents that learned to winter, and one lamp is enough. The spring is the drink, not the bucket-line you wanted.
- **An unvouched Struck in the gift-hall.** The room meets them with faith or with fear, and Daron may already be downwind with a different body.
- **A clerk from the Reckoning** comes asking to name the spring. The wet leaf is already there.

## In-world text

*A notice chalked on the gift-hall door at Eolthael, Cut-year 387, in Vaethod's hand. The Tree is not named as sick.*

> *The hinge is this week. Beds on the camp-streets first. Someone who came to wait is a guest. Someone who came to count us is a clerk, and I can tell.*
>
> *If the Tree speaks we stay. If it does not I will not invent a colour. I will not send until the week is over. I have sent before. I will not pretend I have not.*
>
> *The spring is not a stone. Leave the leaf if you must. Do not ask me what it buys. It buys nothing. That is why we leave it.*

The notice is Vaethod saying, in their own chalk, that a cohort has been sent from this square before. It is good for one week, and by the time the gospel repeats the wording, it reads as a slight.

## GM Notes

- **The town's older work: devotion** — the wait is a pilgrimage people can walk to, and that is the older work. Visitors from thinner districts are here out of necessity, and the town will not call it that. Prestige does not book a thin vale. Do not make devotion, necessity, and prestige all the seat's theme, and do not clone the hamlets' leftover onto this square.
- **Road-hands passing through** — they sleep in the town that was chosen early and stayed thin, on the way to a town that was not chosen this year. Do not clone them as an Eolvaeth chapter.
- **Production.** Do not clone Road-hands or the Slide as a district, and do not give this Tree Harrow's luck in order to make a capital feel like a reward.
- **Do not make Eolvaeth the "good town" that waited faithfully.** They were an early pot that stayed thin, and congregation did the rest. Luck is horticulture plus the queue. Vaethorn will preach otherwise, and history does not agree.
- **Not Saelvaeth's march.** Harrow's is luck you can see from a ford, and Eolvaeth is a gospel you walk to. Pair them in a campaign week when you want both kinds of Heskoren's need on the table, and do not collapse them into one frontier flavour.
- **The older work.** Devotion, the wait kept as a destination, is the theme. Do not make devotion, necessity, and prestige all the seat's theme. A cohort sent toward Saelvaeth is friction, not a second theme. Prestige that lands here is lost, rich, or lying.
- **Do not clone 7.2's stone.** The spring is the water, the wet leaf is not a ribbon, and the absence of a mile-shrine is the leftover, not a missing prop.
- **Keystone:** thin, sick, or few grafts stay the player-facing cause. Do not let Vaethod assert the mind. "The soil was never quite right here" is horticulture.
- **Keep a thin year visible.** If the pilgrim-town is only pretty, and the leftover is only picturesque, waiting turns into a virtue.
- **Hooks.** A missed fall at the waiting-seat, a cohort Vaethod will or will not send, an unvouched Struck in the gift-hall, a wet leaf, a clerk from the Reckoning asking to name the spring, a Road-hand sleeping here with a pot for someone else, a Kept child treated as Mother-kept in the one town that made waiting holy, and a session paired with Harrow's so luck and gospel have to share a week.
