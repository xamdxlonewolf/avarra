# Ornsael

> Ornsael is a Rain-Shadow walk-hold whose drink is leaving. Ornsael keeps the conservative drift of its name, *(ORN-sael · [[The Old Tongue|orn]] "land, ground" + [[The Old Tongue|sael]] "sowing" = "the sown-ground.")*. Everyday speech says *Ornsael*. Travellers from the core still say *the west-road well*, and they are corrected, because the well is why anyone stopped and it is not why they stay this year. The town is not [[Saelthael]]'s capital. The three corner seats are [[Eolvaeth]] · [[Orentel]] · [[Maiethlir]].

## Overview

East of [[Maiethorn]]'s [[Named Ground|Rain-Wall]], on the leeward side away from the Old Crossing, the country is dry hills and sand, and the towns sit at wells. Ornsael is one of those wells. A [[Turning Tree|Tree]] stands beside it, young for the Motherland and old enough to have Turned local children for a generation. **It Speaks most years.** A missed Eolthael, the week the Tree is supposed to speak, is weather, not the town's crisis. The crisis is the drink.

[[Saelthael]] is the polity of people who still sow. Ornsael is a square of that habit, not a throne. Plenty sits on the other side of the range. Pilgrimage here used to be a leftover the town could not retire, because the wood was thin. The wood grew up. The well did not. Children still go west in the neighbour's week, not to hunt a colour, but because the Hale-month crowd needs water this well cannot host. Core pilgrims who arrive in Eolthael expecting a holiday extra mile find a necessity-column already on the west-road, and they are shocked. They thought waiting was a Heskoren feeling. They were not wrong about waiting. They were wrong about *what* is waited for.

[[Kitsune|Fox of the Sands]] hearths are densest here, and that is the terrain, not a flag ([[Kinds of the Turning]]). A town with *no* fox-born would be the thing worth asking about. Mixed Kinds drink the same share.

The town's daily work is meeting need. The west-road is the neighbour's week lived as water, the week people have to leave because the drink will not stretch. Devotion belongs to people who already have a healthy canopy and a wet well. Nobody books a dry hill.

## Site

The well is why anyone stopped. The range takes the rain, and this side keeps the dust. Settlement is nucleated around the water. The graft was married to the well on purpose, because living earth wants a drink in sand-country as much as a child does. The Hand took, and now the well is what is failing.

The **west-road** is the main street. It used to be how you reached a healthier canopy, then how you reached the origin, then how you reached the core's Hands-towns after those towns stopped coming east. It is still how this year's extra people go, when the share will not stretch through Hale-month. Beyond the last house the road is a pale cut toward the range-pass. East of the well it peters into tracks and Taken-In plantings that used to look like stubbornness and now look like a second drink.

Water is the well, a few cisterns, and the dew-catch on the north walls. The cisterns are the civic argument: who filled them, who may draw, and whether a planting may have a bucket before a child. Food is terrace-gardens, goats, and whatever the west-road pays when it still pays. Fuel is dung, and whatever the range-trade carries over the pass. The [[The Taken-In|Taken-In]] are the backbone here, because thirsty land honours green, and they are not a nation. Their pots also need water.

No walls stand around the well. The boundary is the place where the well's water no longer reaches and the dust does.

## The square (not a district grid)

- **The Tree at the well** — the civic and sacred heart, as [[Daily Life]] requires. It is a reasonable Hand. Most Eolthaels it Turns. The town-warden Speaks when there is a colour and does not invent one when there is not, and that is ordinary work, not the town's plot. A missed week still shuts the Given-door. Struck remains the later mercy. There is still no second-chance year.
- **The well** — the day's drink and the year's fear. A rope hangs in it with knots at the old water-lines, and the share is chalked on the well-lip, not on a warden-lath. Climbing-scuff marks the lip, from children who have heard a stone-story.
- **The west-road** — the main street, with mile-markers toward the pass. One stone still stands in the well-square because the town grew around a day's water. Ribbons on that stone point west, which children from the core find backwards. The column that leaves at dusk is the people the share refused.
- **The well-market** — stalls in the morning shade. Fox-born are densest in the crowd, and they are not gated. It is a hearth-neighbourhood in the sense [[Kinds of the Turning]] allowed, a fox-market without the town being a Kind. Stock ancestries drink here, a Tengu comes through on a pass-errand, and a Selkie who married in from [[The Hinge Shore]] is dry and funny about it.
- **The greens** — potted, hauled, and argued over. There is no lush lot. Lesna's planting is the only green for a day's walk in some weeks, and it drinks. The greens-keepers' liturgy does not run a chapter here.
- **The ranging** — dry hills and night-work, where Two-Bodied know the difference between a sand-fox and a Fox. It sits at the edge, and it is not a quarter.
- **The far shed** — Returned work, downwind of the well, because you do not put the dead on the drink. There is no infirmary. A travelling stiller is a pass-story.

Homes bend to the becomings people are in the middle of: north-side cool rooms, green pots beside the doors, and shutters that open for a Far-Voiced hour because there is no hall to book. What you are looking at, at eye level, is Saelthael's own combination, thin infrastructure, high theology, and low governance.

## What the square still does

This is Maiethorn, the Motherland, Hands-country in the core, the country where a town Tree was supposed to be enough. The extra mile is supposed to be optional, and this canopy Turns at home. The well is dropping. Children and Hale-month guests still go west, and the road that used to go further still does. The word *sael* is the same word as in [[Saelvaeth]] on the live front: sowing, said on two continents. If the wood sickened, a Hand could fail. It has not. The drink has.

Vaethorn-guilt imported to sand that never asked for it is still an injustice, and it is the wrong sermon for this square. Waiting read as unworthiness was about a colour. This year the town empties itself for water. A core hymn about thin soil will be heard as someone who has never drawn a bucket.

The well-rope is knotted at last year's water-line, and this year's knot is wet even when the water stands below it. Ornsael will not say why. Theisva moves the knot, Lesna says the planting tastes the same when it is wet, and Bovaer puts sand in a west-facing shoe and does not call that a rite.

## People & Powers

Three people keep the argument at the well.

- **Theisva** *(THAYS-vah)* — well-keeper. Speaks the conservative way, not a Kind, so the keeping is a practice and not a people Theisva was born. Theisva keeps the share, will cut a guest's bucket before a child's and a planting's before a guest's, and will not pretend the chalk is a hymn. Theisva is tired of being asked whether this is fox-country. Theisva may be Sands-Fox, and may not. Either way the question is the slight.
- **Lesna** *(LES-nah)* — [[The Taken-In|Taken-In]], keeping the planting east of the well. Lesna wants one more bucket than the share allows, because the green is also the town's food, and will not follow you home. Home is dust if the planting dies.
- **Bovaer** *(bo-VAIR)* — west-road guide. Bovaer still sends people west. Some years the Tree has Spoken and Bovaer sends anyway, because the drink is the limit. Core pilgrims hear faith. Bovaer hears a date and a water-mark.
- **The square that watches, and the west-road that watches instead** — some years the witness is local. Some years a person has to walk home from a wetter town over the pass, not because the wood failed, but because the week could not be hosted. Vouching-at-a-distance on the Motherland is still the tell that Ornthael is unfinished, the years when a town's own Tree was supposed to make the extra mile optional.
- **Road-hands** — rare on this road, because the circuit prefers the next town that can pay. A pot for Ornsael is a fight with the queue, not a chapter-house.
- **Saelthael-folk custom** — light, with the wells treated as civic fact. The well-share is the town-law. There is no list of persons. [[Polity Archetypes|Threnmaieth]] would like one. Plenty on the other side of the range is [[Maiethvael]]'s problem, not this well's.

A town-warden still Speaks the fall. The square does not turn on that warden.

## What can enter a scene

What follows is not the weather told in a paragraph. It is a thing that arrives while someone is already talking.

- **The well-mouth at first light.** Theisva draws, the mark is lower than yesterday, and a crowd is already there with jars. Someone will take a second bucket. Someone will be seen doing it.
- **A cistern-fight.** Lesna's planting got a night-bucket, and a camp-street child did not. The planting is also breakfast.
- **A west-road leaving at dusk.** Bovaer's column is ten-year-olds, Hale-month guests, and a core pilgrim who came for a hymn and is being sent on. The Given-door is still one week. A dry well is not a second-chance year.
- **A collapse.** The lip shears and the rope goes, and for an hour the town has a hole and no share.
- **Range-weather.** Night-work comes home with a person who needs water now, and the share was already written at dawn.

## In-world text

*Well-share marks, chalked on the lip in Hale-month, Cut-year 387, in Theisva's hand. The Tree is not mentioned.*

> *Dawn-mark: below last knot. Child-jars first. Guest-jars after the planting's one bucket, and I will hear about the planting.*
>
> *West-road at dusk if the noon-mark does not rise. Bovaer has the names. I have the rope.*
>
> *A man from the Reckoning asked to name the well on a roll. We named the well Ornsael, which it already was. He wrote a colour-year. The sand does not keep his hand. The knot is wet. I did not wet it.*

Theisva is keeping the water, and will not file a year. Lesna will read the marks as a slight on the green. Bovaer will already be on the road.

## GM Notes

- **This is the clone-break.** [[Eolvaeth]] keeps the maybe-Tree, the spring, the warden who will not invent a colour, and the wet leaf. Ornsael does not. The physical problem is the well. The institutional answer is a share. The cast is a gang. The mystery is a wet knot below the water. Do not slide back into a warden who will not call a false fall.
- **Not a Kitsune nation.** Sands-Fox hearths are the densest, and the Kinds are mixed. A fox-market is a neighbourhood, not a quarter with a gate.
- **Necessity as water, and not a maybe-Tree.** The Hand Speaks most years. If you need a thin fall, it is weather, not the plot. If you need a Hand that fails, that square is [[The Mill-hold]].
- **Not the Thaw-Break's silted well-town.** That cistern belongs to another leeward well-town ([[The Thaw-Break]]). Some locals date the first low mark after 233. The rope does not. Do not confirm the date.
- **Not a capital of Saelthael.** The seat is [[Larbril]].
- **Keystone:** a thin take in dry soil stays horticulture in the player text. Do not let the well assert the mind. Do not import Vaethorn's sermon as the GM's voice.
- **The west-road remains necessary.** Plenty on the other side of the range does not shorten it.
- **Hooks.** A west-road column the Tree did not require, a night-bucket, a wet knot, Threnmaieth naming the well on a roll the town will not use, a Taken-In planting that is the only green and the other drink, and a session paired with [[Harrow's Green]] so both squares are visible and the two scarcities are not the same. Pair the week with [[The Dry Stair]] if you want a climb that Gives nothing on the same dry east. The Stair is not this hill. Do not narrate Ornsael as history and Harrow's as frontier flavour. Both are this year.
