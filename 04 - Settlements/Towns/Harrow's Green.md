# Harrow's Green

> Harrow's Green is a live-front Tree-town, the luckiest kind of square [[Heskoren]] has: a scion that arrived in the Cut-year 200s, spoke a generation later, and has now Turned local children for a few generations. New towns everywhere still say the Harrow-sentence, *we Turned at Harrow's Tree before we had our own* ([[Turning Tree]]). Here that sentence is the decade, not an old story. People call the place the neighbour's canopy.

> Everyday speech already had *Harrow's green* as a Seat sentence for [[The Tree-Wardens|Cutting-leave]]. The warden is *Haelin*. The town is not a capital of [[Saelvaeth]], and the next scion is not a reward.

## Overview

The town is a grafted pocket on the Sundered Reach. Settlement clustered where the wood took, as [[Heskoren]] already says civilization here is a map of surviving Trees, and Harrow's is old enough, for the front, to be a town: a canopy at the centre, a square that used to be a road, and borrowed Turnings coming in from [[The Three Hamlets Past the Ford]] every Eolthael, the week the Tree is asked to speak.

It is not the end of the wave. It is a Hand that grew up. The three hamlets can see the green from the ford, and the Seat has already written that the wood will take here and will not, this year, take there. Harrow's lives inside that sentence whether it asked to or not.

[[Polity Archetypes|Vaethorn]]'s gospel is spoken on many hearths, and not always as a flag. This square lives in [[Saelvaeth]]'s orbit, the live-front march, and it is not that march's capital. [[The Old Ways]] are neighbours, not a museum. A first meal at a later planting, when a cut from this Tree is set down in an older green, is a live argument, not a courtesy-card.

The town's daily work is the neighbour's week, the week other places send their children here. Devotion leaves a ribbon on the stone. Nobody books a winter here.

## Site

The houses sit on a low rise above **[[Named Ground|the Rise-water]]**, which becomes the ford downstream. The graft took on the rise, and the old pilgrim-road climbed it because that was the day's water. Towns on [[Heskoren]] do not pick a pretty hill and then invent a Tree. The wood picked, and the hearths followed.

Water is the stream, a cistern by the warden-hearth, and the **stone in the square**, which is still the day's drink for anyone using the road as a mile. Food is the pocket's gardens, ranging beyond the last house, and whatever the neighbour's week brings in Hale-month, when the roads fill with crowds, dust, and a complaint. Fuel is the same woods the [[Two-Bodied]] use as ranging. The Taken-In keep the greens around the Tree from going to seed indoors. There is no resident [[The Stilled|stiller]], and a travelling one is a summer's luck.

The square is the last holy mile, and beyond the boughs the old way is a cart-track to the ford. Children are told the road goes to the Motherwood. It goes to the mill, and then to three hamlets that can see the canopy. Both are true if you keep walking.

## The square (not a district grid)

The town is nucleated around the canopy, organic rather than a grid. The oldest houses face the Tree, and later houses filled the wedge between the road and the stream. No walls stand here. The boundary is the ranging and the last garden.

- **The Tree at the centre** — mature enough to Turn the local children, and young enough that grandparents still remember the borrowed years, when this town's own children had to go elsewhere. Leaf-Fall is held in the square because there is nowhere else a crowd fits. The town's age can be read in the wood: a Hand, not a First.
- **The stone** — the last mile-shrine of the road that used to go further, with climbing-scuff on it from [[The Child Who Climbed the Stone]]. The Road-hands still take water here, and some years there are ribbons. **Haelin** *(HAY-lin)*, the town-warden, wants the stone moved before the next Leaf-Fall because it stands in the way of the crowd. A hamlet child will climb it and ask how far it is to the Tree.
- **The upper room** — still too many beds for the trade the town has now. It opens for the neighbour's week, and it is not yet a merchant-loft. A Road-hand sleeps here on the way to a town that was not chosen, sometimes with a pot. Hale-month is dust and extra people. Ledger-month is local.
- **The greens** — potted groves and a garden-common around the Tree, enough to live from, and not a [[The Greens-Keepers|lot]] with a ticket. They will not follow you home. The Taken-In here are neighbours, not a quarter.
- **The ranging** — at the town edge, not a beast-yard. Two-Bodied work the woods that feed the hearth, and no Kind-flag hangs on the trees.
- **The far shed** — Returned plague-and-body work, downwind of the houses, on the same logic as a tannery. It is not an infirmary, and it is not a district.

Homes accommodate becomings the way [[Daily Life]] already said: a window that opens, a room kept green, and a threshold a Long-Lived waits to be asked across. Mixed Kinds live here. A Fox of the Wilds at the ranging is unremarkable, and a Tengu on a ridge-visit is a guest.

Tithe-provision, what a person needs in order to stay whole, is frontier-warm and thin: the greens, the ranging, and the upper room as a seasonal outlet for whoever is loud that week. There is no counted hall, and no stiller on a licence-wall. You can be too poor to stay whole here, and the neighbour's week will not fix that.

## What the square still does

The square is the last mile. The road ends at the boughs, Hands-children Turn at home, and the hamlets can see the canopy and still send this year's ten-year-olds. A Road-hand is going the other way with wood. The Harrow-sentence is this decade, not nostalgia.

A warden who pulls the stone down is doing theology with a hammer. They are not wrong that Leaf-Fall needs the space. They are wrong if they think the graves went with the requirement, because the next necessity-column will miss the water. Haelin knows this and still wants the stone moved. That is the tension, and it is not a villain. The cistern-dipper is hung with the bowl toward the stream. Harrow's Green will not say why.

## People & Powers

Haelin is the live-front office this cast points at ([[People of the Turning]]).

**Tora** *(TOR-ah)* of [[The Three Hamlets Past the Ford|Brenod]] is the second person on this square. They walk a neighbour-fall home when they can, carrying the witness of a Turning done here back to the hamlet, and they are here when the stone is to be moved, because the next column still drinks at that stone. Haelin has a Tree. Tora has a ford. Same week.

Offices here:

- **Town-warden (Haelin)** — practice-first, not a Kind, so the office is not a people anyone was born into. Haelin speaks the fall truly enough for a front-town, believer-heavy the way a live Tree makes a person, and still has to lodge the neighbour's week. The scion-queue is not theirs to fix, and it is the sentence they live in. The hearth still keeps the Seat letter from [[The Wrong Green]] (Cut-year 248), the one in which Nathdo Spoke a hug as copper-green. Haelin Speaks slowly, and Hale-month does not always forgive it. A pot from [[Orentel|Sorim]]'s earth-berth may sleep upstairs.
- **The square that watches children Turn** — Harrow's witness is what a hamlet child carries home. Vouching-at-a-distance was invented for an origin-fall, and here it is a walk back to the ford.
- **Road-hands passing through** — they sleep in the town that was chosen, on the way to the one that was not.
- **A Door-Keeper at a later planting** — host-rights, if a cut from Harrow's is set in an old green. The later planting is [[The First Bowl]] (Lonasir, in the Far Yield). What the ground still wants is the first meal, or a claiming.

Saelvaeth's march-voice can be blamed when the queue is ugly. The voice stands at [[Natai]], and it does not sit a throne on this rise. [[Dumu]] is that voice. Haelin keeps this square.

## What can enter a scene

- **Stone-moving day.** There are ropes, a crowd, and Hale-month dust. The stone can tip, and the stream can take a foot. Haelin is not a villain for wanting the square clear. Tora is not a villain for needing the water left where the next column can drink.
- **A child on the climb**, whether from the hamlet or from here, stands on the stone and asks how far it is to the Tree.
- **A pot arriving upstairs.** A Road-hand comes up with the earth-smell of the road, and with Sorim's berth 5 if you want the chain. Haelin can tell a pot from a pilgrim. They will not always say.
- **A Door-Keeper at a later planting** asks about the bowl before they ask about a leave. The bowl sits at [[The First Bowl]].

## In-world text

*A notice chalked on the cistern-wall in Hale-month, Cut-year 387, in Haelin's hand. The stone is not named.*

> *The crowd will need the square. The stone will be walked around until Eolthael, and then we will see. Water stays. Names stay if they were names. Climbing is for after.*
>
> *Beds above: the neighbour's week first. A pot is not a pilgrim. A pilgrim is not a pot. I can tell. I will not always say.*
>
> *If you came from the ford, you are seen here. If you must be seen at home, take someone with you who was here. I will not walk it for you. I have a Tree.*

The notice shows Haelin making room for the crowd, and refusing to walk anyone home. In the square it reads as crowd control. Past the ford it can arrive as a slight.

## GM Notes

- **Road-hands passing through** — they sleep in the town that was chosen, on the way to the one that was not. Do not clone them as a Harrow's chapter.
- **Do not make Harrow's the "good town" that waited faithfully.** They were a paying-enough, soil-enough, chain-enough next on a list written in the Motherwood. Luck is horticulture plus the queue. Vaethorn will preach otherwise.
- **The Harrow-sentence is a tell.** When an NPC says it as nostalgia, they are Maiethorn. When they say it as this year, they are the front. When they say it as a slight, *they Turned at Harrow's because they had no Hand*, they are a hamlet.
- **The town's daily work.** The work is necessity. Do not make necessity, devotion, and prestige all the town's theme, and do not skip [[The Door-Keepers]] to make the town simpler. A ribbon on the stone is texture, not a second theme. A First-Hand-year household here is lost, rich, or lying. The seated road-end is [[Nelath]]. This square's cart-track still goes to the ford, so do not relabel this green into that town.
- **Host-rights.** A later cut from Harrow's, acting on the origin's behalf, still wants the meal if the ground is an old green. That meal is seated at [[The First Bowl]]. Do not clone this square onto that vale.
- **Keystone:** thin, sick, or few grafts stay the player-facing cause. Do not let Haelin assert the mind. "The soil was right here" is horticulture.
- **Keep the cost in sight.** A pretty live-front town whose leftover is only picturesque launders the graves. Keep the hamlets visible from the square in at least one sightline.
- **Hooks.** The hamlets can see the canopy, a borrowed Turning whose witness has to walk home, the stone Haelin wants moved, a Road-hand sleeping here with a pot for someone else, a folk-right slip in a hamlet that took and a Harrow's warden asked to Speak it, a first meal a Road-hand ate or refused on the way through, and the climbing-child from the fable, this year.
- **Opening sits here.** [[The Opening]] and [[The Isolated Fall]] sit on this square in Hale-month, Cut-year 387. Do not rebuild the square into a Phoenix festival. The town's daily work stays necessity. Extra people in the upper room are the thing Haelin will not always say. Do not invent a Care-fortress on the rise.
- **Habit.** One habit, the cistern-dipper, and the count is full. A later note must not explain it, and must not turn it into a power, a relic, or a rite. No new person. The march-voice is [[Dumu]] at [[Natai]], and Dumu is not a warden of this square.
