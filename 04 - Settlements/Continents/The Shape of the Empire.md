# The Shape of the Empire

> **The foundation.** How the Empire is put together — core, frontier, and the provinces between — and *why it works well enough that people are glad to live in it.* This is the administrative spine the rest of the world hangs on. Physical geography is [[Climate & Terrain]]; the players' own body is [[The Assize]].

## What the Empire actually is

From inside, it is not *an* empire among rivals — it is simply **the order of the world**, the way things are arranged so that they work. Its citizens rarely call it anything grander than "the Empire," the way we say "society." What it *does* — the promise every subject can name — is small and enormous: **the roads are safe, the courts are fair, and no one starves.** Everything below is the machinery that keeps that promise.

## The three rings

The Empire is best understood not as a map of borders but as **distance from the network** — how fully a place is woven into the system of roads, rivers, rolls, and courts.

### 1. The Core (the heartland)

The old river-basin provinces around the capital — settled longest, taxed most, governed most lightly *because they need governing least*. Here the Empire is invisible the way good plumbing is invisible: grain moves, coin is sound, disputes go to court, and no one thinks about why. Loyalty here is not enforced; it is simply assumed, because life is good and everyone remembers (or has been taught to remember) that it was once worse. The capital and the breadbasket are here — see [[The Aurenne]].

### 2. The Provinces (the settled middle)

Older conquests, now thoroughly integrated — some for so long that their being "conquered" is a fact of history books, not grievance. They keep their own languages, gods, and customs (the Empire never demanded otherwise), pay their assessments, send their disputes to the circuit courts, and prosper. A long-settled upland like [[The Halden]] is the model: proud, distinct, and quietly certain the Empire is a good deal.

### 3. The Marches (the frontier & the recently-pacified)

The edge, in two flavours:
- **Recently-pacified provinces** — brought in within living memory, still raw. The roads are new, the garrisons visible, the courts busy, and the gratitude *contested*. A rich southern conquest like [[Kessad]] belongs here: it benefits materially and knows it, and still has people alive who remember the taking.
- **The true frontier** — where the network thins to a thread and stops. The [[The Outmark]] is the far cold edge: forts, milestones, and beyond them, not-the-Empire.

> The genius of the arrangement is that the rings are **permeable in one direction.** A march becomes a settled province; a province becomes core. The Empire's implicit promise to the newly-conquered is not "submit," but "*wait — in a generation this will be you.*" And often, visibly, it has been.

## How it is actually administered

A bureaucratically competent empire needs three things kept apart so they can check each other. This one keeps them apart on purpose:

- **The Governors** hold a province: order, garrison, public works, the assessment. They are powerful and *local* — and deliberately **cannot judge their own subjects.**
- **The Circuit Courts** ride *through* provinces on a fixed calendar, answerable not to the governor but to the centre. Justice arrives from outside and leaves again, which is precisely why it is trusted. The players' body, [[The Assize]], is one such circuit. (See below.)
- **The Rolls** — the census, the survey, the tax and grain ledgers — are kept by a separate scribal service whose only loyalty is to the accuracy of the count. The Rolls are how the Empire *knows itself*: who lives where, who owes what, where the grain is, where it's short.

Over all three sits the centre in the capital, whose real instrument of power is not an army but **information moving faster than trouble** — the relay-post that carries a governor's report or a court's ruling the length of the Vaur in days.

### Why the promise holds

| The promise | The machinery |
|---|---|
| *No one starves* | Surplus basin + rivers/canals + the Rolls knowing where grain is short → shortfalls are moved, not mourned. See [[Climate & Terrain]]. |
| *The courts are fair* | Justice rides in from outside on a fixed circuit; no governor judges his own. |
| *The roads are safe* | The same network that moves grain moves garrisons and post; banditry can't outrun the relay. |
| *Trade is protected* | One coinage, one weights-standard, one law of contract along the whole network. |

None of this requires the Empire to be *loved* by decree. It is loved because, measured against the alternative anyone can imagine, it plainly works.

## Where the players ride

[[The Assize]] rides the network province to province, holding court and settling what the local machinery couldn't. The geography is built to carry them: a fixed riding season, a known route down the river-and-road spine, a self-contained case in each province, and — always — one more thing that doesn't quite add up. This is the campaign's episodic engine; each province is an arc.

## The seam (GM Notes)

`visibility: gm` — do **not** surface. This note stays player-safe; the tells below are planted in the open but never explained.

- **The euphemism, hidden in plain civic language.** The scribal service speaks of "**reconciling the rolls**" — routine bookkeeping for balancing the census against reality (a name struck off, a household that no longer answers the count). It reads as pure bureaucracy. It is also, precisely, the warm word from [[The Cost]] for the thing the Empire quietly pays. Kept deliberately *out* of the players' job title (see [[The Assize]] naming decision) so the word stays rare — surfacing mainly in frontier ledgers (see [[The Outmark]]) and old deeds. Nothing here should confirm anything; it should only, much later, *rhyme.*
- **The frontier that went quiet.** The [[The Outmark]] was pacified fast and has been silent ever since — forts still manned, nothing ever coming. Player-facing, this is a triumph of imperial reach. It is also the kind of silence [[The Cost]] would produce, and a good place to eventually hang the question *what exactly are those forts holding the door against?*
- **The measured land.** The Zero Stone / impossible milestone counts (see [[Climate & Terrain]]) hint the land carries a history older than the Empire's own. Seed only. Keep it a curiosity for a long time.
- Nothing in the administrative structure should let a clever player conclude "the Empire is secretly evil." The machinery is genuinely good. That is the trap and the point.
