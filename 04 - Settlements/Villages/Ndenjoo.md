# Ndenjoo

> **A hill-hall on [[The Sundering Isle|Kumbaan]].** *(nden-JOH.)* The own-name comes from the isle register, and sailors say **the Feeding Hill.** The valley comes here on the bright nights. There is no [[Turning Tree|Tree]]. There is a table.

## Overview

Ndenjoo is one of the Paps, a green hill honeycombed underneath, with pasture and standing-stones on the turf and the life below. It is the hall the valley already knows. [[Njunda]] *(NJOON-dah)* opens it because it was their grandmother's, and because it still fits the crowd. That keeping rests on reputation, not on an office. When they die, the valley will come to the next hall that still sets the extra place.

The storm-beach is a walk down, not a quay. **Soonke** *(SOON-keh)* sits that stretch of sand and does not swim out. When a wreck comes in with living people on it, they walk those people uphill, and the first night is spent as *guest* at the hall, not on the sand.

This is an ordinary home that happens to be nearly impossible to reach. The uncanniness is the distance, and the hands you can see without bodies. It is not a paradise, and it is not a monster-haunt. Mainland noon-faiths are *loud* here the way a guest who will not sit is loud.

Kumbaan never had the pilgrim walk, and it never had a Tree. What is left here is the wall: people who arrived and did not leave, and people who want to leave and cannot book a thinning. A thinning is a moon the sitters know, not a ferry.

## Site

The hill is why anyone stayed. The hall is a long room under the turf, with lamps that face the table and not the sea. On the surface there are terrace-gardens, a standing-stone the children dare, and goats. Below that are the hearths, the feast-board, and the extra place, set slightly back from the rest.

Water is a hill-spring and caught rain. Food is the terrace, the shallows when the wall is kind, and whatever the unseen hands fetch, so that a poor house still sets a table that looks like more than its means. Fuel is peat, and whatever a wreck sometimes gives the hall. There is no lot, no counted hall, and no stiller.

There are no walls. The boundary is the last path-cut toward the sand, and the storm.

A slip planted here would die. Dead wood is not a Tree, and no one in this valley is waiting for a colour.

## The hall (not a district grid)

Life is under the hill, so you do not walk *through* a town. You walk into a Pap and come out at a board.

- **The table** is the civic heart and the sacred heart at once, because the rite *is* supper. The extra place sits slightly back, not at the end. Unseen hands fetch and pour, there are songs, and nobody reads a colour or recites a set of terms.
- **The extra place** is laid at every meal that matters, not only at the feast. To omit it is the one impiety. A child of the house learns the work by carrying dishes, and by being told not to sit there.
- **The day-rooms** are cool, and they are where people sleep when the moon is down. Daylight is a Sabbath of sleep, not a fallen hour. A mainlander who is loud at noon is a problem the whole hall feels.
- **The path to the sand** is Soonke's stretch. A hooded lamp stands on it, and the lamp does not call ships. The living go up, and the dead go above the tide.
- **The standing-stone** is a dare, not a mile-shrine. There are no ribbons on it. Visitors from the core will try to climb it as if it owed them a leftover, and it does not.

On one night of the moon sailors call Yoltal, the hall eats the heel of the loaf first. Every other night the heel is last, or it goes to the goats. The valley says the hands like the crust when the mainland is loud. Njunda will not correct that, and will not say it is the reason. They will not say why, and a guest who asks is given more bread.

Homes here accommodate the becomings the isle actually has. A rare Struck is family news, not a class ([[Yumboe]]). Mixed mainland Kinds are guests if they crossed, and they do not grow quarters of their own. A Yumboe is what you are *because that is who lives here*.

## People & Powers

- **[[Njunda]]** keeps the table, and that is the whole job. They want the extra place laid, and they want **Nolas** gone without the place being omitted.
- **Soonke** sits the shore on this stretch. They want the living walked uphill, and they want the lamp left unmistaken for a harbour. They will not seat anyone on the sand.
- **Saalo** *(SAH-lo)* is of the valley, and wants a crossing *out* to find **Waandi** *(WAHN-dee)*, who left two moons ago and sent no word. Saalo is a sibling, not a scholar of Trees.
- **Nolas** *(NOH-las)* came off a [[Netstrand]] hull that tried the wall. They have sat twice, which makes them kin *inside the hall*, and they will not go back to a ship. The valley is tired.
- **The valley** is a few dozen hearths. Staying hosts say the dead's names when they fill the extra place. Serving hosts do not say the names, and they still fill the place. The argument waits until after the dishes.

There is no town-warden, no roll, no First Seat, and no Road-hands. [[The Open Table]] is the faith, and this is a hall that keeps it.

## Crossing (what a session does)

The full custom is on [[The Sundering Isle#How a crossing works]]. What you need in order to start is shorter than that.

You do not book Ndenjoo. You are on the sand, or you are not. Soonke is the one who decides whether you are *alive* to be walked up, and Njunda is the one who decides whether you are *kin*. The second sitting is how you belong **on the isle**, and that belonging does not travel back to a mainland watch. A road-word still works on a [[Strandoren]] quay *before* you sail, and here the question is not asked.

A thinning is a moon the sitters know, not a ferry. Most hulls wreck, turn, or do not return, and the sitters do not send anyone back. Saalo is waiting anyway.

## What can enter a scene

- **A wreck walked uphill tonight.** The people are wet and breathing, and nobody asks them for a colour. It is the first night, so the hall has to open. The extra place was already set, and now there is also a living stranger.
- **The hands stop.** A child sat in the extra place, or the place was used as a spare stool, or a Serving host and a Staying host argued *during* the pour. The valley is hungry, and fixing it is manners, not a miracle.
- **Noon-noise.** Nolas will not sleep the day. The noise is mainland loud, and the hill feels it.
- **A hull rumour.** Someone on the sand saw a sail. Soonke will not swim out to it, Saalo will want to go down, and Njunda will want the hall kept.

## Named wants

These are what each person wants this season.

1. **Njunda** wants Nolas to leave as kin, not as a slight, and wants the extra place to stay laid.
2. **Soonke** wants this wreck off the hill before the next loud night, and wants the lamp still hooded.
3. **Saalo** wants a crossing toward Waandi, or a word that Waandi sat a second night somewhere that is not here.
4. **Nolas** wants the hall as a hearth. They have been counted, and they will not be a passenger again.

A warden guest, if one ever washes up, is still a guest. There is no Tree to tend. The argument about a tenth-year week belongs to the mainland ([[The Open Table]]), not to this valley.

## In-world text

*Said to the extra place before the hands arrive, at Ndenjoo on a bright night. Children are taught it. Njunda added the second line after Nolas sat twice.*

> *We have counted you. Sit. If you have a name we have not learned it yet; if you have a colour we have no tree to read it.*
>
> *If you have sat twice, you are of the table. The table is not a ship. Sleep when the moon is down. The sand does not owe you a second hull.*

It is a grace said over a meal. Sitting twice makes a guest into kin, and that is why the hall fills. A sailor who copies only the first two sentences will sell them on a quay as a proverb.

## GM Notes

- **For play, not a reveal.** Do not run the hall as a keystone vault. The isle remains the cleanest control, and confirmation stays late and stays off this table.
- **No Tree, no graft, and no mile-shrine.** A later story that "finds an old scion" here breaks the isolate and breaks [[The Open Table]]. Dead wood is not a Tree.
- **Do not gloss the heel.** The folk sentence about the crust is not an answer. Do not make that night a ward, a liturgy, or a Leaf-Fall in disguise.
- **Not of her household.** Staying-kin are not [[The Other Hands]]. A rare Struck stays family news ([[Yumboe]]).
- **Not a sixteenth power.** This is a valley, not a flag, and Kumbaan is not a mainland polity.
- **Hospitality is the ~5% dial at its gentlest, until the hall overflows.** Do not turn the Open Table into a trap-cult or a geas. The scare, when you need one, is a wreck, a guest who will not leave, a week of extra guests, or a noon that will not sleep.
- **Soonke and Njunda are two jobs.** The sand decides who is alive. The hall decides who is kin. If they start doing each other's work, that is a thin year, not a merger of houses.
- **Yumboe PC:** the player needs GM leave, the Kind is taken in full, and it is never a mix ([[Kind Heritage]]).
- **Hooks.** Nolas at noon; hands that stop; Saalo and a sail; a warden guest with no Tree; pairing a mainland session ([[The Open Table#Two suppers, or a Turning-witness]]) *after* someone has sat here, so the sentence fails in public later.
