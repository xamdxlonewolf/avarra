# Raillath

> **A halt on the Near Mile.** *(RAY-lath.)* It stands between Brenthael and [[The Mill-hold]], in the gap where those two days of road already were. The mile-shrine is still **on the road**, and it has not been pulled into a square. This halt is not [[Nelath]], it is not an inn-bank, and it is not a sick Hand.

> The keeper of the post is *Lonteith*. A neighbour says the given name, and a stranger hears *Lonteith of Raillath*. There is no house-name.

## Overview

The Near Mile still has days in it. [[The Third Hearth]] lodges the people who stop, Brenthael hosts, and [[The Mill-hold]] is the year the wood stood in water. Raillath is the ordinary halt in the gap between them: a few hearths, a shrine with water, and no claim to be the place a child Turns unless that child already lives here.

There is a Hand here, a village Hand, sound, and not a seat. Leaf-Fall for these hearths is small, and the crowds on the Mile do not stop for it. They stop to drink.

**Raillath's work is being a halt.** [[Nelath]] is where the walk stopped, out on a spur, and this is not that road. Brenthael and the hold are already arguing the neighbour's week. This halt is not their argument, and it is not a deathless note.

## The street

There is one street. The shrine stands where the road does, with a flat top, a cup of water, climbing-scuff on the stone, and an undyed ribbon some mornings. **Lonteith** *(LON-tayth)* keeps the ribbon off the drink, and will not say who leaves it. The cistern is for people, and the water at the shrine is the road's. The houses face the post because the post was here first. There is no wall, and there is no upper room full of a pulled-in day. A loft sleeps a carter, and the loft is not a story.

A day grove-ward is Brenthael, whose Hand is sound, and a day further out is the Mill-hold. In a bad Eolthael the necessity-column passes this post thirsty and keeps going. Lonteith will fill a cup and will not chalk a bed. Hosting the week would make this halt a second Brenthael, and they will not do it.

Food is the kitchen's, and whatever the column does not eat. Fuel comes from the same coppice as the rest of the Mile. The hearths are mixed Kinds, and there is no Kind-quarter.

Someone ties an undyed ribbon here, and the ribbon is not a leaf-colour. Lonteith will not name the hand that ties it. A clerk who calls the ribbon a blank line has already filed the wrong thing.

## What can enter a scene

- **The column at the cistern.** They may be Mill-hold children, or they may be the walk back. They drink, and they do not stay. Someone asks to stay, and the kitchen is already full of its own people.
- **A cart that thought this was the inn.** The cart is a day early, or a day late. The shrine is not Thilim's stone, and it is not Meirim's crowd.
- **The ribbon on the cup.** Lonteith moves it, a child ties it back, and nobody calls it a colour.

## In-world text

*Chalk on the shrine's road-side, written in Hale-month by Lonteith.*

> *Drink. Do not stay the week. Brenthael is grove-ward. The hold is the other way, and their wood is not this post.*
>
> *The ribbon is not a colour. Do not copy it. If you need a bed you have already passed one, or you have not come to it yet.*

"Do not stay" is how this halt is hospitable to a column that has to keep moving. The chalk is meant as a kindness, and it can be read as a refusal.
