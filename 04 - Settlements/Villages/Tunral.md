# Tunral

> **A kitchen on the live-front road.** *(TUN-ral.)* It sits about a week short of [[Harrow's Green]], on the way a traveler already walks between the frontier coast and that canopy. You cannot see the green from here. This kitchen is not the three hamlets, it is not [[Eolvaeth]], and it is not a march-seat.

> The keeper of the post is *Huval*. A neighbour says the given name, and a stranger hears *Huval of Tunral*. There is no house-name. [[Polity Archetypes|Vaethorn]] is a different road, and it does not get a person-list here.

## Overview

[[Saelvaeth]]'s march is a line of grafts and the gaps between them. [[Harrow's Green]] is a Hand that grew up, and the hamlets can see that Hand from the ford. Tunral is further back, one kitchen and a mile-post, where the road toward that luck is still a road and not yet a view. There is no Tree here, and nobody planted a slip to give the post a point. Dead wood is not a Hand, and this village will not audition for one.

People sleep here because the next dry ground is not guaranteed, and **Huval** *(HOO-val)* feeds the night, and the speech is worn. Huval will point toward Harrow's and will not draw the canopy on the post. They have not seen it this month, and the bog, or a flood, or a simple fold of hill, is enough, because weather on the Sundered upland already does this. The village did not invent a marvel.

**Tunral's work is a night on a road that already exists.** The neighbour's week belongs to Harrow's, and to kitchens that can walk home from it. The wait as a destination belongs to Eolvaeth, which cannot see Harrow's and does not want to. This is not a guest-grove. One kitchen is not a cluster.

## The street

The post, the kitchen, and a yard that will take a cart if the cart is small: that is the whole street. The road comes in from the coastward country and goes on toward the rise. There is no square, because there is no canopy here to put a square around, and there is no stone pulled in from a further day. The post is a post. At dusk Huval sets a bowl outside, on the side away from Harrow's, and will not say why. The bowl is not a first meal, and it is not a shrine. A [[The Door-Keepers|Door-Keeper]] who tried to officiate at it would be in the wrong valley. [[The First Bowl]] lies the other direction from the hamlets, and it is not this road.

Food is oats, a smoked fish if the coastward week was kind, and whatever is in the pot. There is no stiller. A [[Two-Bodied]] on the road is a traveler, not a quarter of the village. The Kinds are mixed in the sense that a frontier road is mixed.

A pilgrim who calls the bowl the Mother's portion has already preached. A clerk who calls the bowl a due has already charged for it.

## What can enter a scene

- **A child who is ten.** The green is about a week away if the bog allows the road, and the bog may not allow it. Huval will not invent a colour for the child, and will not send them back to a gospel-town to be consoled.
- **A clear day.** Someone climbs the yard-wall, and there is still no canopy to see. Any argument about whether Harrow's Spoke this week has no evidence in this yard.
- **The bowl.** It is set out at dusk, on the side away from the road's hope. A guest eats from the kitchen and asks about the other bowl. Huval puts supper in their hands instead.

## In-world text

*Said over the pot, not chalked, by Huval in Hale-month.*

> *Supper is the kitchen. The green is a week if the bog allows. I have not seen it this month. I will not draw it on the post.*
>
> *If you are ten, you are ten here too. I am not a warden and this is not a Tree. Eat. The other bowl is not for you. Do not ask me whose it is.*

A week lost to the bog is the march's queue, sitting in one yard. The child is not told that this kitchen is where the gift failed, and the post stays blank.
