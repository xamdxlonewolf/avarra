# The Third Hearth

> **A road-house that stayed a hearth.** It is the third lodging out from the Motherwood on the [[The Walking Years|Near Mile]], the short road in to the origin, and **Thilim** *(THIL-im)* keeps it. Thilim is [[Long-Lived]], one of the ageless keepers of memory, and has kept this house inland since before [[The First Cut]], as later clerks would say. The First Cut is the first time a cutting of the Tree took in new ground, which is where those clerks start their years. Seine's bed is still made. The notes here are smaller than the [[The White Note House|White Note]]'s, and the shrine is larger.

> People say *the third hearth*. The road-house cant is *brenhael*, the name any such house can be called. The neighbour-town is **Brenthael** *(BREN-thael)*. Thilim is not on the quay. Brenthael is not [[Orenbren]]'s capital, and it does not capture the [[The Tree-Wardens|First Seat]], the college of wardens that sits in the wood.

## Overview

This is an old Near-Mile house on [[Maiethorn]], three days' walk out from [[Turning Tree|Thaeloren]] toward the approach-country. It still has beds, porridge, a dry room, a green-lot, and a shed. In Brenvaeth, the years when a child's Turning was the walk to the one Tree, the house sold that walk, the one you could not miss twice. In Ornthael, the later years when a town's own Tree is the ordinary door, the house still opens for Eolthael, the month of the Turning.

It sits in [[Orenbren]]'s lodging-country, the stretch of civic beds within a week's walk of the grove, and it is a seed of that kind of house, not the compact's throne. The inns grew a country. This is one house.

The house's older work is devotion, the extra mile walked as a civic season by people who could already have Turned at home. The loft still sleeps necessity, the people who still have to walk, and, rarely, prestige, the households who come because they can be seen coming. Thilim can tell who is which. They will not always tell you. The White Note sells the First-Hand year, a booked stay at the origin for people who do not need it. This house makes the bed.

The Ledger Coast, the coast that prices a house by what its paper earns, calls this a bed-house that failed to become a bank. Thilim asks who will be here at dark. One description prices the building; the other decides whether supper is enough.

## Site

The Near Mile is a week's door to the origin. Counted from the grove outward, the first hearth is a day out, then the second, then this **third**. Pilgrims walking *in*, toward the wood, hit the neighbour-town before they hit the inn.

**Brenthael** stands a day's walk further from the grove than the inn. It is a Hands-town that grew around a graft, a cutting that took, planted beside the road. Brenthael's own children Turn at home, under that Tree, and the Mile goes on past the town: [[Raillath]], then [[The Mill-hold]], then the rest of Maiethorn. Children there are told the road goes to the Motherwood. It does, if they keep walking past the inn, two more hearths, and the wood. Most Hands-children do not.

The Third Hearth kept the older job, which is beds for people still going in. Water is a well in the yard and the **stone** by the road. The stone is a day's-walk shrine, a mark of one day's walking, that never got pulled into Brenthael's square, because the inn refused to let the town have it. Thilim uses the stone for water and will not join the argument about pulling it down. Brenthael's warden wants it moved. The stone stands in the way of a crowd that is not this house's crowd.

Food is the pot, the green-lot, and whatever the extra mile pays in Hale-month, the month the road is crowded. The shed is still a shed.

## Features

- **The upper room** still has too many beds, and it has not been converted to anything else. It opens in Hale-month and is quiet in Ledger-month, the quieter month after the crowd. A devotion-column, a necessity-family, and a Road-hand going the other way with a pot, away from the grove rather than toward it, can sleep in it in the same week. That is two kinds of need in one afternoon. A prestige household that did not book the White Note's winter is rare, and when one lands here it is conspicuous.
- **Seine's bed** is Held, kept made for Seine of the far cape, who was due in a tenth summer and did not come. The mother said next year. Next year was last year, and it has been last year for a length of time Thilim will not convert into Cut-years, the clerks' numbered count. It is not a shrine Thilim will admit, and it is a shrine everyone else can see.
- **The slate** is still kept. The verso still does not match the recto, the back of the board still does not say what the front says, and the full text is in [[The Walking Years]].
- **The stone** stands by the road, not in Brenthael's square, and it has climbing-scuff on it anyway, because people still climb it. Road-hands still aim for the water. Brenthael wants the stone down so the extra mile does not look like a requirement on their approach. Thilim will not move a name.
- **Green-lot and shed** are still a pot and a shed, not Tithe-infrastructure, not the civic works a town builds so people can pay the upkeep a Condition asks of them. A cutting sits in the pot and is still not a Tree. Thilim missed a green once, and that miss is not the First Cut.
- **Brenthael's square** is a neighbour, not a second type of house. A Tree stands at the centre, the Mile goes on past the boughs, and Leaf-Fall, the day the leaves come down on the children, is the town's civic season. Lodging-guild pressure comes in from Orenbren, and there is no throne under it. Mixed Kinds live there, people of more than one birth. The town's own version of the extra mile is a holiday. From the last house they can see the inn's stone, and they resent it as a grave that is also an advertisement: a remembered name, and a sign that still draws the walk.

## What the house still does

Brenthael Turns at home. The Near Mile is optional now, and Orenbren is the country that houses the people who still want the origin. The stone still waters a mile. Seine's name is still cut over the door. A necessity-family from thinner soil still pays on the home-walk, paying when they walk home. A Hand can fail. A Hand is a town's grafted Tree, and [[The Mill-hold]], two days past Brenthael, put a Hands-town back on the neighbour's week this year, the week you walk to someone else's Tree when your own will not serve. Brenthael's Tree has not sickened, and Thilim does not say the age is over.

The two fates of the same origin still stand next to each other. The White Note collected, on a [[Strandoren]] quay, and the Third Hearth kept making the bed. Mataero, from that other house, sees this one only as a warning.

At dark, Thilim sets the yard-broom across the doorway and steps over it, and they will not say why.

## People & Powers

[[Thilim]] is a person now, with a life of their own on [[People of the Turning]].

- **[[Thilim]]** is Long-Lived, inland, and tired. When Thilim says *I walked*, the phrase can mean four different things, and the four are set out under [[The Walking Years|the four meanings]]. A Blood-Read, the memory a Long-Lived can take from blood, will give a true fragment of a walk. It will not tell you which meaning Thilim intends when they make the bed. They want the stone's water for the pot at dark. The document of this house is **Seine's bed**, made, and it is not a notice anyone can file.
- **Meirim** *(may-REEM)* is Brenthael's town-warden, and wants the stone. Meirim is practice-first, so the warden's place is held by the work and is not reserved to a Kind, the ancestry a person is born with. Meirim speaks a fall, the town's Leaf-Fall, in a square that used to be a road. This Hale-month Meirim also has a necessity-column, a group still walking because they have to, coming *in* from [[The Mill-hold]]. The chalked complaint under the lip of the stone is Meirim's.
- **Orenbren lodging-custom** makes hospitality into rent in Eolthael. Thilim charges less than that custom, and is called a failure for the difference. The compact does not own this house.
- **The First Seat** sits in the Motherwood *beside* this country, and it is not the country's crown. A pilgrim who asks Thilim where the college is will be pointed at the wood, not at the inn.

## What can enter a scene

- **A living guest this week** may be a devotion-column, a necessity-family, or a prestige household that missed the White Note. Thilim can tell which guest is which, and will not always tell you. The loft is one afternoon of two kinds of need.
- **Meirim at the door**, wanting the stone down before the extra mile starts to look like a requirement on Brenthael's approach.
- **A note-collector** from the White Note's kind of paper, looking at empty beds and calling the emptiness a failure.
- **A name at the door** that is not Seine and thinks it is, or that is Seine, so that Thilim has to decide whether the bed is still Held.

## In-world text

*A chalked complaint from Meirim's warden-hearth, carried to the Third Hearth and left under the stone's lip. It is Cut-year 387, after Hale-month. Thilim has not answered on paper.*

> *The crowd will need the approach. Your stone is a mile that is no longer owed. Water we have. Names we can keep on a ribbon in the square, which is where a remembered name belongs.*
>
> *You house the extra mile. We Turn at home. If you make a shrine of a bed, that is a house matter. A stone in the road is a town matter.*
>
> *We are not pulling a grave. We are pulling a requirement that has ended.*

The complaint shows that Brenthael wants the stone out of its crowd's way, and that it offers a ribbon in the square as the place for the remembered name. The Held bed is still made. If Seine comes, the stone will still be standing in the way of a crowd that is not hers.

## GM Notes

Food is the pot, the green-lot, and whatever the extra mile pays in Hale-month. The shed is still a shed. Do not clone [[The Greens-Keepers]] onto the yard.

Do not add a court around the inn. Do not move Thilim to the quay to tidy the two fates into one place. Do not kill Thilim to close the Held bed. Do not invent a second banking-order out of this inn. Do not make Brenthael [[Orenbren]]'s capital, and do not let it capture the [[The Tree-Wardens|First Seat]].

The two fates of the same origin are already set. The White Note collected, and the Third Hearth kept making the bed, so do not have Thilim "lose" because the paper went to the coast.

The house's older work is devotion, the extra mile. Do not make devotion, necessity, and prestige all into the house's theme at once. Necessity in the loft is Thilim's eye, not a second town-theme. Prestige that lands here is the household that missed the White Note, and it is friction, not Orenbren's brand.

Do not clone a deathless house and call it the state. Orenbren is many hearths, Brenthael is a neighbour square and not a capital, and the three archetype seats are [[Eolvaeth]], [[Orentel]], and [[Maiethlir]]. Do not retcon Brenthael into [[Maiethlir]].

Thilim's four jobs stay disagreed. When Thilim says *I walked*, do not pick which sermon they are giving.

Mataero's blindness stands. Do not correct that junior in Thilim's voice unless play wants the collision.

On the keystone, do not let the stone assert a mind. It is a day's water and a name.

The habit count is one, and the count is full. It is the broom in the doorway. A later note must not explain it, and must not turn it into a power, a relic, or a rite. Do not add a person. Thilim stays the one person here.

The hooks are Seine's name over the door, Brenthael wanting the stone down, a First-Hand year and a neighbour's week in the same loft, a note that outlived a family, Thilim asked to convert a house-year into a Cut-year for a court, a cutting in the pot that is still not a Tree, a pilgrim who thought Orenbren *was* the Seat, wool-merchants from the White Note's loft looking at empty beds and calling them failure, and Meirim hosting [[The Mill-hold]]'s cohort while still wanting this stone down.

Do not sicken Brenthael to tidy the sick-Tree type. That square is [[The Mill-hold]].
