# The Seeing-Ring

> **A high place that was over-built, then politely emptied.** On a hill in [[Maiethorn]]'s old Watching country, the hymn-country where people kept a watch on the Tree before they gave it a mother, north-east of [[Maiethlir]] on the Known Map, stands a ring of stones older than any name a clerk will be allowed to write. This note does not record the days from Maiethlir. Someone later put a shrine on the ring. Someone after that took the shrine's roof off and left the stones. The everyday name is **the Seeing-Ring.** Folk call it **the High Stones.** Watchers keep a night there and will not call it a temple. The leftovers of the count are in [[The Other Count]].

> The lower ring is undated, and it is not a date for the Tree. The upper work is overlay, from the Hands years or from late in the Walking years, and which of those it is remains argued. The Hands years are the years when a town could Turn under its own Tree. The Walking years are the older years, when Turning meant the walk to the one Tree. **Tithsael** *(tith-SAYL)* keeps the present night.

## Overview

The hill is not Thaeloren, the origin Tree in the Motherwood, and it does not want to be. On a clear Eolthael, the month of the Turning, you can see a smear of Motherwood weather from the top, which is why later people built a shrine. If the First Hand is *there*, her hand at the origin, showing in that weather, a seeing-place *here* can be talked into hers.

The lower work is a ring, not quite a circle and not quite a wall, of stones set to make a person turn as they walk, so that you look *out*. That looking-out is the whole argument [[The Watching]] needs. The Watchers keep the Tree and say it was already seeing, and that a mother-name came after the seeing. Each layer on this hill is a story laid over a seeing.

The upper work is cut from a kinder quarry, stone that is not the ring's own. The steps do not match the ring's tread, and a socket once held a door-beam. A Tender's mark, the sign a Tree-warden pecks to bless a place, is pecked over a withheld pattern. The roof is gone, and the mark is still there if you know to rub the lichen. Wardens call the emptying *respect*, and Watchers call it *finally*.

## Features

- **The ring-walk.** Clockwise is the local habit, and a Watcher will not tell you why. A warden will tell you it is Turning-Week in stone, the week of the Turning copied into a direction, which is overlay so confident it sounds like folklore.
- **The out-looks.** Four gaps are wider than the rest. One faces the smear of the origin, one faces the Old Crossing weather, and two face nothing a present map cares about. Nobody says what those two face.
- **The emptied shrine.** What is left is steps, a beam-socket, and no roof. A ribbon sometimes appears on the socket in Eolthael and is gone by Leddorn ([[The Socket Ribbon]]), Leddorn being the later month. Tithsael takes the ribbon off if it is a colour-ribbon, a ribbon tied for a colour a Tree Gives. A hug-ribbon, tied for the hug that Keeps a child and gives no colour, they will leave, which is how a Watcher does a kindness the Seat will not understand.
- **The pecked mark.** The Tender's blessing sits over a withheld geometry. You can feel both if you put a thumb on them, the warden's sign and the older pattern under it, which is both practices in one touch.
- **The night.** Tithsael keeps it, not as a service but as a seeing. People come with a cough, a question, or a child who was not Held and was not Given, neither kept by a Tree nor given anything by one, and who wants a hill that does not owe them a name.

## How you enter

- **The hill path.** The path is ordinary, and you climb it in a morning. Once you are in the ring, clockwise is the local habit.
- **The emptied shrine steps.** They are the later tread, and they do not match the ring. A warden will call these steps the door, and a Watcher will call them overlay, work laid on after the ring.
- **Under-ring crawl.** Between the lower stones and the hill, on the sea-gap side, there is a squeeze a small person can do and a mainlander can do with a scraped back. It may be a drainage-gap or a seeing-gap. It goes a body-length, opens on one out-look from underneath, and there is no temple-crypt.
- **The night.** Tithsael lets you sit if you did not bring a colour-ribbon and did not bring a year to cut.

## Pressures (while you are there)

- **A clerk** wants a Cut-year, a numbered year, cut into the ring. Tithsael will not let the chisel touch, and a party can stand with either side.
- **A warden** wants a roof put back on the socket, and calls that *respect*. Watchers call a roof the start of a congregation.
- **A colour-ribbon** shows up on the socket in Eolthael, and Tithsael takes that one off, while a hug-ribbon is left where it was tied.
- **Maiethvael** will try to furnish the night, to bring oil and a lamp, without putting anyone on a list. Furnishing a night is already a bill.
- **Weather.** The smear of the origin shows only on a clear Eolthael. The other two out-looks show themselves in any weather, which is worse for a pilgrim who came wanting a meaning.

## Discoveries (still no year)

You might find a second withheld cut under the Tender's mark, a pot-ring of ordinary clay, a view of the origin that makes a pilgrim weep and a Watcher shrug, a scratch under the ring that is not a letter, and lichen that grows only on the overlay quarry, the later stone and not the ring's.

A visitor can see a stone circle, a ruined shrine, and a night-watch. From that they can guess two builds, a later overlay, and a faith that will not start its history at a knife, which is to say it will not let the first cutting of living wood be the year the count begins. Nobody says why two gaps face nothing on a present map, or what "seeing" meant before it meant a mother.

## Archaeology and play

You can climb the hill in a morning, and you can keep the night with Tithsael if you came without a colour-ribbon and without a year. Someone may ask you to rub the pecked mark and say which hand was first. You can be hired to stop a clerk from writing a Cut-year on the ring, or to put a roof back on the socket, or to take a colour-ribbon off it.

## In-world text

*Tithsael's night-slate, one line a week, kept under a stone that is not in the ring. It is a watch kept in writing, not a service.*

> *Ribbon off. Clerk came with a year; I would not let the chisel touch. A child asked if the hug lives here. I said the hill does not Give and does not Keep. The child said that was a better door than a slight. I did not correct them. The origin was a smear. The sea-gap was weather. The other two were themselves. If you are a Speaker, this is not a colour. If you are Limrae's child, this is not a row. If you are Hildal's child, this is not a seat. I am going to sleep on the inward step of a different stone. I am not taking the hill with me.*

The slate shows that Tithsael turned the clerk's chisel away, heard the child call the hill a better door than a slight, and did not correct that. It refuses a Speaker's colour, refuses to be a row for Limrae's child, and refuses to be a seat for Hildal's child. Then Tithsael walks off to a different stone and does not take the hill along.

## GM Notes

This is an over-built Watching site. The lower ring is uncounted, the upper shrine is overlay, and the shrine has been emptied. Do not discover that it was her first temple, and do not discover that it was a Closed Seat outer gate. That hunger belongs to [[The Low Wall]] and to the grove.

It is keystone-adjacent. Seeing without a mother's name is the Watchers' Before. That reading is compatible with her being real, and with the name having come later. Do not decide her species here.

Tithsael is a present person, not a historical founder of the hill. They keep a night. They are not a pope of the hill, and the night is not a church with them at the head of it.

Do not date the ring. Do not hand the table a confirmation that she stood here, a date for the lower ring, a first sermon, or the claim that it was a Closed Seat gate. That last hunger belongs to [[The Low Wall]]. Do not say which hand was first at the pecked mark, and do not make Tithsael Rithim's cousin to tidy a map.

The pattern this site serves is the keystone's pattern: reach and seeing are older than the name. Skeptics still win the mundane reading, which is a high place with later piety laid on it. Both readings share the same hill. The child at the night can be Limrae's if the slate's joke is wanted. Pair the hill with [[Maiethlir]] if the party already knows a Speaker who will not say the line. It is the same hymn-country and a different hill.

The hooks are a year stopped before it is cut, a colour-ribbon taken off, a night sat with a child who wants a door that is not a slight, the pecked mark rubbed and left undecided, a Maiethvael guild trying to furnish the night, and the two out-looks that face nothing.
