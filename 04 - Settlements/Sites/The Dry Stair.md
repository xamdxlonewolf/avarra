# The Dry Stair

> **A stair that does not climb to a Tree.** In the dry east of [[Saelthael]], the Rain-Shadow, where the towns sit on wells, a rise above a well-town holds a flight of steps cut into the rock. They climb to a door-stone and a basin that has not held water in any summer a living person will swear to. The everyday name is **the Dry Stair.** Folk call it **the stair that does not climb to a Tree**, and they say that both as a joke and as a warning. What never received a year sits with the chronicle leftovers in [[The Other Count]].

> The stair is undated. It is not the Tree's original temple, not a first Fox-shrine of the fox-born who keep hearths in this dry country, not a Kind-nation ruin left by one born people ruling the rise, and not Ornsael's hill.

## Overview

The Rain-Shadow still walks west after the core stopped. The core is the country that used to walk that road and then stopped, and Saelthael's dry east did not stop, so the well-towns still send people toward a Tree. This stair does not join that walk. It climbs *up*, only as far as the rise, to nothing a Hands-child is trained to want. A Hands-child is a child of the present years, raised to expect a Tree, a living wood, at the end of a climb like this, and the top of these steps is bare stone.

The steps are worn in the middle, because people have gone up for a long time. At the top there is a slab with a threshold cut into it, standing like a door with no wall, and behind it a basin pecked into the rock that is dry. Taken-In who know wells, and who are the people a dry town asks when a hole in rock will not hold, say the rock was never a good cistern, because the pecking is wrong for holding. Old Ways visitors on a visiting week leave a first-meal at the threshold, the meal they set down where they are willing to call something a guest, and they do not call the slab a guest. Children dare the last step and come down louder than they went up.

A later warden tried to plant a slip in the basin, a cutting of living wood carried in a pot, "so the stair would have a point." The slip died ([[The Dry Slip]]), and dead wood is not a Tree. The Seat, from which a second cutting would have come, did not send one. Saelthael already knows about dirt: the soil is why a slip dies on this rise.

## Features

- **The worn flight.** The steps are countable, and the count changes if you ask three children. One step is broken, and people skip it and call the skip luck, or a slight, or nothing, without agreeing which.
- **The door-stone.** It is a threshold cut into the slab, with no wall and nothing over it, so you can stand in it and be on both sides of a door that does not enclose anything. [[The Door-Keepers]], who officiate at a real door and at the meal that makes a guest, will not officiate when they have come this far west, though they will eat if a bowl is there.
- **The dry basin.** The pecking is the wrong shape for a well and the right size for a pot, which is why the warden tried a slip there. A ring of salt-white sits in the pecking. It is not motherwood-ash, not ash brought from the Motherwood, and it is not a colour.
- **The well-town below.** It is not Ornsael. It is a lesser walk-hold, a well-town that still sends its people west on the road. The stair is the town's dare and its shame at once: visitors ask where the Tree is, the town points west, and then someone laughs at the stair.
- **Names in the risers.** A few steps have a scratch that might be a person. Watchers, who will not put a date on stone this old, would not date them. A Threnmaieth clerk, the kind of clerk who copies a mark onto a record, would want them copied. The town rubs them with a thumb and does not read them aloud.

## How you enter

- **The worn flight.** The climb takes a quarter-hour, whether you skip the broken step or don't, and the count changes if you ask three children.
- **The door-stone.** You can stand in the cut and be on both sides, because there is no wall and nothing over it. Door-Keepers who have come this far west will eat if a bowl is there, and they will not officiate.
- **Behind the basin.** A crawl starts where the pecking is wrong. It is a slit a hand can disappear into, and then a shoulder, and it opens on a **second basin**, lower in the rise, also dry, with the same salt-white and no Tree-pit, no hole a Tree was set in.
- **The well-town below.** It is not [[Ornsael]]. It is a lesser walk-hold that still sends people west. You enter the Stair from their shame: visitors ask where the Tree is, the town points west, someone laughs, and then someone dares you.

## Pressures (while you are there)

- **A warden with a pot** has another slip, because the last one died, and still wants the stair to have a point.
- **A clerk** wants the scratches on the risers copied, while the town rubs them with a thumb and does not read them, and a party can stand with either.
- **A missed child** is unTurned, and not Kept. The child missed Eolthael, the month when a child is brought to a Tree to Turn, and so never Turned. Kept is the name for a child the leaves closed around and gave no colour, and the town will not put that name on a miss. Someone will ask you to sit the door-stone overnight so the child has a door that is not a slight and not a Tree.
- **Fox-of-the-Sands hearths** want no outsiders claiming the slab as a Fox shrine, and outsiders will try.
- **Night.** The last step is louder going down than it was going up, and the wind in the basin is not water.

## Discoveries (still no year)

You might find that the salt-white is mineral, or ash of ordinary brush, or something the town does not have a word for. You might find the second basin, a west-road name scratched over an older scratch, pecking-tool marks that are the wrong angle for a cistern on purpose, and a dead slip's fibres still in the upper basin.

A visitor can see the stairs, a dry fountain, and a dare. From that they can guess a failed planting, a habit of doors that have no grove behind them, and a town that walks west because this place is not a Tree. Nobody says what the basin was for, or why the pecking is the wrong angle on purpose.

## Archaeology and play

You can climb it in a quarter-hour, and you can dare the last step once you are up. The town will also hire you. They may want a slip carried up that is going to die, or a clerk stopped from copying the risers, or someone to sit the door-stone overnight because a child missed Eolthael and the town will not call them Kept.

## In-world text

*A west-road child's count, said going up, copied by a Road-hand who should have been going the other way, and who wrote the dare down instead of walking on.*

> *One, the broken. Two, the thumb-name. Three, the laugh. I am not to say the last number because the last step is the dare. At the top there is a door that does not keep you in. There is a bowl that does not keep water. We go west for the week. We come here so we know the difference. If a warden plants again I will water it, and I will not call the death a slight. If a clerk writes a year I will rub it. If you are from the core, this is not your mile-shrine. We climb those too. They have ribbons. This has a dare.*

The count shows that the town climbs the Stair to know the difference between a dare and a mile-shrine, one of the ribboned climbs on the same west road. The Road-hand who copied it wrote a dare down as if it were a mile.

## GM Notes

This is not a Tree-ruin. The whole point is the climb that refuses to be a Hand, a Tree someone planted and then claimed. A reveal that it was a Tree all along breaks the leftover and breaks the Rain-Shadow's joke, which is how the dry country tells the truth about the stair.

It is not a Kind-nation. The Sands-Fox are densest in the country, and this hill is still a town's dare rather than their shrine. Do not make the door-stone a Fox shrine.

Read it as keystone-adjacent by absence. A place that never held a Hand, next to a country that still walks to one, is compatible with bounded reach and with dirt. Skeptics win the mundane reading easily here, because the soil is enough. Do not overplay the tell.

Do not hand the table any of the closed answers. Do not say this was her first Hand, or a Fox temple, or a Closed Seat well-gate, or a year, or that it was a Tree all along. Pair the stair with [[Ornsael]]'s west-road so both are in one week. Ornsael's crisis is a falling drink, and this Stair Gives nothing.

The hooks that belong here are a second slip to stop, a missed child to sit the door with, a year rubbed off a riser, an Old Ways meal at a slab that is not a guest, Ornled slate set against the Saelthael joke, and that same week shared with [[Ornsael]]'s west-road.
