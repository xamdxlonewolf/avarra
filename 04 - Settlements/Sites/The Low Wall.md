# The Low Wall

> **A wall that does not know its enemy.** In [[Orenbren]] lodging-country, the Near Mile country of inns within the first days' walk of the Motherwood, a low run of stone leaves the present road, stays outside the [[Rothallo|Inner Close]], walks a fold of ground, and stops. The everyday name is **the Low Wall.** Folk call it **the Wall Against No One.** The Close, the walled ground people still live in, will tell you the wall is theirs. The stone is a different colour from the Close's own stone. What no one could date sits with the leftovers in [[The Other Count]].

> The stone is from before the Walk, the years that have no start, and it is undated. It is not a date for the Tree. It is not the Inner Close.

## Overview

The wall is shoulder-high in most places, higher where the ground dips, and broken in three gaps that people use as gates. It does not enclose a town, and it does not face the grove of the Motherwood. It faces *out*, if "out" still means anything, toward a fold of ground that is now lodging-fields and the back garden of an old road-house.

No one living built it. [[Before the Walk]] has no start, no first year anyone can honestly write, and this stone is of that kind of old: present, stubborn, and uncounted. [[The Watching]] will fight any Cut-year a clerk tries to scratch into it. A Cut-year is a numbered year in the count clerks use, and the Watchers will not let that count be cut into stone that was never given a beginning. [[The Old Ways]] will leave a first-meal at a gap, the meal they set where they mean a guest, and will not call the wall a guest-Tree. Motherfaith wardens, who serve the faith that gives the Tree a mother, want a ribbon taken off the wall, because children already climb it the way they climb a mile-shrine, a stone of the old walk with a ribbon on it, and this wall is not a mile-shrine.

[[The Closing]] made a *different* wall, and Hildal's Retreat is that work: dressed stone, taller than this, and still lived in. People who say the Low Wall is the Close's outer ring are doing a claim. Scratch the lichen and the quarries part company: the Close is one quarry, and this wall is another.

## Features

- **The three gaps.** Folk use them as gates. One has a socket that might have held a post, one has been widened so a cart can pass, and one is only wide enough for a person. The Close's labour-roster sometimes "assigns" a walk through that last gap as owed work. The Book, the Close's own record of who owes, cannot make that walk count as owning the stone.
- **The inward step.** On the field side, the footing is built as if someone expected to stand *behind* the wall and look out. There is no walkway left, and there is a habit of standing there anyway.
- **The blank name-stone.** Mid-run, a slab carries a panel that once held letters. The letters are gone, and they were taken off rather than worn away. Watchers say that taking is correct. A Closed Heir, someone who still claims a rank inside the Close, says the panel was theirs. A lodging-guild says a later pilgrim stole a luck-name, a name people keep for luck.
- **The garden end.** The wall dives into the back of a hearth that still lets beds. It is a road-house of the same kind as [[The Third Hearth]], not Thilim's house. The innkeep waters the last course of stone as if it were a pot-ring, the circle where a pot would sit, and it is not a pot-ring.

## Who dug

It was one summer, and it was not a school of diggers. In Hale-month of Cut-year 381, the month when crowds come to climb, **Nurreith** *(NUR-rayth)* and three lodging-guild hands opened the name-stone's bed. [[The Walled Book|Delamem]] had paid for a chip that could be filed as the Close's quarry. A Watcher stood in the cut and would not let a chisel touch the panel. **Thuda** wanted the gang off the garden by dark, and pulled them.

What they fought over was the chip, and under the chip the right to call the wall a page. Delamem needed the quarry to match, so the chip could be filed as the Close's own stone. Nurreith needed the labour back on the guest-beds, and also needed the chip to be worth the afternoon, or the guild would call the day a waste. The Watcher needed no Cut-year on the stone. Thuda needed the person-gap empty and the last course left as a pot-ring, which it is not.

The chip was the other quarry, and nobody scratched a year. They filled the bed the same week, and the fill is newer than the taking of the letters, so the fill can date the quarrel and cannot date the wall, the Tree, or the Before. The next person with a shovel is still a person with a shovel. No office came of the afternoon, and no method came of it either. The Close still wants a confirmation, the Watchers still want the panel blank, and the garden still wants its evenings.

Nurreith is a given name, the kind a neighbour says out loud. A stranger who hears *Nurreith of the garden-end* has already made more address out of it than Thuda likes. There is no house-name. This is not a list.

## How you enter

- **The cart-gap.** It has been widened, so you walk in the way a delivery would. The Close prefers this gap when they want the wall to look like theirs.
- **The person-gap.** It is only wide enough for one. The labour-roster "assigns" a walk through it as owed work, so you can be sent in, or you can send someone out.
- **The socket-gap.** It might have held a post. Old Ways leave a first-meal here some weeks, and you can step over a bowl to go through.
- **Under the name-stone.** The panel's bed is loose if you have an afternoon and a reason. The way in is a crawl, not a dungeon-level. It goes along the footing toward the inward step and stops, and there is no under-city.
- **The garden-end loft.** Thuda *(THOO-dah)* lets beds against the last course. You can sleep here and wake with the wall in the window.

## Pressures (while you are there)

- **The Close** wants a confirmation they can file. Delamem will pay for a quarry-match that is not true, a chip declared to be their stone when the quarry is the other one.
- **A Watcher** will not let a Cut-year touch the panel, and will stand in the chisel's way.
- **The lodging-guild** wants climbers off the high dip before Eolthael, and wants the labour off the garden now.
- **Thuda** wants the person-gap empty by dark, will feed you, and will not call the stone a shrine.
- **Time.** Hale-month is when the crowds climb. Ledger-month is the quieter one, and it is meaner about who hired you.

## Discoveries (still no year)

You might find a different quarry's chip in the name-stone's bed ([[The Other Chip]]), and the Cut-year 381 fill on top of that chip, which dates a quarrel and not the wall. You might find a pot-ring that is not motherwood, a knife-ribbon someone left because they thought this was the nameless knife's first house (it is not), a second footing behind the inward step that never got a walkway, and scratch-wear on the person-gap that is newer than the Close's claim. A knife-ribbon is a ribbon left for the nameless knife, and this wall is not that knife's house.

A visitor can see a ruined wall, a climb, and an inn's back garden. From that they can guess two quarries, a claim, and a Book that assigns a walk through a gap. Nobody knows who the wall was against, or whether "against" is the right word.

## Archaeology and play

You can walk the wall in an afternoon, sleep in the garden-end loft, and be asked to fetch a child down off the high dip. The hire changes with who finds you. The Close will pay to have the wall "confirmed" as theirs, a Watcher will pay to stop a clerk from dating it, and Thuda will pay to pull a labour-gang out of the person-gap.

## In-world text

*A lodging-guild complaint, unsigned, nailed to the garden-end for a summer and then taken in. The Close had sent people to walk the gap as if the walk made the stone theirs.*

> *The Close sent three Conditioned to walk the person-gap as owed work. They are not our beds and they are not the Close's wall. The children will climb whether we complain. If a Speaker asks, it is not a shrine. If Delamem asks, it is not a page. If a Watcher asks, we did not scratch a year. Pull your labour off our garden. The stone was here when the beds were full, and it will be here when the notes are.*

The complaint calls the three walkers Conditioned, people who carry a Condition, and it dates the stone by "when the beds were full," which already counts years without cutting a Cut-year into the panel. A Speaker, in that note, is someone who would ask whether the wall is a shrine. Delamem would treat a gap in the wall as a row in the Book, and a gap is not a row.

## GM Notes

The wall is uncounted on purpose. It is the Before, kept thin enough that you can still walk it. Do not add a first king, a first war, or a first enemy-nation. A forgotten flood-bank, a forgotten raid, or a forgotten watching may be leaned on in play. The note does not pick among them.

It is keystone-adjacent, and it does not confirm anything. An old wall near the grove is compatible with people having organized around a Tree that was already there, and with people organizing around anything tall. Do not use the wall to decide what she is.

Do not solve the wall. Do not hand the table a year that starts the Tree, a first enemy with a flag, a gospel in the panel, or "it was the Close's outer ring all along" as an answer.
