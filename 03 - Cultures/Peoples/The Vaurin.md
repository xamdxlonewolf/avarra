# The Vaurin

> The heartland people of the basin — and the ones who have quietly *forgotten they are a people at all.* To a Vaurin, there is no Vaurin culture; there is just "the way things are," and everyone else has a culture. They are the water the Empire swims in, and the campaign's most invisible design problem: the majority who cannot see their own particularity.

## Overview

The Vaurin are the basin-folk of [[The Aurenne]] — the oldest-settled, most numerous, most prosperous population of the core, whose tongue became **Imperial Common**, whose river gave the trunk-waterway ([[Climate & Terrain|the Vaur]]) its name, and whose habits became "normal." They did not conquer the Empire so much as *be at the middle of it* while it grew, until their local customs quietly turned into the default and their local speech turned into the language every contract is written in.

The crucial fact about the Vaurin is that they experience themselves as having no ethnicity — the way tap water has no flavour. Ask a Vaurin what their people's traditions are and they will look at you blankly and then describe *the Empire's* holidays, as if the two were the same thing. To everyone else, they are unmistakably a people with a very strong flavour indeed.

## What they kept, what they lost

The Vaurin are the one people this frame breaks on, and usefully so:

**Kept:** everything — and therefore they never *notice* keeping it, because nothing was ever threatened.

**Lost:** the *awareness* of being distinct. When your customs become the neutral standard, you lose the ability to see them as customs. A Kessadi keeps two calendars and knows exactly which is his; a Vaurin keeps one calendar and believes it is simply *the* calendar. This is not a grievance — it costs the Vaurin nothing they can feel — but it is the quiet arrogance the conquered peoples all recognize and the Vaurin cannot: the certainty that they are the world's baseline and everyone else is a variation.

## How they feel (comfortable, and a little blind)

- **They love the Empire the way you love a climate** — without noticing, because they have never stood outside it. Their loyalty is not gratitude (they were never rescued) and not ideology (they never had to argue for it); it is simply the shape of the water. This makes them the Empire's most *reliable* subjects and its least *reflective*.
- **The kind-hearted Vaurin** genuinely believes the Empire is fair to everyone, points at [[The Halden|the content Haldar]] as proof, and cannot understand why [[Kessad]] stays sullen — "we gave them everything we have." He is not lying and he is not cruel. He simply cannot see from inside the default that being handed *someone else's* normal is not the same as keeping your own.
- **The Vaurin in imperial service** — clerks, judges, [[The Assize|circuit staff]] — are disproportionately Vaurin, and disproportionately certain they are neutral. The most dangerous phrase in the Empire is a Vaurin magistrate saying, in perfect good faith, *"I have no bias; I simply apply the law."*

> A believer PC is very likely Vaurin by default — and the table should feel the pull of that comfortable certainty *before* it learns to distrust it. The Vaurin are not villains. They are the reader's own assumptions, given a homeland.

## Customs & belief

- **The unmarked calendar.** The Vaurin festival year — sowing, the grain-fleets, harvest, the courts' riding season — *is* the imperial calendar, because the Empire adopted the basin's rhythm wholesale. Every other people schedules their life around a calendar they know is theirs; the Vaurin schedule theirs around one they think is nobody's.
- **Ancestor-thrift, not ancestor-worship.** The Vaurin keep no hill-gods and recite no clan-lines. Their piety is *practical continuity*: the tended granary, the kept ledger, the well-maintained house passed down intact. Their sacred act is *not wasting*, and their afterlife-ish comfort is *being counted* — remembered accurately in the [[The Shape of the Empire|Rolls]], your name in the count, your line unbroken on paper. Where the Haldar recite ancestors from memory, the Vaurin trust the archive to do it.
- **The good word.** Vaurin civic speech is full of warm, procedural blessings — *"may your rolls be clean," "settled and squared," "reconciled and at peace"* — the vocabulary of a people for whom the highest good is a thing correctly accounted for and gently closed. It is genuinely kind. (See GM Notes.)

## GM Notes

`visibility: gm` — walled off; this note is player-safe.

- The Vaurin exist to make the players' *own* comfort visible. The horror of [[The Cost]] lands hardest through a Vaurin lens — a decent person who administers something monstrous while believing, correctly on every visible metric, that they are the fair one.
- **The euphemism lives most naturally in Vaurin mouths.** Their reflex toward warm accounting-language — "reconciled and at peace," a name "settled and squared" — is the exact register [[The Cost]]'s gentle euphemism hides inside. Keep these as ambient pleasantries; do not let any single utterance confirm anything. The point is that when the word finally turns, the players realize they have been *hearing it as a blessing* for a hundred sessions.
- Because the Vaurin can't see their own particularity, they make the "gentle euphemism / seeing clearly is the transgression" motif ([[Themes]]) structural rather than plotted: the Vaurin literally *cannot look*, and are lovely people, and that is the trap.

> **Resolved (2026-08-12): single dominant-but-invisible core people.** The Vaurin are the heartland default, as drafted — the majority who mistake themselves for neutrality. **With one addition:** the Vaurin monoculture is dominant, *not* total. In hard-to-reach pockets — high valleys, marsh-islands, remote coasts, places the network reaches thinly — older and minority cultures persist and even thrive, precisely because the smoothing pressure of "the default" is weakest where the roads and Rolls are thinnest. These are not designed yet (a reservoir of minor peoples for later, keyed to the frontier/interstitial geography); note only that the Empire's cultural uniformity *thins with distance from the spine*, mirroring how its administrative grip does ([[The Shape of the Empire]]'s ring model). The believer-PC's Vaurin certainty is the baseline; the pockets are where it meets its exceptions.
