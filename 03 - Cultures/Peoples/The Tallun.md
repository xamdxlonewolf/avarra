# The Tallun

> The people of the cold edge — marginal, weathered, and bound to the Empire by the most literal bargain of all: *the forts feed us, and nothing crosses the line.* Not conquered so much as **absorbed**, quietly, when the line was drawn through their country. The one people whose loyalty is neither grievance nor comfort but something closer to a shared, unspoken duty.

## Overview

The Tallun are the scattered marchfolk of [[The Outmark]] — herders, trappers, and stone-cutters of thin high or far-northern country that barely repays living in. They were there before the forts, in small feuding kin-bands with no shared name; *Tallun* is an imperial convenience, from an old word for the marker-cairns their people raised on the passes. When the Empire ran the line of garrison-forts through the marches a few generations back, it did not so much conquer the Tallun as *build around them* — and then, quietly, provision them.

Their relationship to the Empire is unlike any other people's. The Kessadi lost a country; the Haldar gained a peace; the Vaurin never noticed. The Tallun made a trade so plain it barely needs saying: the interior sends grain up the long supply lines so that even here, at the last milestone, *no garrison starves and no Tallun family starves either* — and in return the Tallun keep the forts supplied, the passes marked, and the watch. They are the Empire's edge, and they know exactly what an edge is for.

## What they kept, what they lost

**Kept:** their scattered kin-bands, their cairn-marking, their weather-lore, their Talunnic tongue (guttural, half-swallowed, full of words for kinds of cold and kinds of quiet). The Empire had no reason to change any of it — there was nothing here to extract but loyalty.

**Lost:** their old freedom of movement across country that is now *the line*, and their independence from the interior's food. A Tallun family that once starved free now eats imperial grain and is not free to leave the watch. Some feel this as safety. Some feel it as a leash you cannot resent because it is also the only thing feeding your children. Mostly they do not discuss it.

## How they feel (dutiful, weathered, unsentimental)

- **They are the least ideological subjects in the Empire.** No one up here argues about whether the Empire is *good*; the argument is meaningless against the cold. The Empire is the thing that provisions the forts, and the forts are the thing that keeps the country legible, and a legible country is one where you and your children do not die in the dark. That is the whole of Tallun politics.
- **The garrison families and the old kin-bands have merged** over generations — veterans who stayed, married in, kept the watch when their term ended. A Tallun today is as likely to be a soldier's grandchild as a trapper's, and usually both. This is the one place the Empire and a conquered people have genuinely *fused* rather than layered.
- **What grievance exists is not political — it is being forgotten.** The interior barely thinks about the Outmark; the comfort of the Aurenne is paid for, in part, by cold people the basin never pictures. A Tallun does not want the country back or the water-judges restored. A Tallun wants the basin to *remember the line is held by someone.* (Compare [[Themes|complicity]]: the Vaurin cannot see what their comfort rests on, and the Tallun are part of what it rests on.)

## Customs & belief

- **Cairn-keeping.** The Tallun raise and tend stone markers — on passes, on graves, at the last edge of safe country — and a cairn is *added to,* never finished: every passer lays a stone. Their dead are cairned, not buried; you keep the dead the way you keep the line, one stone at a time, against being lost. The imperial milestones simply got absorbed into this — a Tallun tends an official milestone with the same care as an ancestor's cairn, and privately makes little distinction.
- **They do not name the cold, or the dark, or what's-beyond-the-line directly** — you speak *around* it (the Long Guest, the Far Weather, "what the cairns face"). Naming a thing, in Tallun lore, is halfway to calling it. Outsiders read this as quaint frontier superstition. The Tallun read outsiders as people who have never had to stand a night watch and mean it.
- **The Watch is a courtesy to the dead as much as a duty to the living.** You keep the watch your grandfather kept, on the wall he kept it on, facing the country he faced — even though, in living memory, *nothing has ever come.* To let the watch lapse would not be a security failure. It would be an abandonment of everyone who ever stood it before you.

## GM Notes

`visibility: gm` — walled off; this note is player-safe.

- **Handle with maximum restraint.** The Outmark is the campaign's most patient approach to [[The Cost]] (the "dam against the dark" / the frontier that went quiet). The Tallun's whole culture — *the watch against a thing that never comes, the refusal to name what's-beyond, the cairns facing outward* — is deliberately shaped to be read, for many sessions, as **ordinary frontier folkways.** Real marginal peoples are superstitious and dutiful; real won frontiers are quiet. The innocent reading and the correct reading must stay indistinguishable until very late. Do **not** confirm anything.
- **The euphemism in cold bureaucratic form** lives here: a Tallun-manned garrison ledger that "reconciles" something on a schedule (tie to [[The Shape of the Empire|reconciling the rolls]] and [[The Outmark]]). To the Tallun it is just the count. Leave it as texture.
- The "don't name what's-beyond" taboo is a gift for the late game: a people who have culturally agreed *not to look* — the [[Themes|seeing clearly is the transgression]] motif, embodied by the people standing closest to the thing. But it must first be, and long remain, just weather-superstition.
- Design-contract check: the Tallun are neither aggrieved nor grateful — they are *dutiful*, which keeps them off both clichés and makes the eventual reveal land as sorrow rather than outrage. They are the people who were right to keep the watch, even before anyone knew why.

> **Fork (needs user):** Are the Tallun a distinct fourth people, or a *sub-culture of veterans-plus-marchfolk* rather than an ethnicity in their own right? **Recommendation: keep them a distinct (if small and fused) people.** A frontier people whose entire folk-religion is *the watch* is too load-bearing for the iceberg to leave as mere garrison demographics — and their fusion of conqueror-and-conquered is a texture no other people gives you. But if the map needs fewer peoples, they could fold into the Outmark note as "who lives on the line" without much loss.
