# The Kessadi

> A delta civilization taken within living memory — proud, ancient, and unable to decide how it feels about being saved. *Kessadi* is what the Empire calls them, after the province. Among themselves the old federation is **the Karesh** — the Sworn Cities — and a person is a Karan or a Semedi or a Tovalan *first*, and Kessadi only to a tax-clerk.

## Overview

The Kessadi are the people of the southern delta-cities in [[Kessad]] — canal-builders, water-lawyers, and merchants who were running walled cities with their own courts, coin, and calendars when the Empire arrived two generations ago. They were not a wilderness that got civilized. They were a civilization that *lost*, and they have never once forgotten the difference.

There is no single Kessadi feeling about the Empire, and that is the whole of them. The delta holds, in one market square, a governor's clerk who will tell you the roads have never been safer, an old woman who buried two sons in the siege of Ish-Karan, and her grandson who wants nothing more than a posting in the capital — and all three are Kessadi, and all three are right.

## What they kept, what they lost

**Kept:** their language, their delta-gods, their pre-imperial canals (the Empire simply adopted and improved them), and — tellingly — their own notable families, who still administer the cities under an imperial governor. The Empire never demanded they stop being Kessadi.

**Lost:** their sovereignty, their coin, and their **water-judges**. This is the wound that matters. In a delta, water *is* law — every field, every wharf, every year of flood is a dispute — and the Karesh cities were ruled by hereditary colleges of water-judges who settled water-rights and all other law together, from the same bench. The Empire kept the canal-engineering and stripped the judging: now justice rides in from outside on [[The Assize|the circuit]], famously fair, answerable to no local house. To the Empire this is its proudest gift to Kessad. To the Kessadi it is the precise organ of their identity, cut out and mailed back as a courtesy.

## How they feel (it is not one thing)

- **The notable families** administer, prosper, and make the uncomfortable pro-imperial case aloud: the granaries are fuller, the courts fairer, no delta child starves in a flood year anymore. Some mean it. Some are survivors saying what survivors say.
- **The water-judge families** — dispossessed of function, not of pride — keep the old forms alive as heritage, and as something harder. This is the soil a rebellion grows in.
- **The old** remember the siege and grieve a country. **The young** frequently don't want the country back — they want to *rise inside the Empire*, which is a better ladder than the Karesh ever offered them. Their elders grieve this most of all: not conquest by sword, but a slower death, their own children choosing the winner.

> The uncomfortable truth the rebellion must swallow: the old Karesh order was an **oligarchy of water-judge houses**, and imperial law is, measurably, *fairer to the common Kessadi than their own city ever was.* The people with the sharpest grievance are the people who used to be on top. Ask a delta fisherman whether he wants the water-judges back and you may not get the answer the poets want.

## Customs & belief

- **Two of everything.** Two calendars, two names for every street, two memories of the same war — the imperial *pacification* (bloodless in the telling) and the Karesh *siege* (with a body count). Contracts are dated in both calendars and notarized twice; a Kessadi will give you an imperial answer and a Karesh answer to the same question and see no contradiction.
- **The delta-gods** are gods of flood, silt, and return — the water takes and the water gives back, and the dead are "given to the water" to come round again. Water-blessings open every venture; the flood-festival is the year's hinge.
- **The Phantom Assizes.** Once a year, at the old flood-mark, the surviving water-judge families still convene and "hear cases" — issuing rulings in a law the Empire no longer recognizes, that no one will enforce, purely to keep the office from dying. It is grief wearing the robes of a court, and it is the most Kessadi thing there is.

- The "given to the water / return" theology faintly rhymes with the deeper cosmology of return-cycles. This is a *whisper only* — never foreground it. Play it as ordinary delta religion.
- Design-contract check: material benefit undeniable; loss undeniable; and the oligarchy twist means even here there is no clean side. That is correct. Keep it.
