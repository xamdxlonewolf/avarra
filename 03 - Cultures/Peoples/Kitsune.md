# Kitsune

> **A custom born-ancestry (Kind), not a Condition.** One of the homebrew ancestries kept for this world (with [[Selkie]] and [[Tengu]]), sitting alongside Daggerheart's stock roster. You are *born* Kitsune — it is your birth-shape, mundane in this world. **Refined 2026-08-16: Foxfire dropped; two features — Slip the Frame + your Fox (Wilds / Snows / Sands), each a full feature.** See "Open questions" for anything still open.

## Overview

Kitsune are the fox-born: slight, quick-eyed people with a fox's ears and a brush of tail. In folklore the fox is a shapeshifter and a liar. **Neither is true here.** A Kitsune keeps one shape their whole life like anyone else, and they are no more dishonest than their neighbours — the fox-born are simply **hard to pin down.** They redirect. They step out of the frame of a situation and leave you looking at where they were. The tail is real and worn openly.

**Not one fox but many.** Just as the world holds arctic, desert, and forest foxes, the fox-born come in kinds shaped by the country they were born to — the **Fox of the Wilds** (red-and-russet, the forest and hedgerow fox), the **Fox of the Snows** (white-coated, born to the cold north and the high snows), and the **Fox of the Sands** (pale and huge-eared, born to the deserts and dry country). All are Kitsune, share the same slipperiness, and marry and mingle freely; a child takes after one parent's line or the other. Each carries a gift bred into it by its home ground — the pounce of the forest hunter, the vanishing of the snow-fox, the impossible ears of the desert fox.

**The fresh angle:** in a world where three of every four people acquire a [[Conditions|Condition]], the Kitsune are a Kind defined not by *changing* but by *never quite being where you left them.* Their whole reputation is misdirection-without-deceit — the fox slips the trap, it doesn't lie about the trap being there.

## Values & Worldview

Kitsune prize **the graceful exit** and the **unfought fight** — the deal that dissolves before it comes to blows, the door found before the room fills with smoke. They tend to distrust cornered situations (their instinct in a corner is to *make a new corner*) and to admire cleverness over force. A common fox-born saying: *"Never the last one holding it."* They keep their word carefully precisely because, being so slippery, an untrustworthy Kitsune would be trusted by no one — so a Kitsune's given word tends to be *very* solid, and they know it's their most valuable possession.

## Customs & Daily Life

Because they defuse rather than escalate and can never quite be cornered, Kitsune are the world's natural **mediators, brokers, and night-couriers**: they carry delicate news between rival houses, hold the neutral ground in a negotiation, walk the dark roads that others fear, and are trusted to be somewhere unnoticed and gone again without a scene. Older Kitsune, by tradition, carry themselves with more tails in the *telling* of it (the folklore of nine tails survives as reputation and honorific, not as literal biology or power).

## Daggerheart (ancestry features)

Every Kitsune has **Slip the Frame**, **and** chooses one **Fox** at character creation. The Fox is a full feature — each grants both a lasting knack and an active signature move.

- **Slip the Frame** — When you would take damage from an attack, you can mark a **Stress** to slip aside: halve the damage, then immediately move up to **Very Close** range to a position out of the attacker's reach. If you end this movement out of line of sight, you are **Hidden**.
- **Your Fox** — Choose the fox you were born to:
  - **Fox of the Wilds** *(red / forest fox)* — You are at home in tangled, broken, or overgrown country: you have **advantage** on Agility Rolls to climb, leap, tumble, or move through difficult terrain, and you ignore its movement penalty. When you make a melee attack against a target within **Very Close** range, you can mark a **Stress** to **pounce** — close the distance in a spring and, on a success, deal an extra **2d6** damage and knock the target back to Close range.
  - **Fox of the Snows** *(arctic fox)* — You ignore the effects of cold, and you can vanish where there is no cover to be had: you have **advantage** on rolls to **Hide** or move unseen in open, snowy, pale, or exposed terrain. Once per scene, you can mark a **Stress** to **go to ground** — become **Hidden** even while observed and in the open, so long as you don't take an action that would obviously reveal you.
  - **Fox of the Sands** *(fennec)* — You ignore the effects of heat, and your great ears miss nothing: you have **advantage** on rolls to hear, notice, or locate hidden creatures, sounds, or movement, by day or in the dark. When you or an ally within **Close** range would be targeted by an attack you could hear coming, you can mark a **Stress** to shout a warning — the target gains a **+2 bonus to their Evasion** against that attack.

## Open questions (to refine next)

- **Slip the Frame power band (noted, kept)** — halve-damage + reposition + conditional Hidden is deliberately a notch above the SRD ancestry band (cf. Faerie *Wings* = +2 Evasion for a Stress). Kept at full strength by choice; flag here in case a play-test wants it trimmed.
- **Fox parity check** — each Fox now = a lasting knack + a Stress-gated active (Wilds = pounce/damage; Snows = go-to-ground/stealth; Sands = warn-ally/defense). They pull in different directions (offense / stealth / support), which is good, but confirm none feels clearly weakest at your table.
- **Pounce vs. Faun's Kick** — Wilds' pounce (mark Stress, +2d6, knockback) is modeled closely on the SRD Faun *Kick* so it sits in-band. Fine, or want it more distinct?
- Physical read: how fox is a Kitsune — full ears+tail, or subtler? (Fox *variety* also colours this — Snows reads white-furred, Sands huge-eared and pale, Wilds red-russet.)
- Room for more foxes later (grey/tree fox, swift/plains fox) — the pattern scales.
