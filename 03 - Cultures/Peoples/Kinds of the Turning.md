# Kinds of the Turning

> How **Kinds**, the ancestries a person is born into, sit on the map of **The Turning**. The distribution rule is fixed: no Kind is a country. Naming, which is how a person sounds when they say who they are, lives in [[Naming People in the Turning]]. The table rulings live in [[Kind Heritage]] and are not restated here. Those rulings are the Hearth-Mark, the mark a stock character takes from the place that raised them; Mixed Ancestry, a character drawn from two Kinds; and the one surprise keyword. The four custom ancestries have their own notes, [[Kitsune]], [[Selkie]], [[Tengu]], and [[Yumboe]].

## The rule

**You are raised by a place, not by your ears.**

A Kind is a birth-shape, the body a person brings to the [[Turning Tree|Tree]], and nobody blinks at a fox-tail in a [[Maiethorn]] market or at wings on a [[Heskoren]] ridge. A Condition is the becoming the world organizes itself around, the change a person comes into after birth. Most people carry one, and the share each Condition has of the population is kept on [[Conditions]]. Carrying a Condition also tells you the Tithe, which is the upkeep that Condition asks, and often the guild or licence that goes with the work. So there are no Kitsune kingdoms, no Selkie thalassocracies (no empires of the sea), and no Tengu mountain-empires, because any of those would make a whole people into one country, and that is not how Kinds work here.

What the custom ancestries do have are **hearths**. A hearth is the terrain they were born to, a forest, a coast, a ridge, a dry country, or an isle, and it is where they are densest, not where they are the only ones allowed to live, so it is not a flag and it is not a government.

The one exception that proves the rule is the [[Yumboe|Yumboes]], who are rare off [[The Sundering Isle|Kumbaan]] because of distance, the storm-wall that keeps the isle hard to reach, and not because a Kind is a nationality. On the isle they are ordinary, and on the mainland they are a sailor's tale, the same people in both places, with the map as the thing that separated them.

## How to read a person

| Layer | What it tells you | What it does not |
|---|---|---|
| **Kind** | The birth-shape, and sometimes a hearth, the coast, ridge, wood, or isle where that shape is densest | Loyalty, faith, legal standing, or which country they belong to |
| **Condition** | What they became. Most people carry one, and the share of each is on [[Conditions]]. It also tells you the Tithe, the upkeep that Condition asks, and often a guild or a licence | Ancestry, which is the Kind they were born |
| **Place** | The [[The Old Tongue|drift]] they speak, the local shape of the old tongue, the names they use for institutions, the polity (the country or city-power) that vouched for them, and whether the second name is a byname or a house-name, the two patterns in [[Naming People in the Turning]] | What ears they have |

A [[Kitsune]] clerk on the docks of [[Polity Archetypes|Lestrand]], a [[Tengu]] pilgrim in [[Polity Archetypes|Threnmaieth]], and a [[Selkie]] vouching-witness in [[Polity Archetypes|Vaethorn]], a person who stands for someone else, are all ordinary, and none of those lives is a contradiction. The interesting collision is always Kind, Condition, and the place that raised them.

## The four custom hearths

### [[Kitsune]] — three hearths, one Kind

The fox-born already come in three Foxes, written up under [[Kitsune#Daggerheart (ancestry features)|Wilds / Snows / Sands]]. That split is the distribution, and it is written into the body: there is not one fox-country, there are three grounds. They marry and mingle, and a child takes after one parent's line. You meet Kitsune in every port and every court, and you meet more of a given Fox where that Fox's ground is.

| Fox | Hearth | Where on the map |
|---|---|---|
| **Wilds** (red / forest) | tangled wood, hedgerow, Motherwood | densest in [[Maiethorn]]'s Motherwood and [[Heskoren]]'s deep forests |
| **Snows** (white / high cold) | high snow, pale open country | densest on [[Maiethorn]]'s [[Named Ground|Rain-Wall]] and [[Heskoren]]'s highland spines |
| **Sands** (pale / dry) | dry hills, rain-shadow, sand-country | densest in **[[Maiethorn|Maiethorn's Rain-Shadow]]** — the leeward east of the Rain-Wall, the one true dry country in the known world |

A Fox of the Sands in a wet [[Strandoren]] harbour is unremarkable, because people move. A town in the Rain-Shadow with no fox-born at all would be the thing worth asking about.

Old Kitsune hearths still have a word for a fourth fox, and nobody is born as that fox anymore. A grandmother may still say the word, and she will not say what it means.

### [[Selkie]] — the coasts

Selkies belong to two homes at once, shore and sea, so their hearth is the line between those homes: the strand, the estuary, the cold harbour, and the towns that face the water. They are densest on [[Strandoren]]'s indented coasts, and that continent is almost all shoreline. They are common in [[Maiethorn]]'s western ports on the [[Named Ground|Old Crossing]], present on [[Heskoren]]'s wild coast, and thinner inland. They are not native to [[The Sundering Isle|Kumbaan]]. A Selkie who makes that crossing is a story, not a population.

Inland Selkies do exist, because the skin Anchors, which is how they can always find their way back to water. They are simply fewer, and they miss the place they are not in, which they consider normal.

### [[Tengu]] — the ridges

Tengu hearths are the peaks, the cliff-shelves, and the wind that lives there. They are densest on [[Maiethorn]]'s [[Named Ground|Rain-Wall]] and on [[Heskoren]]'s highland spines, the two continents that actually have killing height. They are rare as a settled people on low [[Strandoren]]. The ones you meet there are usually sky-couriers, a family, a perch on a clock-tower, and a run over the Old Crossing, and not a ridge-town picked up and set down on a quay.

Tengu settlements cling to crags and are reached by wing or by rope. A lowlander who arrives on foot is a guest who climbed, and that climb is the courtesy.

They keep a running, half-teasing rivalry with the Selkies, sky-folk and sea-folk each convinced their element is the one worth belonging to. That rivalry is cultural colour, and it is not a border.

### [[Yumboe]] — the isle

**Kumbaan, and almost nowhere else.** As the ancestry note and [[The Sundering Isle]] say, they are mythic-rare off the isle and ordinary on it. On the isle there is no Leaf-Fall, the tenth-year rite in which a Turning Tree Gives a Condition, and a Condition that arrives because someone was Struck is possible but rare. Their Kind is their own, and so is their place at the table. A Yumboe on the mainland is usually the only one anyone in that town has ever seen.

They have no mainland hearth, because the storm-wall keeps them on the isle.

## How a hearth travels

A hearth is terrain, and not a government. Kitsune still sound like Kitsune on a [[Polity Archetypes|Lestrand]] quay, and that is not a fox-principality at work, only a habit of kitchens.

**You are named by the place that raised you, and you are taught by the other kitchen.**

The civic year happens at home: the drift of the old tongue, the byname, and the town that will watch you Turn, the tenth-year gathering where a child may be Given a Condition or Kept without one. The Kind-year is shorter, and it is borrowed from kin. In the summer before a custom-Kind child's tenth year, or the first summer the family can spare, they are sent to another kitchen of the same Kind. It is not a pilgrimage, and it is not the [[The Walking Years|walk to a Tree]]. It is an aunt's coast, a cousin's crag, or a fox-market three valleys over, and people just say it that way.

| Kind | What they call the season | What moves |
|---|---|---|
| [[Kitsune]] | a **fox-summer** | A child of one Fox-ground sleeps in another, so the Wilds, the Snows, and the Sands stay one Kind. The register is taught in person, and foxfire is shown, not described. |
| [[Selkie]] | a summer on **another strand** | Kin from a second coast attend the skin-days, or the child is sent. The stitch-pattern of a skin is a family thing, and it does not travel well as a letter. |
| [[Tengu]] | a season at the **other perch** | A visiting ridge, or a sky-courier family carrying a child over the Old Crossing. A first solo flight may be off a crag that is not the home one. |
| [[Yumboe]] | the **hill-feast** | They do not run a diaspora season. The isle is the hearth. A Yumboe on the mainland keeps the one register by being the only one in the room. |

Families who cannot send a child still teach at home. Poverty, a sick Tree-year, a war, or a child needed on the boat, and the register survives those. What the other kitchen does is keep a dispersed Kind from thinning into twenty private dialects. A fox-born child who has never left Hallowquay still answers to a Kusawe name, the fox-born hearth-register, because someone who had left came through to trade and to talk.

What travels, instead of a flag, is people, and not an office. Sky-couriers, night-brokers, salvage kin, and a grandmother who "does the summers" carry it from kitchen to kitchen. Faiths and guilds still recruit by Condition, by faith, and by office, and there is no Kitsune guild. The crafts that travel have to be taught with the hands in the room: foxfire, a skin-stitch, a wind-call, a service at the hill-feast. A recipe that worked from a letter would have made a nation, and these do not travel that way. A few objects go along that are slightly wrong in the next town: a fox-lamp whose pale fire only a shown hand can start, a perch-feather worn at a wedding on the strand, and a current that Selkies can name and will not draw.

A few things stay small, and people will not explain them: the word for the fourth fox, a mark on a ridge that lowlanders are not taught, and a current that Selkies can name and will not draw on a chart.

A hearth-neighbourhood, a Selkie quay, a Tengu perch, or a Rain-Shadow fox-market, is the other kitchen made local, and the town around it is still mixed. [[Ornsael]] is the worked example of that kind of town.

## Mixed-Kind children

**Mainland mixes are allowed.** Daggerheart's Mixed Ancestry is a legal heritage here: the top feature from one Kind, the bottom feature from the other, and no hearth feature in the mix. See [[Kind Heritage]]. Two parents means two hearths and a body that can wear both, and nobody files the marriage. There is no mixed-Kind licence, and nobody calls the marriage marrying out. The live question is still whose kitchen, not whether the marriage was allowed.

A mixed mainland child carries two features, one from each side, which is the pairing the published rules describe, and not two full custom packages stacked together. If either side is a custom Kind, the child answers to a register, often the kitchen they spent the summer in, or simply the name that stuck, and the byname still comes from the place ([[Naming People in the Turning]]). Both sides show on the body, ears and webbing, or a fox-brush under a wing, and that is ordinary, not a secret third feature. If a Kind they wear keeps another kitchen, they may visit it, and they may visit both, without growing a second hearth feature just for having gone.

[[Kitsune]] Fox-lines are still one Fox when the child is a full Kitsune, Wilds or Snows or Sands. A mix that only takes Slip or Glamour, two of the fox features and not a Fox-line itself, is fox-touched, and is not a Fox of the Sands.

**[[Yumboe]] do not mix.** A Yumboe at the table needs the GM's leave, because they are mythic-rare off the isle, and if one is seated they are the full three-feature Kind. A child of a Yumboe and a mainlander is a full Yumboe, under that same leave, or the mainland Kind, with at most a story-tell, something people say about the child and not a feature. There is never a top-and-bottom split that includes a Yumboe feature, because the good people do not come in halves.

A Condition arrives on its own clock. A mixed-Kind child Turns, or is Kept and so carries no Condition, or is Struck into one later, the same as the neighbour's child, and Kind does not vote.

## Stock ancestries (the other eighteen)

Daggerheart's stock ancestries, Clank, Drakona, Dwarf, Elf, Faerie, Faun, Firbolg, Fungril, Galapa, Giant, Goblin, Halfling, Human, Infernis, Katari, Orc, Ribbet, and Simiah, live everywhere the Trees do. They are not given continents. A few of them are more common in some kinds of country, and that is a tendency, not a law. The sections below name those tendencies, and they also name the ancestries that did not have a tendency written down yet.

**You are raised by a place, not by your ears.** The tendencies are not rules, so a Ribbet judge in inland [[Polity Archetypes|Threnmaieth]] is ordinary, a Giant on a [[Polity Archetypes|Lestrand]] dock is ordinary, and a Giant who works as a Lestrand banker is ordinary too. If a town has more of one ancestry, that is a fact about the town, which is why [[Raillath]], [[Denlad]], and [[Tunral]] do not belong to any ancestry, and neither do [[Maiethlir]] and [[Orentel]].

Humans are common, and they are not the normal body that everyone else is a variation on. [[Daily Life]] already says the same thing about Conditions, and there is no unmixed household that counts as the default.

A character whose two ancestry features both come from this stock list takes a [[Kind Heritage|Hearth-Mark]] from the place that raised them, which is how those characters stay playable next to the four custom Kinds, who each have three features. The Mark names a place, and it does not turn a tendency into a country. Mixed Ancestry, and the one surprise keyword, stay on [[Kind Heritage]]. The kitchens below use the ordinary larder ([[Ecology of the Turning]]), and an ancestry does not grow its own staple crop.

### The water-born

**Ribbet, Galapa.**

Ribbet and Galapa are most common on the same coasts as [[Selkie|Selkies]], and they are not Selkies. Those coasts are [[Strandoren]]'s indented shore, [[Maiethorn]]'s western ports on the [[Named Ground|Old Crossing]], and, less often, [[Heskoren]]'s wild coast. A Ribbet can breathe underwater, a Selkie holds their breath and you cannot keep a Selkie under, and Galapa live on the same quays as both. A Ribbet who grew up inland grew up in that town.

**Kitchen.** They cook on the quay, close enough to hear the tide, and the meal is bread softened in fish broth, with seaweed or a cockle when the tide was good.

**What they do not own.** They do not own the coast, the Old Crossing, the deep water, or the Selkie strand. Some Seaborne communities live in the same weather, and Seaborne is a community, a life people share, not a Kind ([[Kind Heritage]]).

### Beside the ridges

**Giant.**

Giants are more common on [[Heskoren]]'s uplands, next to [[Tengu]] ridges, and they do not take those ridges over from the Tengu. Some people raised on ridges, or in wild country, live in the same weather, and Ridgeborne and Wildborne are communities, lives people share, not a Giant law ([[Kind Heritage]]). A Giant who grew up on a Lestrand dock grew up on that dock.

**Kitchen.** They eat the usual upland meal, oat mash with dried peas, and cheese or smoked fish when the house has them, and they do not eat it on a [[Tengu]] perch.

**What they do not own.** They do not own the ridge, the wind, the high road, or the sky-courier's run. On [[Maiethorn]]'s [[Named Ground|Rain-Wall]], Tengu and Snows foxes are already common, and a Giant on that road is a neighbour.

### The old wood

**Elf, Faerie, Fungril, Firbolg.**

Elves, Faeries, Fungril, and Firbolg are more common in [[Maiethorn]]'s Motherwood, beside the hearths of the Wilds foxes, and the clearing is still mixed. [[Heskoren]]'s deep forests are Wilds-fox country too, so an Elf there is a neighbour in that forest. The place where this group is common is the Motherwood.

Faeries are small and they can fly, which does not make them [[Yumboe]]. Halflings are small and people call them lucky, and that is a different thing from a Faerie's wings, and it still does not make them the good people of the isle. A winged neighbour in the Motherwood is someone the town already knows.

**Kitchen.** They cook oat pottage with dried peas, and mushrooms when the week was wet, and they start the pot for whoever is already in the clearing, fox-born neighbours included. A Fungril household sets one spoonful aside, and they will not say who it is for.

**What they do not own.** They do not own the Motherwood, the graft (a cutting of a Turning Tree), the Wilds hearth, the air, or [[The Sundering Isle|Kumbaan]].

### Hedgerow and herd

**Faun, Halfling.**

Fauns and Halflings are more common on farmland, in the pastures of the Mother-core, on the [[Named Ground|Chart-run]] plain, and in the valleys under Heskoren's spines, wherever a hedge and a herd already feed a town. If a village that already has a name has more of them, that is a fact about the village, and it does not make the village theirs. Halflings are small, and people call them lucky, and they are not [[Yumboe]].

**Kitchen.** They cook cabbage in the morning's milk and eat it in the yard, before they let the herd through the hedge. That is how that house eats, and it is not [[The Guest-Meal]], the stranger's bowl.

**What they do not own.** They do not own the field, the herd, luck, the small, or the isle.

### Loft, mast, and canopy

**Katari, Simiah.**

Katari and Simiah are more common where the work is up high, in the Motherwood canopy, in the rigging on [[Strandoren]], and in the lofts over a market or a drying floor. They live next to the old wood and next to the quays, and the town below is still mixed.

**Kitchen.** They eat a handful of hazelnuts, or cold fish, in the place where they were working, a loft, a mast, or a branch, and they still come down to the table for the town's supper.

**What they do not own.** They do not own the canopy, the mast, the night, or the market.

### The warm bench

**Clank, Dwarf, Goblin, Drakona.**

Clank, Dwarves, Goblins, and Drakona are more common where a town already mends things, a flood-wall, a hull, a mill, a drying loft, or the bottom of a road that climbs to a perch. [[Maiethlir]] and [[Orentel]] have that work, and the work does not become a neighbourhood that belongs to them. You will meet more Drakona in a house that keeps a work-fire going from morning until dark. Some of them are Ember-Answered, the fire-work of [[The Element-Guilds|Ember]], and that is common. Ember is not a Drakona chapter ([[The Element-Guilds]]).

**Kitchen.** They eat bread and beans at the bench while the bench is still warm from the work. They are using the job's heat, and they did not build a separate kitchen for the Kind.

**What they do not own.** They do not own the wall, the quay, the mill, the forge, [[The Element-Guilds|Ember]], or the stone under a Tengu ridge. Faiths and guilds still recruit by Condition, by faith, and by office. Nobody is recruited because of their ears.

### The carrying yard

**Orc.**

Orcs are more common where the day's work is lifting, on the thaw-roads, at the landings of the Salt Walk, in the dock-gangs, and hauling on a live front. Other Kinds do that same lifting. A crew that is mostly Orcs is a fact about that crew, and the town is still mixed.

**Kitchen.** They eat a pot of peas in the yard while the cart is still hitched, with smoked fish if the road met a quay.

**What they do not own.** They do not own the road, the ford, the watch, or a war.

### Human

Humans are common on every continent that has Trees, and they are no more common on one of those continents than on another. A street with many humans is just that street. They are not the body everyone else is measured against ([[Daily Life]]).

**Kitchen.** They cook what the town cooks, the same meal as the street they live on.

**What they do not own.** They do not own being the ordinary person, the standard for any other Kind, or a government that belongs to humans.

### Born to the blood

**Infernis.**

Infernis are born with demon blood, so the blood is a Kind, and they are not [[Bound]], because Bound is a contract a person swears and therefore something they become. The blood and the contract are different things, and [[At the Table]] keeps the distinction. They have no home landscape and no country of their own. They are born wherever the Trees are, and a street with more of them is a family that stayed there. An Infernis who swears a contract is [[Bound]] like anyone else: the blood is what they were born, and the contract is what they became.

**Kitchen.** They eat from the same pot as the rest of the house, and they do not set a separate bowl because of the blood.

**What they do not own.** They do not own the contract, Bound, or a homeland.

Table rules for feature count, Mixed Ancestry, and the Hearth-Mark are in [[Kind Heritage]].

## GM Notes
*(Stripped on player-safe export. Keyed to the keystone via `reveals: [keystone-adjacent, the-other-hands]`.)*

- **Distribution is a reach-tell only for the Yumboes.** The mainland Kind-hearths are mundane geography, foxes in the woods, seals on the coasts, and crows on the ridges. Kumbaan's isolation is the one Kind-fact that also maps the [[Is the Leaf-Mother Real|keystone edge]]: there is no Tree there, and no Gift, and the people are themselves without her. Do not let a player-facing read treat "Yumboes have no Conditions" as a racial immunity. It is a fact about the place ([[The Other Hands]]).
- **Do not grow Kind-nations later.** If a later note wants a "fox principality," make it a Rain-Shadow polity that happens to be dense with foxes, running on the same theology, reach, and governance as everyone else. The moment a government exists *for* a Kind, Kind has become a country, and the model this note is built on has broken.
- **The lost fourth Fox is a grandmother's word.** If you ever use it, use it as folklore that stayed when a hearth died, a vanished dry-lake or a wood that was cut, and do not turn it into a secret ancestry feature. The Fungril spoonful is the same kind of habit, so do not turn that into a feature either.
- **The other kitchen is not a faction.** If a later pass builds "the fox-summer office," the kitchen has become a Kind-nation with a summer name. Keep it as families, couriers, and a grandmother who does the summers.
- **L.6 glances stay in this note.** There are nine groups here, not eighteen countries. Do not file a Kind-nation note beside them, and do not charter the warm bench. Infernis stay born, and they stay off any Bound homeland. A Ribbet inland and a Giant on a Lestrand dock stay ordinary. Do not move the Hearth-Mark, Mixed Ancestry, or the surprise keyword off [[Kind Heritage]].
- **Mainland Mixed Ancestry is the SRD rule.** Do not walk it back to one-Kind-unless-asked. [[Yumboe]] stay under GM leave and the full Kind, and do not invent a blood-purity sermon to justify that.
- **Canon status.** 🔒 **Locked 2026-08-23** (user-approved): hearths, not nations; the four custom hearths; stock ancestries lean. **Story R.3 (2026-08-30):** the other kitchen; Mixed Ancestry allowed as the SRD allows it; [[Yumboe]] PCs need GM leave and are always the full Kind. **Story L.6 (2026-10-02):** stock hearth-glances — where a group is dense, one kitchen, what they do not own. No new features. No Kind-nations. Resting on earlier locks: Kind is mundane and stacks with Condition ([[The Premise]]); the four customs are locked; [[Yumboe|Yumboes]] are native to [[The Sundering Isle|Kumbaan]] and mythic-rare off it; polities are corners of theology / reach / governance, not blood ([[Polity Archetypes]]). A thicker stock pocket is a town fact. Table rulings → [[Kind Heritage]].
- **For later notes.**
    - **Factions** recruit by Condition, guild, and faith, not by Kind. A tree-warden order that took only Kitsune would mean a faction had started recruiting by Kind, and that is the break to catch.
    - **Settlements** can have a hearth-neighbourhood (a Selkie quay, a Tengu perch, a Rain-Shadow fox-market) without the town *being* that Kind. [[Ornsael]] is the worked example: fox-market by the well, mixed Kinds, no gate. A stock pocket is the same kind of fact: a creek, a street, a crew. The town stays mixed. The pocket does not become a seat, a guild, or a feature.
    - **People** get a Kind (including a mainland mix), a Condition or Kept, and a *place*. The place names them; see [[Naming People in the Turning]]. A mixed-Kind childhood is a kitchen question, not a tragic half-blood plot, and [[Yumboe]] stay GM-leave and whole.
