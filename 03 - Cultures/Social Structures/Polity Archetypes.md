# Polity Archetypes

> **What this is.** Three polities sit at three different corners, and what sets them apart is theology, reach, and governance: how gift-devout the place is, whether it stands among healthy [[Turning Tree|Trees]] or at a thin edge, and how far the state reaches onto a person. [[Law and Citizenship]], [[Economy and the Tithe]], and [[Daily Life]] hold across all ~15 great powers, because they flow from the Trees and from the Conditions, the changes people carry. Each of those notes ends by saying that a polity's character comes from how it sets those three things. This note shows the three corners, so the same laws, the same economy, and the same family life can be seen producing three different places.

> **Names at the table.** Say **the Waiting Lands**, **the Ledger Coast**, and **the Tallied Crown**. Charters, rites, and learned maps write *Vaethorn, Lestrand,* and *Threnmaieth*. The written forms still show the way each polity's own tongue moved, and the spoken handles keep the three corners distinct in play.

## The three things that vary (recap)

Every polity in The Turning sets three things, and it sets them independently of one another. The full definitions live in [[Law and Citizenship#How polities vary]]. What follows is the short form you need in order to read the corners below.

**Theology** is how gift-devout the polity is. At one end an ardent [[The Leaf-Mother|Motherfaith]], the faith of the Leaf-Mother, takes the gift into ordinary life. At the other end the polity is frankly secular and pragmatic, and it does not take the gift into the life of the place. That choice is already at work before the law speaks, because it decides how people read the Struck, who changed later and alone, the Kept, whom a Tree passed over, and the Conditions a place has learned to fear.

**Reach** is the difference between a Tree-rich core and a Tree-poor edge. Where healthy [[Turning Tree|Trees]] stand, reach is full, and the Given, the people who received a gift at a Tree, are dense. Where the Trees are few, young, or sickening, the population runs higher on the [[Law and Citizenship|Kept and the Struck]].

**Governance** is how far the state reaches onto the person. It runs from light guild self-rule, which is the warm default, all the way to a crown that writes its subjects down.

The notes on law, economy, and daily life each name a stance that sets one of the three. The Warm is high theology, the Watchful is governance at its extreme, and the Frontier is low reach. The polities below set all three at once, so each of them is a Warm, a Frontier, or a Watchful, and two other things besides.

## The set at a glance

Each pair of the three shares exactly one setting and differs on the other two.

| Name | **Theology** | **Reach** | **Governance** | The one-line feel |
|---|:---:|:---:|:---:|---|
| **the Waiting Lands** *(written Vaethorn)* | ● high | ○ low | ○ low | Faith is ardent, the Trees are thin, and almost no state reaches a person: a warm, poor, half-lawless pilgrim edge |
| **the Ledger Coast** *(written Lestrand)* | ○ low | ● high | ○ low | Faith is worn lightly among dense Trees, and guilds do the governing a crown would do elsewhere: a rich, cool, transactional merchant power |
| **the Tallied Crown** *(written Threnmaieth)* | ● high | ● high | ● high | Sacred and surveilled together: a wealthy, pious core where keeping the census is a holy duty |

The Waiting Lands and the Ledger Coast share low governance, and that is the only setting they share. Both keep a light-touch state, and everything else swings the other way: one is warm, poor, and faithful, and the other is rich, cool, and secular. Governance stays put, and theology and reach are what move.

The Waiting Lands and the Tallied Crown share high theology, the same ardent faith, lived in wildly different circumstances. One is devotion at a starving edge, and the other is devotion in a sanctified core that watches its people. Theology stays put, and reach and governance are what move.

The Ledger Coast and the Tallied Crown share high reach, the same wealth of Trees, and they spend that wealth in opposite directions, the Coast in free markets and the Crown on a sanctified panopticon, a census of persons held as holy. Reach stays put, and theology and governance are what move.

Three more corners have notes of their own: [[Ornled]] is a secular frontier, [[Maiethvael]] is a devout but lightly governed rich core, and [[Trenledd]] is a cold surveillance-state with no faith to sanctify it, while the other twelve powers sit between these.

## The Waiting Lands *(Vaethorn)*

*(formal VAETH-orn; spoken Wetorn · theology **high** · reach **low** · governance **low**)*

A broad, thinly-peopled land at the world's Tree-poor margin, where the faith burns hottest precisely *because* the Trees are scarce — and where the ruling mood is *waiting*: for a graft, young wood set in this ground, to take, for a distant [[Turning Tree|Tree]] to be reached, for a gift that may never fall. This is the **Frontier stance with theology at its highest** — and the combination is not a contradiction, it's a feedback loop: where the Trees are few and young, more children come away [[Law and Citizenship|Kept]], more people are [[Conditions|Struck]] later and alone, and the [[The Leaf-Mother|Motherfaith]] answers that scarcity not with less devotion but with *more* — pilgrimage, longing, and a theology built around waiting for a gift that may not come.

**Law here.** Governance is almost absent — no crown reaches the individual, and custom outruns written law the way it does on any [[Law and Citizenship|frontier]]. Citizenship is witnessed in the oldest, most literal way: a settlement small enough that everyone *was* there when you Turned. But the Lands' thin reach makes citizenship-by-witness fragile: with few [[Long-Lived]] to remember and few [[Bound]] to vouch, the [[Law and Citizenship|three pillars]] that make proof-by-people work are stretched thin, and the **unvouched Struck** — a person who changed alone, with no [[Law and Citizenship|guild]] to catch them and few witnesses to stand for them — is a constant, aching problem the Waiting Lands meet with faith rather than machinery. A feared Condition here is more likely to be met with a rope or a blessing than a licence, because there is no guild to issue one.

**Economy here.** There is little formal [[Economy and the Tithe|Tithe-infrastructure]], the provision that keeps a person whole, and there are few specialists, but the theology reframes the frontier's poverty as *charity*. Where a purely secular frontier improvises Tithe-provision as barter and favor, the Waiting Lands do it as **alms and congregation**: the venting a [[Far-Voiced]] needs happens at worship, the green a [[The Taken-In|Taken-In]] needs is furnished by neighbours who count it a sacred duty, and the [[The Taken-In]] whom rich cities keep at social arm's length (and still need on the lots) are here the honoured **backbone of the land** — working the living earth *is* the holy economy. It is commons-minded generosity with almost nothing to fund it: real warmth, real want, and a persistent thin edge where the provided-for [[Economy and the Tithe|Tithe-poor]] slide with only prayer for a net.

**Daily life here.** Ceremony is thin, and devotion makes what there is of it enormous. A child's [[Turning Tree|Turning]] at a small, hard-won grafted Tree is the event of a decade, held as a joyous, anxious sacrament, and the **Kept child is felt most sharply here**, because in a land starved of the gift, to be passed over reads (wrongly, and cruelly) as being passed over *by the Mother herself.* Medicine is a travelling [[The Stilled|stiller]], a Stilled healer, if the settlement is lucky, and a rope and a prayer if it is not. The gap between core and edge is at its widest here. Families are warm, mixed, and self-reliant; a lapsing Condition with no infirmary to reach is a frontier tragedy the faith consoles but cannot cure.

**Its injustice** *(the soft-frontier blend).* Not a boot — a **guilt**. The scarcity of the gift becomes a theology of *unworthiness*: the Kept wonder what they lacked, the Struck wonder why the Mother came to them at the wrong door and alone, and a hard, quiet piety can shade into treating want as a spiritual failing. The Waiting Lands are the kindest place in the world to a whole neighbour and the loneliest to an unvouched one.

**The seat.** [[Eolvaeth]] *(EOL-vaeth)* is a pilgrim-edge vale behind the Strandoren-facing coast. People walk here to wait, and the Tree at the end of that wait is not a certainty. The seat is not [[Harrow's Green]], not the three hamlets, and not [[Saelvaeth]]'s march.

**What they remember.** The wave arrived late, and [[The First Cut]] is the name of that arrival. [[The Hinge Hush]] says they may bless a Heskoren landing and may not levy that landing as a gospel-due. [[Ornled]] has no hymn, and [[Saelvaeth]] may take a pot without their waiting-price. The guilt-theology wants both unworthy, and the history kept in [[The Other Count]] does not agree.

## The Ledger Coast *(Lestrand)*

*(LES-trand · theology **low** · reach **high** · governance **low**)*

A dense, Tree-rich merchant power — think a Bruges- or Florence-like merchant city taken to its logical end — where the [[Turning Tree|Trees]] are mature and many, the Given are dense, and the faith is worn *lightly.* This is the **secular Tree-rich merchant republic** the three notes above keep naming as a corner: deep markets, thin welfare, and a cool, transactional read on the whole business of becoming. It shares the **Waiting Lands' light-touch governance** — no crown writes anyone down — but where the Lands' light state leaves a *gap the faith fills*, the Coast's light state leaves a *space the guilds fill,* completely.

**Law here.** The world's warm default at its most developed: **guild self-rule** carried as far as it goes. The state is a charter-granting council of merchant houses and craft-guilds; it charters the [[Law and Citizenship|Stilled's guild]] and the element-guilds and watches the guild, never the individual. Citizenship-by-witness runs *smoothly* here precisely because reach is high — abundant [[Long-Lived]] notaries, [[Bound]] whose sworn word closes every contract, [[Far-Voiced]] on every dock and in every court — and because a city this size **nests** the witness as hearths (a quay-gang, a courtyard), not as twenty thousand people sharing one memory ([[Law and Citizenship#Turning in a city — the hearth stood]]). The [[Law and Citizenship|three pillars]] aren't reverence here; they're **infrastructure**, the machinery that lets a huge commercial city run on trust without a register. Travelers clear on a **road-word** or a guild-mark; the gate asks three questions and does not ask your colour. The **Struck** are vouched-in briskly and without much fuss — not graciously (that's the Warm), just *efficiently*, because an unvouched person is a transaction that can't clear. The [[Law and Citizenship|Inviolate Will]] is cited less as sacred bedrock than as sound commercial law: a [[Bound]]'s contract binds property, not persons, and the Coast likes that line because it makes the Bound the perfect notary.

**Economy here.** The richest corner, and the coolest. This is where **deathless banking** reaches full flower — [[Economy and the Tithe|deathless houses]], houses that do not die, whose trusted notes are the nearest thing the world has to a gold standard, century instruments, the whole [[Economy and the Tithe|endow-not-inherit loop]] running at scale (the great endowed [[Long-Lived|libraries and universities]] cluster here, funding the [[Long-Lived]]'s own novelty-Tithe). [[Economy and the Tithe|Self-paying]] Conditions prosper — the [[The Answered|Answered]] crafts, the [[Two-Bodied]] haulage and courier trades, the [[The Stilled|Stilled]] surgeons — because their labor *is* their upkeep and the market pays for the labor. **Tithe-provision is a private market, not a public good:** you buy your green, your novelty, your outlet, and if you can afford it you stay whole in comfort. **Its injustice is the market's** — the provided-for [[Economy and the Tithe|Tithe-poor]] get *thin welfare in a rich city*, the starkest have-and-have-not gap in the world, and the **[[Economy and the Tithe|shadow economy]]** (smuggled green, black-market novelty, sold vouching, off-book [[Bound|contracts]] feeding the [[Economy and the Tithe|Given-Over]]) runs biggest here because the legitimate market is biggest here. The concentration risk the [[Economy and the Tithe|deathless]] pose is at its most acute: a handful of houses that never die, quietly steering the wealthiest polity through patronage that never disperses.

**Daily life here.** Superb Condition-medicine (the great [[The Stilled|Stilled]] infirmaries and deathless-endowed hospitals are *here*, in the rich core) — for those who can reach it; the queue outside it is longest here too. Family life shrugs at theology: a **marriage market that reads Conditions as assets** — a [[Two-Bodied]] bloodline is a trade dynasty, a [[Long-Lived]] match is a century of credit, and the aching [[Daily Life|Long-Lived marriage]] gets weighed coolly as a balance of grief against advantage. The [[Turning Tree|Turning]] is a civic and commercial milestone more than a sacrament — a family's Leaf-Fall is watched for what guild the child's colour opens. Warm it is not; free and prosperous it very much is.

**Its injustice** *(the market blend).* The coolest of the three. Nothing is *policed* — your marriage, your gift, your faith are your own — but everything is *priced*, and a person who cannot pay slides with a whole rich city stepping around them. The Coast will never file you or conscript you. It will simply let you fade if you run out of coin, and feel that it owed you nothing.

**The seat.** [[Orentel]] *(OREN-tel)* is a salt-quay city on [[Named Ground|the Old Crossing]]. [[The White Note House]] sits on the third quay, north side, and it is not the crown.

**What they remember.** Salt-Walk paper outlived the gate of the Closed Seat, and the notes outlived [[The Grey Summer]]. The rate the first quay still charges is [[The Hinge Hush]], and Sirtol was among the negotiators. [[Brenledd]] piled a compact rather than be priced one hearth at a time. The White Note is one desk-house and not the polity itself, and the chronicle of that memory is [[The Other Count]].

## The Tallied Crown *(Threnmaieth)*

*(THREN-mai-eth · theology **high** · reach **high** · governance **high**)*

**A pious, Tree-rich core kingdom that is also [[Law and Citizenship|Watchful]].** It keeps *a census of persons and their Conditions* in the wealthiest, most devout, most Tree-dense land. Here the roll is not a nervous king's paranoia but **sanctified duty**: it is kept because the Mother's gifts are precious and must be *accounted for.* This is the hardest of the three to resist, because it dresses its injustice in the two things the world most trusts, faith and plenty.

**Law here.** The [[Law and Citizenship|aberration]] in its holy form. The Tallied Crown keeps **a record held on people** — every subject and their gift written down, not as guild rolls or tax ledgers (everyone has those) but as a **census of persons**. At home it is called reverent stewardship of the Mother's blessings; elsewhere it is felt as surveillance. [[Trenledd]] keeps a roll of persons too. The difference is that the Tallied Crown calls its roll holy, and Trenledd does not. Other cities nest the witness as a hearth-stand and then stop. Threnmaieth copies that stand onto the [[Naming in the Turning|Threnhael]], the roll of persons this crown keeps, and that copying is the whole difference. Because reach is high, there is a great deal to count — dense Given, dense guilds, abundant [[Long-Lived]] whose memory the crown *distrusts precisely because it isn't the crown's* (the Tallied Crown's deepest tell: it will not rely on the [[Law and Citizenship|three pillars]] the rest of the world runs on, because living memory answers to itself and a ledger answers to the crown). The **unvouched are hunted and the Struck pursued to be catalogued;** the [[Bound]] are watched far past their real hazard — pure stigma dressed as safety, since the [[Law and Citizenship|Inviolate Will]] means the Whisper cannot command a person's will, and the roster's own clerks know it. Devotion supplies the justification the bare Watchful crown lacks: *you would not hide a gift from the Mother, so why hide it from her stewards?*

**Economy here.** [[Economy and the Tithe|Tithe-need turned to leverage]] — and consecrated. The crown provides Tithe-upkeep **efficiently, even generously** (a pious, rich state can afford to), but *holds* what it provides: the green, the novelty, the outlets, the [[The Stilled|Stilled]] medicine, all furnished through channels the crown controls and can withhold. A population whose staying-whole runs through the state is a population that **cannot afford to refuse it** — the most complete control in the world, and here it wears a halo: to receive your Tithe-provision is to receive the Mother's bounty through her appointed hands, and to be cut off is framed as *your* lapse, not the crown's lever. Its deathless-house finance is superb and centuries-deep — but the [[Long-Lived]] here are courtiers and crown-archivists as much as bankers, their incorruptible memory bent to the census rather than free to check it.

**Daily life here.** The sacrament and the census are fused into the same hour. A child's [[Turning Tree|Turning]] is a **joyous holy day that is also a state registration** — the colour that falls is celebrated with a hymn *and* recorded by a clerk. Marriages of heritable Conditions ([[Two-Bodied]] bloodlines especially) are *noticed*, encouraged or discouraged, filed. The medicine that furnishes a Tithe is excellent and runs through the crown's channels. A household whose **bloodline and upkeep are both on file** is a household that cannot easily refuse the crown anything — [[Daily Life|domestic surveillance]] with a blessing said over it. The Kept are *pitied* here in the Warm's way *and* recorded in the Watchful's — passed over by the Mother, and noted as such.

**Its injustice** *(the sanctified-surveillance blend).* The worst of both high settings, theology and governance, each excusing the other. The devotion sanctifies the surveillance (*it is reverence to be counted*), and the surveillance enforces the devotion (*to refuse the count is impiety*). There is no clean villain and no cackling tyrant — only a wealthy, faithful, orderly people who genuinely believe that accounting for the Mother's gifts is the highest form of tending them, and a machinery of control that this belief makes almost impossible to resist. Elsewhere the roll reads as chilling, because only two crowns keep one, and here the faith is what lets it feel like grace.

**The seat.** [[Maiethlir]] *(MAI-eth-lir)* is a [[Named Ground|Core-thaw]] Hand in the Sacred Core. Pilgrims still walk on from here to the Awakening Tree, and the crown's roll, the [[Naming in the Turning|Threnhael]], is kept here. The seat does not own [[Turning Tree|Thaeloren]]. The [[The Tree-Wardens|First Seat]] sits in the Motherwood beside it.

**What they remember.** [[The Two Papers]], in Cut-year 67, are what this crown calls Limrae's desk and the year the reckoning stood. The crown-count starts in that year, and the present year of the count is **Crown-year 320**. [[Maiethvael]] is the cousin who would not be named. The [[The Walled Book|Inner Close]] keeps another list, of rank and not of care, and will not share ink. The chronicle is [[The Other Count]].

## Reading the set

The three settings are independent. The Waiting Lands and the Tallied Crown are both ardently devout, and they could not be more different: one is a starving, stateless edge, and the other is a sanctified, surveilled core. The Waiting Lands and the Ledger Coast are both lightly governed, and they could not be more different either. Warm faith fills the gap in one, and cool markets fill it in the other. The Ledger Coast and the Tallied Crown are both rich and Tree-dense, and they spend that wealth in opposite directions, free markets on the Coast and a holy roll of persons under the Crown.

Theology is not a measure of good or bad. High theology produces both the *kindest* corner to a whole neighbour, the Waiting Lands, and the *most inescapable* control in the world, the Tallied Crown. Devotion is a multiplier on whatever else the polity is, not a virtue or a vice by itself.

Each corner owns its injustice. Warm faith, free markets, and sanctified order produce different harms, and they produce those harms through different institutions.

A polity's name for a thing shows its stance. Each shared institution — the venting-hall, the ranging-commons, the census, vouching — has a common-tongue name and three polity variants in [[Naming in the Turning]]. A devout land's "gift-hall" is a surveillance-crown's "counted hall". The three written names are Vaethorn, Lestrand, and Threnmaieth, and they come down from [[The Old Tongue]].

## GM Notes
*(Stripped on player-safe export. Keyed to the keystone via `reveals: [keystone-adjacent]`.)*

Treat these as three mechanisms, not one harm reskinned three times. The Waiting Lands use guilt, the Ledger Coast uses price, and the Tallied Crown uses sanctified surveillance, and those three have to stay independent of each other.

The reach-edge cause stays on the GM side, in every corner. The Waiting Lands' high Kept and Struck rates, the dense Given of the Ledger Coast and the Tallied Crown, and the medicine gap between core and frontier all read, inside the world, as plain distance and biology. That mundane reading is the skeptics' whole case. The real reason — [[Is the Leaf-Mother Real|her reach is bounded and thins where the Trees are thin]] — is the locked keystone edge, and it stays GM-side. Do not let a player-facing note assert the cause. The Waiting Lands are the sharpest test of this. They are the place where a devout population most wants an explanation for the thin gift, and where the wrong answer, which is the true one, would land hardest.

These are archetypes, not the whole named map: three of ~15 great powers, and deliberately three corners. The other twelve are named in [[Powers of the Turning]], and these three are the reference points you interpolate from. Their seats are [[Eolvaeth]], [[Orentel]], and [[Maiethlir]].

The naming unlock was finished on 2026-08-21. The archetypes triggered a deferred naming pass, and that pass is complete. [[The Old Tongue]] holds the root liturgical tongue, the three daughter drifts, and the three polity names. [[Naming in the Turning]] is the institution dictionary, a common-tongue name plus three stance-variants for each institution. The design lever landed as built: the name a polity gives a shared institution reveals its stance. The venting-hall is a "gift-hall" in Vaethorn, a "release-house" on Lestrand, and a "counted hall" in Threnmaieth. The other twelve powers were named in Story 7.1, in [[Powers of the Turning]], on the same three drifts and with no new rules.

The corners open hooks you can play. In the Waiting Lands, an unvouched Struck whom no one will stand for forces a congregation to decide whether faith or fear wins, and a Kept child can be treated as spiritually deficient. On the Ledger Coast, a deathless house can call a century-old debt, a Tithe-poor family can fade while a rich city steps around them, and the shadow market in green or vouching is already lived on [[The Slide]], [[The Holding Desk]], and [[The Standing Trade]]. Under the Tallied Crown, the roll is a thing to steal, forge, or escape. The named offices sit on [[The Reckoned Offices]]. A family can find that a private Turning was recorded against their will, and the crown's Tithe-channels are a control grid a party might sabotage.
