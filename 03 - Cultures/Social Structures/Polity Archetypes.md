# Polity Archetypes

> **What this is.** Three polities, each set differently on three things: theology, reach, and governance. [[Law and Citizenship]], [[Economy and the Tithe]], and [[Daily Life]] hold across all ~15 great powers, because they flow from the [[Turning Tree|Trees]] and the Conditions. Each of those notes ends by saying that a polity's character comes from how it sets those three things. This note shows three polities at three different corners, so the same laws, the same economy, and the same family life can be seen producing three different places.

> **Names at the table.** Say **the Waiting Lands**, **the Ledger Coast**, and **the Tallied Crown**. Charters, rites, and learned maps write *Vaethorn, Lestrand,* and *Threnmaieth*. The written forms still encode each polity's drift; the spoken handles keep the three corners distinct in play.

## The three things that vary (recap)

Every polity in The Turning sets three things, independently. (Full definitions live in [[Law and Citizenship#How polities vary]]; the short form:)

- **Theology** — *how gift-devout is it?* Ardent [[The Leaf-Mother|Motherfaith]] gift-integration at one end, frank secular pragmatism at the other. Sets how the Struck, the Kept, and the feared Conditions are *read* before the law even speaks.
- **Reach** — *Tree-rich core or Tree-poor edge?* Where healthy [[Turning Tree|Trees]] stand, reach is full and the Given are dense; where Trees are few, young, or sickening, the population runs higher on the [[Law and Citizenship|Kept and the Struck]].
- **Governance** — *how far does the state reach onto the person?* From light guild self-rule (the warm default) all the way to a crown that writes its subjects down.

The three notes above each name a stance that sets one of them: the Warm (high theology), the Watchful (extreme governance), the Frontier (low reach). The polities below set all three, so each is a Warm or a Frontier or a Watchful *and* two other things.

## The set at a glance

Each pair of the three shares exactly one setting and differs on the other two.

| Name | **Theology** | **Reach** | **Governance** | The one-line feel |
|---|:---:|:---:|:---:|---|
| **the Waiting Lands** *(written Vaethorn)* | ● high | ○ low | ○ low | Ardent faith, thin Trees, almost no state — a warm, poor, half-lawless pilgrim edge |
| **the Ledger Coast** *(written Lestrand)* | ○ low | ● high | ○ low | Faith worn lightly, dense Trees, guilds not crowns — a rich, cool, transactional merchant power |
| **the Tallied Crown** *(written Threnmaieth)* | ● high | ● high | ● high | Sacred *and* surveilled — a wealthy, pious core where the census is a holy duty |

- **Waiting Lands ∩ Ledger Coast** share **low governance** → same light-touch state, opposite everything else: warm-poor-faithful vs. rich-cool-secular. *Governance held constant; theology and reach swing.*
- **Waiting Lands ∩ Tallied Crown** share **high theology** → same ardent faith, wildly different circumstances: devotion at the starving edge vs. devotion in the sanctified, watched core. *Theology held constant; reach and governance swing.*
- **Ledger Coast ∩ Tallied Crown** share **high reach** → same Tree-wealth spent two opposite ways: free markets vs. a sanctified panopticon. *Reach held constant; theology and governance swing.*

Three more corners have their own notes: a secular frontier ([[Ornled]]), a devout but lightly governed rich core ([[Maiethvael]]), and a cold surveillance-state with no faith to sanctify it ([[Trenledd]]). The other twelve powers sit between these.

## The Waiting Lands *(Vaethorn)*

*(formal VAETH-orn; spoken Wetorn · theology **high** · reach **low** · governance **low**)*

A broad, thinly-peopled land at the world's Tree-poor margin, where the faith burns hottest precisely *because* the Trees are scarce — and where the ruling mood is *waiting*: for a graft to take, for a distant [[Turning Tree|Tree]] to be reached, for a gift that may never fall. This is the **Frontier stance with theology at its highest** — and the combination is not a contradiction, it's a feedback loop: where the Trees are few and young, more children come away [[Law and Citizenship|Kept]], more people are [[Conditions|Struck]] later and alone, and the [[The Leaf-Mother|Motherfaith]] answers that scarcity not with less devotion but with *more* — pilgrimage, longing, and a theology built around waiting for a gift that may not come.

**Law here.** Governance is almost absent — no crown reaches the individual, and custom outruns written law the way it does on any [[Law and Citizenship|frontier]]. Citizenship is witnessed in the oldest, most literal way: a settlement small enough that everyone *was* there when you Turned. But the Lands' thin reach makes citizenship-by-witness fragile: with few [[Long-Lived]] to remember and few [[Bound]] to vouch, the [[Law and Citizenship|three pillars]] that make proof-by-people work are stretched thin, and the **unvouched Struck** — a person who changed alone, with no [[Law and Citizenship|guild]] to catch them and few witnesses to stand for them — is a constant, aching problem the Waiting Lands meet with faith rather than machinery. A feared Condition here is more likely to be met with a rope or a blessing than a licence, because there is no guild to issue one.

**Economy here.** Little formal [[Economy and the Tithe|Tithe-infrastructure]] and few specialists — but the theology reframes the frontier's poverty as *charity*. Where a purely secular frontier improvises Tithe-provision as barter and favor, the Waiting Lands do it as **alms and congregation**: the venting a [[Far-Voiced]] needs happens at worship, the green a [[The Taken-In|Taken-In]] needs is furnished by neighbours who count it a sacred duty, and the [[The Taken-In]] whom rich cities keep at social arm's length (and still need on the lots) are here the honoured **backbone of the land** — working the living earth *is* the holy economy. It is commons-minded generosity with almost nothing to fund it: real warmth, real want, and a persistent thin edge where the provided-for [[Economy and the Tithe|Tithe-poor]] slide with only prayer for a net.

**Daily life here.** Thin ceremony made enormous by devotion. A child's [[Turning Tree|Turning]] at a small, hard-won grafted Tree is the event of a decade, held as a joyous, anxious sacrament — and the **Kept child is felt most sharply here**, because in a land starved of the gift, to be passed over reads (wrongly, and cruelly) as being passed over *by the Mother herself.* Medicine is a travelling [[The Stilled|stiller]] if the settlement is lucky and a rope-and-prayer if it is not; the gap between core and edge is at its widest here. Families are warm, mixed, and self-reliant; a lapsing Condition with no infirmary to reach is a frontier tragedy the faith consoles but cannot cure.

**Its injustice** *(the soft-frontier blend).* Not a boot — a **guilt**. The scarcity of the gift becomes a theology of *unworthiness*: the Kept wonder what they lacked, the Struck wonder why the Mother came to them at the wrong door and alone, and a hard, quiet piety can shade into treating want as a spiritual failing. The Waiting Lands are the kindest place in the world to a whole neighbour and the loneliest to an unvouched one.

**The seat.** [[Eolvaeth]] *(EOL-vaeth)* — a pilgrim-edge vale behind the Strandoren-facing coast. People walk *here to wait*. The Tree is not a certainty. Not [[Harrow's Green]], not the three hamlets, not [[Saelvaeth]]'s march.

**What they remember.** The wave arrived late ([[The First Cut]]). [[The Hinge Hush]] says they may bless a Heskoren landing and may not levy it as a gospel-due. [[Ornled]] has no hymn; [[Saelvaeth]] may take a pot without their waiting-price. The guilt-theology wants both unworthy. History does not agree. Chronicle: [[The Other Count]].

## The Ledger Coast *(Lestrand)*

*(LES-trand · theology **low** · reach **high** · governance **low**)*

A dense, Tree-rich merchant power — think a Bruges- or Florence-like merchant city taken to its logical end — where the [[Turning Tree|Trees]] are mature and many, the Given are dense, and the faith is worn *lightly.* This is the **secular Tree-rich merchant republic** the three notes above keep naming as a corner: deep markets, thin welfare, and a cool, transactional read on the whole business of becoming. It shares the **Waiting Lands' light-touch governance** — no crown writes anyone down — but where the Lands' light state leaves a *gap the faith fills*, the Coast's light state leaves a *space the guilds fill,* completely.

**Law here.** The world's warm default at its most developed: **guild self-rule** carried as far as it goes. The state is a charter-granting council of merchant houses and craft-guilds; it charters the [[Law and Citizenship|Stilled's guild]] and the element-guilds and watches the guild, never the individual. Citizenship-by-witness runs *smoothly* here precisely because reach is high — abundant [[Long-Lived]] notaries, [[Bound]] whose sworn word closes every contract, [[Far-Voiced]] on every dock and in every court — and because a city this size **nests** the witness as hearths (a quay-gang, a courtyard), not as twenty thousand people sharing one memory ([[Law and Citizenship#Turning in a city — the hearth stood]]). The [[Law and Citizenship|three pillars]] aren't reverence here; they're **infrastructure**, the machinery that lets a huge commercial city run on trust without a register. Travelers clear on a **road-word** or a guild-mark; the gate asks three questions and does not ask your colour. The **Struck** are vouched-in briskly and without much fuss — not graciously (that's the Warm), just *efficiently*, because an unvouched person is a transaction that can't clear. The [[Law and Citizenship|Inviolate Will]] is cited less as sacred bedrock than as sound commercial law: a [[Bound]]'s contract binds property, not persons, and the Coast likes that line because it makes the Bound the perfect notary.

**Economy here.** The richest corner, and the coolest. This is where **deathless banking** reaches full flower — [[Economy and the Tithe|deathless houses]] whose trusted notes are the nearest thing the world has to a gold standard, century instruments, the whole [[Economy and the Tithe|endow-not-inherit loop]] running at scale (the great endowed [[Long-Lived|libraries and universities]] cluster here, funding the [[Long-Lived]]'s own novelty-Tithe). [[Economy and the Tithe|Self-paying]] Conditions prosper — the [[The Answered|Answered]] crafts, the [[Two-Bodied]] haulage and courier trades, the [[The Stilled|Stilled]] surgeons — because their labor *is* their upkeep and the market pays for the labor. **Tithe-provision is a private market, not a public good:** you buy your green, your novelty, your outlet, and if you can afford it you stay whole in comfort. **Its injustice is the market's** — the provided-for [[Economy and the Tithe|Tithe-poor]] get *thin welfare in a rich city*, the starkest have-and-have-not gap in the world, and the **[[Economy and the Tithe|shadow economy]]** (smuggled green, black-market novelty, sold vouching, off-book [[Bound|contracts]] feeding the [[Economy and the Tithe|Given-Over]]) runs biggest here because the legitimate market is biggest here. The concentration risk the [[Economy and the Tithe|deathless]] pose is at its most acute: a handful of houses that never die, quietly steering the wealthiest polity through patronage that never disperses.

**Daily life here.** Superb Condition-medicine (the great [[The Stilled|Stilled]] infirmaries and deathless-endowed hospitals are *here*, in the rich core) — for those who can reach it; the queue outside it is longest here too. Family life shrugs at theology: a **marriage market that reads Conditions as assets** — a [[Two-Bodied]] bloodline is a trade dynasty, a [[Long-Lived]] match is a century of credit, and the aching [[Daily Life|Long-Lived marriage]] gets weighed coolly as a balance of grief against advantage. The [[Turning Tree|Turning]] is a civic and commercial milestone more than a sacrament — a family's Leaf-Fall is watched for what guild the child's colour opens. Warm it is not; free and prosperous it very much is.

**Its injustice** *(the market blend).* The coolest of the three. Nothing is *policed* — your marriage, your gift, your faith are your own — but everything is *priced*, and a person who cannot pay slides with a whole rich city stepping around them. The Coast will never file you or conscript you. It will simply let you fade if you run out of coin, and feel that it owed you nothing.

**The seat.** [[Orentel]] *(OREN-tel)* — a salt-quay city on [[Named Ground|the Old Crossing]]. [[The White Note House]] sits on the third quay, north side, and is not the crown.

**What they remember.** Salt-Walk paper that outlived the Closed Seat's gate. Notes that outlived [[The Grey Summer]]. [[The Hinge Hush]] (Sirtol among other negotiators) is the rate the first quay still charges. [[Brenledd]] piled a compact rather than be priced one hearth at a time. The White Note is one desk-house, not this polity. Chronicle: [[The Other Count]].

## The Tallied Crown *(Threnmaieth)*

*(THREN-mai-eth · theology **high** · reach **high** · governance **high**)*

**A pious, Tree-rich core kingdom that is also [[Law and Citizenship|Watchful]].** It keeps *a census of persons and their Conditions* in the wealthiest, most devout, most Tree-dense land. Here the roll is not a nervous king's paranoia but **sanctified duty**: it is kept because the Mother's gifts are precious and must be *accounted for.* This is the hardest of the three to resist, because it dresses its injustice in the two things the world most trusts, faith and plenty.

**Law here.** The [[Law and Citizenship|aberration]] in its holy form. The Tallied Crown keeps **a record held on people** — every subject and their gift written down, not as guild rolls or tax ledgers (everyone has those) but as a **census of persons**. At home it is called reverent stewardship of the Mother's blessings; elsewhere it is felt as surveillance. [[Trenledd]] keeps a roll of persons too. The difference is that the Tallied Crown calls its roll holy, and Trenledd does not. Other cities nest the witness as a hearth-stand and stop. Threnmaieth copies the stand onto the [[Naming in the Turning|Threnhael]]. That is the whole difference. Because reach is high, there is a great deal to count — dense Given, dense guilds, abundant [[Long-Lived]] whose memory the crown *distrusts precisely because it isn't the crown's* (the Tallied Crown's deepest tell: it will not rely on the [[Law and Citizenship|three pillars]] the rest of the world runs on, because living memory answers to itself and a ledger answers to the crown). The **unvouched are hunted and the Struck pursued to be catalogued;** the [[Bound]] are watched far past their real hazard — pure stigma dressed as safety, since the [[Law and Citizenship|Inviolate Will]] means the Whisper can't command and the roster's own clerks know it. Devotion supplies the justification the bare Watchful crown lacks: *you would not hide a gift from the Mother, so why hide it from her stewards?*

**Economy here.** [[Economy and the Tithe|Tithe-need turned to leverage]] — and consecrated. The crown provides Tithe-upkeep **efficiently, even generously** (a pious, rich state can afford to), but *holds* what it provides: the green, the novelty, the outlets, the [[The Stilled|Stilled]] medicine, all furnished through channels the crown controls and can withhold. A population whose staying-whole runs through the state is a population that **cannot afford to refuse it** — the most complete control in the world, and here it wears a halo: to receive your Tithe-provision is to receive the Mother's bounty through her appointed hands, and to be cut off is framed as *your* lapse, not the crown's lever. Its deathless-house finance is superb and centuries-deep — but the [[Long-Lived]] here are courtiers and crown-archivists as much as bankers, their incorruptible memory bent to the census rather than free to check it.

**Daily life here.** The sacrament and the census fused. A child's [[Turning Tree|Turning]] is a **joyous holy day that is also a state registration** — the colour that falls is celebrated with a hymn *and* recorded by a clerk. Marriages of heritable Conditions ([[Two-Bodied]] bloodlines especially) are *noticed*, encouraged or discouraged, filed. The medicine that furnishes a Tithe is excellent and runs through the crown's channels. A household whose **bloodline and upkeep are both on file** is a household that cannot easily refuse the crown anything — [[Daily Life|domestic surveillance]] with a blessing said over it. The Kept are *pitied* here in the Warm's way *and* recorded in the Watchful's — passed over by the Mother, and noted as such.

**Its injustice** *(the sanctified-surveillance blend).* The worst of both high settings, theology and governance, each excusing the other. The devotion sanctifies the surveillance (*it is reverence to be counted*), and the surveillance enforces the devotion (*to refuse the count is impiety*). There is no clean villain and no cackling tyrant — only a wealthy, faithful, orderly people who genuinely believe that accounting for the Mother's gifts is the highest form of tending them, and a machinery of control that this belief makes almost impossible to resist. Elsewhere the roll reads as chilling, because only two crowns keep one, and here the faith is what lets it feel like grace.

**The seat.** [[Maiethlir]] *(MAI-eth-lir)* — a [[Named Ground|Core-thaw]] Hand in the Sacred Core. Pilgrims still walk on from here to the Awakening Tree, and the crown's roll, the [[Naming in the Turning|Threnhael]], is kept here. Does not own [[Turning Tree|Thaeloren]]. The [[The Tree-Wardens|First Seat]] sits in the Motherwood beside.

**What they remember.** [[The Two Papers]] (Cut-year 67): Limrae's desk, the year the reckoning stood. Crown-count starts there; present **Crown-year 320**. [[Maiethvael]] is the cousin who would not be named. The [[The Walled Book|Inner Close]] keeps another list, of rank and not of care, and will not share ink. Chronicle: [[The Other Count]].

## Reading the set

- **The three settings are independent.** The Waiting Lands and the Tallied Crown are both ardently devout, and could not be more different (a starving, stateless edge vs. a sanctified, surveilled core). The Waiting Lands and the Ledger Coast are both lightly governed, and could not be more different (warm faith fills the gap vs. cool markets fill the gap). The Ledger Coast and the Tallied Crown are both rich and Tree-dense, and spend that wealth in opposite directions (free markets vs. a holy roll of persons).
- **Theology is not a measure of good or bad.** High-theology produces both the *kindest* corner to a whole neighbour (the Waiting Lands) and the *most inescapable* control in the world (the Tallied Crown). Devotion is a multiplier on whatever else the polity is, not a virtue or a vice by itself.
- **Each corner owns its injustice.** Warm faith, free markets, and sanctified order produce different harms through different institutions.
- **A polity's name for a thing shows its stance.** Each institution (venting-hall, ranging-commons, the census, vouching) has a common-tongue name and three polity variants in [[Naming in the Turning]]. A devout land's "gift-hall" is a surveillance-crown's "counted hall". The three names are Vaethorn, Lestrand, and Threnmaieth ([[The Old Tongue]]).

## GM Notes
*(Stripped on player-safe export. Keyed to the keystone via `reveals: [keystone-adjacent]`.)*

- **Three mechanisms, not one reskinned harm.** The Waiting Lands use guilt, the Ledger Coast uses price, and the Tallied Crown uses sanctified surveillance. Keep those mechanisms independent.
- **The reach-edge cause stays GM-side, in every corner.** The Waiting Lands' high Kept/Struck rates, the Ledger Coast's and Tallied Crown's dense Given, the core-vs-frontier medicine gap — all read in-world as plain distance-and-biology (the skeptics' whole case). The real reason — [[Is the Leaf-Mother Real|her reach is bounded and thins where the Trees are thin]] — is the locked keystone edge and stays GM-side. Don't let a player-facing note assert the cause. The Waiting Lands are the sharpest test of this: they are the place where a devout population *most wants* an explanation for the thin gift, and where the wrong (true!) answer would land hardest.
- **These are archetypes, not the whole named map.** Three of ~15 great powers, and deliberately three *corners*. The other twelve are named in [[Powers of the Turning]]. Use these as reference points to interpolate from. Seats of the three: [[Eolvaeth]] · [[Orentel]] · [[Maiethlir]].
- **Naming unlock — done (2026-08-21).** The archetypes triggered the deferred naming pass, now complete: [[The Old Tongue]] (root liturgical tongue + three daughter drifts + the three polity names) and [[Naming in the Turning]] (the institution dictionary — common-tongue name + three stance-variants for each). The design lever landed as built: **the name a polity gives a shared institution reveals its stance** — the venting-hall is a "gift-hall" in Vaethorn, a "release-house" on Lestrand, and a "counted hall" in Threnmaieth. The other twelve powers named Story 7.1 → [[Powers of the Turning]] (same three drifts; no new rules).
- **Hooks the corners open.** *The Waiting Lands:* an unvouched Struck no one will stand for and a congregation deciding whether faith or fear wins; a Kept child treated as spiritually deficient. *The Ledger Coast:* a deathless house calling a century-old debt; a Tithe-poor family fading while a rich city steps around them; the shadow market in green or vouching ([[The Slide]] · [[The Holding Desk]] · [[The Standing Trade]]). *The Tallied Crown:* the roll as a thing to steal, forge, or escape; named offices on [[The Reckoned Offices]]; a family whose private Turning was recorded against their will; the crown's Tithe-channels as a control grid to sabotage.
