# Daily Life

> **What this is.** The *universal grammar of everyday life* in **The Turning** — what it is to marry, raise children, fall ill, and live in a town when **3 of 4 people carry a [[The Premise|Condition]]**. This is where the setting's **~5%-scary** dial is set at eye level: almost everything is warm, ordinary, and lived-in, with a real ache underneath in the specific cases. Built for [[Roadmap|Epic 2, Story 2.3]], on [[Law and Citizenship]] (the legal grammar) and [[Economy and the Tithe]] (the economic one). How the ~15 polities vary is the last section; concrete cities come in [[Roadmap|Epic 7]].

> **📖 Names: descriptive here, in-world names now exist.** Institutions and customs keep **plain descriptive terms** in the body for legibility, but the [[The Old Tongue|naming pass]] is done (2026-08-21) — each has a settled common-tongue name + three stance-revealing polity variants in [[Naming in the Turning]]; the root language is [[The Old Tongue]]. Good working canon, open to a later polish pass.

## Marriage & family — the whole spectrum, from mundane to aching

The first thing to understand about family in The Turning is that **most of it is utterly ordinary.** Two people marry; some of their household carries a Condition and some doesn't; the children haven't [[Turning Tree|Turned]] yet and won't until their tenth year. A born-[[Selkie]] wife, a husband [[Returned]] from a drowning, a Kept grandmother, three children who are simply children so far — this is not a remarkable household. It is *the* household. Because Conditions **stack** onto Kinds and arrive across a whole life, nearly every family is "mixed" in some way, and precisely because nearly every family is, **mixed families are not a category anyone thinks about.** There is no "interracial marriage" tension here in the Condition layer, because there is no unmixed baseline to marry away from.

So family life spreads along a spectrum, and the spread itself is the point:

**At the warm, mundane end** (most families): a Condition is just a texture of the household, the way a trade or a temperament is. A [[Two-Bodied]] parent's other self has a *name* and the children know it — "that's Bramble, mother's otter" — and grows up as much a part of the family as a pet that is also a person. A [[Far-Voiced]] household is a *loud* one, because the Tithe means feeling must be aired daily; you do not get to sulk quietly in a home with a Far-Voiced in it, and families learn to treat the evening's singing or weeping or laughing as ordinary weather. A [[The Taken-In|Taken-In]] parent keeps the windows open and the rooms green. These are quirks, not tragedies — the small daily accommodations of living with people who are also *becomings.*

**In the middle**, the Tithe becomes a shared family labor. Where a Condition is provided-for (see [[Economy and the Tithe]]), keeping a family member whole is a household expense and a household duty: the green for the Taken-In, the books for the [[Long-Lived]], the outlet for the Far-Voiced. Families organize around it the way ours organize around a member's chronic need — mostly without drama, sometimes with strain, and with the quiet knowledge that **to let a loved one's Tithe lapse is to watch them slide toward the scary edge.** A family too poor to furnish a Tithe carries a particular grief: they can see exactly what their kin needs and cannot buy it.

**At the aching end** sits the marriage the whole setting is quietly organized around: **loving someone whose becoming and yours don't share a timespan.** Chief among these is marrying a **[[Long-Lived]].** Their aging simply stops; their spouse's does not. A Long-Lived can have children and a full family, but the Condition is **not reliably inherited** — most children age and die like anyone — so a Long-Lived does not build a deathless dynasty; they **outlive it.** They watch a spouse grow old across a marriage that stays, on one side, unchanged. They bury their children. The old saying is that *the cost of the Long-Lived is measured in funerals*, and it is a cost paid inside the family, at the dinner table, over decades. A Long-Lived who has done this once faces the choice their whole existence turns on: **stop** — live on alone, keep no more households — or **begin again**, marry again, love again, and outlive that family too. Neither choice is cowardly and neither is free. This is the setting's deepest, quietest ache, and it lives in the most ordinary place there is: a marriage.

A few threads worth pulling:

- **The threshold courtesy in the home.** A Long-Lived never crosses another's home threshold uninvited — held as the deepest good manners, the mark of someone safe to let in. Inside a *family*, this softens into something tender: to be welcomed across a Long-Lived's own threshold, or to welcome them across yours without a thought, is a real intimacy. A door held open is how this world says *you are mine and I am yours.* (A door refused is felt more than a Long-Lived will ever say.)
- **The Returned come *home.*** A person who died and Returned is, contrary to every outside expectation, **not cold** — a Returned is calm, deliberate, unafraid, and *quietly respected*, and their loved ones get them back. The chill only comes if their Purpose lapses (see [[Economy and the Tithe|the Tithe]]): a Returned neglecting the unfinished thing that holds them "runs cold," and the living can feel the wrongness at a touch. So a Returned's family learns a hard and intimate literacy — **you can tell, by an embrace, whether the one you love is tending what holds them here.** A warm embrace means they are well. A cold one is a family emergency dressed as a hug.
- **Two-Bodied bloodlines are family lines.** Because the Condition runs in blood (children of a Two-Bodied parent usually Turn the same animal), "wolf-folk," "hawk-folk," and "otter-folk" are real extended families, and — per [[Economy and the Tithe|the economy]] — the family animal, the Condition, and often the family trade descend as a single inheritance. Marrying into such a line is marrying into a shape your children will likely wear.
- **Raising a child before they Turn.** Every parent lives the same open question until their child's tenth year: *what will they become, or will they be Kept?* In devout homes this is held as a hopeful mystery (the Mother will give what the child can carry); in anxious ones it is a decade of quiet dread or ambition. Parents who carry a heritable Condition (Two-Bodied especially) can half-guess; most cannot. The [[Turning Tree|Leaf-Fall]] is therefore not only a civic day but the emotional hinge of a family's life — the day you finally *meet* who your child has been becoming all along. In [[The Walking Years]] the question had a prior: *can we even get there?* A household that could walk only one child still lives in the present as a Given/Kept sibling pair; see the split household on that note. Misses, refusals, orphans, and whether anyone gets a second standing: [[Turning Tree#Questions a warden gets asked]].
- **Mixed-Kind children are allowed.** Two mainland Kinds may sit in one body the way Daggerheart already allows Mixed Ancestry — a fox-and-seal child is as ordinary as a Returned husband. Nobody files it. The live question is **whose kitchen** the summer before they Turn (see [[Kinds of the Turning]]), not whether the marriage was allowed. [[Yumboe]] are the exception: GM leave to play one, and always the full Kind, never a mix ([[Kind Heritage]]).

## Medicine — miraculous, and strange to outside eyes

Medicine in The Turning is genuinely astonishing for a [[The Premise|late-medieval world]], and it is astonishing for one reason: **the [[The Premise|Condition-labor advance]] shows up hardest at the sickbed and the deathbed.** Two Conditions turn medicine into something a world of this technology should not have — and to anyone from outside, a Turning clinic is a deeply *uncanny* place, wonder and wrongness in the same room.

**The [[The Stilled|Stilled]] are the surgeons.** A stiller's gaze **arrests motion** — it holds a wound from bleeding, slows a poison's spread through the blood, keeps a patient perfectly still under the knife without holding them down. It does not petrify and does not harm; it simply *stops* what is moving for a beat. In a world with no anaesthetic worth the name and no way to halt a hemorrhage, this is the difference between a survivable operation and a death. The best surgeons in the world are Stilled, and a great infirmary is built around them. But sit in that operating room as an outsider and watch what it *is*: a surgeon whose flat, patient gaze freezes the blood in your veins on command, working fast because the Grey is filling in them as they work (their Tithe — using the Gaze fills a clock that, left full, locks *their own* limb to stone). They drill and spar between patients to burn it off. The most gifted healers in the world are also the most tightly **licensed** and quietly **feared** (see [[Law and Citizenship]]) — the same gaze that saves you on the table is the one the law watches hardest. Wonder and dread, one person, one room.

**The [[Returned]] do the work that kills the living.** Plague wards, mine collapses, the recovery of bodies from places the living cannot safely go, and the tending of the dead — this is Returned work, done by those death cannot take a second time until their Purpose is met. A Turning mortuary is staffed by the dead, calmly and well; a plague ward that would empty a town's living physicians is walked by Returned who are unshaken by rot and contagion. To an outsider this is ghoulish. Inside, it is simply **respected** — the Returned are the reason a plague does not take the doctors too, and everyone knows it. There is a real tenderness in it, too: no one speaks for the dead, or tends them, as gently as someone who has been dead.

**The rest of medicine is ordinary — and ordinarily unequal.** Midwives, bonesetters, herbalists, the [[The Answered]]'s craft turned to apothecary and instrument-making — the everyday care of a 1400s town, better staffed than ours because more of the population carries a useful becoming. But the Condition-medicine that makes this world exceptional is **not evenly available.** The great Stilled infirmaries and the deathless-endowed hospitals cluster in the rich, Tree-dense core (see [[Economy and the Tithe|center and periphery]]); the frontier makes do with a travelling stiller if it is lucky and a rope-and-prayer if it is not. And the deepest inequity is the one [[Economy and the Tithe|Tithe-poverty]] already named: **you can be too poor to stay whole,** and the medicine that treats a lapsing Condition is exactly the medicine the poor can least reach. The miracle is real. So is the queue outside it.

## Living in a Turning town — the shape a city takes

*(Design principles, not places. Concrete settlements — quarters, named cities — are [[Roadmap|Epic 7]]; this section hands that epic the pressures a Turning-city bends around.)*

A city here is shaped by a fact no other world's city faces: **most of its adults have an upkeep, and some of those upkeeps are dangerous or expensive to meet.** So the town builds around the Tithe the way ours build around water and waste — as **civic infrastructure, visible and load-bearing:**

- **The Tree at the centre.** Every town is *organized around* its [[Turning Tree]] — it is the civic and sacred heart, the site of the year's most-attended day, the thing the oldest quarter grew up around. A town's age and standing can be read in its Tree (a mature grafted Tree = an old, reach-rich town; a young Tree or none = a new or frontier settlement). Geography and civic layout both point back to it. [[The Years of Hands|Residues]] layer this: a stone in the square, an upper room, a road that ends at the boughs. Playable squares → [[Settlement Seeds]] / [[Harrow's Green]] · [[The Three Hamlets Past the Ford]] · [[The Third Hearth]] · [[Ornsael]]. Archetype seats → [[Eolvaeth]] · [[Orentel]] · [[Maiethlir]].
- **Tithe-provision as public utility.** Where the polity treats provision as a public good (see below), the town furnishes it in the open: **venting-halls** for the [[Far-Voiced]] (part concert hall, part release valve — often doubling as the town's performance venue), **potted groves and garden-commons** so the [[The Taken-In|Taken-In]] don't "go to seed" indoors, **endowed libraries** feeding the [[Long-Lived]]'s novelty-Tithe (and, per [[Economy and the Tithe|the endow loop]], often *built* by a Long-Lived), and **ranging-commons** at the town edge where the [[Two-Bodied]] can be their other selves. These are as normal a part of the streetscape as a well or a market cross.
- **The dangerous trades on the edges.** The work that carries a bystander hazard sits where such work always sits — at the margins and the edges. Stilled infirmaries and Answered element-workshops (fire, forge, tide) are guild-quartered and set slightly apart; the [[Returned]]'s mortuaries and plague-work sit at the town's edge, near the dead. Not from disgust — from the same logic that puts a tannery downwind.
- **Homes that accommodate becomings.** Ordinary domestic architecture quietly bends to the Conditions: windows that open and rooms kept green for a Taken-In guest, a household's evening rhythm built around a Far-Voiced's vent, a threshold a Long-Lived waits to be asked across. A Turning home is legibly a home *for becomings.*
- **The reach-edge writes the map, again.** Dense, Tree-rich core towns are thick with all this infrastructure; frontier settlements have little of it and improvise (see [[Economy and the Tithe]] and [How polities vary](#How%20polities%20vary)). You can read a town's place in the world off how much of its Tithe-infrastructure is public, private, or simply absent.

## How polities vary

As with law and economy, everyday life varies the way a real world's does — along the same **independent axes** ([[Law and Citizenship#How polities vary]]: theology, reach, governance), so a place's daily texture is the *combination* of its settings, not one of three fixed types. The three touchstone points have these domestic faces:

**The Warm** *(high theology).* Family, medicine, and civic life all carry the gift-theology: a child's Turning is a joyous sacrament, mixed households are blessings compounded, Tithe-provision is a public good offered in the open, and the sick are tended as people whose gifts must be kept whole. **Its soft injustice reaches the hearth:** the Kept child is *pitied* for being passed over, marrying "beneath your gift" is quietly frowned on, and a family is expected to spend its members' Conditions as the community sees fit — love and pressure wearing the same warm face.

**The Watchful** *(governance at its extreme).* The register-keeping crown (see [[Law and Citizenship]]) reaches into the family: marriages of heritable Conditions may be *noticed* and encouraged or discouraged, a child's Turning is an event the state records, and the medicine that furnishes a Tithe runs through channels the crown controls. **Its cold injustice is domestic surveillance** — a household whose upkeep and bloodline are both on file is a household that cannot easily refuse the crown anything.

**The Frontier** *(low reach).* Thin infrastructure, thin medicine, thin ceremony. Families improvise their own Tithe-provision or do without; a travelling stiller is a rare blessing; the [[The Taken-In|Taken-In]] whom core cities keep at arm's length are *neighbours and kin* here, the backbone of the place. Freer (no one polices your marriage or your gift) and harsher (a lapsing Condition with no infirmary to reach, an untrained Struck child with no guild to catch them). Warmer in feeling, poorer in safety-net.

**Mind the combinations.** A devout frontier holds joyous Turnings with almost no infirmary behind them; a secular Tree-rich republic has superb medicine and a marriage market that shrugs at theology; a Watchful-and-pious core turns the sacrament of a child's Turning into a state census with a hymn over it. The touchstones are corners of the space, not a catalogue of it.

## GM Notes
*(Stripped on player-safe export. Keyed to the keystone via `reveals: [leaf-mother-is-real]`.)*

- **R2 social guard — the injustice reaches the hearth, and stays real.** Per [[Is the Leaf-Mother Real|the keystone]], the Leaf-Mother's benevolence is **cosmological, not social.** She gives real gifts; she did **not** author the family pressures, the unequal medicine, or the domestic surveillance built around them. The frictions here — the Kept child pitied at their own table, families conscripted to "spend" a member's gift, Condition-medicine that clusters where the money is while the Tithe-poor slide, the Watchful crown filing bloodlines and Turnings, marriage quietly policed for its gift-value — are **not misreadings to be dissolved by the reveal.** Confirming she is real and kind must **not** make the family or the clinic fair. The keystone answers *is anyone there and do they mean well* (yes); it pointedly does not answer *is it fair to be poor, or Kept, or Struck, in this society* (often no). Protect that gap — it is where the play is.
- **The Long-Lived marriage is the setting's quietest, most reusable ache.** Loving someone you will outlive and bury — a spouse, a child — is the deepest emotional Tithe in the world and it plays at the table without any monster in the room. Reserve it for weight. (It also seeds NPCs: the deathless widower who has stopped; the one who keeps beginning again.)
- **The "cold embrace" is a diagnostic and a hook.** A Returned's family can feel a lapsing Purpose at a touch. A loved one who has "run cold" is a quiet family crisis — and a ready adventure seed (what unfinished thing is slipping? can the party help meet it before the Returned settles or turns cold to everyone?).
- **The reach-edge (medicine and infrastructure clustering in the core) has a GM-side *cause*.** In-world the frontier's thin medicine reads as plain distance-and-poverty; the deeper reason — [[Is the Leaf-Mother Real|her reach is bounded and thins where the Trees are thin]] — stays GM-side. Don't let a player-facing note assert the cause.
- **Naming is done (2026-08-21).** In-world names for the customs and institution terms here (venting-hall, ranging-commons, garden-commons, etc.) live in [[Naming in the Turning]] (common-tongue + three stance-variants), built on [[The Old Tongue]]. Body keeps plain descriptors for legibility. Remaining deep-language work is [[Roadmap|Epic 4]].
- **Hooks.** A Long-Lived asked to marry into a mortal family and the grief everyone can see coming; a Returned whose embrace has gone cold and the race to find their unfinished thing; a Tithe-poor family watching a child slide for want of green or novelty; a Watchful clerk recording a Turning that a family wanted kept private; a frontier town with no stiller when a birth goes wrong; a Kept child made to feel a failure at their own Leaf-Fall. Feed to [[Roadmap|Epic 5 (Factions)]] and Epic 7 (Settlements).
