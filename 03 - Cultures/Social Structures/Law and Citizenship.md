# Law and Citizenship

> **What this is.** How law and citizenship work where **3 of 4 people carry a [[Conditions|Condition]]**: a change a person comes to carry and does not put down, either when a leaf falls for them at a [[Turning Tree]] or later, when the change arrives with no one there to see it. Citizenship is a place agreeing that you belong to it. Both follow from the Tree and the Conditions, so the same law holds, with local accents, across all ~15 polities. The last section says how the polities differ. [[Polity Archetypes]] shows three of them in full.

> **Names.** The institutions below are given in plain words, so a new reader can follow the office before learning its local name. Each office has a common-tongue name as well, and each polity says that name in its own way: a devout land's "gift-hall" is a surveillance-crown's "counted hall." The dictionary of those names is [[Naming in the Turning]], and the root they drift from is [[The Old Tongue]].

## The through-line: you belong because a hearth *saw* you Turn

Everything below rests on one morning in a child's tenth-year week, when the child stands under a [[Turning Tree]] and someone who already knows them is there to watch. That week is the [[Turning Tree|Leaf-Fall]], still the most-attended week of a community's year, and kin, neighbours, and the [[The Tree-Wardens|tree-wardens]] all come to it, there to see a leaf fall on the child or to see the Tree hug them.

A **Kind** is what that child is by birth, already true of them before the week starts. What the week decides is a **Condition**, the change three of every four people come to carry across a life. When a leaf falls, the warden Speaks a colour, and the child is **Given** the Condition that colour names. When the Tree hugs the child and there is no colour, the child is one of the **Kept**, witnessed in the same hour as everyone else and carrying no Condition at all. **Citizenship** is settled in that same hour: you are of a place because a living circle watched you become who you are, and not because a clerk wrote the colour into a book.

In a village that someone is the whole settlement. In a city it is a **hearth** — a street, a quay-gang, a courtyard, a guild-stair — not twenty thousand people pretending to remember one child. The legal fact does not change: you are of a place because a living circle *watched you become who you are.* How a city actually does the week is [Turning in a city](#Turning%20in%20a%20city%20—%20the%20hearth%20stood), and the rite is [[Turning Tree#Turning-Week in a city]].

So citizenship in The Turning is **witnessed, not recorded**: the law is the memory of the people who stood there, and it does not turn into a register when the crowd grows.

> **There is no general register of the people.** No town and no church keeps a list of everyone and the gift they carry. A warden's week-slate only says which hearths stand at which bell, so it is a festival schedule, and a guild-roll is only the roster of people who practise a dangerous art, and neither document is a census of persons. The [[The Walled Book]] keeps its own heir-roll inside its walls, which is not a list of everyone outside them. Belonging is living communal memory, nested down to the size of a hearth once a settlement outgrows what one memory can hold. Two crowns are the exception and do keep a roll of persons: the Tallied Crown calls its roll holy, and the Worn Count calls it the roll and does not call it holy. See [How polities vary](#How%20polities%20vary). The rest of the world finds both unsettling.

That morning is also why a stranger can be known without papers, why a town can licence a dangerous practice without listing every neighbour, and why someone with no one to stand for them is the person the law leaves exposed. The arrangement comes down from the Hands, the age [[The Walking Years]] records, when the crowd that saw you Turn was the crowd under Thaeloren, or there was no crowd at all if you never arrived, and [[The Walking Years|vouching-at-a-distance]] is how that origin-witness is carried home. The law on this page is how the present works, and the road under it is [[The Ages of the Turning]] and, as people lived it, [[The Walking Years]]: the one-week door, the split household, and the origin-orphans.

## Turning in a city — the hearth stood

A village of three hundred can remember every child who stands, and a salt-city of tens of thousands cannot, so witnessed citizenship does not become a register at that scale. It nests instead, the memory moving down into hearths, and the city does not open a book to replace them.

The Tree is still at the centre of the week, Turning-Week is still the door the city comes in by, and the square is still full, but the legal fact is that your hearth stood, not that [[Orentel]] saw you as if a whole city could be one witness.

A hearth, in a city, is eighty to two hundred people who already know the child. They bring their ten-year-olds in a named slot of the week, the warden Speaks the colour or names the hug, and the city, standing there as a crowd, sees only a crowd, while the hearth is who sees the child.

One **pillar** of that hearth carries the memory when a desk far from the city asks. A pillar is a [[Long-Lived]] who stood, whose memory is incorruptible and runs back centuries; or a [[Bound]] who stood, whose sworn word is literally binding, so that a false vouch is not a lie they can cheaply tell; or a [[Far-Voiced]] who stood, whose voice betrays real feeling under strain, so that they cannot smoothly lie on your behalf. Kin and neighbours carry the memory first, and the pillar is how the stand travels once the child is no longer in front of the people who saw it. An orphan is still brought by kin, by the household that feeds them, or by the warden-hearth ([[Turning Tree#Questions a warden gets asked]]). A child no hearth will bring is a miss, the same as in a village.

To prove you are of a city, you name a hearth and produce someone of it, or a pillar who stood, so that "I am of the third quay" is a citizenship sentence and "I am of Orentel" is geography.

[[Polity Archetypes|Threnmaieth]] and [[Trenledd]] write the stand down, and that writing is still the aberration, while everyone else keeps the week-slate as hours rather than as gifts.

## How you prove who you are — by *people*, not papers

If no one is written down, a stranger still has to prove citizenship in a far city, and the answer is one of the world's quiet [[Conditions|Condition-labor advances]]. Condition-labor is the work and the witness a Condition makes possible, and here it means the Turning never had to invent a papers-and-registers state, because trust runs through people who cannot be faked. You are known by witnesses, not by documents.

A [[Long-Lived]] remembers you, and the memory cannot be forged the way a clerk's copy can, because it is incorruptible and it runs back centuries. A [[Bound]] vouches for you, and a Bound's sworn word is literally binding, so a false vouch is not a cheap lie. A [[Far-Voiced]] speaks for you, and the voice betrays real feeling under strain, so they cannot smoothly lie on your behalf.

To establish yourself in a new place is to be vouched into it, which is to acquire people who will stand for you. This is warmer and more human than an ID-check, and it has a sharp consequence. The friendless and the rootless are the truly vulnerable, because they have no one to vouch, and the law's exclusions fall on the **unvouched** — a person no one will stand for — rather than on anyone you would call undocumented. A traveler is not, by default, that person, and the next section is how they stay vouched on the road ([How a traveler stays vouched](#How%20a%20traveler%20stays%20vouched)).

## How a traveler stays vouched

The unvouched remain the vulnerable class, while a legitimate traveler is on a word rather than on a paper a state issued them. The Hands-era road inherited the custom from [[The Walking Years|vouching-at-a-distance]]: the witness of your origin had to travel home, and it still does.

A watch that stops a stranger asks three things: your name, your last hearth, and who stands for you. Answer any two and the road continues; answer none and you are held until dawn, or until someone will stand, or until you are walked to the next gate. Routine harassment is reserved for the nameless. A company that can name a last host and show one mark is how this law wants people to move, a party included.

The table is the set of words and marks a watch will actually take. Where it says Gaze, it means the Stilled practice, the one surgeons, riot-lines, and demolition use, and the one a later charge means when someone has held a citizen still. A Cry is a Far-Voiced voice let out in earnest, in a hall or over a crowd. An element is what one of the Answered works, in a yard or on a boat. A mark that names one of those uses is permission to practise it. It is not a list of who carries which Condition.

| Custom | What it is | What it is not |
|---|---|---|
| **Road-word** | A named person at your origin or your last town, the one you mean by "Ask Halim of the third quay." A Far-Voiced can call that person, a Bound can send a word, and a letter is only a token of the person who vouched. | Not a passport: the scrap is worthless if the person who vouched is dead or will not stand. |
| **Company-vouch** | A party stands for each other, so one locally known or ticketed member covers the rest for the passage. | Not citizenship: you may sleep in the town, and you are not of the town. |
| **Guild-mark** | A stiller's ticket, a Crae journeyman-mark from the element-guilds, or a Voice-ticket. Craft marks travel with the person who earned them. | Not a Condition-census: the mark says you may Gaze, work an element, or Cry in a hall. |
| **House-note** | Paper from a deathless house, credit that doubles as a light stand for the bearer. | Not required, and most walkers have none. |
| **Guest-right** | A host who takes you in stands for the stay, whether that host is an inn, a table, or a road-house, and "I ate at X" holds for about a week. | Not a vouch for sale, because the host must have taken you in. |
| **Walk-custom** | Someone on a named walk — the Near Mile, the Salt Walk, the Long Mile, or a First-Hand year — is vouched as a walker by the road itself, through mile-shrines, road-houses, and a berth-slate. | Not permanent belonging, because when the walk ends, the word ends. |

People Struck on the road, meaning they took a Condition where no one from home was watching, are vouched anew at the next desk that will take them, and that desk is [[The Intake]] when the door they came through is hazardous. Changing unwitnessed is still a seam in the law, and it is not a crime to have changed.

Sold vouching still exists, as a nameless underside described in [[Economy and the Tithe]], and it is not this custom. A paid voucher who will not answer a Far-Voiced call is how that market fails in public.

### The storm-wall is not a road

[[The Sundering Isle|Kumbaan]] is not a jurisdiction under this law, so you do not arrive there already vouched. A road-word, a company-vouch, or a guild-mark can still help you leave a Strandoren quay. It does not file you on a hill of the isle, and it does not make the isle a place this law administers.

On the isle, guest-right is the table, and eating at that table a second time makes you kin there without turning the meal into a last-hearth you can spend when a mainland watch stops you. How a crossing works is [[The Sundering Isle#How a crossing works]], the hall a party can play is [[Ndenjoo]], and what a mainland watch does when it is handed a sentence from the isle, instead of a witness, is on [[The Open Table]].

## The Struck — those who changed *unwitnessed*

Here is the seam the whole shape of Condition-law grows around, and the seam is not "someone fell off a list." A [[Conditions|Struck]] person acquired their Condition later, and alone — by a drowning, a death, or a sworn contract — long after their tenth-year Turning. The town watched everyone else become who they are, and no one watched the Struck, who changed with no witnesses.

So a Struck person must be vouched anew, which means the community has to learn them again as what they have become, on the word of the people who will stand for them. That is the plain root of the old unease that the Struck are "a little suspect," and the unease is not only prejudice, because their becoming happened where no one could see it. A change everyone witnessed is trusted, and a change that happened in the dark has to be spoken for. Every polity has people whose transformation went unwitnessed, and every polity feels differently about them.

Two things make that seam sharper, and the first is that the dangerous Struck arrive raw. A Struck [[The Stilled]] or a Struck [[Far-Voiced]] manifests untrained, with the hazard already live and no guild that caught it while they were young. For these, being vouched in is bound up with mandatory guild-intake: you are taken in hand and apprenticed at once, for everyone's safety, whether you wanted the vocation or not.

The second is that some doors sit next to crimes. The [[Bound]] are Struck by a sworn contract, and the [[Returned]] are Struck by dying. To vouch a Bound into a community is to acknowledge a contract no one witnessed being sworn, which is why contract-law and the acceptance of the Bound stay tangled together in the edge cases below.

## The Inviolate Will — the one thing every polity agrees on

Beneath the local variation sits one principle firm enough to underlie every polity's law, and it is firm because it is true of the Conditions. No Condition can compel, control, or bend another person's will. The [[Bound]]'s Whisper cannot, the [[Far-Voiced]]'s Cry cannot, and the [[Long-Lived]]'s regard cannot. Every Condition's danger stops at property, at pain, at fear, and at the edge of another person's choosing. The law copies that limit into a maxim taught to every child:

> **"The will is one's own."** *(the doctrine of the Inviolate Will)*

The consequences of that maxim carry the rest of the code, beginning with the fears a horror story needs, which are void in this law. There is no such crime as being mind-controlled by a vampire, because that cannot happen, and the people a tale would call monsters live as neighbours because the one thing that would make them impossible to live beside is impossible, and everyone knows it.

Because a Condition cannot seize a will, harm done with one splits into two different charges. The first is negligent **Tithe**-lapse. A Tithe is the upkeep a Condition keeps requiring, and if you let it lapse the dangerous side shows where other people have to live with it: your element vents, your Cry breaks, or your green goes to seed, which the law treats as regulation or as a public nuisance. The second charge is willful weaponizing, deliberately turning a gift on someone, which is ordinary violent crime and no gentler because the instrument was a Condition. In practice only the [[The Stilled|Stilled's]] gaze even supports a charge that you held a citizen still, and most of what the law fears is negligence rather than assault.

That same maxim is the shield the feared hide behind. A [[Bound]] can point to the Inviolate Will and say, truthfully, that their contract cannot make you do anything, and whether a given polity listens is exactly where the polities diverge.

## The safety-licence — how hazard is regulated (and by whom)

The one place the law does take hold of a particular person is public safety, and it takes hold narrowly. A **ticket** attaches to a hazardous use, not to the fact of being Conditioned. The rule of thumb is the same across the world: a licence attaches where a practice is dangerous to other people, never where a gift is merely powerful, and never as a way to keep track of people. It is the same logic by which any world licenses a few dangerous trades and does not take a census.

The three ticket-Conditions are [[The Stilled]], [[Far-Voiced]], and [[The Answered]], and together that is about three people in ten, with the shares kept on [[Conditions]]. They are a large minority who can be asked for a ticket if they practise the hazardous use. They are not a third of the world written on a crown's list.

The pool of people who might be asked is not a roll of persons. A ticket names a competence — stiller, hall-voice, or Crae-hand — and does not function as an identity paper for a Condition.

| What | Who holds paper | Who does not |
|---|---|---|
| **[[The Stilled]] — personal Gaze-ticket** | Anyone who Gazes in public, including surgeons, riot-lines, and demolition. Most stillers hold one, because the vocation is the Gaze. | A Stilled who never Gazes, which is rare, though the guild still wants that person in the yard. |
| **[[Far-Voiced]] — Voice-ticket / hall-hour** | Those who Cry in a crowd or keep a scheduled hour: criers, performers, and sworn witnesses. [[The Hall-Keepers]] stamp the competence and sell the hour at the same desk. | A field-voice who vents at the hedge. The hall-slate is a list of hours, not of every blue-leaf in the city, and blue-leaf is the ordinary name for a Far-Voiced. |
| **[[The Answered]] — shop charter / journeyman-mark** | Those who let an element speak in a crowded yard. The [[The Element-Guilds\|Crae]] charters the door, and journeymen sit under that charter. | A Tide-Answered on their own boat, under a master's eye. They answer to the shop, and the shop does not keep a person-file. |
| **[[Bound]] — a table, not a body-ticket** | No licence is issued for the body. The honest table oversees the Terms, and the Whisper cannot command. | Not a ticket. A Bound is not counted among the licensed. |
| **No ticket** | — | [[Two-Bodied]], [[Long-Lived]], [[Returned]], [[The Taken-In]], [[The Unbound]], [[Phoenix]], and all the Kept. Most people hold no ticket, and they are simply neighbours. |

Active papers in a working city are fewer than the people who could be asked for one, and fewer again on the frontier, where a feared Condition is still as likely to meet a rope as a stool. The pool is who might be asked, not a census of people under supervision.

The reason this does not become a general surveillance system is institutional, and it holds in four ways.

Three guilds keep the papers, and they do not keep a shared roll. A stiller cannot ask the Crae for a list of all the Answered, and a hall-keeper cannot ask the Stillers for "all pale-stone," pale-stone being the ordinary name for the Stilled. There is no union of the lists except in [[Polity Archetypes|Threnmaieth]] and [[Trenledd]].

The state watches the charter rather than the person, so it can pull a guild's leave to operate and it cannot ask for a list of this city's Far-Voiced.

The slates name hours, plants, and chairs, not gifts: a bed-slate is rows of beds, a hall-slate is bells, and a guild-roll is the practitioners of an art. Only the two crowns described below as Watchful write down persons and the gifts those persons carry.

Most people never hold a paper at all. The Kept do not, nor do the Two-Bodied, who are the plurality, nor the Taken-In, the Returned, the deathless, or the [[The Unbound|Hollow]], and among people who do carry a Condition only a large minority can be asked for a ticket if they practise. That arrangement is a world of crafts, not a world that keeps a register of neighbours.

**Who holds the pen matters as much as the rule.** Licences are guild-issued, not state-issued, which is the late-medieval arrangement still in force. A guild knows its own members the way any craft guild does. The state charters the guild and lets the guild police its own, under the threat of losing the charter. No single authority thereby holds the Conditioned, and the arrangement makes its own friction: guilds guard their monopolies, tickets become property, and an unlicensed practitioner threatens the guild's charter as much as the public's safety. The houses where this is lived are [[The Stillers]], [[The Hall-Keepers]], [[The Element-Guilds]], and [[The Intake]]. A mill, a hull, and a road have fellowships that are not these papers: [[Craft Fellowships]].

## The three pillars — why the Conditioned *are* the legal system

The deepest fact about Condition-law is that it is not a set of rules imposed on the Conditioned from outside. Three Conditions are the legal apparatus itself, and they are exactly the people who make a register unnecessary.

The [[Long-Lived]] are incorruptible memory, and they serve as the archivists, the notaries, and the judges, so that a matter of record is whatever they say they witnessed, even when the witnessing was a century ago. Where a forged document would be the worry, a Long-Lived simply remembers.

The [[Bound]] are the binding word: a sworn oath from them is literally binding, they hear a lie as it is told, and so they are the witnesses, the oath-keepers, and the debt-arbiters. When a treaty must truly hold, a Bound is the one who swears it.

The [[Far-Voiced]] are feeling a person cannot fake, which is why courts trust them as sworn witnesses: the voice betrays real emotion under strain, and they cannot lie well once they are on the stand.

Evidence and contract in The Turning are therefore made of people. Courts here rely on perfect memory, on oaths a Bound can hear being broken, and on testimony the witness cannot fake. The people who do those jobs were Given their Condition at the Tree or Struck with it later, so the courts do not need a paper bureaucracy to do the work. A world with witnesses like these does not need to write everyone down, which is why the two crowns that insist on writing everyone down look so strange to everyone else.

## Harm, the watch, and the hearing

The Inviolate Will has already sorted the charge into negligent Tithe-lapse or willful weaponizing, and the ladder below is the path that charge walks, with no crime anywhere on it for merely *being* a Condition.

1. **Neighbour-word.** Most harm dies on the street, settled by restitution, by an apology, and by a Bound's word if the people involved have one to swear, and the watch is not called.
2. **The watch.** Night-watch and ward-watch are the ordinary civic hold. The people who stand them are often Kept, on nights that are not themselves a Tithe, and [[Two-Bodied]], for whom the ranging *is* the Tithe, with a [[Far-Voiced]] along for the shout. They hold a body until morning, they do not investigate Conditions, and when the matter needs someone who can speak for a hearth or a craft they fetch a pillar or a guild. Where other notes say "the watch," they mean this hold, not a secret police, except where a Watchful crown has made the watch into one. Who stands the bench, what a cohort is, and what the two wars left are on [[The Watch and the Cohort]].
3. **Guild-hearing.** Tithe-lapse comes here, and so does an unlicensed Gaze, and an element let go in a shop. The guild fines, or it suspends the ticket, or it binds the person to an apprenticeship. The state is not in the room unless the charter is at risk.
4. **Open hearing.** Willful harm is heard in the open, before witnesses, especially the three pillars, and the charge is the act itself. "You Gazed a man" is assault, and "You are Stilled" is not a crime. A Gaze used to hold a citizen still is the rare charge the Stillers' own code already names.
5. **What the hearing can do** without writing you down is a short list of real consequences. It can order restitution in coin or in labor, or order you to furnish a Tithe you broke, and it can strip the ticket so you may not practise the dangerous act. It can require a Bound's word or a term, and it can exile you from the hearth, so that you become unvouched in your own city. That exile is the civic death, and it does not require a file. For murder, and for the rare holding of a citizen still, it can send you to the rope or the wall, which is commoner on the frontier and rare in the Warm.

The two Watchful crowns write you down on top of whatever else the hearing did. That record is their extra punishment, and it is why they feel like a different country.

*A door-custom, pinned inside a Lestrand night-gate, with no crest on it. The back of the page is a week-old rain note, and the writing on the front is what a stranger reads before the watch speaks.*

> *Name. Last hearth. Who stands. Two will do. None, and you sit the bench until the second bell, or until someone comes to stand for you, or we walk you to the next gate. We do not ask your colour. We do not keep the three answers past dawn. If you are on a walk, say which. If you ate at the bed-house, say so. If you have a ticket, show the stool-mark, not a story. The bench is not a cell. The bench is until morning.*

The watch that pinned this up thinks "we do not ask your colour" makes it unlike Threnmaieth. The three questions are the whole of how citizenship works, run at a gate, and the watch does not write the answers down. That is how a city of tens of thousands still runs on hearths.

## Rights of the Kept

The Kept are the quiet centre of citizenship, and their legal position is best described as **gloriously unremarkable.** They were witnessed at their Turning like everyone else, they carry no Tithe, they need no licence, and they answer to no guild. In most polities the Kept are the default legal person, the frame the rest of the law is drawn around. In one surviving compact they are also a ruling class: [[The Walled Book]] ranks the tithed and will strike a Closed Heir for taking a colour.

Two wrinkles keep them from being a footnote, and the first is that Kept status is not a permanent immunity. About a third of hugged children are still Struck later, so a Kept person lives untithed and unlicensed without ever standing wholly outside the safety rules. The day they are Struck, they too must be vouched anew, because in law "Kept" describes your present and not a promise about your future.

Where reverence for the gift runs hot, the Kept can be quietly pitied or pressured, treated as people who missed out and leaned on to explain why the Tree passed them over. That is a social harm the law rarely names, and it runs hardest in the most devout polities, which the next section describes.

## Edge cases the law must hold

The [[The Unbound|Hollow]], the Unbound, raise a question of capacity and of protection. Freed from a contract but missing the Pledge they paid, an isolated Hollow is pulled to sign again. Kinder polities extend a protective status, which can be a guardian, a claim on connection, or a bar on presenting them with fresh contracts, while harsher polities leave them to the pull.

The Given-Over, also called the Forfeit, is the sharpest wound this law has. It is a [[Bound]] who kept re-signing until the counterparty effectively owns the person, a living case of legal personhood erased by accumulated contract. The contract-guilds have a term of art for it precisely because the law has never fully decided at what point a person has bargained away enough of themselves to stop being one. The question keeps returning, and it has not become a settled rule. The pipeline that feeds it has a lived face on [[The Holding Desk]]. Sold vouching, which is a paid voucher rather than a paper, is [[The Standing Trade]].

[[Phoenix]] is not a licensing problem at all, because a single legendary person who cannot hide is a political problem instead: a symbol no ruler controls, handled by whoever currently wants to own the one who is alive, or to protect them.

**UnTurned** is not the same status as Kept: a child who missed the week, refused to stand, or never stood has no hug and no colour, and folk will still call that child Kept even so. The law should file them as the person the town raised, vouched if someone will stand, and unwitnessed at the Tree. The procedure a warden is walked through is [[Turning Tree#Questions a warden gets asked]]. A second Leaf-Fall is not a remedy, and the doors that Strike a person later still open in their own time.

## How polities vary

The rules above hold everywhere, and the tone does not. The world is as varied as a real one, region to region and culture to culture. Three things carry most of the difference, and every polity sets each one separately.

**Theology**, how gift-devout the place is, runs from an ardent [[The Leaf-Mother|Motherfaith]] of gift-integration, the faith in which a Condition is a blessing and belonging is a birthright, through civic religion worn lightly, to frank secular pragmatism, and the odd local heresy. That choice sets how the Struck, the Kept, and the feared Conditions are read before the law even speaks.

**Reach** is whether the place is a Tree-rich core or a Tree-poor edge. It is geography, following where the Trees grew from the first one ([[Turning Tree#One Tree, many hands — the graft|the graft]]). Where healthy Trees stand, reach is full and the Given are dense. Where Trees are few, young, or sickening, the population runs higher on the Kept and on the Struck.

**Governance**, how far the state reaches onto the person, runs from light guild self-rule, the ordinary arrangement, in which the craft polices its own and the crown charters the guild, all the way to a crown that writes its subjects down. That choice sets how far a safety licence becomes a way of watching people.

The combinations give different places: a devout frontier, with ardent faith, thin reach, and almost no bureaucracy; a secular merchant republic rich in Trees, with dense reach, light governance, and faith worn lightly; a pious core kingdom that is also Watchful, in which the watching of people is dressed as sacred duty; and a cold trade-city with no reverence at all. Each has real supporters and a real cost, three stances recur, and [[Polity Archetypes]] works through three whole polities.

**The Warm** *(high theology).* Law flows straight from the Motherfaith's gift-theology, so a Condition is a blessing, the Struck are vouched in graciously as people the Mother met at a later door, and licences are framed as helping you tend your gift rather than as policing you. The stance is warmest near the [[Turning Tree|Awakening Tree]], and its injustice is soft and hard to name: everyone is expected to serve their gift, so a guild's "vocation" shades into pressure, the Kept are pitied for missing out, and declining your Condition's obvious use reads as ingratitude to the Mother herself. A polity can be Warm and a frontier at once, and lightly or heavily governed, because theology is only one of the three things that vary.

**The Watchful** *(governance at its extreme).* At the far end of governance are crowns that break the world's norm and do write their people down. They do not trust living memory, and they keep a roll of every subject and of that subject's Condition. What makes this unusual is not the mere keeping of written records, since every polity has guild rolls, notarial acts, and tax ledgers. It is the keeping of a census of persons and their gifts, a record held on people, and two crowns do it. The Tallied Crown ([[Polity Archetypes|Threnmaieth]]) calls its roll holy, and the Worn Count ([[Trenledd]]) calls it the roll and does not. At home the roll is framed as prudence and order, and elsewhere it is felt as surveillance. The unvouched are hunted, the Struck are pursued so they can be catalogued, Tithe-needs become points of leverage, and the [[Bound]] especially are watched far past their real hazard, since the Whisper cannot command and the clerks know it. Most of the world finds the two rolls chilling, because nobody else keeps a roll of everyone. The Walled Book keeps an heir-roll inside its walls, and that roll lists only its own heirs.

**The Frontier** *(low reach).* Far from any healthy Tree, even the witnessing runs thin, and custom outruns law. These lands run higher on the Kept and higher on the Struck: more hugs, more later doors, fewer coloured falls. In town, people read that as distance and as young wood. They are freer in the good sense, because no one asks for a licence, and the [[The Taken-In]], whom cities keep at a social arm's length and still employ on the lots, are simply neighbours here and the backbone of the frontier economy. They are lawless in the bad sense, because unvouched Struck have no one to stand for them and no guild to train them, and feared Conditions are met with a rope rather than a licence. Reach and governance often drift together, a thin-reach edge tending to be thinly governed too, but they need not. A devout frontier exists, and so does a lightly governed core.

## GM Notes
*(Stripped on player-safe export. Carries the keystone tie-in this note is keyed to via `reveals: [keystone-adjacent]`.)*

- **Keep each legal harm attached to an actor and a procedure.** When you put a harm on the table, name who does it and what they do: a watch asks for a road-word, a guild refuses a ticket, a crown-clerk writes a person down, or a creditor absorbs a standing. Do not replace those scenes with a general speech about injustice.
- **Design note — witnessed, not recorded (user call, 2026-08-20).** The original draft made a universal Turning-register the way citizenship worked, and the user rightly flagged "records kept on everyone at ten" as too invasive, and off-tone for a world that is only about ~5% scary. The reframe, which is what the player text now carries, is that citizenship is witnessed communal memory, and that proof runs through vouching people, the Long-Lived, the Bound, and the Far-Voiced. The only per-person paper is a guild safety-licence for the ~3 hazardous Conditions. The register survives only as the roll of the two Watchful crowns, the Tallied Crown's, which is called holy, and Trenledd's, which is called the roll. Keep the baseline warm, and keep the record creepy and rare.
- **Story R.6 — licence math, city scale, travelers, crime (2026-08-30).** This story did not change the locked engine: citizenship stays witnessed rather than recorded, there is no universal register, the Given-door is still one week, and tickets stay guild-issued for the three hazardous uses only. On the licence rhetoric, the old claim that ~90% of the Conditioned carry nothing is false, and so is the old 28–33% scare, which counted the ticketable pool together with people who touch a Bound table. Do not add the Bound's contract lane to the licence rate. The ticketable pool is the Stilled, the Far-Voiced, and the Answered, ≈ 29% of people. A Bound remains a table rather than a fourth body-licence, a ticket names a competence rather than a Condition, the three guilds keep no shared roll, and the state watches the charter, which is how a large ticketable minority stays a world of crafts and not a census. City witnessing is nested hearths, not a city-memory and not a roll. Travelers move by road-word, company-vouch, guild-mark, guest-right, and walk-custom, and a watch asks for a name, a last hearth, and who stands, with any two of the three enough to pass. Crime runs from a neighbour's word to the watch, who hold someone until morning, then to a guild-hearing, then to an open hearing. The punishments do not require a file, and exile from the hearth is the civic death. The two Watchful crowns still write you down, and do not invent a passport office to explain how a traveler moves. The lived faces of sold vouching and of the Given-Over pipeline are [[The Standing Trade]] and [[The Holding Desk]].
- **The reach-edge is the cause of the Frontier stance, and that cause is GM truth.** In the player-facing body, the frontier's higher rates of Kept and of Struck read as plain distance and as biology, which is the skeptics' whole case. The reason, that [[Is the Leaf-Mother Real|her reach is bounded and thins at the margins]], is the locked keystone edge and stays on the GM side of the note. It is the same observable fact under two readings, and a player-facing note must not assert the cause. History's mundane face of the same edge is that far children once stayed Kept for want of a walk, and some still walk ([[The Ages of the Turning]]).
- **Naming is done (2026-08-21).** The in-world naming pass is complete. [[The Old Tongue]] holds the root tongue, the drifts, and the three polity names, and [[Naming in the Turning]] is the dictionary of institutions, with a common-tongue name and three stance-variants for each. The body of this note keeps plain descriptors on purpose, so a reader can follow it, and the in-world names live in those two notes.
- **Hooks this opens.** A party can be handed the Watchful crowns' roll as a thing to steal, forge, or escape; an unvouched Struck they must decide to stand for or to turn away; a Given-Over case of personhood with no settled law ([[The Holding Desk]]); a paid voucher waiting at a hinge ([[The Standing Trade]]); a Warm polity's "vocation" that is really conscription; a stranger who cannot be vouched, and the cost of that; a hearth that will not stand for its own child; a night-gate that asks the three questions; or a ticket-stripping that is really a monopoly protecting itself. The lived ticket-houses are [[The Stillers]], [[The Hall-Keepers]], [[The Element-Guilds]], and [[The Intake]]. Threnmaieth's named offices are [[The Reckoned Offices]].
