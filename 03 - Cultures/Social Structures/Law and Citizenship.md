# Law and Citizenship

> **What this is.** The *universal legal grammar* of **The Turning** — how law and citizenship bend around a world where **3 of 4 people carry a [[The Premise|Condition]]**. This is the portable layer: it flows from the [[Turning Tree]] and the Conditions, which are locked and universal, so it holds (with local accents) across all ~15 polities. **How individual polities *vary* on it** is the last section; specific governments come later ([[Roadmap|Epic 5]]/7). Built with `governance-systems` for [[Roadmap|Epic 2, Story 2.1]].

> **📖 Names: descriptive here, in-world names now exist.** The institutions below keep **plain descriptive terms** in the body for legibility — but the [[The Old Tongue|naming pass]] is done (2026-08-21), so each now has a settled common-tongue name **and** the three polity variants that reveal each polity's stance (a devout land's "gift-hall" is a surveillance-crown's "counted hall"). The working dictionary is [[Naming in the Turning]]; the root language is [[The Old Tongue]]. Names are good working canon, still open to a polish pass with the wider Epic 4 language work.

## The through-line: you belong because your town *saw* you Turn

Everything below rests on one fact already locked in the world: **every child passes through their town [[Turning Tree]] in their tenth year**, and **the whole town is there to watch.** The [[Turning Tree|Leaf-Fall]] is the most-attended day of a community's year — kin, neighbours, the [[The Leaf-Mother|tree-wardens]], all gathered. They *see* the leaf fall on a child, or see the Tree hug them.

So citizenship in The Turning is **witnessed, not recorded.** You are of a place because that place *remembers you* — it watched you become who you are. This is the deliberate, warm default of the setting, and it is worth stating plainly what it is *not*:

> **There is no register of the people.** No town, church, or crown keeps a list of everyone and their gift. That would be to hold a record *on* people, and it is not how this world works. Belonging is living communal memory, the way a real village knows its own — not a ledger. (The one kingdom that *does* keep such a list is a notorious exception — see [How polities vary](#How%20polities%20vary) — and it reads as unsettling precisely *because* no one else does it.)

This gives the world its citizenship engine, its regulatory hook, and its central injustice, all from the same warm root. **It is also a Hands-era default.** In [[The Walking Years]] the town that saw you Turn was the crowd under Thaeloren — or no one, if you never arrived. Vouching-at-a-distance exists to *carry that origin-witness home.* The grammar below is how the present works; the road underneath it is [[The Ages of the Turning]] and, lived, [[The Walking Years]] (the one-week door, the split household, origin-orphans).

## How you prove who you are — by *people*, not papers

If no one is written down, how does a stranger prove citizenship in a far city? The answer is one of the world's quiet [[The Premise|Condition-labor advances]]: **The Turning never needed to invent the papers-and-registers state, because trust runs through people who cannot be faked.**

You are known by **witnesses**, not documents:
- A **[[Long-Lived]]** remembers you — and their memory is incorruptible and runs back centuries.
- A **[[Bound]]** *vouches* for you — and a Bound's sworn word is literally binding, so a false vouch is not a lie they can cheaply tell.
- A **[[Far-Voiced]]** speaks for you — and their voice betrays real feeling under strain, so they cannot smoothly lie on your behalf.

To establish yourself in a new place is to **be vouched into it** — to acquire people who will stand for you. This is warmer and more human than an ID-check, and it has a sharp social consequence: **the friendless and the rootless are the truly vulnerable**, because they have no one to vouch. The law's exclusions fall not on "the undocumented" but on **the unvouched** — a person no one will stand for.

## The Struck — those who changed *unwitnessed*

Here is the seam the whole shape of Condition-law grows around, and it now has a warmer, truer mechanism than "off the list." **A [[The Premise|Struck]] person acquired their Condition *later, and alone*** — a drowning, a death, a sworn contract, long after their tenth-year Turning. The town watched everyone else become who they are. **No one watched the Struck.** They changed with no witnesses.

So a Struck person must be **vouched anew** — the community has to re-know them as what they've become, on the word of those who'll stand for them. That is the plain root of the old unease that the Struck are "a little suspect": not (only) prejudice, but the simple fact that *their becoming happened where no one could see it.* A change everyone witnessed is trusted; a change that happened in the dark must be spoken for. Every polity has people whose transformation went unwitnessed, and every polity feels differently about that.

Two things make the seam sharper:
- **The dangerous Struck arrive raw.** A Struck [[The Stilled]] or [[Far-Voiced]] manifests *untrained*, with the hazard already live and no guild to have caught it young. For these, being vouched-in is bound up with **mandatory guild-intake** — you are taken in hand *and* apprenticed at once, for everyone's safety, whether you wanted the vocation or not.
- **Some doors are crimes-adjacent.** The [[Bound]] are Struck by a sworn contract; the [[Returned]] by dying. To vouch a Bound into a community is to acknowledge a contract no one witnessed being sworn — which is why contract-law and the acceptance of the Bound are tangled together (below).

## The Inviolate Will — the one thing every polity agrees on

Beneath all local variation sits a single principle so firm it functions as constitutional bedrock, and it is bedrock because it is *true*: **no Condition can compel, control, or bend another person's will.** Not the [[Bound]]'s Whisper, not the [[Far-Voiced]]'s Cry, not the [[Long-Lived]]'s regard — every Condition's danger stops at property, at pain, at fear, at the edge of another's choosing. The law mirrors the metaphysics in a maxim taught to every child:

> **"The will is one's own."** *(the doctrine of the Inviolate Will — name provisional)*

Its consequences are large and load-bearing:
- **The horror-story fears are void in law.** There is no such crime as "being mind-controlled by a vampire," because it cannot happen. This is the legal floor under the setting's **~5%-scary** tone — the monsters are neighbours because the one thing that would make them un-neighbourly is *impossible*, and everyone knows it.
- **It sorts the criminal code.** Because a Condition can't seize a will, harm done with one splits cleanly into two very different things: **negligent Tithe-lapse** (letting your element vent, your Cry break, your green go to seed — a regulatory or public-nuisance matter) versus **willful weaponizing** (deliberately turning a gift on someone — ordinary violent crime, no gentler for the instrument). In practice only the [[The Stilled|Stilled's]] gaze even supports a true "you petrified a citizen" charge; most of what the law fears is negligence, not assault.
- **It is the shield the feared hide behind.** A [[Bound]] can point to the Inviolate Will and say, truthfully, *my contract cannot make you do anything.* Whether a given polity *listens* is exactly where they diverge.

## The safety-licence — how hazard is regulated (and by whom)

The one place the law *does* attach to individuals is public safety — and it does so narrowly. Because every Condition carries a **Tithe**, there's a natural hook: **the handful of Conditions whose lapsed Tithe or lost temper can hurt bystanders are the only ones that require a licence to practice.** The rule of thumb is consistent across the world: **a licence attaches where a Condition is *dangerous to others* — never where it is merely powerful, and never as a way to keep track of people.** It is the same logic by which any world licenses a few dangerous trades, not a census.

By that rule, only these need a licence — and it's held by the person as a mark of competence, like a master's ticket. How common each Condition is lives on [[The Premise]], not here.

| Licence weight | Condition | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Strictest** | [[The Stilled]] | The one gaze that can hold a person still forever. Real oversight and a strict code — the model the others imitate. |
| **Heavy, training-first** | [[Far-Voiced]], [[The Answered]] | A Cry in a crowded hall, an element "letting go" in a workshop — real bystander hazard, mastered by guild apprenticeship and scheduled outlets. |
| **Contractual, not bodily** | [[Bound]] | Overseen less for bodily hazard (the Whisper can't command) than because a Bound *is* a walking legal instrument (below). |
| **None** | [[Two-Bodied]], [[Long-Lived]], [[Returned]], and all the Kept | No bystander danger worth a licence. They are simply neighbours. |

**Who holds the pen matters as much as the rule.** Licences are **guild-issued, not state-issued** — the very late-medieval arrangement, and the thing that keeps this from being surveillance. A guild *knows its own members* the way any craft guild does; it isn't a registry of the population, it's a roster of practitioners of a dangerous art, kept by the art's own masters. The state *charters* the guild and lets it police its own under threat of losing the charter. So the [[The Stilled|Stilled's guild]] licenses stillers and the element-guilds channel the [[The Answered|Answered]], while the state watches the guild, not the individual. This diffuses power (no single authority holds the Conditioned) and breeds its own frictions: guilds guard monopolies, licences become property, and an unlicensed practitioner threatens the guild's charter as much as the public's safety.

## The three pillars — why the Conditioned *are* the legal system

The deepest fact about Condition-law is that it is not law imposed *on* the Conditioned from outside — three Conditions **are the legal apparatus itself**, and they are exactly the people who make a register unnecessary:
- **[[Long-Lived]]** — incorruptible memory. The archivists, notaries, and judges; a matter of record is whatever they say they witnessed a century ago. Where documents would be forged, a Long-Lived simply *remembers*.
- **[[Bound]]** — binding word. A Bound's sworn oath is *literally* binding and they hear a lie as it's told, so they are the witnesses, oath-keepers, and debt-arbiters. **When a treaty must truly hold, a Bound swears it.**
- **[[Far-Voiced]]** — unfakeable feeling. Trusted as sworn witnesses precisely *because* their voice betrays real emotion under strain, so they cannot lie well on the stand.

Evidence and contract in The Turning are therefore *made of people.* Its 1400s reaches trust — perfect memory, lie-heard oaths, unfakeable testimony — that ours needed paper bureaucracies (and centuries) to approximate, because here the machinery is alive and Given. **A world with witnesses like these does not need to write everyone down** — which is exactly why the one kingdom that insists on doing so looks so strange to everyone else.

## Rights of the Kept

The Kept are the quiet centre of citizenship, and their legal position is best described as **gloriously unremarkable.** They were witnessed at their Turning like everyone; they carry no Tithe, need no licence, answer to no guild. In most polities the Kept are the **default legal person** — the frame the rest of the law is drawn around. In one surviving compact they are also a **ruling class**: [[The Walled Book]] ranks the tithed and will strike a Closed Heir for taking a colour.

Two wrinkles keep them from being a footnote:
- **Kept status is not permanent immunity.** About a third of the Kept are still **Struck** later. So a Kept person lives untithed and unlicensed but never wholly outside the safety system's reach — the day they're Struck, they too must be vouched anew. Legally, "Kept" describes your present, not a promise about your future.
- **Where reverence for the gift runs hot, the Kept can be quietly *pitied* or pressured** — treated as having "missed out," leaned on to explain why the Tree passed them over. This is a social harm the law rarely names, and it runs hardest in the most devout polities (below).

## Edge cases the law must hold

- **The [[The Unbound|Hollow]] (the Unbound)** raise a *capacity and protective-status* question. Freed from a contract but missing the Pledge they paid, an isolated Hollow is pulled to sign again. Kinder polities extend a **protective status** (a guardian, a claim on connection, a bar on presenting them fresh contracts); harsher ones leave them to the pull.
- **The Given-Over (the "Forfeit")** is the setting's sharpest legal wound: a [[Bound]] who kept re-signing until the counterparty effectively *owns the person*. This is a living case of **legal personhood erased by accumulated contract** — and the contract-guilds have a term of art for it precisely because the law has never fully decided *at what point a person has bargained away enough of themselves to stop being one.* A recurring, unresolved question rather than a settled rule. The pipeline that feeds it has a lived face: [[The Holding Desk]]. Sold vouching — a bought mouth, not a paper — is [[The Standing Trade]].
- **[[Phoenix]]** is not a licensing problem at all — a single legendary person who cannot hide is a *political* problem (a symbol no ruler controls), handled by whoever currently wants to own or protect the one alive.
- **UnTurned is not Kept.** A child who missed, refused, or never stood has no hug and no colour. Folk will call them Kept. The law should file them as the person the town raised, vouched if someone will stand, unwitnessed at the Tree. Procedure: [[Turning Tree#Questions a warden gets asked]]. A second Leaf-Fall is not a remedy. Struck doors still open later.

## How polities vary

The grammar above is universal; the *tone* is not — and the world is as varied as a real one, region to region and culture to culture. That variation is not a short menu of country-types. It comes from a handful of **independent axes** that every polity sets *separately*, so the diversity lives in the combinations. Three axes carry most of it:

- **Theology — how gift-devout is it?** From ardent [[The Leaf-Mother|Motherfaith]] gift-integration (a Condition is a blessing, belonging is a birthright) through worn-lightly civic religion to frank secular pragmatism, and the odd local heresy. Sets how the Struck, the Kept, and the feared Conditions are *read* before the law even speaks.
- **Reach — Tree-rich core or Tree-poor edge?** Geography, per the [[The Premise|motherland/graft]] model: where healthy Trees stand, reach is full and the Given are dense; where Trees are few, young, or sickening, the population runs higher on the Kept and the Struck. This is the ground-level face of the keystone edge.
- **Governance — how far does the state reach onto the person?** From light guild self-rule (the world's warm default — the craft polices its own, the crown charters the guild) all the way to a crown that writes its subjects down. Sets how much the safety-hook becomes a surveillance-hook.

**Diversity falls out of the combinations.** A *devout frontier* (ardent faith, thin reach, almost no bureaucracy). A *secular Tree-rich merchant republic* (dense reach, light governance, faith worn lightly). A *pious core kingdom that is also Watchful* (surveillance dressed as sacred duty — the scariest mix). A cold pragmatic trade-city with no reverence at all. Each is a **good-faith** system with real supporters and a real cost, never an evil-empire cartoon — with one deliberate near-exception at the far end of the governance axis. Three recurring points on these axes are worth naming as touchstones (they are *illustrations*, not the whole space):

**The Warm** *(high theology).* Law flows straight from the Motherfaith's gift-theology: a Condition is a blessing, the Struck are vouched-in graciously as people the Mother met at a later door, licences are framed as helping you tend your gift rather than policing you. Warmest near the [[Turning Tree|Awakening Tree]]. **Its injustice is soft and hard to name:** everyone is *expected* to serve their gift, so guild "vocation" shades into pressure, the Kept get pitied for missing out, and to decline your Condition's obvious use reads as ingratitude to the Mother herself. (A polity can be Warm *and* frontier, *and* lightly or heavily governed — the theology is one knob, not the whole machine.)

**The Watchful** *(governance turned to its extreme — a deliberate aberration).* At the far end of the governance axis sits a crown that breaks the world's norm and *does* write its people down — a ruler (a nervous king, or a would-be dictator) who does not trust living memory and keeps a **roster of every subject and their Condition.** Note precisely what makes this aberrant: it is *not* that they keep written records at all — every polity has guild rolls, notarial acts, and tax ledgers — it is that they keep **a census of persons and their gifts**, a record held *on people*, which no one else does. Framed at home as prudence and order; experienced as **surveillance**, because that is what it is. The unvouched are hunted, the Struck are pursued to be catalogued, Tithe-needs become points of leverage, and the [[Bound]] especially are watched far past their real hazard — pure stigma dressed as safety, since the Whisper can't command and the roster's clerks know it. **This is the concrete face of the R2 injustice, and it reads as chilling precisely because no other polity keeps such a list** — the creepy record is one paranoid crown's invention, not the setting's baseline. (Nothing forces this crown to be irreligious *or* frontier: a Watchful kingdom that is also devout and Tree-rich is the darkest corner of the whole space.)

**The Frontier** *(low reach).* Far from any healthy Tree, where even witnessing is thin and custom outruns law. These lands run **higher on the Kept and higher on the Struck** — more hugs, more later doors, fewer coloured falls. In-town that is read as distance and young wood. Freer in the good sense (no one asks for a licence; the [[The Taken-In]] whom cities keep at arm's length are simply *neighbours* here, the backbone of the frontier economy) and lawless in the bad (unvouched Struck with no one to stand for them and no guild to train them, feared Conditions met with a rope rather than a licence). Reach and governance often *drift* together — a thin-reach edge tends to be thinly governed too — but they need not: a devout frontier and a lightly-governed core both exist.

## GM Notes
*(Stripped on player-safe export. Carries the keystone tie-in this note is keyed to via `reveals: [keystone-adjacent]`.)*

- **R2 social guard — keep this friction REAL.** Per [[Is the Leaf-Mother Real|the keystone]], the Leaf-Mother's benevolence is **cosmological, not social.** She authored the gifts; she did **not** author the injustice built on top of them. The unjust structures above — the Watchful kingdom's register, unvouched-Struck suspicion, Bound over-watching, guild conscription of the Given, Tithe-need as leverage, the Kept pitied, the Given-Over's lost personhood — are **not misreadings of a kindness to be dissolved by the reveal.** They are the moral spine of the setting. If the campaign ever confirms she is real and kind, that must **not** retroactively make the society just. The keystone answers *is anyone there, and do they mean well* (yes); it pointedly does not answer *is what people built around her fair* (often no). Protect that gap — it is where the play is.
- **Design note — witnessed, not recorded (user call, 2026-08-20).** The original draft made a universal Turning-*register* the citizenship engine; the user rightly flagged "records kept on everyone at ten" as too invasive and off-tone for a ~5%-scary world. Reframed: citizenship is **witnessed communal memory**, proof runs through **vouching people** (Long-Lived / Bound / Far-Voiced), and the only per-person paper is a **guild safety-licence for the ~3 hazardous Conditions.** The register survives *only* as one aberrant kingdom's paranoia (the Watchful). Keep the baseline warm; keep the record creepy and rare.
- **The reach-edge is the *cause* of the Frontier stance, and it's GM truth.** In the body, the frontier's higher Kept/Struck rates read as plain distance-and-biology (the skeptics' whole case). The *reason* — [[Is the Leaf-Mother Real|her reach is bounded and thins at the margins]] — is the locked keystone edge and stays GM-side. Same observable fact, two readings; don't let a player-facing note assert the cause. History's mundane face: far children once stayed Kept for want of a walk, and some still walk ([[The Ages of the Turning]]).
- **Naming is done (2026-08-21).** The in-world naming pass is complete — [[The Old Tongue]] (root tongue + drifts + the three polity names) and [[Naming in the Turning]] (institution dictionary: common-tongue name + three stance-variants each). The body here keeps plain descriptors on purpose (legibility); the in-world names live in those two notes. Remaining language work (deep grammar, the other polities' tongues) is [[Roadmap|Epic 4]].
- **Hooks this opens.** The Watchful kingdom's register as a thing to steal, forge, or escape; an unvouched Struck the party must decide whether to stand for or turn away; a Given-Over personhood case with no settled law ([[The Holding Desk]]); a bought mouth at a hinge ([[The Standing Trade]]); a warm polity's "vocation" that is really conscription; a stranger who *cannot be vouched* and what that costs them. Lived ticket-doors: [[The Stillers]] · [[The Hall-Keepers]] · [[The Element-Guilds]] · [[The Intake]]. Threnmaieth's named mouths: [[The Reckoned Offices]].
