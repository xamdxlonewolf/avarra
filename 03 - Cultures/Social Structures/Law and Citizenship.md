# Law and Citizenship

> **What this is.** The *universal legal grammar* of **The Turning** — how law and citizenship bend around a world where **3 of 4 people carry a [[The Premise|Condition]]**. This is the portable layer: it flows from the [[Turning Tree]] and the Conditions, which are locked and universal, so it holds (with local accents) across all ~15 polities. **How individual polities *vary* on it** is the last section; specific governments are built later ([[Roadmap|Epic 5]]/7). Built with `governance-systems` for [[Roadmap|Epic 2, Story 2.1]].

## The through-line: the Turning is where the law first sees you

Everything below rests on one fact already locked in the world: **every child passes through their town [[Turning Tree]] in their tenth year.** The rite that *Gives* a Condition or *Keeps* a child is therefore also the moment the state first writes them down. Law did not have to invent a census — the faith already runs one, every solstice, in every town. So the civic order and the religious order share a spine, and share a clergy: the **[[The Leaf-Mother|tree-wardens]]** who read the leaf-colours are also the keepers of the register.

This gives the world its citizenship engine, its regulatory hook, and its central injustice, all from the same root.

## The Roll — the citizenship engine

The **Roll** (formally the *Turning Roll*; every town keeps its own, wardens' hand, lodged at the Tree-house) is the civic register written at each [[Turning Tree|Leaf-Fall]]. **To be Turned is to be written into the Roll.** A person entered at their Turning is **Rolled** — documented, expected, a citizen by the ordinary course of life.

Crucially, **both the Given and the Kept are Rolled at ten.** A child who takes a colour is entered with their Condition named; a child the Tree only *hugs* is entered as one of **the Kept**. Neither is more a citizen than the other — they went through the same door on the same day. This is *why* the Kept are full members and not a leftover category: the rite that makes citizens doesn't care whether a colour fell.

The Roll is mundane, powerful paper. It fixes name, town, kin, year, and Condition-if-any. It is what a guild checks, what a court calls, what an inheritance turns on. In a [[The Premise|late-medieval world]] run partly by **[[Long-Lived]]** archivists, the Rolls of the older towns run back centuries in a single unbroken hand — which makes them very hard to forge and very hard to argue with.

## The Struck problem — the seam in the system

The Roll has one structural hole, and the whole shape of Condition-law grows around it. **A [[The Premise|Struck]] person acquires their Condition *outside* the ceremony** — a drowning, a death, a sworn contract, years after their Turning. They were Rolled at ten as whatever they then were (Kept, or Given something else), and the Roll now says something *false* about them.

So the Struck must be **read in** — registered after the fact, the wardens amending the Roll to name what they've become. A registered Struck is **read-in**; one who has not come forward (or been caught) is **unread**.

That single word carries the weight the world puts on the Struck. To be **unread** is to be, quite literally, *not yet accounted for* — and that is the plain administrative root of the old saying that the Struck are "a little suspect." It is not (or not only) prejudice; it is a documentation gap the metaphysics *guarantees*, because the register is keyed to a childhood rite and the Struck arrive by a later door. Every polity has an unread population it can't fully see, and every polity feels differently about that — see [How polities vary](#How%20polities%20vary).

Two aggravations make the seam sharper:
- **The dangerous Struck arrive raw.** A Struck [[The Stilled]] or [[Far-Voiced]] manifests *untrained*, with the hazard already live and no guild to have caught it young. Reading-in for these is bound up with **mandatory guild-intake** — you are registered *and* apprenticed at once, for everyone's safety, whether you wanted a vocation or not.
- **Some doors are crimes-adjacent.** The [[Bound]] are Struck by a sworn contract; the [[Returned]] by dying. Reading-in a Bound means the law now knows a contract exists that it did not witness — which is why contract-law and the reading-in of the Bound are tangled together (below).

## The Inviolate Will — the one thing every polity agrees on

Beneath all local variation sits a single principle so firm it functions as constitutional bedrock, and it is bedrock because it is *true*: **no Condition can compel, control, or bend another person's will.** Not the [[Bound]]'s Whisper, not the [[Far-Voiced]]'s Cry, not the [[Long-Lived]]'s regard — every Condition's danger stops at property, at pain, at fear, at the edge of another's choosing. The law mirrors the metaphysics in a maxim taught to every child on the Roll:

> **"The will is one's own."** *(the doctrine of the Inviolate Will)*

Its consequences are large and load-bearing:
- **The horror-story fears are void in law.** There is no such crime as "being mind-controlled by a vampire," because it cannot happen. This is the legal floor under the setting's **~5%-scary** tone — the monsters are neighbours because the one thing that would make them un-neighbourly is *impossible*, and everyone knows it.
- **It sorts the criminal code.** Because a Condition can't seize a will, harm done with one splits cleanly into two very different things: **negligent Tithe-lapse** (letting your element vent, your Cry break, your green go to seed — a regulatory or public-nuisance matter) versus **willful weaponizing** (deliberately turning a gift on someone — ordinary violent crime, no gentler for the instrument). In practice only the [[The Stilled|Stilled's]] gaze even supports a true "you petrified a citizen" charge; most of what the law fears is negligence, not assault.
- **It is the shield the feared hide behind.** A [[Bound]] can point to the Inviolate Will and say, truthfully, *my contract cannot make you do anything.* Whether the law *listens* is exactly where polities diverge.

## The Warrant — how hazard is regulated (and by whom)

Because every Condition carries a **Tithe**, the law has a natural licensing hook that needs no new machinery: **regulate the Tithe, and you regulate the danger.** A person certified current and safe on a hazardous Tithe holds a **warrant** (of practice). The rule of thumb is consistent across the world: **a warrant attaches where a lapsed Tithe or a lost temper can hurt bystanders or property — not where a Condition is merely powerful.**

By that rule, the warranted Conditions are the ones with a real public surface:

| Warrant weight | Condition | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Strictest** | [[The Stilled]] | The one gaze that can hold a person still forever. Licenses, oversight, a strict code — the canonical model all other warrants imitate. |
| **Heavy, training-first** | [[Far-Voiced]], [[The Answered]] | A Cry in a crowded hall, an element "letting go" in a workshop — real bystander hazard, mastered by guild apprenticeship and scheduled outlets. |
| **Contractual** | [[Bound]] | Warranted less for bodily hazard (the Whisper can't command) than because a Bound *is* a walking legal instrument (below). |
| **None** | [[Two-Bodied]] (~18%), [[Long-Lived]], [[Returned]] | No bystander danger worth a licence. The plurality of the Conditioned are simply *unregulated citizens.* |

**Who holds the pen matters as much as the rule.** Warrants are **guild-issued, not state-issued** — the very late-medieval arrangement. The state *charters* a guild and delegates the danger to it; the guild polices its own members under threat of losing the charter. So the [[The Stilled|Stilled's guild]] licenses stillers, the element-guilds channel the [[The Answered|Answered]], and the state mostly watches the guild rather than the individual. This diffuses power (no single authority controls the Conditioned) and creates its own frictions: guilds guard their monopolies, warrants become property, and an unwarranted practitioner threatens the guild's charter as much as the public's safety.

## The three pillars — why the Conditioned *are* the legal system

The deepest fact about Condition-law is that it is not law imposed *on* the Conditioned from outside — three Conditions **are the legal apparatus itself:**
- **[[Long-Lived]]** — incorruptible memory. The archivists, notaries, and judges; the Rolls are in their hand; a matter of record is whatever they say they witnessed a century ago.
- **[[Bound]]** — binding word. A Bound's sworn oath is *literally* binding and they hear a lie as it's told, so they are the witnesses, oath-keepers, and debt-arbiters. **When a treaty must truly hold, a Bound swears it.** Contract-guilds are built on them.
- **[[Far-Voiced]]** — unfakeable feeling. Trusted as sworn witnesses precisely *because* their voice betrays real emotion under strain, so they cannot lie well on the stand.

Contract-law and evidence-law in The Turning are therefore *made of people.* This is one of the world's [[The Premise|Condition-labor advances]]: its 1400s has an evidentiary machinery — perfect records, lie-heard oaths, unfakeable testimony — that ours reached only much later, because the machinery is alive and Given.

## Rights of the Kept

The Kept are the quiet centre of citizenship, and their legal position is best described as **gloriously unremarkable.** They are Rolled at ten like everyone; they carry no Tithe, need no warrant, answer to no guild. In most polities the Kept are the **default legal person** — the frame the rest of the law is drawn around.

Two wrinkles keep them from being a footnote:
- **Kept status is not permanent immunity.** About a third of the Kept are still **Struck** later. So a Kept person lives Rolled-and-untithed but never wholly outside the warrant system's reach — the day they're Struck, they too must be read in. Legally, "Kept" describes your present, not a guarantee about your future.
- **Where reverence for the gift runs hot, the Kept can be quietly *pitied* or pressured** — treated as having "missed out," leaned on to explain why the Tree passed them over. This is a social harm the law rarely names, and it runs hardest in the most devout polities (below).

## Edge cases the law must hold

- **The [[The Unbound|Hollow]] (the Unbound)** raise a *capacity and protective-status* question. Freed from a contract but missing the Pledge they paid, an isolated Hollow is pulled to sign again. Kinder polities extend a **protective status** (a guardian, a claim on connection, a bar on presenting them fresh contracts); harsher ones leave them to the pull.
- **The Given-Over (the "Forfeit")** is the setting's sharpest legal wound: a [[Bound]] who kept re-signing until the counterparty effectively *owns the person*. This is a living case of **legal personhood erased by accumulated contract** — and the contract-guilds have a term of art for it precisely because the law has never fully decided *at what point a person has bargained away enough of themselves to stop being one.* A recurring, unresolved question rather than a settled rule.
- **[[Phoenix]]** is not a licensing problem at all — a single legendary person who cannot hide is a *political* problem (a symbol no ruler controls), handled by whoever currently wants to own or protect the one alive.

## How polities vary

The grammar above is universal; the *tone* is not. Across the ~15 polities, three stances recur — and each is a **good-faith** system with real supporters and a real cost, never an evil-empire cartoon:

**1. The Warm (gift-integrationist).** Law flows straight from the [[The Leaf-Mother|Motherfaith's]] gift-theology: a Condition is a blessing, the Roll is a birthright, the Struck are *read in* graciously as people the Mother met at a later door, warrants are framed as helping you tend your gift rather than policing you. Warmest near the [[Turning Tree|Awakening Tree]]. **Its injustice is soft and hard to name:** everyone is *expected* to serve their gift, so guild "vocation" shades into pressure, the Kept get pitied for missing out, and to decline your Condition's obvious use reads as ingratitude to the Mother herself.

**2. The Ordered (regulatory-legalist).** Law treats Conditions as *managed assets*: warrants for everything defensible, the Roll worked as a surveillance tool, the unread pursued, the read-in kept on probation. Safe, efficient, superb records (its [[Long-Lived]] bureaucracy is centuries deep and never forgets a name). **Its injustice is cold and legible:** the Conditioned are catalogued, their Tithe-needs become points of leverage, and the [[Bound]] especially are regulated far past their actual hazard — pure stigma dressed as prudence, since the Whisper can't command and the law knows it.

**3. The Frontier (thin-reach).** Far from any mature Tree, where the register is patchy and custom outruns law. The keystone pattern is visible on the ground here: **the giving thins at the margins, so these lands run higher on the Kept and higher on the Struck** — more people the rite reached faintly or not at all. Freer in the good sense (no one checks your warrant; the [[The Taken-In]] who cities keep at arm's length are simply *neighbours* here, the backbone of the frontier economy) and lawless in the bad (unread Struck with no protection and no guild, feared Conditions met with a rope rather than a licence). Cosmology and governance point the same way: where her reach is thin, the law is thin too.

## GM Notes
*(Stripped on player-safe export. Carries the keystone tie-in this note is keyed to via `reveals: [leaf-mother-is-real]`.)*

- **R2 social guard — keep this friction REAL.** Per [[Is the Leaf-Mother Real|the keystone]], the Leaf-Mother's benevolence is **cosmological, not social.** She authored the gifts; she did **not** author the injustice built on top of them. The unjust structures above — unread-Struck suspicion, Bound over-regulation, guild conscription of the Given, Tithe-need as leverage, the Kept pitied, the Given-Over's lost personhood — are **not misreadings of a kindness to be dissolved by the reveal.** They are the moral spine of the setting. If the campaign ever confirms she is real and kind, that must **not** retroactively make the society just. The keystone answers *is anyone there, and do they mean well* (yes); it pointedly does not answer *is what people built around her fair* (often no). Protect that gap — it is where the play is.
- **The reach-edge is the *cause* of the Frontier stance, and it's GM truth.** In the body, the frontier's higher Kept/Struck rates read as plain distance-and-biology (the skeptics' whole case). The *reason* — [[Is the Leaf-Mother Real|her reach is bounded and thins at the margins]] — is the locked keystone edge and stays GM-side. Same observable fact, two readings; don't let a player-facing note assert the cause.
- **Hooks this opens.** Contested Roll entries (a forged or "corrected" century-old hand); an unread Struck the party must decide whether to shelter or turn in; a Given-Over personhood case with no settled law to lean on; a warm polity's "vocation" that is really conscription; an Ordered polity leveraging a PC's Tithe-need. Feed these to [[Roadmap|Epic 5 (Factions)]] and Epic 7 (Settlements) when the named polities get built.
