# Economy and the Tithe

> **What this is.** How a late-medieval economy works when **3 of 4 people carry a [[Conditions|Condition]]**, a change laid over the **Kind** they were born. A Kind is what a person is by birth, already true before any leaf falls, and a Condition is the change that comes after. Each Condition carries a **Tithe**, a permanent upkeep, and if the upkeep lapses the Condition's dangerous side shows. The world stays late-medieval except where Condition-labor carries it further, meaning work that exists because someone carries a Condition, and those are its only advances past that age. The roster lives on [[Conditions]], the law under the markets is [[Law and Citizenship]], and the last section says how the ~15 polities vary.

> **Names.** Institutions are given in plain words here, so the office is clear before its local name. Each has a common-tongue name, and each polity says that name its own way, in [[Naming in the Turning]]. The root language is [[The Old Tongue]].

## The Tithe shapes the economy

In our world, an economy is organized around producing what people want. In The Turning it is also organized around keeping three of every four people able to carry what they've become. Every [[Conditions|Condition]] has a **Tithe**, an ongoing need that must be met or the Condition's dangerous side shows. Once three in four people carry one, that need stops being a private matter between a person and their gift.

**Tithe-provision** is a permanent civic utility, in the same way wells and roads are. You can see it in every town, people fight over it, and the people who depend on it cannot do without it. It is not the size of grain, which feeds everyone, every day. The greens and the halls keep an exposed minority from sliding, and they keep the neighbours from having to live beside that slide. That is enough to organize a sector of the town's work, and it is not food.

Society organizes around the Conditioned for that reason, rather than from reverence or from fear: the work fits the Tithe, and there is a net under the people whose Tithe does not pay itself. Most Conditioned pay by working, because for them the Tithe and the job are the same act. A smaller share has to be furnished by someone else. The ones who furnish, and the ones who need furnishing, are often the same people, and whoever holds the gate holds power.

### Who actually buys provision

The shares for each Condition are on [[Conditions]]. This table sorts people by how they pay, and it gives no numbers of its own.

| Who | Conditions | What "provision" is |
|---|---|---|
| **Self-paying by vocation** | [[The Answered]] · [[Two-Bodied]] · [[The Stilled]] | The work is the Tithe, so they do not buy a sector of the economy, and they are a labor market. |
| **On the seam** | [[Far-Voiced]] · [[Returned]] | They pay their own Tithe when the job fits, and when it does not they buy a hall-hour or walk the need unpaid. |
| **Provided-for** | [[The Taken-In]] · [[Long-Lived]] · [[The Unbound]] · [[Bound]] Terms | Someone else supplies the green, the novelty, the warmth, or whatever clause the contract set. |
| **The Kept** | no Condition | They have no Tithe, so they buy bread and do not buy a lot-hour. |

Green is free on a farm, and most Taken-In live where it is free. The purchased or civic customer base is a minority of the people, concentrated in the stone towns: urban Taken-In, hall-users who have no stage of their own, people seeking novelty, and warmth that money can only approximate. The traffic at those doors is real, and so are the queues, and the doors are not granaries. A city fights over lot-hours, which are time on the living green, and over hall-bells, which are the hours a voice is allowed to vent, the way it fights over wells: because a wilt for lack of green, or a Cry breaking in the square, is a public problem, and not because Tithe-goods rival bread by the cartload.

## The structural key: self-paying vs provided-for Tithes

The single most important economic fact is that not all Tithes cost the same to pay, and some do not cost coin at all, because paying them is doing useful work. That sorts the Conditioned along a spectrum from self-paying to provided-for, and almost everything else follows from where a Condition sits on it. The line is a spectrum, not a wall. The cases worth arguing about sit on the seam, and which side a person lands on can depend on the work they actually do, not only on the Condition. Far-Voiced and the Returned, below, are where that shows.

**Self-paying Tithes are the ones where the work is the payment.** For several Conditions, the upkeep is discharged by doing exactly the labor other people need done. For these people, the cost of keeping the Condition and the work they produce are the same act.

| Condition | The Tithe | ...paid by doing |
|---|---|---|
| [[The Answered]] | use the element in earnest | The craft itself, so that a Tide-Answered shipwright slakes the Tithe by building the ship. |
| [[Two-Bodied]] | spend time as the animal | The ranging work — herding, rescue, courier, scout — because being the beast is the job. |
| [[The Stilled]] | burn off the Grey by exertion | Active labor — surgery, riot-lines, demolition — because the vigorous work clears the clock. |

**On the seam, a person self-pays if the vocation fits, and falls to being provided-for if it does not.** Two Conditions do not sit cleanly in either column. Where they land turns on what work the person actually does, which is the seam in the spectrum showing itself.

| Condition | The Tithe | Self-pays when… | Falls to provided-for when… |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[Far-Voiced]] | vent strong emotion each day | The vocation is the vent: performers, criers, negotiators, and sworn witnesses feel in earnest on the job, and that feeling discharges the Tithe. | The voice has no outlet, as in an isolated posting, dull labor, or simple silence, and they need a venting-hall furnished for them. |
| [[Returned]] | act toward the Purpose | The Purpose, the aim they came back carrying, lines up with the work, so plague wards, recovery, and tending the dead pay the Tithe by being the deadly job. | The Purpose is personal or unmet by any trade — a vengeance, a vow, a person still to find — so no employer can furnish it, and it pulls against ordinary work. |

**Provided-for Tithes are upkeep someone else has to supply.** For the rest, the Tithe is a cost with no productive by-product, so someone has to furnish the green, the novelty, or the warmth, and that furnishing is the Tithe-infrastructure of the town.

| Condition | The Tithe | ...must be provided |
|---|---|---|
| [[The Taken-In]] | rest on living land | Green, trivial on the farm and a real expense in a stone city, where it means potted groves and garden-leave. |
| [[Long-Lived]] | learn something genuinely new | Novelty: books, travel, correspondence, and the ordinary traffic of a university town. |
| [[The Unbound]] | keep connecting to others | Warmth, which is company and being known, and it is the one Tithe money buys least well. |
| [[Bound]] | keep the Terms | Whatever the counterparty set down, which is idiosyncratic and sometimes ruinous. |

The self-payers are prosperous almost automatically, because their Condition is a trade, though prosperity is not the same thing as ease. The [[The Stilled]] self-pay and are still the most tightly licensed people in the world, and the [[The Answered]] self-pay under heavy guild discipline, as [[Law and Citizenship]] sets out. Self-paying buys a living, and it does not buy social comfort along with the living. The provided-for are economically exposed, because they depend on someone furnishing the upkeep, and whoever furnishes it holds power over them. The people on the seam are the most precarious of all. A Far-Voiced who loses the stage, or a Returned whose Purpose turns personal, can slide from self-paying to exposed overnight. That slide is where the injustice runs, and the next section is the shape of it.

## Tithe-poverty and the safety net

Because the provided-for Tithes cost money, this world has a poverty ours does not, and you can be too poor to stay whole. A Taken-In who cannot afford green, a Long-Lived with no access to anything new, a Far-Voiced with nowhere to vent: these people do not only suffer want. They slide toward the scary edge, into the wilting, the fade, or the involuntary Cry, so that destitution here has a visible, dangerous face.

Who pays for that provision is this world's version of the question of welfare, and polities do not answer it the same way.

Where provision is treated as a public good, which is the Warm answer, the town keeps it the way it keeps roads and wells. There are town greenhouses, free venting-halls, and endowed libraries, because a town whose Conditioned stay whole is a town that stays safe and keeps working. The pious way of saying the same choice is that you help every person tend their gift.

Where provision is left as a private burden, harsher polities hand it to the individual and to the guild. The Tithe-poor fall, they become a real hazard to the people around them, and the same polities then police the fall instead of preventing it.

The cynical middle is leverage: the town, the guild, or the employer does provide the Tithe, and holds what it provides, so that the furnisher can withhold it. How that maps onto the stances is in [How polities vary](#How%20polities%20vary).

## Labor: who does what, and why the fit is so tight

Set the roster's jobs in a line and the striking thing is that the Tithe and the vocation are usually the same shape, because the world did not assign these jobs and the Conditions are the jobs. How large each workforce is lives on [[Conditions]], and what follows is vocation rather than a census.

The [[Two-Bodied]] are the plurality, and they are the versatile backbone of physical labor: herding, haulage, rescue, courier, scouting, and war. The Condition runs in bloodlines, so a family can be wolf-folk or hawk-folk straight through, and the family trade and the family Condition come down as one inheritance.

The [[The Taken-In]] are the food-and-timber base, the farmers and foresters and herders and wardens, and the frontier economy runs on them because they thrive on living land. Cities need their grain and keep a working minority of them inside the walls, for the reasons in [Why Taken-In live in stone cities](#Why%20Taken-In%20live%20in%20stone%20cities). The arm's length a city keeps is social, and it is not an absence of the people.

The [[Far-Voiced]] do coordination, and they do trust at a distance. They are the signal and relay network, criers posted on coasts and roads and borders, and they are performers, negotiators, and sworn witnesses in court. That network is a [[Conditions|Condition-labor advance]]: a real-time long-distance signal, which a medieval world would not otherwise have.

The [[The Answered]] do the elemental crafts, and they are guilded by element, so Ember covers the smiths and the glasswrights, Tide the shipwrights and the dyers, Root the masons and the potters, and Gale the sailmakers and the millers, with the same people rotating through firefighting and rescue.

The [[Returned]] do the work that kills the living, because death cannot take them twice: plague wards, mine and cave-in recovery, deep and dangerous salvage, and tending the dead.

The [[The Stilled]] do precision stillness: surgeons and medics, peacekeepers on the riot-lines, controlled demolition, and stonework. They are licensed, and the guild disciplines them, as [[Law and Citizenship]] describes.

The [[Long-Lived]] do the memory professions: archivists, notaries, judges, scholars, and bankers, which the banking section below takes up. They are few by design, because a world with too many of the deathless would ossify.

The [[Bound]] are the enforcement and the contract class: oath-keepers, debt-arbiters, treaty-swearers, and witnesses whose word is literally binding. The [[The Unbound]] sit in this same contract-lane, a released Bound rather than a second workforce, and they go back to whatever they did before, needing connection with other people if they are to keep at it.

[[Phoenix]], only a trace in the population, has no characteristic sector. A Phoenix is a political symbol, and the economy does not employ them as a class of worker.

And the Kept are the quiet inversion of the whole ledger. They carry no Tithe, so they are unburdened by any upkeep, and they carry no Condition, so they are unclaimed by any Condition-guild. Economically that makes them the free generalist labor, able to take ordinary work and to move where the work is. Where some of the faithful pity the Kept for missing a gift, as [[Law and Citizenship]] describes, the ledger tells the other half of the story: the Kept are the least encumbered people in the world, a real economic freedom hiding under a social slight.

## Why Taken-In live in stone cities

Green costs money in a stone city, and the cities keep the Taken-In at arm's length because the Taken-In thrive on the frontier. Both are true, and arm's length is how the polite districts stand toward the Taken-In, bark and antler included, without the lots being empty.

They are in the city for five ordinary reasons.

1. **They were Given here.** A copper-green leaf fell for them at a city Tree, and family, work, and a hearth-witness are what keep them. Leaving the city would mean leaving the circle that saw them Turn, which is the witnessing [[Law and Citizenship#Turning in a city — the hearth stood]] describes.
2. **Grain and timber come in, and people come with them.** Seasonal Taken-In work the quays, wintering, and some of them stay, the harvest-hands who do not go home in Hale-month.
3. **The lot is a job.** [[The Greens-Keepers]] need Taken-In labor, so the city imports that green-skill for the commons and then will not sit the workers at the high table.
4. **The door can open in a city's old green.** Being lost and dying in a deep wild is the Struck path for this Condition, and a walled grove, a lot at night, or a river-garden after a flood can open the same door, rarely, and often enough to count.
5. **They cannot afford to leave.** Green-poverty is a trap: you need the lot if you are to stay whole, the lot is gated, you work for the hours on it, and you never save the cart-fare home, which is the urban book of [[The Slide]].

The Taken-In share of the whole world is on [[Conditions]]. A stone city runs lower than that share, and the Taken-In who are there concentrate on the lots, the orchards, the river-gardens, and the week-keeper's pot. There are enough of them to make a queue, enough for a wilt two streets off the lot, and enough for the Slide to have a customer, while the frontier keeps the density and the honour and the city keeps the gate.

## Banking and the deathless houses

The headline fact in the finance of The Turning is that its banks do not die. A [[Long-Lived]] banking house is founded by a person who is still personally running it two centuries later, with the same memory, the same word, and the same unbroken ledger. In a late-medieval world of letters of credit and merchant houses, that continuity is the great Condition-labor advance in finance, and it has three consequences.

**The trusted note.** A note from a deathless house is trusted almost as coin, because everyone knows the banker cannot forget a debt, cannot be quietly succeeded by a defaulting heir, and will personally answer for the paper in fifty years. Their paper circulates further than any crown's, and it holds its value better. The nearest thing this world has to a gold standard is the word of a house that will not die.

**The long instrument.** Deathless houses lend and endow on horizons no mortal lender would risk: century loans, multi-generation trusts, and the patient financing of cathedrals, canals, and universities. These are letters of credit and endowments, and there are no stock exchanges and no joint-stock companies.

**The concentration risk.** Wealth that never passes to an heir never disperses through inheritance, so the great scattering that mortal death forces on every other fortune simply does not happen here. There are few of the deathless, as [[Conditions|the deathless share]] records, and even so they can slowly gather an outsized share of the capital that stands still. That gathering is the risk their small number is supposed to limit, and the economy has never fully resolved it. Endowing does not undo the gathering: an endowment moves the coin out and keeps the steering, so the influence concentrates even while the liquid wealth flows into institutions the same hands still direct, which is the mechanism the inheritance section takes up in full.

**Where the houses came from.** Many of them began as road-houses in [[The Walking Years]], inns and beds and century-credit for families who had to walk to Thaeloren. The lived version is the house still in the act of being an inn (*brenhael*; the Held bed; a note you pay on the home-walk). When the walk stopped being required in the core, the same Long-Lived still remembered every debtor on the road, the paper outlived the pilgrimage-season, and the inns became banks. The present tense of that memory is [[The Years of Hands]], where two fates are still standing: [[The White Note House]] collected on the paper, and [[The Third Hearth]] kept making the bed. The trusted note is a Walking-era instrument that outlived the road it was written for.

## Inheritance and the problem of not dying

Inheritance law is warped by the deathless, and the warp is a poignant one. A [[Long-Lived]]'s children are not guaranteed to inherit the Condition, so most of them age and die while the parent does not. A deathless person therefore outlives most of the heirs, and the estate never enters the inheritance stream at all. The question is what becomes of a fortune that is never passed down.

The usual answer is that the deathless endow. They have no line of heirs waiting on the death, and their own Tithe needs a steady supply of novelty — new books, new scholarship, new things to learn — so a Long-Lived characteristically funds the institutions that generate knowledge: the libraries, the universities, the archives. The deathless banker becomes the deathless patron, and pays their own Tithe by building the places that feed it. It is also how they grieve: an endowment in the name of a child who died a century ago. This is not the same thing as letting the fortune go. An endowment converts coin into patronage that is permanent and directed, and because the founder does not die, the founder keeps the chair, names the scholars, and steers the institution for centuries. Endowment eases the concentration of coin, because liquid wealth flows out into public works, and it deepens the concentration of influence, because the same few deathless hands still guide the universities, the courts, and the archives their money built. The concentration of wealth among a few deathless houses does not disappear, and it changes from hoarded gold to entrenched patronage.

For everyone else, inheritance is ordinary, except in the [[Two-Bodied]] bloodlines, where the Condition, the family's animal, and often the family's trade descend together, so that a place in the guild and a Condition can be a single inheritance. The sharpest question the law still has open, the one [[Law and Citizenship]] states, is economic at the root. The Given-Over is a [[Bound]] who contracted away so much of themselves that the counterparty effectively owns the person. That is the ultimate inheritance failure: an estate, and a self, absorbed by a creditor.

## Center and periphery

The economy is not spread evenly, and the unevenness follows the same geography that shapes the law. Near the mature Trees, in the core, Conditions lie thick, the guilds are strong, and specialized Condition-labor and the goods that come from it are what the core sells to everyone else. Out at the margins, on the frontier, the giving thins, so more of the people there are generalist [[Law and Citizenship|Kept]], or Struck and untrained, and fewer of them are Given specialists.

The core exports the specialized work and the finished goods — Answered crafts, Stilled medicine, deathless-house credit — and it imports the raw staples. The frontier leans hardest on the [[The Taken-In]], who thrive there and are neighbours, and on Kept labor that can turn its hand to whatever the week needs. It exports food, timber, and raw materials, and it imports the specialists it cannot grow. Distance, spoilage, and the thin reach together make the friction real, and they leave the frontier specialist-poor and self-reliant. Cities still keep a working minority of Taken-In behind a gate, and they do not ship the Condition out of town.

## The shadow economy

Every gate grows a black market, and the gates in this economy are Tithe-provision and vouching.

**Illicit Tithe-supply.** Where the legitimate provision is rationed, gated, or unaffordable, a shadow market fills the gap with smuggled green for a Taken-In cut off from living land. That is the urban book of the trade: the city-Given, the winter-stayers, and the people caught in the lot-hour trap. The same market keeps unlicensed venting-rooms, and it sells black-market novelty, rare texts and forbidden knowledge, to a Long-Lived who is starving for anything new. The customer is someone already sliding toward the scary edge of their Condition, which makes the trade both lucrative and cruel, and the lived face of it is [[The Slide]].

**Off-book contracts.** The whole domain of the [[Bound]] has an underside: contracts sworn outside the honest tables, unregistered terms, and the predatory brokers who arrange them. That pipeline feeds the Given-Over, its lived face is [[The Holding Desk]], and it is not the Slide.

**Sold vouching.** Citizenship here is [[Law and Citizenship|vouching]], a place counting you as its own because a person will stand for you rather than because a clerk has written your name down. The unvouched will buy a voucher, and a market exists in dubious character-witnesses. The one thing that cannot be faked is a Long-Lived's memory, which is why an honest vouch from them is priceless and a corrupt one is catastrophic. The lived face of that market is [[The Standing Trade]], and it is not the Slide.

## How polities vary

The rules of the Tithe are the same everywhere, and who actually receives what is politics. It follows the same three measures as the law — theology, reach, and governance, set out in [[Law and Citizenship#How polities vary]] — and each of the three has an economic face, with three stances that keep recurring.

**The Warm** *(high theology).* A Warm polity treats Tithe-provision as a public good. It keeps town greenhouses, venting-halls and libraries that someone has endowed, and ranging-commons held in common. The Conditioned are kept whole at the town's expense, because a whole populace is a safe and productive one, and because the faith says to tend every gift. The economic injustice is soft, because the same help arrives with an expectation attached: you are helped to tend your gift, and you are quietly obligated to spend it as the community sees fit, until the subsidy shades into conscription.

**The Watchful** *(governance at its extreme).* A crown that writes its people down — the Tallied Crown and the Worn Count, as [[Law and Citizenship]] describes them — turns the need of a Tithe into leverage. It provides, and it provides efficiently, even generously, and it holds what it provides. The green, the outlet, and the novelty all come through channels the crown controls and can withhold. Its deathless-house finance is superb and centuries-deep, and the injustice is cold and precise, because a population whose upkeep runs through the state is a population that cannot afford to refuse the state. It is the most complete control in the world, and it is dressed as care.

**The Frontier** *(low reach).* There is little formal infrastructure for the Tithe, and there are few specialists. People improvise a way to meet the need, or they go without. The freedom is real: no guild monopoly, no crown's leash, and the Taken-In are neighbours rather than curiosities. The harshness is also real, because the provided-for Tithe-poor slide with no net, and untrained Struck manage dangerous Conditions alone. At the edge the economy is barter and favor, cash-poor and land-rich.

A polity's economy is set by where it stands on all three measures at once. A devout frontier runs commons-minded charity with almost no infrastructure to fund it. A secular Tree-rich republic has deep markets and thin welfare. A Watchful-and-pious core turns generous provision into the tightest leash in the world.

## GM Notes
*(Stripped on player-safe export. Keyed to the keystone via `reveals: [keystone-adjacent]`.)*

- **Keep the economy mechanical.** If someone controls the green, the outlets, the novelty, or the terms, they have leverage over the person who needs those things. Guild conscription, the concentration in deathless hands, illicit provision, and the Given-Over each need a price, an actor, and a transaction the party can walk in and interrupt.
- **The sharpest economic lever in play** is the person who furnishes a provided-for Tithe, because that person holds power over the bearer. A PC Taken-In whose only green is the guild's, a Long-Lived whose patron rations the novelty they need if they are not to fade, a Bound whose counterparty is also their employer: each of those is coercion with a friendly face, the ~5%-scary edge of the setting put into economic form.
- **Story R.6 — volume and urban green (2026-08-30).** The food-or-fuel claim was softened on purpose. The purchased and civic Tithe sector serves about a seventh of the people, and it concentrates in the stone towns, where something between a twentieth and a twelfth of a stone city's adults are Taken-In. Those figures are GM estimates, not census figures, and the shares themselves are on [[The Premise]]. The sector is a real utility, and it is not a second staple. Urban Taken-In stay in the city for five ordinary reasons: they were Given there, they came as harvest-hands, the lot is labor, rare city-doors, and the lot-hour trap. Arm's length is manners, not an empty map. Do not invent a grain-sized Tithe market to make the old sentence true. The lived faces of sold vouching and of the Given-Over are [[The Standing Trade]] and [[The Holding Desk]].
- **The reach-edge, the distribution between core and frontier, has a cause that stays on the GM side.** In the world, the thinning at the margins reads as plain distance and as biology. The real reason, that [[Is the Leaf-Mother Real|her reach is bounded]], stays on this side of the note. Do not let a player-facing paragraph assert that cause.
- **Naming is done (2026-08-21).** The in-world names for every institution in this note live in [[Naming in the Turning]], a common-tongue name plus three stance-variants, built on [[The Old Tongue]]. The body keeps the plain descriptors so a reader can follow them.
- **Hooks.** A party can be pointed at a monopoly in Tithe-supply and asked to break it; at a deathless house calling in a century-old debt; at an endowment that arrived with strings; at a ring smuggling green or novelty; at a frontier town with no net on the day its Struck cannot cope; or at the Tithe-channels of a Watchful crown, which are a system of control to escape or to sabotage. The lived official face is [[Tithe-Infrastructure]]. The lived illicit faces are [[The Slide]], [[The Holding Desk]], and [[The Standing Trade]].
