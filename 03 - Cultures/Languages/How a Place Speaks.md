# How a Place Speaks

> **How to use this.** This is how people talk, for play. The sounds, and the old written names, stay in [[The Old Tongue]]. Person-names, and the second name that follows a given name, stay in [[Naming People in the Turning]]. What follows is what a place says out loud for six ordinary things, and four tones a player can actually keep up for a scene. Speak the common word. A rite, a charter, or a scholar's copy still carries the long written name, which is the liturgical form, the one that belongs to writing and to ritual rather than to the street.

A traveller can say **Turning Tree**, **the Mother**, **a hug**, **a colour**, **a tree-warden**, and **a graft**, and be understood on every mainland road. Turning Tree is the shared name for the Tree, and the Mother is the one the pious core calls Maieth in the street and the docks call the old rite. A colour is what a Tree speaks when it can already speak one, and a hug is the no-colour. A tree-warden is the person named with that Tree, under a different word in each column below, and a graft is wood that has been brought and set and cannot yet Turn. The table is what a place says when it is being itself, rather than reaching for the word a stranger would already understand. The core keeps the *th* sound, which this note calls the thorn. Waiting towns soften that thorn to *t*, the docks drop it, and [[The Sundering Isle|Kumbaan]] speaks a language of its own. The old liturgical tongue belongs to the mainland, and the hill was never part of it.

## What they call the six things

Common speech only. These handles are already in everyday speech, worn by the drift, which is the regular way a place has worn the old sound down. The long written compounds stay on paper.

| | **Conservative** — pious core | **Worn** — waiting lands, live front | **Eroded** — the docks | **Kumbaan** — the hill |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **the Tree** | **the Mother's Hand** | **the Thallow** | **Tallo** *(TAL-oh)* | **the far tree** |
| **the Mother** | **Maieth** *(MY-eth)* | **Mait** *(MAYT)* | **the old rite** | **the loud story** |
| **a hug** | **a keeping** | **a hug** | **a clear** | **the place** |
| **a colour** | **the colour** | **the gift** | **the fall** | **a test** |
| **a warden** | **the Reckoned Hands** | **Hands-folk** | **a tree-tender** | **the loud guest** |
| **a graft** | **a Hand carried in** | **a carried Thallow** | **a carrying** | **their cutting** |

**The core.** A neighbour in [[Maiethlir]] or the Tallied Crown says **Maieth** in the street, the way another town says *the Mother*, and the polity's own street-name is still **the Tallied Crown**. **The Mother's Hand** is the Tree that can already speak a colour. Wood that has been brought and set, and cannot yet Turn, is **a Hand carried in**, and at the rite they call the no-colour **a keeping**. The Tallied Crown's clerks file that child as *unmarked*. That word belongs to the desk, and the square does not use it. [[Maiethvael]] shares **Maieth** and **the Mother's Hand**, and calls the warden a **tree-warden**. **The Reckoned Hands** is the crown's word for the same person.

**Waiting speech, and the live front.** Careful prayer puts the thorn back, and **Maieth** returns for as long as the prayer is open. The kitchen says **Mait** *(MAYT)*, which is *Maieth* with the thorn tired, and **the Thallow** is their name for the Tree. A colour is **the gift**, and a no-colour is still **a hug**, which they do not dress as a prize. Young wood on the road is **a carried Thallow**. On the live front, a young graft or a neighbour's canopy such as [[Harrow's Green]], people say **the wood** until that wood can Turn.

**The docks.** **Tallo** *(TAL-oh)* is how a quay says *the Thallow* once the thorn is gone. Say it on the water, and keep it off the charter, because the written name stays the Turning Tree, or the old liturgical name when the paper is a rite. They call her **the old rite**, and they call a no-colour **a clear** — the child came up clear, free to be hired. A warden there is **a tree-tender**. A scion in the hold, their word for a graft being carried, is **a carrying**. [[Trenledd]] is this speech with a list behind it. The gate asks the house, and the gate will not put the thorn back to be polite.

**The hill.** [[Ndenjoo]] has a table, and it has no Tree and no graft, so it does not grow a word that would put one in the valley. **The far tree** and **their cutting** are mainland things, said of someone else's country. **The loud story** is what they call the Mother when a guest brings her up: a tale about being chosen, told at noon-volume. **The place** is what they offer where a mainlander expects a hug. **A test** is their word for a colour, and they will not use one on a guest. A warden who climbs the hill is **the loud guest**, and is still fed.

## Tones a player can use

There are four tones. Each one is a sentence length, the thing they say again, and the thing they will not say while mixed company is present. Each mainland drift still has one phrase it says whole. The hill has its own. Nobody stops to translate them.

### Devout core

This is the speech of [[Threnmaieth]], of [[Maiethlir]], and of a pious square, and it is also [[The Walled Book|the Inner Close]] when the room is a household rather than a file.

**Length.** They use two sentences, and the first one finishes. If that first sentence left **the Mother's Hand** or **Maieth** out, the second puts them back. A fragment sounds like the dock, and they will hear it.

**They repeat.** What they say again is **Maieth**, and **the Mother's Hand**. Drop either, and the next sentence restores it.

**They will not say in company.** They will not say *Tallo*, *the old rite*, or *a clear*, and they will not say *unmarked* while the rite is still open, because that word belongs to the desk. They will not lead with a house-name to a neighbour, and they will not joke a colour.

A player who needs the sound of the core can say this, and then stop. The last words are the phrase, left whole.

> "The Mother's Hand kept her. We say Maieth before the square moves. Lei thulen." *(lay THOO-len)*

### Dock

This is [[Lestrand]], [[Orentel]], and the Ledger Coast quays, and it is [[Trenledd]] when the list is what is speaking.

**Length.** They give about six words to a breath, and they take another breath only if the number or the berth needs it. Then the phrase, and the quay lets it go.

**They repeat.** They say **clear** again, and the number, and the berth. What they repeat is the last hard fact, not a person's feelings about it.

**They will not say in company.** They will not say *Maieth* or *the Mother's Hand*, they will not restore a thorn to sound devout, and they will not call a clear a sadness. They will not ask *what house?* while the paper is shut. While a filing house has the paper open, they will — house first, then the given name — and they still will not hymn it.

The dock example keeps the phrase whole, the way the quay would actually let it go.

> "Clear. Berth five. The fall's on Tallo. Ka doven." *(kah DOV-en)*

### Waiting, or the live front

One tone covers two repeats. Waiting towns, [[Polity Archetypes|the Waiting Lands]] and [[Eolvaeth]] among them, say again the week that has not come. The live front, [[Harrow's Green]] or a young graft or a neighbour's canopy, says the wood again.

**Length.** They use one sentence, and that sentence does not close the week. Twelve words is already long for them. They reach for the old word and let the thorn thin.

**They repeat.** Waiting speech says **not this week**. The live front says **the wood**, and then **the neighbour** if the wood will not speak.

**They will not say in company.** They will not promise the week, they will not say *the old rite*, and they will not offer a house-name. They will not call the Thallow *Tallo*, because that is the coast, and on this road it sounds like a slight. Prayer puts the thorn back. The kitchen keeps the tired form to itself.

Waiting speech, with its phrase left whole:

> "Not this week, and the Thallow is still the Thallow. Len soret." *(len so-RET)*

The live front uses the same length and the same phrase, and the other repeat:

> "The wood first, and the neighbour if the wood won't. Len soret."

### Hill-hall

This is [[Ndenjoo]], and any attic that is still keeping the table, and they keep a night pace. The welcome is the whole theology they are willing to say with guests in the room.

**Length.** They spend three short sentences on the welcome, a welcome, then a pause, then one practical thing, and they do not explain a Tree inside that welcome.

**They repeat.** What they say again is **sit**, and **the place**.

**They will not say in company.** They will not read **a test**, they will not point to a graft on the hill, and they will not ask *of where?* or *what house?* as the price of a seat. They will not say *Maieth*, and a mainland house-name, or a mainland *of-the-town*, is not the name they use at the table.

The hill's own phrase stays inside the welcome, untranslated.

> "Sit. The place is set. Aman ndo." *(ah-MAHN ndoh)*

## What they ask you

The second name is the name that is not the given name. In a witness-town, a place that still names you by who saw you, a stranger gives the given name and *of the place*. In a list-land, a place that writes people down so the paper can find them later, a clerk gets the house first and then the given name. A house-name is a local drift-word, a word from that place's own wearing-down of the old tongue, often an old given name that stuck to a line. It is that place's word, and it is not an English surname. The worked rows for what you say when asked are in the house-name section of [[Naming People in the Turning#House-names — where a list outlives the witness]].

| Who is asking | What you give |
|---|---|
| **A neighbour** | the given name |
| **A clerk in a list-land** | the house first, then the given name |
| **A witness-town, and you are a stranger** | the given name, and *of the place* |
| **Kumbaan** | the given name |

The list-lands are **Threnmaieth**, **Trenledd**, **the Inner Close**, and **a filing house while the paper is open**. Shut the paper, and the quay goes back to the witness way. A witness-town asks *of where?* because the name that stands for you is the place that saw you. A given name with no place on it has not been stood for, because the place that saw you is the part of the name that does the standing. Leading with a house sounds like a column on a roll, and that town does not keep the column.

A list-land asks *what house?* because the paper has to find you after the witness is gone. The house is the row the paper looks up, and the given name alone is an unfinished line.

Kumbaan asks neither question. The given name is the name, and a mainland house, or a mainland *of-the-town*, is not your name on the hill.

## GM Notes

This note is voice only. Deep grammar stays deferred on [[The Old Tongue]], so do not grow a paradigm out of these tones. A paradigm would be a full set of word-forms, and this page is not that. Do not grow a fourth mainland tongue here, and do not grow a sentence grammar.

Coinage stays frozen. *Lei thulen*, *len soret*, and *ka doven* are in-use phrases, one per drift. *Aman ndo* is the hill's phrase. It is not a Maiethren drift and not a root. Do not gloss them. Do not etymologize them into the root list, which would mean inventing an old-root origin and filing it with the other roots. Do not enter them as names or liturgical compounds.

***Tallo*** is dock pronunciation of *the Thallow*, and ***Mait*** is *Maieth* with the thorn tired, the form careful prayer restores to *Maieth*. Neither is a charter form, and neither is a new everyday name beside Turning Tree.

There is no graft on Kumbaan. *Their cutting* is a mainland object said in hill speech, so do not seat wood in the valley.

The second-name rule of 2026-09-28 and 2026-09-29 still stands. The living cast was named on the person notes in L.4. Do not rename them here. Textbook rows stay textbook.
