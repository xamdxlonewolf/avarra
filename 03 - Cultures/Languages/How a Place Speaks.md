# How a Place Speaks

> **How to use this.** This is a mouth, for play. The sounds and the old written names stay in [[The Old Tongue]]. Person-names and the second name stay in [[Naming People in the Turning]]. Here is what a place says out loud for six ordinary things, and four tones a player can actually keep up for a scene. Speak the common word. A rite, a charter, or a scholar's copy still carries the long written name.

A traveller can say **Turning Tree**, **the Mother**, **a hug**, **a colour**, **a tree-warden**, and **a graft**, and be understood on every mainland road. The table below is what the place says when it is being itself. The core keeps the thorn. Waiting speech tires it. A dock lets it go. [[The Sundering Isle|Kumbaan]] speaks in its own mouth. The old liturgical tongue is a mainland box, and the hill was never in it.

## What they call the six things

Common speech only. These handles are already in the mouth, worn by the drift. The long written compounds stay on paper.

| | **Conservative** — pious core | **Worn** — waiting lands, live front | **Eroded** — the docks | **Kumbaan** — the hill |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **the Tree** | **the Mother's Hand** | **the Thallow** | **Tallo** *(TAL-oh)* | **the far tree** |
| **the Mother** | **Maieth** *(MY-eth)* | **Mait** *(MAYT)* | **the old rite** | **the loud story** |
| **a hug** | **a keeping** | **a hug** | **a clear** | **the place** |
| **a colour** | **the colour** | **the gift** | **the fall** | **a test** |
| **a warden** | **the Reckoned Hands** | **Hands-folk** | **a tree-tender** | **the loud guest** |
| **a graft** | **a Hand carried in** | **a carried Thallow** | **a carrying** | **their cutting** |

**The core.** A neighbour in [[Maiethlir]] or the Tallied Crown says **Maieth** in the street, the way another town says *the Mother*. The polity's own street-name is still **the Tallied Crown**. **The Mother's Hand** is the Tree that can already speak a colour. Wood that has been brought and set, and cannot yet Turn, is **a Hand carried in**. At the rite they say **a keeping**. The Tallied Crown's desk has another word for that child, and the square leaves it on the desk. [[Maiethvael]] shares **Maieth** and **the Mother's Hand**, and calls the warden a **tree-warden**. **The Reckoned Hands** is the crown's word.

**Waiting speech, and the live front.** Careful prayer puts the thorn back, and **Maieth** returns for as long as the prayer is open. The kitchen says **Mait**. **The Thallow** is the Tree. A colour is **the gift**; a no-colour is still **a hug**, and they do not dress it as a prize. Young wood on the road is **a carried Thallow**. On the live front, until it can Turn, people say **the wood**.

**The docks.** **Tallo** is how a quay says *the Thallow* once the thorn is gone. Say it on the water. Leave it off the charter. The written name stays the Turning Tree, or the old liturgical name when the paper is a rite. They call her **the old rite**, and they call a no-colour **a clear** — the child came up clear, free to be hired. A warden is **a tree-tender**. A scion in the hold is **a carrying**. [[Trenledd]] is this mouth with a list: the gate asks the house, and the gate will not put the thorn back to be polite.

**The hill.** [[Ndenjoo]] has a table. It has no Tree and no graft, and it does not grow a word that would put one in the valley. **The far tree** and **their cutting** are mainland things, said of someone else's country. **The loud story** is what they call the Mother when a guest brings her up: a tale about being chosen, told at noon-volume. **The place** is what they offer where a mainlander expects a hug. **A test** is a colour, and they will not use one on a guest. A warden who climbs the hill is **the loud guest**, and is still fed.

## Tones a player can use

Four tones. Sentence length, the thing they say again, and the thing they will not say while the room is mixed. Each mainland drift still has one phrase it says whole. The hill has its own. Nobody stops to translate them.

### Devout core

[[Threnmaieth]], [[Maiethlir]], the pious square. Also the Inner Close when the room is a household rather than a file.

**Length.** Two sentences. The first finishes. The second puts **the Mother's Hand** or **Maieth** back in, if the first left them on the floor. Fragments sound like the dock, and they will hear it.

**They repeat.** **Maieth.** **The Mother's Hand.** Drop either, and the next sentence restores it.

**They will not say in company.** *Tallo.* *The old rite.* *A clear.* They will not say *unmarked* while the rite is still open — that word belongs to the desk. They will not lead with a house-name to a neighbour. They will not joke a colour.

> "The Mother's Hand kept her. We say Maieth before the square moves. Lei thulen." *(lay THOO-len)*

### Dock

[[Lestrand]], [[Orentel]], the Ledger Coast quays. [[Trenledd]] when the list is what is speaking.

**Length.** About six words to a breath. Another breath only if the number or the berth needs it. Then the phrase, and the quay lets it go.

**They repeat.** **Clear.** The number. The berth. They repeat the last hard fact, not a person's feelings about it.

**They will not say in company.** *Maieth.* *The Mother's Hand.* They will not restore a thorn to sound devout. They will not call a clear a sadness. They will not ask *what house?* while the paper is shut. While a filing house has the paper open, they will — house first, then the given name — and they still will not hymn it.

> "Clear. Berth five. The fall's on Tallo. Ka doven." *(kah DOV-en)*

### Waiting, or the live front

One tone, two repeats. Waiting towns ([[Polity Archetypes|the Waiting Lands]], [[Eolvaeth]]) repeat the week that has not come. The live front ([[Harrow's Green]], a young graft, a neighbour's canopy) repeats the wood.

**Length.** One sentence, and it does not close the week. Twelve words is already long. They reach for the old word and let the thorn thin.

**They repeat.** Waiting: **not this week.** The live front: **the wood**, then **the neighbour** if the wood will not speak.

**They will not say in company.** They will not promise the week. They will not say *the old rite*. They will not offer a house-name. They will not call the Thallow *Tallo* — that is the coast, and on this road it sounds like a slight. Prayer puts the thorn back. The kitchen keeps the tired form to itself.

> "Not this week, and the Thallow is still the Thallow. Len soret." *(len so-RET)*

Live front, same length, same phrase, the other repeat:

> "The wood first, and the neighbour if the wood won't. Len soret."

### Hill-hall

[[Ndenjoo]], and any attic that is still keeping the table. Night pace. The welcome is the whole theology they are willing to say with guests in the room.

**Length.** Three short sentences. A welcome. A pause. One practical thing. They do not explain a Tree inside the welcome.

**They repeat.** **Sit.** **The place.**

**They will not say in company.** They will not read **a test**. They will not point to a graft on the hill. They will not ask *of where?* or *what house?* as the price of a seat. They will not say *Maieth*. A mainland house-name, and a mainland *of-the-town*, are not the name they use at the table.

> "Sit. The place is set. Aman ndo." *(ah-MAHN ndoh)*

## What they ask you

The second name is locked. The rule, and the worked rows for what you say when asked, live in [[Naming People in the Turning#House-names — where a list outlives the witness]]. Those rows stay as they are.

| Who is asking | What you give |
|---|---|
| **A neighbour** | the given name |
| **A clerk in a list-land** | the house first, then the given name |
| **A witness-town, and you are a stranger** | the given name, and *of the place* |
| **Kumbaan** | the given name |

The list-lands are **Threnmaieth**, **Trenledd**, **the Inner Close**, and **a filing house while the paper is open**. Shut the paper, and the quay goes back to the witness way. A house-name is a local drift-word, often an old given name that stuck to a line. It is that mouth's word. It is not an English surname.

A witness-town asks *of where?* because the name that stands for you is the place that saw you. A given name with no place has not been stood for. Leading with a house sounds like a column, and that town does not keep the column.

A list-land asks *what house?* because the paper has to find you after the witness is gone. The house is the row. The given name alone is an unfinished line.

Kumbaan asks neither. The given name is the name. A mainland house, or a mainland *of-the-town*, is not your name on the hill.

## GM Notes
*(Stripped on player-safe export.)*

- **Voice only.** Deep grammar stays deferred on [[The Old Tongue]]. Do not grow a paradigm, a fourth mainland tongue, or a sentence grammar out of this note.
- **Coinage stays frozen.** *Lei thulen*, *len soret*, and *ka doven* are in-use phrases, one per drift. *Aman ndo* is the hill's phrase. It is not a Maiethren drift and not a root. Do not gloss them. Do not etymologize them into the root list. Do not enter them as names or liturgical compounds.
- ***Tallo*** is dock pronunciation of *the Thallow*. ***Mait*** is *Maieth* with the thorn tired; careful prayer restores *Maieth*. Neither is a charter form and neither is a new everyday name beside Turning Tree.
- **No graft on Kumbaan.** *Their cutting* is a mainland object in hill speech. Do not seat wood in the valley.
- **Second names.** The 2026-09-28 / 2026-09-29 rule stands. Do not rename the cast here. Story L.4 does that. Textbook rows stay textbook.
- **Do not** date the Tree, name the cutter, lock her limit, or add a power. World book untouched.
