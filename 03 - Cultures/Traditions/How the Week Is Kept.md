# How the Week Is Kept

> **The week, as a town spends it.** The engine, the colours, and the questions a warden gets asked stay on [[Turning Tree]]. The words a house will and will not say stay on the faith notes. Tones stay on [[How a Place Speaks]]. This note is the baskets, the bells, the berths, and the loaf.

[[The Reckoning of the Year|Turning-Week]] brackets the High Solstice, in Eolthael, the month of that solstice. Every child who has turned ten since the last Turning-Week either meets this one or misses it. A city nests the witness by hearth, among the people who already know the child, and a village stands as one circle. The hill does not keep the week.

## A devout square

In [[Maiethlir]] the Hand stands by the thaw. The Hand is this square's Tree, and on the first morning the stalls go up under it. There is bread, a chair-mender, ribbon for the people who still want ribbon, and a song that is also a vent, because a [[Far-Voiced]] is in the fair whether the fair planned on one or not.

The hearths stand to the bell in the morning, again in the afternoon, and again at eve. The slate on the warden-hearth names the street and the hour, and it does not name the colour. [[Rithim]] Speaks the word of the fall aloud even when the square already knows the common fall, and the next errand waits until the people who stood have stepped off the root.

A Watcher household stands with their child. When the square's line is said, they say their own, quietly, and Maieth is not in it. The words of that line live on [[The Watching]]. Towns that have shared this square a long time barely turn their heads, and a clerk does.

A follower of [[The Fair Hand]] stands at the edge with a book held shut. They see the Leaf-Fall as an agreement being signed, and they did not come to ask.

Before the fair eats, someone in from the road sets a bowl at the root and goes back to a stall. The square lets the bowl sit, and setting that bowl down is not the standing, not the moment a child is brought to the Tree.

A guest who keeps a hill-table will not walk a child down to the wood, though a neighbour may offer, and the week either has days left for that walk or it does not. The full quarrel is on [[The Open Table]]. A child who misses the Tree that week cannot be Given. In this square that quarrel is rarer than it is on a salt-stair, and the cost is the same when it comes.

The thaw is louder than it was in Hale-month. The square will not explain it ([[Maiethlir]]).

The rest of the week is the fair itself. Cousins who have not shared a loaf since last Eolthael share one now, and a cohort, the children who Turned the same summer, says so to one another. A [[Long-Lived]] who has stood at this stall long enough to know which parents will not be at next year's is still at that stall. The ache of that knowing is [[Daily Life]]. The stall is still a stall.

A tablet-clerk copies the slate of bells, which is the street and the hour. A clerk who copies the colours has started a different document, the one [[Turning Tree#Turning-Week in a city]] already describes.

## On a dock

At [[Orentel]] they say **the fall** on the crane, and they call the wood **Tallo**. If a charter is open, it still carries the long name. The standings follow the quay-gang, the people who already know the child, and they happen at the tide that gang can spare. The city is too big to be one circle, and the legal fact of that is already written. What it feels like is a gang that will not start the lift until their ten-year-old has been seen.

A **clear** gets the shout you use for a berth that just opened, and nobody on this water calls a clear a sadness.

Salt crusts the roots. The quay will not explain it ([[Orentel]]).

In the same days the book-hands settle a year's term where the gang can see. If a [[Bound]]'s oath-day falls in the week, the oath is recited and marked. The warden still Speaks. The quay calls the Mother **the old rite**, and it does not put the thorn, the *th* the devout keep in her name, back into the word to be polite. Ink moves between the warden-hearth and the book-room all week, and the two rooms collide over a tenth year, not over a barrel.

On a salt-stair, a host who keeps the table will not spend a child's week on a colour. A neighbour who did Turn will walk the child if the host will not, and [[The Open Table]] already has both sides of that landing. The week here smells of salt and pitch either way. Empty berths on the first quay are a different purchase, households who paid to walk a walk they do not need ([[Orentel]]). The city's own children stand at Tallo.

## In a waiting town

[[Eolvaeth]] fills before the week arrives. In Hale-month the camp-streets double, and when Eolthael comes the town faces the canopy it has, which some years speaks and some years does not.

If [[Vaethod]] will call a fall, the town stands small, and the gift-hall pot is open. A hug is not dressed as a prize, and a sermon may still pity the person who received one. That pity is the Warm face already named on [[Daily Life]]. The pot does not wait for the sermon to finish.

If the wood will not speak, the sentence in the street is **not this week**. A cohort may be sent toward a luckier Hand, a place whose wood is speaking. The town remembers which summers the children were sent on. A missed week still shuts the Given-door, and that law is on [[Turning Tree]]. What you feel then is packs on the track, and the people who stay to keep the pot.

The spring has a wet leaf, or too many, or none. The town will not explain it ([[Eolvaeth]]).

In those same days some houses walk the old doors, leave food, and do not ask for a colour. If the graft is sick, the warden and a door-keeper, someone who still walks those doors, can both be at the wood before noon, each sure the other is the guest ([[The Old Ways]]), and they still eat. A Watcher who walked this far keeps the night before in silence, and will not start history at a cut. A book in a pack belongs to a Settler, a follower of the Fair Hand, and that book is their business rather than this square's.

[[Harrow's Green]] is the other repeat of this tone, in the years when its wood will speak: the neighbour's week, and the canopy first. The mis-Speaking people cite there is [[The Wrong Green]], and that spring is not this town's spring.

## What the hill-hall does instead

[[Ndenjoo]] keeps the night mainland sailors want to call Yoltal, and the hall sets supper the way it sets a bright night. No child is stood up, no one asks a colour, a guest who brought a tenth-year is fed, and nobody offers the path to a Tree. There is no Tree in the valley, and the hall does not grow a word that would put one there.

Lamps face the table, and the place is already set. The loaf comes heel-first, and they will not explain the heel. The rest of the night is dishes and songs the hall already knows ([[Ndenjoo]] · [[The Open Table]]).

[[The Shore-Sitters|The shore]] does not come up the path to make a rite, and the sand stays the sand.
