# How the Week Is Kept

> **The week, as a town spends it.** The engine, the colours, and the questions a warden gets asked stay on [[Turning Tree]]. The words a house will and will not say stay on the faith notes. Tones stay on [[How a Place Speaks]]. This is baskets, bells, berths, and a loaf.

[[The Reckoning of the Year|Turning-Week]] brackets the High Solstice, in Eolthael. Every child who has turned ten since the last one meets the week or misses it. A city nests the witness by hearth. A village is one circle. The hill does not keep the week.

## A devout square

[[Maiethlir]]. The Hand stands by the thaw. On the first morning the stalls go up under it: bread, a chair-mender, ribbon for people who still want ribbon, a song that is also a vent because a Far-Voiced is in the fair whether the fair planned them.

Hearths stand by the bell. Morning, afternoon, eve. The slate on the warden-hearth names the street and the hour. It does not name the colour. [[Rithim]] Speaks. The square often knows the common fall already. The word is still said aloud, and the next errand waits until they have stepped off the root.

A Watcher household stands with their child. When the square's line is said, they say their own, quietly, and Maieth is not in it. The words live on [[The Watching]]. Towns that have shared this square a long time barely turn their heads. A clerk does.

A person at the edge holds a book shut. They watched a signing, by their lights. They did not come to ask.

Before the fair eats, someone from the road sets a bowl at the root and goes back to the stall. The square lets the bowl sit. It is not the standing.

A guest who keeps a hill-table will not walk a child down to the wood. A neighbour may offer. The week has days left, or it does not. The full quarrel is the mainland lintel on [[The Open Table]]. In this square it is rarer than on a salt-stair, and it spends a life the same way when it comes.

The thaw is louder than it was in Hale-month. The square will not explain it ([[Maiethlir]]).

The rest of the week is the fair. Cousins who have not shared a loaf since last Eolthael. A cohort saying they Turned the same summer. A Long-Lived who has stood at this stall long enough to know which parents will not be at next year's. The ache of that knowing is [[Daily Life]]. The stall is still a stall.

A tablet-clerk copies the slate of bells. A clerk who copies colours has started a different document ([[Turning Tree#Turning-Week in a city]]).

## On a dock

[[Orentel]]. They say **the fall** on the crane and **Tallo** for the wood. A charter, if one is open, still has the long name. Standings follow the quay-gang: the people who already know the child, at the tide that gang can spare. The city is too big to be one circle. The legal fact of that is already written. The feel of it is a gang that will not start the lift until their ten-year-old has been seen.

A **clear** gets the shout you use for a berth that just opened. Nobody on this water calls it a sadness.

Salt crusts the roots. The quay will not explain it ([[Orentel]]).

In the same days, book-hands settle where the gang can see. A year's term closed. A Bound's oath-day, if one falls in the week, recited and marked. The warden still Speaks. The quay calls her **the old rite** and does not put the thorn back to be polite. Ink moves between the warden-hearth and the book-room all week. They collide over a tenth year, not over a barrel.

On a salt-stair, a host who keeps the table will not spend a child's week on a colour. A neighbour who did Turn will walk the child if the host will not. [[The Open Table]] already has the two grammars on that landing. The week here smells of salt and pitch either way. Empty berths on the first quay are a different purchase: households who paid to walk a walk they do not need ([[Orentel]]). The city's own children stand at Tallo.

## In a waiting town

[[Eolvaeth]]. Hale-month the camp-streets double. Eolthael they face the canopy they have, which some years speaks and some years does not.

If [[Vaethod]] will call a fall, the town stands small. The gift-hall pot is open. A hug is not dressed as a prize, and a sermon may still pity one. The pity is the Warm face on [[Daily Life]]. The pot does not wait for the sermon to finish.

If the wood will not speak, the sentence in the street is **not this week**. A cohort may be sent toward a luckier Hand. The town remembers which summers were sends. A missed week still shuts the Given-door. That law is on [[Turning Tree]]. The feel is packs on the track, and the people who stay to keep the pot.

The spring has a wet leaf, or too many, or none. The town will not explain it ([[Eolvaeth]]).

In those same days some houses walk the old doors, leave food, and do not ask for a colour. If the graft is sick, the warden and a door-keeper can both be at the wood before noon, each sure the other is the guest ([[The Old Ways]]). They still eat. A Watcher who walked this far keeps the night before in silence, and will not start history at a cut. A book in a pack is a Settler's business. It is not the square.

[[Harrow's Green]] is the other repeat of this tone, when its wood will speak: the neighbour's week, the canopy first. The cited mis-Speaking there is [[The Wrong Green]]. It is not this town's spring.

## What the hill-hall does instead

[[Ndenjoo]], on the night mainland sailors want to call Yoltal. The hall sets supper the way it sets a bright night. No child is stood up. No one asks a colour. A guest who brought a tenth-year is fed. Nobody offers the path to a Tree. There is no Tree in the valley, and the hall does not grow a word that would put one there.

Lamps face the table. The place is already set. The loaf comes heel-first, and the rest of the night is dishes and songs the hall already knows ([[Ndenjoo]] · [[The Open Table]]).

They will not explain the heel.

[[The Shore-Sitters|The shore]] does not come up the path to make a rite. The sand stays the sand.
