# Naming People in the Turning

> **How to use this.** To name a person, start with the place that raised them, and then ask whether they were born Kitsune, Selkie, Tengu, or Yumboe. Those four are the custom Kinds, ancestries with a hearth-register of their own, and a Kind is simply the ancestry someone is born into. Stock ancestries have no private tongue, so a human neighbour draws a given name from the town, not from a hearth. If the person is one of the four, pick the given name from that Kind's list. If not, pick it from the local Maiethren drift, the old tongue as that place wears it on the street. Then add the **second name that place uses**, either a byname or a house-name, and never a name taken from the Kind. The first time you write the name, mark the stressed syllable in capitals (CAPS). The lists and the recipes are below. One rule governs them:

> ## **A person is named by the place that raised them. A custom Kind may also keep a hearth-name.**
> The four custom Kinds keep a *register*, a sound of their own for given names, for kin-names, and for talk inside the group, and they still wear the local [[The Old Tongue|Maiethren drift]] once they are out on the street. A hearth-name is the given name drawn from that register, the name a person answers to inside the hearth. You can often hear both homes in one introduction. The place that did the raising also decides whether the second name is a byname or a house-name.

## The two-name pattern

Most people have two names, and the civic line uses only those two.

The first is **a given name**, chosen by the family. Custom-Kind families often draw it from the **hearth-register** below, and everyone else draws it from the local Maiethren drift.

The second is **the second name the raising-place uses**, either a **byname** or a **house-name**. The civic name carries one of those, not both, and it is never a Kind-name.

### Bynames — where people are witnessed

A byname is how a place names you when someone there saw you. You are *of a town*, *of a Tree*, *called such for a deed*, or *of the house you actually sleep in* when that house is not itself a written list. This is the habit anywhere a witness still does the naming and a roll does not: on the road, on the live front, in the waiting towns, and under every power that refuses to write persons down.

A fox-born broker on a Ledger Coast quay that does not file people might be **Raki of Hallowquay**, hearth-given and a Strand-byname, the given name from the fox register and the second name from the quay. A human neighbour at the same desk might be **Valen of Hallowquay**. They share the street and the civic shape of the name, and the first sound is what tells you which home the given name came from.

### House-names — where a list outlives the witness

Some places keep an inherited house-name in the slot where our world keeps a surname, and the children wear it. A neighbour says the given name, while a clerk says the **house first**, then the given name. The house-word is still from that place's drift, often an old given name that stuck to a line. It is not an English family name, and it is not a town.

You do not hand over every piece you own. The place that asks decides which piece, and in which order.

A neighbour gets the given name. A clerk in a list-land, a place that writes people down so it can find them after the witness is gone, gets the **house first, then the given name**. That order is the file, and a given name alone is an incomplete row. A witness-town gets the given name, and *of the place* if you are a stranger, and leading with a house-name there sounds filed, or cold. [[The Sundering Isle|Kumbaan]] gets the given name, and a mainland house, or a mainland *of-the-town*, is not your name there.

A marriage in a list-land chooses which house the new household is written under, and the children wear that house. Refusing the writing is a fight with the clerk, not a blank where the surname should be. In [[Trenledd]], where the gate already says the house first, the column may file you under the house that pays, including one you dislike. In the Inner Close, where the line is said when rank is in the room, the line can cast you out and keep the name on you anyway. *Closed* on a roster is that rank, not a second name.

Harrow's will not use a roll-name unless it means to be cold. [[Maiethlir]], which already keeps a house when it has one, will invent a house for a walker who stays, and the walker will feel filed.

A mixed mainland child answers to **one register** if either side is custom, usually the kitchen they spent the summer in, or else the name that stuck, and still takes the **second name of the place that raised them**, byname or house-name. A Selkie-and-Tengu child might be *Lavo of Highgate*. The perch is in that byname, a perch or a pass rather than a harbour, and the child does not wear two hearth-registers at once. See [[Kinds of the Turning#Mixed-Kind children]]. A [[Yumboe]] keeps the one register and is never a mix.

[[Yumboe|Yumboes]] are the exception you can hear. They are too rare on the mainland to have grown a local byname-layer, so they keep the one register, and that is why a Yumboe introduction *sounds foreign the instant it's spoken*.

## The common-tongue / Maiethren person-register

Everyday person-names are worn [[The Old Tongue]]. They favour liquids and nasals, the *l* and *r* and the *m* and *n*, a soft *th*, and long vowels. A drift is one regular way that sound has worn, and there are three of them, the same three that institution-names travel. You push a person-name through the drift of the place, and you do not invent a fourth.

| Drift | Place | What happens to a person-name | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Conservative** | [[Polity Archetypes|Threnmaieth]], pious [[Maiethorn]] | *th* and long vowels kept | **Maethaem** (MAY-thaym), **Reimaethe** (RYE-may-theh), **Thilim** (THIL-im), **Rithnali** (rith-NAH-lee), **Valein** (vah-LAYN) |
| **Worn** | [[Polity Archetypes|Vaethorn]], [[Heskoren]] folk-speech | *th* softens; endings simplify | **Nathdo** (NATH-doh), **Saelid** (SAY-lid), **Tora**, **Seine** (SAY-neh), **Nethiro** (neth-EE-ro) |
| **Eroded** | [[Polity Archetypes|Lestrand]], [[Strandoren]] docks | *th* → t/d or drops; clipped | **Valen**, **Tilim**, **Mataero** (mah-TAY-ro), **Ledan**, **Sorel** |

**Stress** is the syllable you mark in capitals (CAPS) the first time a name is written. Stress follows the Old Tongue rule in [[The Old Tongue]]: a compound, a name you can still hear as two roots, stresses its first root.

There are three drifts and no fourth human language. This *is* the person-language of the Tree-touched world. A named power, one of the lands kept in [[Powers of the Turning]], uses the same roots and the drift that matches how it stands. It does not get a new language unless it refused the Tree. [[Vaelhesk]] keeps the older *vael* and the frontier *hesk-*. [[The Sundering Isle|Kumbaan]] already has its own register.

## The four hearth-registers

Each custom Kind's register is a **naming-language**: a sound, a short recipe, and a list of names, rather than a grammar. The own-names, the names the peoples use for themselves, are settled, and the common-tongue names outsiders say, [[Kitsune]], [[Selkie]], [[Tengu]], and [[Yumboe]], stay.

In the tables, C means a consonant and V means a vowel, so CV is a consonant followed by a vowel, and CVC closes on another consonant. F is the consonant allowed to end that syllable, and the table names which ones. A prenasal is a nasal spoken on the front of a stop, the *mb*, *nd*, *nj*, and *nk* sounds. A diphthong is two vowels spoken as one glide, such as *ai*, *ea*, and *oa*. An onset is the consonant or cluster that begins a word. A sound in the forbidden row already belongs to another home, and the register does not take it.

### [[Kitsune]] — *Kusawe* (koo-SAH-way)

**Feel:** light, slipping, a little sibilant, which is to say full of *s* and *sh* — names that slip round the corner of a sentence.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Consonants** | k s sh n r m t w y f h |
| **Vowels** | a i u e o ai |
| **Syllables** | CV · CVF (F = n/m/r) · 2–3 syllables |
| **Forbidden** | *th* (Maiethren sacred), prenasals *mb/nd/nj* (Yumboe), *kr/gr* (Tengu) |

**Given names:** Raki, Nenyu, Wishi, Kiraye, Suti, Hatu, Ranyu, Sinro, Foryo, Heka, Sofan.

A Kitsune raised devout in Threnmaieth, where the conservative drift keeps the *th* and a clerk says the house first, may still be *Raki* at home and *Maethaem, Raki* on the roll. The hearth-name is the one they answer to when someone is trying to pin them down; they consider this funny.

### [[Selkie]] — *Sakoa* (sah-KOH-ah)

**Feel:** liquid, rounded, coastal — names you can say with cold air in your mouth.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Consonants** | l r n m s v f d b k w |
| **Vowels** | o u a ea oa i e |
| **Syllables** | CV · CVC · 2–3 syllables |
| **Forbidden** | *th*, *sh*, *kr/gr*, prenasals |

**Given names:** Lavo, Fesa, Moka, Dovo, Neson, Foaro, Veakea, Didel, Roadoa, Woanun, Laknea.

The day a child is first given the run of their own skin is when the hearth-name is spoken *to the water*. The civic second name comes later, from the town that watched them Turn: a byname, or a house-name if that town writes people down. Two homes, two names; they do not consider this a conflict.

### [[Tengu]] — *Gonan* (GO-nan)

**Feel:** clipped, windy, high-country. The names are shorter, and they start hard, on a single consonant or on an onset such as *kr*, *gr*, and *hr*, said as if the wind might take the end. It does not sound like Kusawe, because this is ridge-speech rather than court-speech.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Consonants** | k t g n r h s b d · onsets kr gr hr |
| **Vowels** | a o u e *(few i — that vowel lives in Kusawe)* |
| **Syllables** | CVC · CV · 1–3 syllables, prefer 2 |
| **Forbidden** | *th*, *sh*, prenasals, long *ai/ea/oa* diphthongs |

**Given names:** Teku, Kukra, Gobak, Hosen, Kotak, Buka, Rekren, Kuhos, Hrarat, Gonan *(Gonan is also the people-name; reuse as a given name is a hearth-honor, like naming a child after the ridge).*

A Tengu raised where people are witnessed often takes a **perch** or a **pass** as the byname (*Teku of Highgate*, *Hosen of the White Stair*), not a harbour. Raised on a roll, they wear the house-name like anyone else; the perch stays the name the ridge uses.

### [[Yumboe]] — register in the ancestry note

The full register lives in the ancestry note. It uses open vowels and the prenasal sounds *mb*, *nd*, *nk*, and *nj*. The own-name, the name they use for themselves, is **Bakhna Rakhna**, and the homeland is **Kumbaan**.

**Added given names** (same sounds as the ancestry note's register): Sambiyo, Lunji, Yaaba, Saalo, Mbela, Soonke, Njili, Yendo, Waandi, Koriwaa.

The isle's place-names are in [[Yumboe#Naming & tongue]].

## Collision rules (keep the cast distinct)

Before you lock a name for a player character or anyone else at the table, say it next to the names already in the room.

Do not seat *Raki* and *Ranyu* in the same party, and do not seat *Teku* and *Kukra* as twins unless they *are* twins. The shared initial and the shared cadence will make the table hear one person twice.

A Selkie named *Wishi*, or a Tengu named *Maethaem*, is a tell. It says the person was raised away, or adopted, or is passing. Use that on purpose, or do not use it at all.
