# A Buyer for the Knife

A buyer with a purse of coin is on the Near Mile, and the week they are buying is not the keystone and not [[The Opening]]. The cutter, the hand that would be on the first knife, stays unpicked.

## What the table can play

The buyer wants a Near-Mile house to say that their ribboned kitchen knife is the nameless knife, will not give a house-name, and wants a founder they can carry. The house knows which summer the scorch belongs to, and that summer was the Closing. The house does not turn out for that summer, and they will not sell the knife as the first copy.

The party can be asked to persuade the house, to expose the buyer, to forge a provenance, or to walk away. Forging a provenance is a lie the house can spot, because the summer is local memory, something the house already lived, and not a cipher a stranger can solve.

## What is true

[[The Ribboned Knife]] is a kitchen knife, the sort a house uses, and [[The One Knife]] has never been produced, so no sale finishes the search. [[The First Cut]] still has five attributions and no hand on the handle, and [[Heroes and Villains]] still leaves the cutter unpicked.

## Where to stop

Do not let the coin name a cutter, and do not plant the one knife in the loft, the wall, or the Close. Do not braid this week with [[The Spent Leaf]] or with Harrow's Green. The reason a serious asker will not name a house is already on [[The One Knife]], and it is the only reason this note allows: a house would pick a hand. Do not add a second reason.

## Player misreadings

Players will want the ribbon in front of them to be the first knife, or they will decide the buyer already knows the hand and is only testing the party, or they will make the Low Wall the knife's first house. None of those is what the week contains.
