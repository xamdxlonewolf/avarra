# The Meaning of Avarra

> **GM ONLY.** The flagship reveal chain, decoded by the *players* through pattern recognition — never delivered by an NPC as exposition.

## The secret (GM truth)

*Avarra* is the true name of the unified reality that fractured into [[The Fracture|Avera and Vara]]. It is canonically correct and known to almost no one. Each half's **everyday word for "the world" is an unrecognized descendant** of it — Averan *Avera* (ah-VEH-rah) and Varan *Vara* (VAH-rah), both meaning simply "the world / home." Neither people knows its word is a *name*, let alone that the other holds a sibling of it. See [[Names & Descent]].

## How it surfaces (clues, in order)

The word appears constantly and untranslated long before it means anything — a god, a dead empire, the antiquarian guild's name, a root on ruins scholars argue over. The trail runs through **inscriptions**, decoded by phonetics:

1. An old inscription reads **AVARRA**. The party assumes it's just an archaic name for *Avera* — reasonable.
2. A Varan inscription reads **VARA**. Someone says "that's obviously their word for their world" — also reasonable, and treated as unrelated.
3. An older inscription pairs **AVARRA** with a gloss: *the world*. The floor drops out: *Avera* and *Vara* aren't two words — they're two surviving descendants of **one** word. Neither civilization named its world; both inherited a broken piece of the name of the world before.

The meaning-ladder underneath: *"Avarra was whole" → "When Avarra divided, we remained" → "We are Avarra"* — not the ancients, not an empire, not a planet, but the name reality had when it was one thing.

## Player misreadings (what they'll think first)

- "Avarra" = the lost ancient civilization / empire.
- *Avera* and *Vara* are unrelated words in two different languages — each just "the world" to its own people, no shared root suspected.
- Each people assumes its own word is *generic* ("why wouldn't the world be called Avera?"), never a proper noun — so no one thinks to compare them.

## The set-piece (first contact)

An NPC on the other side asks what the party calls *their* world. The party answers. The NPC pauses — *"That's what we call ours."* The players understand before the NPC does.

## Do-not-violate

The title moves setting name → archaeological mystery → name of the lost whole → **what the players choose to become**. The ending creates a *new* Avarra that contains difference; it does **not** restore the old one.
