# The Cost

> **GM ONLY — the core secret.** Why the Empire works. This note fixes the *direction* and the *design constraint*; the exact mechanism is deliberately still open (see below) and will be built with `moral-parallax`. Never surfaced as exposition — revealed only through the iceberg. See [[The Premise]] and [[Themes]].

## The direction (chosen): a fusion

The stability is bought two ways at once, and the two halves reinforce each other:

1. **A small, ongoing, unbearable act.** For centuries the Empire has quietly paid a price in *people* — a tiny number, chosen and "spent" so that the vast majority can live in peace, fairness, and plenty. (Utilitarian bookkeeping at the scale of a civilization: a rounding error of lives for the safety of millions.)
2. **…that also holds something worse at bay.** The act is not *only* a price for good governance. It is load-bearing against a **returning catastrophe** — something the Empire's founding generation faced, survived, and has been keeping at the door ever since. The sacrifice is the lock on that door.

Neither half alone is enough. Sacrifice-the-few alone risks reading as mere cruelty-with-good-PR; a dam-against-the-dark alone risks "the Empire was right all along." **Fused, they hold the parallax:** the cost is genuinely monstrous *and* the benefit is genuinely real *and* the danger is genuinely uncertain.

## The knife (what keeps both sides right)

**No one alive has seen the threat.** It has been held back so long, so well, that whether it is *still real* — or ever was as terrible as the founding claims — cannot be known from inside the peace. So:

- **The Empire's defenders are right:** stop paying, and you may unleash a horror no one remembers how to survive. Every generation that paid was proven "right" by the fact that nothing came.
- **The rebels are right:** you are murdering people, generation after generation, to ward off a danger you *cannot even confirm exists* — and calling it peace.

The players will get a **complete answer to "what is the cost,"** a **sufficient answer to "what is the threat,"** and — by design — **no certain answer to "is it still necessary."** That last question is not a fact to be uncovered; it is the *choice*, and it belongs to the players. (Compare the ending contract: the finale is a decision *with a price*, never a victory that erases the price.)

## The euphemism (candidate, not locked)

The setting needs a warm word that lets good people not-look — spoken as a blessing by those who mean well. Strong candidate: **"reconciliation"** — to be *reconciled* is the gentle civic term for being the one who pays. ("It's why we've had such peace since.") Evocative and load-bearing for the "gentle euphemism" motif in [[Themes]]; confirm or replace when we name things.

## Design constraint (inherited, do not violate)

Everything about the mechanism must pass [[The Premise|the design contract]]:

> *Could a thoughtful, decent person, knowing the full truth, still choose to keep it running?*

Keep the victims **few**; the benefit **vast and real**; the threat **genuinely uncertain**; the selection **not obviously unjust** (not "the Empire just kills its enemies" — that fails the test instantly). The most dangerous failure mode is anything that lets the players (or the GM) settle into "so they're evil after all." Guard against it.

## Deliberately open (build next, with `moral-parallax`)

- **The mechanism:** who is chosen and how; by what body/office; is it random, volunteered, hereditary, drawn by lot, chosen for a quality? *(Selection design is where "still defensible vs. monstrous" is won or lost.)*
- **The threat:** what is being held back — and is the ambiguity about its reality maintained *how*?
- **Magic's role:** is the act ritual/magical, bureaucratic, or both? How does bounded Daggerheart magic relate?
- **What the rebellion knows** and how they learned it.
- **What stays never-explained** (the deliberate cosmic-mystery reserve, if any) vs. fully answered.
