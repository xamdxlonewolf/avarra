# Is the Leaf-Mother Real

> **The keystone GM secret (LOCKED 2026-08-19).** This is the canon-layer answer — *what is actually true*, regardless of what anyone in the world believes. Epic 1 (the Turning Tree & the Leaf-Mother religion) and the setting's theme all aim at this. It is decided, not fleshed: the one-line truth below is load-bearing; the texture grows around it. **Do not re-litigate.**

## The Secret (GM truth)

**Yes. The Leaf-Mother is real, and she is benevolent.**

- She **exists** — a genuine, present power, not a metaphor and not a dead god whose name survived her.
- The **Turning Trees are genuinely her hands.** The town scions are not a natural phenomenon that people *read* as divine; they really are how she reaches into the world. When a Tree chooses, *she* is choosing.
- The **Conditions are real gifts.** The Leaf-Fall is an act of love: she looks at a child and gives them a becoming that the world will need and that they can carry. The Tithe is the honest weight of a gift, not a hidden price or a trap.
- **The Kept are spared, not slighted.** A hug and no colour is a deliberate, kind choice — some people are given the gift of being left whole. It is not a failure of the ceremony, not her overlooking them, and not a lesser blessing.
- **The Struck are still hers.** A becoming that arrives through a threshold later in life (a drowning, an oath, a death survived) is not a Condition that "escaped her" — she meets people at those doors too. The ceremony is her ordinary hand; the threshold is her catching someone who fell.

So: **the believers are right about the big things.** She is real, she is kind, the gifts are gifts. The skeptics are sincere and thoughtful and *wrong* — but the world is built so that being wrong about this costs them nothing day to day (see *Player Misreadings*). The faith is, underneath everything, simply **true**.

> **Why this answer (design intent).** The user chose the warm reading on purpose. This is a **~5%-scary** world, not a cosmic-horror one; the dread lives in the *Tithes* and in individual stories, not in a rotten foundation. Making the divine layer genuinely benevolent means the setting's tension comes from *living well with a gift* rather than from a betrayal waiting to be uncovered. It also keeps the theological schism (below) *poignant* rather than *ironic*: the skeptics aren't fools being farmed, they're good people reasoning carefully from incomplete evidence about a kindness they can't see the source of.

## What is deliberately still open (belief, not cosmology)

Locking "she is real and kind" does **not** flatten the mystery. The world keeps her as **belief, not confirmed cosmology** *in-fiction* — no one can *prove* her, revelation is quiet, and honest skeptics remain honest. These stay open on purpose, as live design space for later epics:

- **What she is** (a goddess? the Trees' shared soul? something older wearing a mother's face for our comfort?) — undecided; the *maternal, benevolent intent* is what's locked, not her metaphysical species.
- **What she wants**, beyond giving good gifts — undecided. She is kind; her larger purpose (if any) is unwritten.
- **Where she is / whether she can be reached** — undecided. Revelation is deliberately quiet; there is no throne to visit.
- **Whether she is the only such power** — **explicitly open.** Per the user (2026-08-19), the Leaf-Mother is *one faith among several*; other gods and religions get built in the religion-as-a-whole pass. Locking her as real does not make her exclusive.

## How It Surfaces (clues, in order)

Because she is quiet and genuinely kind, revelation is a slow accretion of *coherence*, not a single gotcha. Rough order of escalating certainty:

1. **The gifts fit too well.** Over a lifetime, a town notices that the Conditions Given tend to be the ones that town will *need* — as if something is looking ahead. Easy to dismiss as hindsight and survivorship bias (and the skeptics do).
2. **The Kept are cared for, not merely skipped.** Kept people report the ceremony felt like a *choice made for them*, not an absence. Hard to falsify; easy to call sentiment.
3. **A Tree does something a phenomenon shouldn't.** Rare, quiet moments where a scion acts with apparent *intent* (withholds a colour that would have doomed a child; a scion "reaches" for a specific person). Never flashy — always deniable.
4. **The Struck are met.** Testimony from the Struck that the threshold felt *received*, as if someone were on the other side of the door. The strongest experiential evidence; still only testimony.
5. **The confirming beat (GM's to place).** If the campaign ever truly confirms her, it should land as *warmth and relief*, not horror — the answer to "is anyone there?" is *yes, and she meant it kindly.* Reserve this; the setting works better with it withheld.

## Player Misreadings (what they'll think first)

- **"It's just what the Trees do."** The skeptic's reading — biology/phenomenon, no mind behind it. Sincere, common, and *wrong*, but safe to hold.
- **"If she's real and kind, why the Tithes / why the Struck / why anyone Kept?"** The schism's engine. These have kind answers (a gift has honest weight; she meets people at every door; some are given wholeness) — but the answers aren't *provable*, so the questions stay live.
- **The dark reading** — that a benevolent front hides a harvest/betrayal. This setting **rejects** that (see design intent). A GM who wants horror can house-rule it, but it is off-canon here; the whole point of the locked answer is that the floor is *sound*.

## Consequences for build (what this constrains downstream)

- **Epic 1 notes** ([[Turning Tree]], the Leaf-Mother faith note) are player-facing and must **stay reverent-but-unproven**: present doctrine and skepticism even-handedly, never wink that she's confirmed. Each must carry `reveals: [leaf-mother-is-real]` so player-safe export can withhold anything that would confirm the keystone.
- **The Tithes** are honest costs, not divine leverage — this reinforces the existing per-Condition rule that a Tithe never seizes the PC (see [[The Premise]]).
- **The schism** stays a *good-faith* disagreement, which makes it better long-term fuel: no faction is secretly correct-and-evil.
