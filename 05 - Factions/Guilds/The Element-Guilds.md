# The Element-Guilds

Ember, Tide, Root, and Gale are four ordinary craft charters that share one safety rule. They keep separate fees, masters, and work, and they share a ticket standard because an idle [[The Answered|Answered]] person is a workshop hazard before they are a theological question. An Answered person is someone an element answered, and goes on answering, and the ticket is what lets that element be used in a crowded yard.

Everyday speech is **the element-guilds**, or the **Ember-house**, the **Tide-house**, the **Root-house**, and the **Gale-house**. The umbrella in a formal charter is **the Crae** (kray), from [[The Old Tongue|crae]], "craft; the working of an element." A single Answered person is *of the Crae*, and *of Ember* if you need the door. [[Polity Archetypes|Vaethorn]]'s second word is **gift-shops**, [[Polity Archetypes|Lestrand]]'s is **the craft-houses**, and [[Polity Archetypes|Threnmaieth]] files **the Reckoned Craes**. The common name is **the element-guilds**, and *the Crae* survives mainly in formal charters.

## Charter summary

- **Four doors, one ticket standard.** Ember is smiths, glass, and firefighting. Tide is hulls, dyers, and anything that wants water to behave. Root is masons, potters, and the carrying-earth a Road-hand may buy, living earth coaxed so it can travel. Gale is sails, mills, and instruments. An Ember-master does not set Tide's fees. The shop is what the charter covers, and journeymen sit under a door, which is not a census of every lamp-amber leaf in the city. The licence itself is [[Law and Citizenship#The safety-licence — how hazard is regulated (and by whom)]]. The mill-share, the hull-wrights, and the road-mend are [[Craft Fellowships]]. They keep the season of work, and they do not stamp this ticket.
- **Self-paying until you leave.** Earnest work, the element used at the craft and not in play, slakes the Tithe, which is the upkeep the element demands if it is left idle. The guild is therefore the upkeep. That is why the monopoly has teeth: a benched Answered person is not merely unemployed, because the element will speak whether or not the shop is paying them.
- **The paperwork is boring on purpose.** The boxes are an apprenticeship term, a supervised Reach, a crowded-yard endorsement, a night rotation, and a transfer. A Reach is the element answering through the hands. The danger enters when one of those boxes conceals grief, coercion, or a master protecting a fee.
- **One Condition does not make one temperament.** A Condition is the change a person acquires, and it does not hand the house a personality. An even-tempered Root potter and a short-fused Ember smith share a ticket standard and almost nothing else.

## Counter service

### If you meet one

What you notice first depends on the door: sweat, clay, salt, or chalk-dust, a shop-rule nailed up, and a ticket-stool that looks like any master's stool. You would hire them to build a hull. You would find the *ticket* if you asked *who may let an element speak in a crowded yard.*

They are useful the way a craft guild is useful. A late-medieval town already needed smiths and masons. The Condition made the guilds *necessary twice* — once for the work, once so the work does not let go.

### Four work floors

| Door | The work | The shop-rule you notice | If left idle |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ember** | smith, glass, fire-crew | they never let the last coal go dark | heat finds a place to be |
| **Tide** | hull, dye, anything wet | a cup of yesterday's water over the door | water finds a place to be |
| **Root** | mason, potter, carrying-earth | clay under the nails they will not wash on a work-day | stone and soil find a place to be |
| **Gale** | sail, mill, instrument | a window that is never fully shut | wind finds a place to be |

The "place to be" is already on [[The Answered]]. It is self-contained, and it is never a seized will, so the element does not take hold of someone else. The guild's job is to make sure that place is the shop, not the crowded hall. The coal, the cup, the clay, and the window are a different matter. Each door keeps one of those habits, and no one explains the habit to a guest. They will not say why.

### Weekly rota

The week runs through the shop, then the rotation, which is the fire-crew, the escort, and the watch that is also the Tithe, then the ticket-stool, and then the shop-rule no one explains to a guest.

When a master is pulled to Intake, the paying floor loses a Reach for a week. Ember hates this during a dry month, when the fire-crew can least spare a pair of hands. Tide hates this when a hull is mid-bend. That hatred is why Intake is the underdog.

### Refusals entered on the form

They will not switch an element to suit a labour shortage, because the shade of the leaf named it. They will not bench someone "for their own good" without earnest use waiting, because that is how a room lets go. They will not stamp a raw Struck person. That person goes to Intake. They will not become a fourth clergy: no colour for them to Speak, no graft for them to carry, no drowning-stone for them to keep. They will not export a Crae-college to Kumbaan.

## Ticket eligibility

The ticket is not by Kind. Kind is what a person was born, and Ember is not a Drakona chapter, nor is Gale a Tengu chapter. Drakona and Tengu are born peoples. A [[Tengu]] who is Gale-Answered is a common *lean*, a tendency the house sees often, not a gate that keeps other peoples out.

By practice first, you are of the house when the element answers your work and not the rafters.

The house does keep Condition leans, which are tendencies, and it does not keep Condition-gates.

## Regional charter variations

- **[[Polity Archetypes|Vaethorn]] (gift-shops).** The valley keeps one mixed yard, four tools, and a blessing-to-practice. The soft injustice is that you were answered, so you will spend the element as the valley asks, on the mill, the wall, or the fire-line. Idle is rare because the valley will not let a gift sit.
- **[[Polity Archetypes|Lestrand]] (the craft-houses).** The shops are deep, the tickets are posted, and the monopoly has teeth. You can be priced out of the *chair* and still need the work, which is how a person slides from self-paying to exposed overnight. The charter is money and safety in one paper.
- **[[Polity Archetypes|Threnmaieth]] (the Reckoned Craes).** The crown wants the element and the person on the same column of the roll. Workshops are channels the crown can hold. A benched Answered person is a withheld Reach, a use the crown has not released. The shop looks like a gift-works. The leash is the fire, the slip, the stone, and the wind.
- **[[The Sundering Isle|Kumbaan]].** A wrecked Tide-Answered person is a guest. The guild keeps no Tide-house on the sand.

## Exit grievance

Self-paying is an economist's miracle until you want to stop. The Tithe does not care that you buried a child, that the yard burned, or that you are tired of glass. Idle, the element speaks for itself.

So the guild's kindness, a shop and a crew and a rotation, is also the leash. Leaving is a Tithe-crisis, the upkeep failing because the work has stopped. The Warm, the devout way of speaking that Vaethorn uses, calls that vocation. The quay, which is how Lestrand talks about the same paper, calls that a contract. The crown, Threnmaieth, calls that a channel.

The Motherfaith says she answered. The shop-rule is people's work, written by the house and not by the leaf.

The complaint a person can bring is about the exit. The constraint is the element itself. The charter is local, and it has no authority to make another town take an idle journeyman.

## Accounts

The craft is the living. A person is self-paying when the vocation fits the element, and the watch-rotations and fire-crews are the Tithe and civic labor in the same night. Kept labor is the free generalist around the Reach: the strikers, the pullers, and the person who holds the mundane tool. Lestrand calls that person *free*. Vaethorn's word is *Waited*, and a Waited person is still useful. Intake is a lost Reach on the paying floor, and that loss is why the floor resents it.

## Posted rule

*What follows is a shop-rule from an Ember-house in a Lestrand glass-yard, nailed above the last coal. The back of the sheet is a Tide-house rule from the next quay, used as backing.*

> *The last coal stays. A Reach used in play is not a Reach used in earnest — the ticket will not have it, and the room will. If you cannot work, you will still work: the fire-crew, the night bellows, the watch. We do not bench. We do not rent a spark. The shed is not this door; raw hands go to the door that does not face the street. If you have already let go, this is still the house — come to the coal, not to the square. Gale will not take you. Tide will not take you. The coal will.*

The rule is a craft keeping its people steady, and it is a little fierce about it. "We do not bench" also means "you may not leave." The Tide-rule on the back says the same thing about a cup of water. The two houses did not copy each other.

## GM Notes

Two orders have already been moved off this body of work, and they stay moved. Do not have the Root guild walk a scion for a Road-hand. Do not plant a Tide-house on Kumbaan's sand.

These are four ordinary craft charters and one safety rule. They share a ticket standard because an idle Answered person is a workshop hazard before they are a theological question. They do not know what story they are in, so avoid four elemental paladin-chapters, fire-police, and Avatar-nations. The form stays a craft guild, four doors and one grammar. They want work that slakes the Tithe and a room that does not let go, and they do not want a client.

Do not clone the Orenhael's four offices, the Tree-Wardens' offices, onto four elements. There is no Ember-Speaker and no Tide-Road-hand. Shop-rules are local manners, not a college. Do not clone the Stillers' Grey-roster, the hall's hour, or the greens' gate onto this house. The injustice here is exit. The constraint is the element itself: a local charter, and no authority to make another town take your idle journeyman.

The distances that make the injustice comfortable are causal, the claim that the shop keeps them whole, and social, the claim that idleness is their failure. The engine is exchange, because every rotation is a Tithe paid as civic labor, and accumulation, because every year of "we do not bench" makes the exit harder. The comfort word is *earnest*.

The last coal, yesterday's water, unwashed clay, and an unshut window are practical. The guild does not explain them. Do not explain them as theology, and do not turn them into four relics. When you read the shop-rule, hear "we do not bench" as exit refused in the voice of care. The master cannot afford to see that it also means *you may not leave.*

There are no Kind-nations here. A Gale-Tengu is a lean, an Ember-Drakona is a lean, and leans are not chapters. There is no Kind-gate, no Kumbaan Crae, and no element-pope.

The umbrella name is *the Crae* (kray), from [[The Old Tongue|crae]], "craft; the working of an element." A single Answered person is *of the Crae*, and *of Ember* if you need the door. Vaethorn's second word is *gift-shops*, Lestrand's is *the craft-houses*, and Threnmaieth files *the Reckoned Craes*. The umbrella name is not settled.

The scenes that come out of this house are a benched Ember in a dry month, a Road-hand buying carrying-earth from a Root who wants to walk the scion, a hull that needs Tide and a Book-hand's stamp and only has one, a crown column that wants the element and the person, Intake pulling the only Tide-master off a mid-bend, and a player character who wants to leave the shop.
