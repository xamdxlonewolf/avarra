# Craft Fellowships

A mill, a hull, and a road each have people who keep them through a season, and those people are the fellowships. The work is the ordinary keeping of that mill, hull, or road, and it does not licence a body. The licences already written stay with the houses that write them: [[The Stillers]] stamp a licence they call a Gaze, [[The Element-Guilds]] stamp a licence for an element, [[The Intake]] catches a raw Struck person, someone newly in the change and not yet trained, before the town reaches for a rope, and [[The Hall-Keepers]] stamp a Voice-ticket. A mill-share, a hull-wright, and a road-mend do not stamp any of those.

A Gale-Answered miller can hold an element-guild ticket for the wind and still owe a share of grain. The ticket is the wind, the hazardous act of letting that element speak, and the share is the grain, which is the season of work. A Tide-Answered wright can hold a ticket for the water and still be the person who walks the hull. A Root-Answered mender can hold a ticket for the stone and still be the person with the shovel. In each of those yards the fellowship is the season of work, and the ticket is only the hazardous act.

You are of a fellowship when you have kept the thing through a season and someone of it will say so. There is no Seat set over the other towns, and no apprenticeship a crown confirms. There is no Kind-gate: the fellowship does not ask what Kind a person was born, and it does not keep a chapter of any born people. [[The Sundering Isle|Kumbaan]] does not keep these three. The isle has no race to share, no quay that classes a hull, and no Mile to mend.

## The mill-share

Everyday speech is **the mill-share**. A miller takes a share of the grain that comes through the mill, and the fellowship is the people who will say whose race this is, the mill-race that turns the stones, and whose share of that grain is owed.

At [[The Mill-hold]] the miller is not the person the square turns to, and the share is still taken. When the culvert is opened to keep water off the Hand, that town's Turning Tree, the race drops, and a mill-hand is already there with a count of lost turns. They do not Speak a colour, which would be naming a leaf in the square, and they do not carry a scion. That cart belongs to a [[The Tree-Wardens|Road-hand]], who carries living wood.

A Gale-house may coax the wind at a mill. If the person is Answered and lets the element speak, so that the wind answers the hands rather than the room, the coaxing is a ticket. The stones, the sacks, and the share belong to this fellowship even when nobody in the mill is Answered.

When the race is drawn down, the mill-share sets the stones and leaves one measure of last year's grain on the lip until the water comes back. They will not say why.

[[Polity Archetypes|Threnmaieth]] would like that measure on a roll of persons. The mill-share keeps it with the grain. [[The Walled Book]] gives turns of work to people who live inside its walls, and a turn at a mill is the Book's assignment of a person who lives there. It is not a share of the grain.

## The hull-wrights

Everyday speech is **the hull-wrights**. They know whether a hull will take water, and they look at the bend, the caulk, and the line to know it.

[[The Hinge Shore]] decides whether a hull is a pilgrim, a pot, or cargo, and [[The Book-Hands]] stamp the paper that records the class. A Tide-Answered person may work the bend under an element-guild ticket. The hull-wrights do not classify the cargo, do not set the [[The Hinge Hush|Hush-rate]], the charge on a crossing, and do not sell a witness who will say *I was there*. [[The Standing Trade]] sells that witness. This fellowship sells a hull that floats.

On the [[The Down-Bank]] there is no hull yet. A traveler who rehearses a hull-class at an inn is early, and a wright who starts the classification there has left the craft and begun the Hinge Shore's job. At [[Denlad]] a tied hull is a hull waiting out weather. The wright will not call it a hold and will not charge a house-share for the tying. At [[Orentel]] the fellowship sits under the crane. It is not [[The White Note House]], and it does not keep the berth.

[[Netstrand]] charters hulls. The charter is a crossing, and the wright is the person who will still walk the hull after the quote is written.

Before a new hull takes water, the hull-wrights walk it once empty, from stem to stern, and do not speak. They will not say why.

## The road-mend

Everyday speech is **the road-mend**. They fill a hole, clear a ford, and shore a culvert, and the stretches they keep are the Near Mile, the spur that stops at [[Nelath]], and the days along the Down-Bank.

They are not Road-hands. A Road-hand carries living wood, and a mender carries a shovel. They do not ask a traveler for a name, a last hearth, and who stands for them, because those questions belong to the watch, on [[The Watch and the Cohort]].

A person on a named walk is still vouched as a walker by the road, which means the road itself is what says they may walk it, and the mend is why the road is still there to walk. At Nelath the road ends at the boughs, so the mend stops with it. They do not cut the thorns back to make the spur go on. The town already decided where the road ends.

[[The Walled Book]] can give a turn on the road to a person who lives inside its walls. A mender on that page still knows the work, but the assignment is the Book's.

At the first ford after a town, they leave the shovel standing in the bank overnight after a mend. They will not say why.

## What they refuse

They will not issue a Gaze-ticket, an element-ticket, a Voice-ticket, or a catch, and they will not rent a week, refinance a Term, or sell a standing. A week is [[The Slide]]'s rental, a Term is the paper [[The Holding Desk]] refinances, and a standing is the witness [[The Standing Trade]] sells. They will not follow a [[The Taken-In|Taken-In]] person home. The lot is [[The Greens-Keepers]]' work, and the fellowship does not do it. They have no boss to send for, and there is no town where the fellowship is the government.

## GM Notes

A later note must not explain the measure on the lip, the silent walk, or the shovel in the bank. It must not turn any of those habits into a power, a relic, or a rite.

The element-guilds stay the ticket for an element let loose. Gale does not become the mill-share, Tide does not become the hull-wrights, and Root does not become the road-mend. Road-hands stay the graft, the carrying of living wood, and the Hall-Keepers stay the Voice-ticket. These fellowships do not grow a fourth body-licence, a Seat, a boss, or a Kind-chapter.
