# The Sworn

> The delta movement to win back Kessadi self-judgment — and the campaign's hardest mirror. Not terrorists, not liberators: decent people responding to a real wrong, who cannot agree among themselves what the wrong *is* or what would put it right. The Empire files them as "the delta sedition." Insofar as they agree on anything, the faithful invoke the old water-oath and call themselves **the Sworn** — the Sworn Cities, [[The Kessadi|Karesh]], swearing it a second time. **Names provisional** (the naming pass owns the final call).

## The wound they grow from

Two generations ago the [[The Kessadi|Karesh]] cities ruled themselves — their own coin, their own calendars, their own **water-judges** hearing all law from the same bench. The Empire kept the canals and abolished the bench; now justice rides in from outside on [[The Assize|the circuit]], fair and answerable to no delta house. To the Empire this is its proudest gift to [[Kessad]]. To the Sworn it is the precise organ of their identity, cut out and mailed back as a courtesy.

Every rebel can name a true loss. That is the point, and the trap: the losses are *not the same loss*, and the people who lost most are not the people the Empire treated worst.

## Why now

The Sworn are not an old grief flaring. They are a convergence — four pressures arriving at once, which is why the delta that grumbled for forty years is only now organizing:

- **The window is closing.** The new imperial trunk-road is finished. Garrisons that once took a season to reinforce can now be resupplied in days. Anyone who means to act by force must act *before* the road makes force pointless. The road built the peace and the deadline in the same stroke.
- **The last reconciliation.** The scribal service has just completed the final "reconciling of the delta rolls" — the post-siege census cleanup (see [[The Shape of the Empire|reconciling the rolls]]). Routine bookkeeping, to the Empire. To the Sworn it is the day the Karesh were formally *counted out* of existence, and the day the last unregistered ancestral water-shares were surveyed away.
- **The memory is dying.** The siege generation — the last people who remember being free rather than being told they were saved — is nearly gone. The old want to act before the country they remember dies with them. The young, who never saw the before, hold "Karesh" as a proud identity precisely because they never had to pay for it.
- **A fair verdict that wounded.** The spark is usually a *just* ruling — a circuit court correctly applying imperial law to confirm the road's seizure of a flood-mark, or to dispossess a beloved figure who was, in law, wrong. The Sworn are radicalized as often by the Empire's fairness as by its cruelty. (This is why the players' own body can light the fuse without doing anything unjust.)

## What they want (they do not agree)

There is no single aim, and the movement's survival depends on nobody saying so out loud. Its three wings want incompatible things and would turn on each other the morning after they won:

- **The Restorationists** — the water-judge houses and their clients. Aim: *restore the bench.* Bring back Kessadi self-judgment — which means bringing back the houses that held it. They are the movement's money, its respectability, and its organizing skeleton (the Phantom Assizes). Sincere, cultured, and quietly asking the delta to hand power back to the people who used to have it.
- **The Dispossessed** — commoners the imperial "improvement" left behind: families whose ancestral water-shares or fishing-grounds were rationalized off the Rolls as unregistered, and the siege-bereaved. Aim: *redress*, not restoration. They want their water and their dead honoured. They know the water-judges never helped them either, and their loyalty to the Restorationists is conditional to the point of brittle.
- **The hardliners** — the young, and the ones who took the imperial ladder and hit its ceiling (a Kessadi may rise, but never to govern his own province, never to the centre). Aim: *sovereignty, whatever replaces it.* They don't care about the bench; they want the Empire gone. They are the ones straining toward force.

The stated, deliberately vague banner that holds all three: **"our law, our bench, our water."** Everyone hears a different promise in it.

## How they work

The Sworn have no head. They organize through the **Phantom Assizes** — the annual convening where surviving water-judge families still "hear cases" in a law no one enforces (see [[The Kessadi]]). It is grief wearing the robes of a court, and it is the perfect skeleton for a movement: a legitimate-seeming civic body that meets openly, keeps records, commands loyalty, and cannot be outlawed without outlawing a funeral rite. Around it hang compartmented cells the convening does not control.

Methods run the full spectrum, and the movement is currently *straining* between them:

- **Civil** (Restorationists): flooding the [[The Assize|Assize]] docket with sovereignty petitions, propaganda in both calendars, boycotts, keeping the old forms alive as defiance.
- **Sabotage** (the middle): the canals, the road, and the relay-post are the Empire's nervous system — a fouled lock, a cut relay, a bridge that fails on schedule. Doctrine is non-lethal; discipline is fraying.
- **Insurgency** (hardliners): ambushes on garrison supply, "warnings" to collaborator notables that are starting to end in bodies, and an open rising some want to time to the closing window.

Money comes from the houses and from delta merchants who fund quietly and deny loudly. Recruitment comes from grief, from the ceiling, and — the Sworn's own uneasy secret — from the fact that the Empire's fairness keeps handing them martyrs it believes are verdicts.

## Named figures

- **[[Nemara Ishekar]]** — heir of a water-judge house; presides at the Phantom Assizes. The movement's conscience and unwilling figurehead, who cannot control the violence her legitimacy shelters.
- **[[Davan Kesharan]]** — the firebrand. A former imperial surveyor who helped map the water-reallocation that dispossessed his own neighbours, and now runs the cells that cut the network he built.
- **[[Marsu of the Low Wharfs]]** — the reluctant one. A lock-keeper who lost an ancestral share and a brother to the siege, shelters the Sworn, and is terrified it will destroy her son's imperial posting.

## GM Notes

`visibility: gm` — walled off; this note is player-safe.

- **Both sides stay right — hold it.** Could a decent person join the Sworn? Yes: your sovereignty was taken by force in living memory, your law abolished, your people just administratively erased, your ancestral water surveyed away, and the window to answer is closing. Dignity is not a rounding error. Could a decent person refuse? Also yes: the bench was an oligarchy that let the poor drown in lean years, imperial law is measurably *fairer to you personally*, the granaries are full and your children can rise, the Restorationists want their privileges back and call it your freedom, and the hardliners will get the delta drowned in blood for a sovereignty no one can eat. **Never resolve this.** The Empire's wrong here is real, mundane, and defensible — conquest, erasure, indignity — not secret cruelty. If the table concludes "so the rebels are just right," reach for the oligarchy twist ([[The Kessadi]]); if "so they're just malcontents," reach for the reconciliation and the closing road.
- **The players are the fuse.** The [[The Assize|Assize]] dispenses *real* justice, and its fair verdicts recruit for the Sworn. Complicity is the water (see [[Themes]]): the PCs will hand down a ruling that is correct in law and creates a rebel. Do not let them off this hook by making the verdict wrong.
- **Keep it mundane-political — the grievance is NOT about [[The Cost]]** (locked; see [[Kessad]], [[The Kessadi]]). The Sworn reach for sovereignty and water, never for the secret. The players reach the secret through the *frontier's silence* ([[The Outmark]]/[[The Tallun]]), not the delta's anger. The delta only ever *rhymes*.
- **The one deniable seed (fork below).** A single fringe cell — a Kessadi clerk who once worked the scribal service — insists the reconciliation lists *don't balance*: more names come off the delta rolls than death and emigration explain, on a fixed schedule. His theory is a **mundane atrocity theory** (hidden imperial labour-levy, a tax in people, a disappearance he calls corruption) — wrong in a way that rhymes with [[The Cost]] without touching it. The Sworn's own council thinks he is a crank; the belief stays fringe even inside the movement. It is the *faint seed only*, never confirmation. Its late payoff: the crank was righter than anyone, and also wronger. `reveals: []` kept empty deliberately — this rhymes, it does not expose.
- **Moral parallax engine:** collapses **Social** distance (the malcontents are people making the choice you would) and **Causal** distance (your fair ruling did this). Layered on **Inheritance** (the siege loss passed down; the young inherit peace *and* grievance) and **Exchange** (the delta's comfort is bought with its sovereignty and a quiet class of losers). No clean resolution exists.
- **Design-contract check:** benefit undeniable, loss undeniable, and the three wings want incompatible things, so there is no clean side even *within* the rebellion. That is correct. Keep it.

> **Fork (needs user):** How far to take the ledger-seed? **Recommendation: keep it a single fringe crank the movement dismisses** — present enough to reward a sharp player later, deniable enough never to lead the delta toward the real secret. Do not let the Sworn as a body ever suspect [[The Cost]].

> **Fork (needs user):** How violent is the movement *right now*, at campaign start? **Recommendation: mostly civil + sabotage, insurgent wing straining at the leash** — so the players meet rebels who are plainly not terrorists while feeling the pull toward violence as the road closes the window. Dial the hardliners up or down to set the campaign's temperature.

> **Fork (needs user):** Leadership shape? **Recommendation: coalition with a figurehead but no true head** — the Phantom Assizes give it a civic skeleton and Nemara a public face, but the cells are compartmented and she does not command the violence. The tragedy is that she cannot stop what her legitimacy enables. (Alternative: a genuine cell structure with no face at all, if you want the players unable to negotiate with anyone.)
