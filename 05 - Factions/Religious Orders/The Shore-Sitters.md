# The Shore-Sitters

The Shore-Sitters are a second house on Kumbaan, small, and not an order of the mainland. [[The Table-Keepers]] are the ones who open the hall. The Shore-Sitters sit the storm-beaches. Almost no one reaches the isle, and someone has to be there on the sand when someone does. They share the faith of the [[The Open Table|Open Table]], and the beach is their part of it.

*(Everyday: **shore-sitters**, also **wall-waiters.** Own-name: **Njawaal** — njah-WAHL — a [[Yumboe]] coinage, "the sitting at the wall." Not Maiethren. Table-keepers call them **the first night**, half a joke: a wreck is not yet kin.)*

**In one sentence:** they sit the storm-shore with their back to the hill-hall and wait, and they do not swim the wall, do not test anyone with a colour, and if someone is still breathing they walk them uphill to a table.

## If you meet one

You meet a small person on a bad beach, with the lamp hooded so it does not call ships in. They look as if they have been there a long time, and they will not wade. They will take a living mainlander by the sleeve and start the path up. If the house leans Staying, they will name a drowned body, and if it leans Serving, they will not name the body, and either way they still carry it above the tide.

You would not call them a priest. You would find them if you asked who sits the black sand when the wall is loud.

## What they actually do

**They sit.** The nights that matter are storm nights, wreck-moons, and the weeks after a rumour of a crossing. If a stretch of coast is kept at all, one person keeps it, and the claim is reputation rather than a roster.

**They receive.** A living person gets water and a blanket, and nobody asks what Kind they are or what colour they carry. A dead person is carried above the tide. Cargo is whatever the hall can use. They do not loot it for a market, because there is no quay to sell it from.

**They hand the living uphill.** The living go to a [[The Table-Keepers|table-keeper]]. The first night, the night a newcomer is only a guest, starts at the hall and not on the sand. The shore-sitter is the reason there was a first night at all.

**They refuse the wall.** They do not swim out, and they do not keep a boat for rescue. The wall is not a door they walk. A mainlander who asks why no one came sooner is told *we do not call the sea. We wait.*

They do not keep the extra place. That plate is the hall's work. They do not Speak a colour, stamp a book, or walk the old doors of the mainland, and they do not send anyone back across the wall.

## Who comes

Almost no one comes, and they treat that emptiness as part of the faith rather than as a failure of the watch. A shore-sitter's year may pass with an empty beach, and the job is still to sit. When someone does come, the whole valley hears it as news, not as a line on a shipping schedule.

## How they sit with the table-keepers

They keep the same faith and do two different jobs, so the shore is where someone decides whether the person is alive, and the hall is where someone decides whether the person is kin. A shore-sitter who starts laying the extra place on the sand has started doing the table-keeper's work, and a table-keeper who walks the black sand every storm has started doing this one. Both of those crossings can happen in a thin year, when there are not enough people to keep the jobs apart. Most years the two jobs are still held by different people.

## In-world text

*Said to a sailor who did come back, the night before he was walked uphill. Someone copied it later as a quay-story, and copied it badly.*

> *We did not fetch you. The wall does not owe you a hand. Sit until you can walk. If you walk, there is a hall. If you do not, we will still take you off the sand. Do not thank the sea. It was not kind. We were only here.*

The sitter sounds cold, and the message is plain. They did not fetch the sailor, the wall owes nobody a hand, and what he got was a wait rather than a rescue. They do not say the crossing was a gift, and they do not say it was a pilgrimage the Mother arranged.

## GM Notes

**Canon.** This is a second Kumbaan house, and it is small. The storm-wall is the boundary. There is no Kumbaan mission and no Tree, and hospitality is the faith. They recruit by practice, not by Kind. On the isle they are Yumboe because that is who lives there. The name, and the question of how many stretches of coast keep a sitter, are not settled.

**Not a fourth mainland house.** Do not grow a Shore-Sitter circuit on Strandoren. A Yumboe abroad is still a table-keeper by necessity, not a beach-warden of Lestrand.

**Not ghosts, not a coast-guard paladin order, and not clergy sitting next to the Selkie.** They wait. Selkie hearths are mainland coasts. Do not let this house become a seal-house.

**The keystone stays off this sand.** They are not of her household ([[The Other Hands]]). A wreck is not a Struck-door she authorized. Rare Struck on the isle stay family news ([[Yumboe]]). Read the sitter's message this way: wanting to be saved was never a gift, and the crossing is not a pilgrimage the Mother arranged.

**Taste still open.** The name *Njawaal* is still open, and so is the question of how many coasts keep a sitter, though the lean is few. Whether a Serving host and a Staying sitter argue over naming the drowned is playable, and it is not load-bearing.

**Hooks.** A living mainlander the sitter will not walk up until dawn. A body the hall does not want named. A lamp that faced the sea once and was never lit again. The year no one sat, and a wreck that waited in the tide.
