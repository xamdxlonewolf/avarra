# The Shore-Sitters

> **What this is.** A **second Kumbaan house**, flavor, not a mainland order. [[The Table-Keepers]] open the hall. These sit the storm-beaches. Almost no one reaches the isle; *someone* has to be there when the almost happens. Built thin on purpose. The [[The Open Table|Open Table]] is not rebuilt.


*(Everyday: **shore-sitters**, also **wall-waiters.** Own-name: **Njawaal** — njah-WAHL — a [[Yumboe]] coinage, "the sitting at the wall." Not Maiethren. Table-keepers call them **the first night**, half a joke: a wreck is not yet kin.)*

**In one sentence:** they sit the storm-shore with their back to the hill-hall and wait — they do not swim the wall, they do not test anyone with a colour, and if someone is still breathing they walk them uphill to a table.

## If you meet one

A small person on a bad beach, lamp hooded so it does not call ships in. They look like they have been there a long time. They will not wade. They will take a living mainlander by the sleeve and start the path up. They will name a drowned body if the house leans Staying, or they will not, if it leans Serving, and they will still carry the body above the tide.

You would not call them a priest. You would find them if you asked *who sits the black sand when the wall is loud.*

## What they actually do

- **Sit.** Storm nights, wreck-moons, the weeks after a crossing rumor. One person per stretch if the coast is kept at all. Reputation, not a roster.
- **Receive.** Living: water, a blanket, no questions about Kind or colour. Dead: above the tide. Cargo: what the hall can use. They do not loot for a market. There is no quay.
- **Hand uphill.** The living go to a [[The Table-Keepers|table-keeper]]. First night as *guest* starts at the hall, not on the sand — but the shore-sitter is why there was a first night.
- **Refuse the wall.** They do not swim out. They do not keep a boat for rescue. The wall is not a door they walk. A mainlander who asks *why didn't you come for me sooner* is told *we do not call the sea. We wait.*

They do not keep the extra place (that is the hall). They do not Speak, stamp, or walk old mainland doors. They do not send anyone back across.

## Who comes

Almost no one. That is the theology. A shore-sitter's year may be empty. The job is still to sit. When someone does come, the whole valley hears it as news, not as a shipping schedule.

## How they sit with the table-keepers

Same faith, two jobs. The shore decides *alive*. The hall decides *kin*. A shore-sitter who starts laying the extra place on the sand has started doing the other house's work. A table-keeper who walks the black sand every storm has started doing this one. Both can happen in a thin year. Most years they are different people.

## In-world text

*Said to a sailor who did come back, the night before he was walked uphill. Copied later as a quay-story, badly. `oblique-worldbuilding`.*

> *We did not fetch you. The wall does not owe you a hand. Sit until you can walk. If you walk, there is a hall. If you do not, we will still take you off the sand. Do not thank the sea. It was not kind. We were only here.*

On its face: cold courtesy. Underneath: the isolate's whole claim — wanted was never a gift, and the crossing is not a pilgrimage the Mother arranged. The sailor heard a rescue. It was a wait.
