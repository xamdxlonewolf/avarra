# The Door-Keepers

The Door-Keepers are the working people of [[The Old Ways]] (*Vaeloren*), the faith that still walks the land's old doors. They have no seat in a city and no ladder of rank above them, because the land is the seat, and a person becomes a door-keeper when the district already treats them as one, which for a famous one means three valleys will walk to them. A hill, a drowning-stone, a grove, or an oath-rock is the office they keep, and there is no mother-church that can overrule the door.

*(Everyday: **door-keepers**, also **Kin-speakers.** Liturgical: **the Vaelbren** — VAEL-bren — [[The Old Tongue|vael]] in the *older* sense ("what the land yields") + [[The Old Tongue|bren]] "hearth": those who *keep the yield at the hearth.* The Waiting Lands say **the door-folk**; the Ledger Coast says **grove-talkers**; the Tallied Crown files them as **the Unreached**.)*

**In one sentence:** they are the person three valleys walk to when someone needs to know which drowning-pool still returns and which grove still takes people in, and they are the person who names a Struck — someone who Changed later, at a river, a grove, an oath-stone, or a death — *kin* at the door that made them, instead of walking them to a Tree to be filed as late.

## Shape of the order (at a glance)

The Old Ways cannot have a mother-church, so the land stays the seat. A hill, a drowning-stone, a grove, or an oath-rock is the office, and a person is of the house when three valleys will walk to them, not when a college confirms them.

Two jobs sit in the house, and they are often the same person. A door-keeper knows which doors still open and will walk them at the year's hinges, the solstices, the equinoxes, and the local turns a city calendar does not print. A Kin-speaker speaks at the ancestor-meals, where a place is set for the ones in the ground, and names a Struck *kin* at the door that made them. In a thin-reach district, where a warden may not come for a generation, this is one tired person. In a Guest-reading town that finally got a Tree and will still pilgrimage to it while walking the old doors, the two jobs may split for a generation and fold back into one person when the old one dies.

They are not [[The Tree-Wardens|Road-hands]], and they keep no queue and do no Speaking. A Road-hand carries a scion, a living cutting of the wood, up the road, and Speaking is the confirming of a colour. The Door-Keepers do not carry scions, do not confirm colours, and do not authorise a cut. When a graft sickens in an old green, they argue over who is host, not over the horticulture, and the Road-hands already walk that other job.

This is a land-and-kin house, not a druid-chapter with a war to fight in the trees. They do not police the Mother-name, and many of them also bring a child to a healthy graft when they can reach one. The pressure they actually meet is a grove both houses claim, and a Struck the town has not vouched, and that is a harder ordinary week than any holy war.

## What they actually do

The faith already named the rites ([[The Old Ways#Practice]]). What follows is the walk: who sends for them, what they do with their hands, and what they refuse.

### If you meet one

A door-keeper looks like someone who knows the district. There is mud on the hem, a worn token at the belt, and a sack that is sometimes food for a door and sometimes food for a meal. The token is a river-stone, a grove-twig, or a pinch of hill in a twist of cloth, and no two of them are alike. You would miss them as clergy if you came from Maiethorn looking for a chapel. You would find them if you asked who still walks the drowning-stone, or who will speak at the board for the ones in the ground.

They are useful the way a midwife is useful, except the birth they attend is a later becoming, someone who Changed at a door rather than a child just born, and the map they keep is which of those doors still open. A thin-reach town can live without a warden for a generation. It cannot live long without someone who remembers the water.

### An ordinary season (the walk is the job)

**Most weeks.** Someone asks whether the pool below the ford still returns, and the door-keeper goes and looks. They know which grove took someone in ten years ago, and whether that hill is still a door, and when the hinge is close they carry food for it. Under the reputation they have a day life, ranging or green-tending or a stall, and the reputation sits on top of that life rather than replacing it. A famous door-keeper is simply the one three valleys will walk to. A young one is the apprentice who carried the food last year and was watched doing it.

**The year's hinges.** The walk is not only [[The Reckoning of the Year|High Solstice]] and Turning-Week. They also walk Deep Solstice, the long night and the low year, and both equinoxes, and the local hinges a Maiethorn calendar does not print, the first flood and the night the hill-ice breaks. They walk the old doors of the district and greet them water, then hill, then stone, and they will not say why that order is the one they keep. A guest who reverses it is corrected, and nobody turns the correction into a sermon.

**The meal.** There is a place at the board for the ones in the ground, and the hour is quiet and domestic. The Kin-speaker is the one who knows the names, and in a thin-reach district this is the same tired person who walked the ford at dawn.

**When someone Changed later.** They do not start at the Tree. They start at the door, the river if it was water, the grove if it was green, the oath-stone if it was a vow, and the death-place if it was unfinished work, and there they name the person *kin*. The Motherfaith's quiet receiving-rite, the one that can acknowledge the same person as Given, may happen the same week, and a Book-hand's novation, the rewritten civic terms, may happen the same month. It is three courtesies, not a trial. The exposed edge is the Struck the town will not house. The door-keeper has a name to give and not a civic pillar to stand on, so they may spend a season walking with one person, and they may not be able to.

If the town has a healthy graft, they tend it as a *guest in the grove*. They may Turn under it, and they may not pretend the graft was what invented becoming.

### How a kin-naming actually runs (the hour)

1. They find the door, if it can be found. If it cannot, they use the nearest old door of the same kind, water for a drowning and green for a taking. A door that is only a Tree is a last resort, and a First-reading walker, one who keeps the old doors first and will not take the Tender's blessing, will hate it.
2. Food comes first. The door is greeted, and the person is not tested with a colour.
3. The Kin-speaker says the name the person already had, and then the kin-name the door is owed. There is no leaf and no stamp. The token at the belt is shown, not given, unless this person will walk next year, in which case they will find their own.
4. The town, if it is watching, still wants a warrant before it lets them *stay*. The kin-name is a courtesy, and [[Law and Citizenship|Standing-for]] is the civic act beside it. A door-keeper who is also Long-Lived, Bound, or Far-Voiced can do both, and most cannot.

### The contested grove (when the job collides with a warden)

An ailing graft in an old Taken-In green, a grove of [[The Taken-In]], is the signature fight, and you can see the shape of it from the path. It is warden against door-keeper, guest against host, and the scarcity that sends people on pilgrimage set against the old answer, which is that the grove already had ways. Both are neighbours who love the wood.

For an hour their hands do the same work. They water, they watch the blight, and they sit up with mortal wood. What they are arguing about is not the horticulture of the wood, but whether this Tree is a guest in the grove or a Tree the town failed. A Guest-reading Kin-speaker will go on pilgrimage and still walk the doors, and will tend a dying graft as kin. A First-reading door-keeper will tend the same graft as a *guest in their house* and still refuse the Tender's blessing. Both revere the Struck and the Taken-In. A reading that starts pitying them as "unreached" has already become Motherfaith, and this house will not use that word.

If a Road-hand arrives with Cutting-leave, the door-keeper's job is the meal first, and then the question of whether this grove receives a guest, not the question of whether the door-keeper should have brought the wood. They do not carry scions.

### What they carry

They carry the token from their door, a river-stone or a grove-twig or a pinch of hill, and they carry food for the walk. They carry names in the head, the ones in the ground and the doors that still open. They do not carry a stamp, a colour-book, or Cutting-leave.

### What they refuse (the job's edge)

Confirming a colour is not their work, and neither is officiating a Leaf-Fall. If they bring a child to a healthy graft, the warden Speaks. They will not carry a scion, and they will not sell a cut. They will not call the Struck or the Taken-In "unreached," because that word is the Motherfaith leaking into a house that does not use it. Some doors they will walk and will not publish, so a curious clerk does not leave with a map of every one. They will not become priests of the undeath. An ancestor-meal is for kin who remained in the ground, and the [[Returned]] are a different door.

## Who may keep a door

**Not by Kind.** A [[Tengu]] ridge-child and a human neighbour walk the same stone if they know it and will stand for kin. Vaeloren has no crow-chapter and no grove-guard that only the Taken-In may join. The Taken-In are *revered* here. They are not a membership test, and that rule is load-bearing ([[Kinds of the Turning]]).

**By practice first.** You are taken as a walker once you have carried the food, been watched at a meal, and earned three households that will send for you. Belief about the Mother, whether you read her as Guest or as First, is secondary to that practice. Many Kin-speakers also bring a child to a healthy graft when they can reach one.

**Condition leans, not Condition-gates.**

| Act | Often | Why the Tithe (or the lack) fits | Also, and welcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Walking the doors | [[The Taken-In]] | They are the grove's own children, and a green Tithe plus an old green is what already fits the walk. | [[Two-Bodied]], for whom the walk *is* a ranging, and the Kept, for whom a closed door was weather rather than a slight. |
| Naming the Struck kin | Struck door-keepers | They have been through a door with no warden waiting on the other side of it. | A Wide-Hand warden who will share the week, and a Bound whose oath-stone is the door. |
| Ancestor-meals | anyone of the house | The dead stay in the ground, which is not [[Returned\|the Returned]]'s door. | A Returned guest is a *different* door. Do not make the Kin-speaker their priest. |

The feared-licensed are not barred. A Taken-In door-keeper is ordinary on Heskoren and a frontier embarrassment in a Narrow-Hand sermon, the sermon that will call the Struck "not hers." A [[Phoenix]] is not a walker. There is no old door that answers to white-fire.

Struck door-keepers must still be **vouched anew**. Naming them kin is the house's courtesy at that seam, and it is not a substitute for the three pillars a town still wants, the Long-Lived, the Bound, and the Far-Voiced, before it lets someone stay. The town may still want a warrant.

## How they live in the three corners

**[[Polity Archetypes|Vaethorn]] (the door-folk).** This is home ground, and it is where the fight stays live. The warden is most of the state a town can see, and the door-keeper is the older state the land still keeps. Guest-reading towns that finally got a Tree did not throw their grandparents out of the rite. They go on pilgrimage to the Tree, and they still walk the old doors. In the First-reading districts, where a scion still will not take, they tend the dying guest and they refuse the blessing. What they are up against is Vaethorn's guilt-theology, the reading of scarcity as unworthiness, while the Hands-folk hearth stays a neighbour's house. A closed door is weather, something the land does, and it is neither a slight nor a verdict that the town was unworthy.

**[[Polity Archetypes|Lestrand]] (grove-talkers).** On this coast a door-keeper is a frontier curiosity. One on the docks is a migrant or a story. The Settling will stamp a Struck's terms and call the kin-name poetry, and the house is useful if someone needs a drowning-stone found. It is not a rival for the quay.

**[[Polity Archetypes|Threnmaieth]] (the Unreached).** The crown files them as a gap, people who were not at a proper Tree and doors the roll cannot name. A Kin-speaker in the core is rare enough that a crown-archivist finds the rarity interesting, and the census wants a column to set them in. The land will not become that column. What follows from the want is pressure, and it is not a hunt.

## The host of a dying guest (the order's injustice)

Someone has to decide whether a sickening graft is a *guest in the grove* or a *Tree the town failed*.

The Road-hand arrives with leave and with soil-science, and the door-keeper arrives with grandparents and with food, and both of them can be right about the wood. The years the grove spends arguing are years in which children walk to a neighbour's canopy, and the children who make that walk are Kept-heavy and Struck-heavy. The frontier already knows that mix as weather, the ordinary way of a thin place, and Vaethorn will sanctify the same mix as unworthiness if no one says otherwise.

Their complaint is not a queue. It is who is host, and who will stand for the Struck the town will not. A healthy Tree in the grove would not change which wood came first, and it would not make a closed door into a slight. The grievance stays where they left it. The grafts *are* late, and the Motherfaith *did* talk as if becoming were new.

## Economy of the house

A door-keeper eats at the boards they speak for. There is no stipend from a seat. A famous one is housed because three valleys would be ashamed not to house them.

They hold no Cutting-leave and they charge no fee, because they do not sell wood. If a Guest-reading town wants a scion, that coin goes to the Seat, not to the Kin-speaker. A First-reading district that pays for a Cutting-leave and still refuses the blessing is doing both courtesies at once, and the argument about it will start after the work is done.

Standing for the Struck is unpaid. The people who most need a kin-name are the ones a town will not house. Some door-keepers spend a year on one person, and some cannot, and both are of the house.

They are **not** the Tithe-infrastructure sector. Garden-commons and ranging-commons live in [[Tithe-Infrastructure]], and a Taken-In door-keeper may *live* in a green without holding any licence over it.

## In-world text

*Someone left this tied to an ailing graft in an old Taken-In green, the week a Road-hand's cart was seen at the ford. Two hands, and one knife-cut for the string.*

> *The tree is young. The grove is not. We have fed it as a guest and we will feed it still. If you have come with leave, come to the meal first. If you have come with a second cut in mind, know that the hill already yielded a child this year and does not owe you a colour. We will not let either wood pretend it was first.*

The writer is polite and a little barbed, and the note takes a drowning-stone saying and sets it on a trunk. The house's case is that becoming happens at doors where there is no healthy Tree. The Road-hand who eats first will plant as a guest. The one who cuts without the meal will be remembered as a claiming, and the wood may still take.

## GM Notes

**Moved from the body.** This line used to sit where players could read it, and it should stay out of that body. Do not fold the isle into a grove.

**What this is.** The land is the seat, so the Door-Keepers have no hierarchy, only a reputation. The faith itself lives on [[The Old Ways]], and this note does not rebuild it.

**Canon.** Door-keepers exist. They know the old doors, they stand for the Struck, and they keep grafts as guests. They recruit by faith, by practice, and by a Condition-lean, and never by [[Kinds of the Turning|Kind]]. They are not a Kind-nation of the Taken-In.

**The injustice.** The Seat's scion-queue is the Orenhael's injustice, and it should not be cloned onto the Vaelbren. Their injustice is who is host, and who will stand for the Struck the town will not. The grievance stays where they put it. The grafts *are* late, and the Motherfaith *did* talk as if becoming were new.

**Half-right at the edge, now wearing a walking-stick.** [[The Other Hands]] is where you keep the rest of this. The land-doors are real, *Vaelorn*, *Ornhael*, and *Eloren*, and she does not walk them herself. She authorizes them. Keep the Door-Keepers' grievance real, and keep their miss intact: the guests came with her leave. The last turn of that slip, read by the GM, is that they have correctly felt that becoming happens at doors with no healthy Tree, and they have not asked whether a hand was in the water too, at the end of its reach. The name collision matters. **Vaelbren**, this house, is not **Vaelorn**, the Taking. If you swap the coinage, do not land on a lesser Hand.

**Ancestor-meals are not the Returned.** The dead in the ground are kin who remained, and the Returned are a different door, *Threnvaeth*. Do not make Kin-speakers into priests of the undeath. The Returned already have a Condition and a Tithe of their own.

**The injustice is host-rights and the unvouched, not a scion-queue.** The graft is late, the grandparents' doors still open, and the Road-hand's leave and the door-keeper's meal are two honest claims on one wood. Vaethorn's guilt-theology is the enemy. A closed door is weather, something the land does, and not a slight.

**Do not clone the Orenhael.** Give them no seat, no four offices, no Road-hand circuit, and no desk that hands out Cutting-leave. They walk doors. They do not deliver wood. Do not invent Kin-speakers who carry grafts, because the Road-hands already occupy that shape. The exposed edge here is standing for a Struck a town will not house, which is a courtesy, and not a pillar.

**Taste still open.**

1. **The name *Vaelbren.*** Everyday speech already has *door-keepers* and *Kin-speakers* in canon, so you may swap the liturgical name or drop it, and you must not put *Vaelorn* in its place.

2. **Two jobs, often one person.** The other clean option is that *door-keeper* is the only everyday word, and *Kin-speaker* is what you are called when you are at the meal.

3. **Famous means three valleys will walk to them.** Do not grow a grove-college in a later polish unless play needs a face, and even then the face is a meal, not a Seat.

4. **Guest-reading and First-reading both tend the dying graft.** The fight between them is over the blessing, and not over whether the wood gets fed.

**Do not invent a Kind-grove or a Kumbaan door-walk.**

**The Watchers.** The old lock that said not to let these two houses meet is lifted. They will not triangulate into a neat agreement, and that refusal is the fight, not a reason to keep them apart. The guest-grove is [[The First Bowl]].

**Names.** The liturgical form is *Vaelbren* (VAEL-bren): [[The Old Tongue|vael]] in the older sense, "what the land yields," plus [[The Old Tongue|bren]], "hearth," so the name is those who keep the yield at the hearth. The Waiting Lands say *the door-folk*, the Ledger Coast says *grove-talkers*, and the Tallied Crown files them as *the Unreached*.

**Hooks.** A dying graft that two houses love. A Struck the town will not vouch, and a Kin-speaker who will. A Road-hand told to eat first at [[The First Bowl]]. A First-reading district that paid for Cutting-leave and still withholds the Tender's line. A Night Watch held in the green. A scholar who notices that *vael* still means a harvest here. Do not sicken the First Bowl's guest in order to merge two kinds of grove. The sick Tree is [[The Mill-hold]].
