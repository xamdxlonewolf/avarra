# The Tree-Wardens

The Tree-Wardens are the working clergy of the [[The Leaf-Mother|Motherfaith]], the faith built on the Leaf-Mother, and the work they do is sketched in [[Turning Tree]] and [[The Leaf-Mother]]. They are one order with offices inside it. A town keeps them because someone has to water the wood, say a colour aloud so the reading is civic, and know when a scion is dying. A scion is a living cut of a Tree, carried in earth and planted so a town that does not yet have a mature Tree can grow one. They recruit by office, by practice, and sometimes by Condition, the Tithe a person lives with or the lack of one. They never recruit by [[Kinds of the Turning|Kind]], the people a person comes from.

*(Everyday, everywhere: **tree-wardens**, or just **wardens.** Liturgical: **the Orenhael** — OREN-hael — [[The Old Tongue|oren]] "to witness / speak true" + [[The Old Tongue|hael]] "whole, hale": those who *say the fall truly* and *keep the Tree sound.* The Waiting Lands say **Hands-folk**; the Ledger Coast says **tree-tenders**; the Tallied Crown files them as **the Reckoned Hands.**)*

## Shape of the order (at a glance)

The Motherfaith does not run rival chapters. It is one order with several offices, and those offices sit inside the same house. The live argument is already the three-branch schism on [[The Leaf-Mother]]: Tender and Wanting, Wide-Hand and Narrow-Hand, believer and skeptic. The Orenhael holds that argument inside the house. A second Motherfaith order would be a costume.

The wardens are needed before they are holy, because a town has to keep someone who can water the wood, name a rare colour, and know when a scion is dying. Doctrine is extra. This is why a quiet skeptic can hold a town-hearth, as the section on who may be a warden takes up, and why Lestrand still pays its tenders.

The order is collegial at the origin and singular in the town. The **First Seat** is a college, not a throne, and a town has one warden-hearth. The reach is late-medieval, which means the Seat cannot micromanage a graft that has gone far from the Motherwood, and it does not pretend it can.

They are a gift-religion's hands, and they are not a salvation-religion's inquisitors. They do not police belief. They tend the wood, they speak the colour, and they carry the graft. The pressure they meet is the Tithe that comes with a Condition, and the queue of towns waiting for a Tree. The pressure is not a heresy-court.

## What they actually do

The Tree already implies four jobs, and the offices are those jobs given a name and a place.

### The warden-hearth (the town)

Every town with a mature Tree keeps a **warden-hearth**, a house on the square or in the temple-yard, close enough that blight is smelled before it is announced. The warden, sometimes a small household of an apprentice and an elder, does the civic-sacred work from that house.

**Tree-tending** is the watering rites, the blight-watch, and the ordinary care of mortal wood.

**The Leaf-Fall** is the rite they officiate. They bring the child under the boughs, and they wait there with the town.

**The Speaking** is how they confirm the colour aloud. The folk-known colours, deep red, storm-grey, lamp-amber, sea-blue, pale stone, and copper-green, are ones any neighbour can already read, and the warden's word is what makes that reading civic. Rare, mixed, or argumentative falls are why the office exists. A **white-fire** fall is sent to the First Seat by runner. A town-warden who names [[Phoenix]] from a book has just made history, and a problem.

**Receiving the Struck** is the quiet after-the-fact rite at the Tree. It is not a Turning. The Tree does not Strike. How warmly it is done follows the warden's Wide-Hand or Narrow-Hand lean, and that difference is how the schism reaches the square.

A new town without a mature Tree borrows a neighbour's warden and a neighbour's canopy until its own scion comes of age. That borrowed year is remembered: *we Turned at Harrow's Tree before we had our own.*

### The Speaking (colour-authority)

The Speaking is an *act*, and it is not always a separate person. In a small town the warden Speaks. In a pilgrimage-town, or at the First Seat, a **Speaker** is the elder who holds the rare-colour texts and the last word on a mixed fall.

They do not invent the palette. They *keep* it. The folk already know the commons, and the Speaker is the reason a copper-green is not mistaken for the hug, and the reason a Watcher's second reading ([[The Watching]]) stays a witness *alongside*, not a rival authority. Dual practice is common. Two authoritative Speakers in one square is a fight.

The cited failure is [[The Wrong Green]], Harrow's, Cut-year 248, a hug Spoken as copper-green. You may Speak a second word. You may not stand the child again. The backstory questions a hearth actually gets are on [[Turning Tree#Questions a warden gets asked]].

### The Road-hands (graft and sickness)

**Road-hands** carry living scions in living earth, plant them with ceremony, and are the people sent when a Tree sickens. The everyday name is the one used everywhere. The liturgical name is **Thaelvaeth** — THAEL-vaeth — [[The Old Tongue|thael]] + [[The Old Tongue|vaeth]], "the Tree gone to the waiting."

They are the order's thin edge. A dying Tree is a civic crisis, and a waiting town is a political one. The First Seat writes the **Cutting-leave**, the paper that says who may take wood, and for where. The Road-hand walks that paper. Far from the Motherwood they have almost no authority except the scion in the cart and whatever reputation three valleys will grant. This is the hardest office: an impossible mandate, plain tools, and the blame when the wood dies. The play-shape is under [the Road-hands at the table](#The%20Road-hands%20at%20the%20table).

### The First Seat (the college)

The faith's central authority sits in the **Motherwood** about [[Turning Tree#The Awakening Tree|Thaeloren]], on [[Maiethorn]], in the grove-clearings already named on that continent. Everyday the college is called **the First Seat.** It is not a pope's palace. It is a college of Speakers, elder wardens, and the Cutting-leave desk.

What the Seat actually holds, and this is all it holds, comes to three things.

The graft-source is the first. Scions are cut from the Awakening Tree, Thaeloren, or, in practice, as [[Turning Tree]] already says, from an established healthy town Tree acting on the origin's behalf. The Seat **authorizes** the cut. It does not uniquely possess every knife. A far college-town that cuts without leave is in schism with the Seat, not with the Tree.

The rare-colour texts are the second. They cover Phoenix, and the argumentative mixed falls, and they are the book a town-warden has never opened.

The third thing the Seat holds is the queue, the decision of which waiting settlement receives the next scion, and that decision is the order's real power and its injustice.

The Seat does **not** appoint every town-warden. A hearth is taken by apprenticeship and confirmed by the town that *watched* the new warden tend a year, citizenship-by-witness applied to the clergy. The Seat confirms Road-hands and Speakers. That is as far as a pre-print world can reach.

## Who may be a warden

**Not by Kind.** A [[Kitsune]] hearth-child and a human neighbour hold the same office if they can tend wood and speak a colour. The Orenhael has no Fox-chapter, no ridge-chapter, and no Kind-nation inside it. That rule is load-bearing ([[Kinds of the Turning]]).

**By practice first.** You are taken as a hand when you are apprenticed to a warden-hearth, and you are confirmed when the town has watched you through a Turning-Week. Orthopraxy is the rule here: what you *do* at the Tree makes you of the order. What you privately believe about *who* chooses is the schism, and it is not the hiring test.

**Skeptics at the hearth; believers at the Seat.** A town-warden may be a sincere skeptic, the person who will say "it is what the Trees do," and still be a good warden, because the wood is mortal and the colours are folk-known. The First Seat runs believer-heavy. Pilgrimage wants a Someone, and the rare-colour texts are kept as *hers*. A skeptic Speaker at Thaeloren is possible, and uncomfortable, which is the point.

**Condition leans, not Condition-gates.** Offices attract the Tithes that fit them. This is vocation-shape, the same grammar as [[Economy and the Tithe]], and it is not a rule that locks anyone out.

| Office | Often | Why the Tithe fits | Also, and welcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Town-warden | [[Law and Citizenship\|the Kept]] | They are untithed, so they can sit a hearth year-round. | Anyone who will tend |
| Speaker (Seat and pilgrimage-towns) | [[Long-Lived]] | The memory of a rare fall stays incorruptible, and the Phoenix texts are a century's job. | A sharp Kept scholar; a Wide-Hand Struck who has seen a threshold-colour from the other side |
| Road-hand | [[Two-Bodied]] | The road *is* the ranging. | Kept; [[The Taken-In]] when the job is a sickening grove |
| Grove-tending / blight | [[The Taken-In]] | A green Tithe, lived next to living wood. | Root-[[The Answered\|Answered]] for the carrying-earth |
| Threshold-receiving | Struck wardens (Wide-Hand lean) | They have been on the far side of the door. | Any warden the town will stand for |

The feared-licensed ([[The Stilled]], heavy [[Far-Voiced]], [[Bound]]) are not barred. They are *rare* at the First Seat. A Stilled Speaker is a civic comfort and a pilgrimage fright. A Bound warden is easier on [[Polity Archetypes|Lestrand]], where the faith is already a profession, than in the Motherwood, where a written Term next to a gift-doctrine looks like [[The Fair Hand]] leaking in. A [[Phoenix]] is never clergy. They are a problem the Seat cannot file.

Struck wardens must still be **vouched anew** like any Struck ([[Law and Citizenship]]). The order is not a hiding-place from that seam.

## How they live with the other houses

Orthopraxy is the peace. The Orenhael officiates the Leaf-Fall, and the other four houses stand at it. Conflict stays local, as [[Faiths of the Turning]] already locked.

|---|---|---|
| [[The Watching]] | The same Tree, and two blessings. A Watcher may offer a *second reading* of a rare fall, a witness alongside and not over the warden. | Threnmaieth wants one name on the [[Naming in the Turning\|Threnhael]]. A Speaker who lets the withheld blessing stand is a column the crown can count. |
| [[The Fair Hand]] | The Leaf-Fall is a festival and a signing-watch. Book-hands and wardens share a square on Lestrand and ignore each other's sentences. | Gift-language and debt-language meet over the same Bound soul. A Bound warden is the seam. |
| [[The Old Ways]] | A healthy graft is a guest. Guest-reading Kin-speakers make the pilgrimage and still walk the old doors. | When a Tree sickens in an old grove, the question is who is host, the warden or the door-keeper. Both are neighbours who love the wood. |
| [[The Open Table]] | A warden who crosses the storm-wall is a guest at supper, and there is no Tree there to tend. ## How they live in the three corners

The order is the same order in each corner, and what changes is the name and the injustice. The lever is [[Naming in the Turning|the name reveals the stance]].

**[[Polity Archetypes|Vaethorn]] (Hands-folk).** In the Waiting Lands the warden *is* most of the state. The reach is thin, the faith is hot, and there is almost no guild to issue a licence. A Road-hand here is doing the Seat's real work: sick Trees, borrowed Turnings, a scion that may not take. The soft injustice of the Warm lands sits on this hearth, a Kept apprentice pitied for "missing" the gift they are about to spend a life tending.

**[[Polity Archetypes|Lestrand]] (tree-tenders).** The warden is a paid civic office. The Leaf-Fall is a commercial milestone, and the warden Speaks so the guild-ticket can open, with the faith worn light. From the docks the Seat's queue looks like any other scarce good, and Cutting-leave can be brokered. That brokering is Lestrand's clergy-sin, and it does not require a heresy.

**[[Polity Archetypes|Threnmaieth]] (the Reckoned Hands).** This is the darkest pressure, because the First Seat stands in the same Sacred Core as the Tallied Crown. The crown wants wardens *named to the Threnhael* as officers of the Reckoned Faith, the colours written down and the Speakers working as clerks of stewardship. Some Speakers cooperate, because they believe they are keeping the realm hale. Some refuse. The Tree witnessed, they say, and a ledger is a second, lesser seeing.

## The scion-queue (the order's injustice)

A graft takes a generation to Turn, and there are always more waiting towns than there is carrying-earth, the living earth a scion travels in, so someone has to decide the order.

The First Seat frames that queue as **horticulture**: the wood will take *here*, the soil is thin *there*, and the carrying-earth is needed for the first. All of that can be true. It is also a choice about who lives another twenty years sending children down the road to a neighbour's canopy, and those years run **Kept-heavy and Struck-heavy**, which the frontier already knows as weather. The Motherfaith's kindness, if you hold it, did not write the queue. People did.

Road-hands walk the consequence. They plant what they were given leave to plant, they sleep in the town that was not chosen, and they learn not to say *queue*.

## The Road-hands at the table

A table can drop a party into the order without inventing a special-forces chapter.

The mandate is to keep sickening Trees alive, and to deliver a living scion to a town that has already waited too long. The enemy is time, thin soil, and a Cutting-leave written for someone else.

The constraints are three, not seven. They work in living earth and mortal wood only, which means no print and no miracle crate. They are a skeleton circuit of hands for a whole thin-reach. Their authority expires a week out from the Motherwood. They advise, and they do not command a town that has decided to meet a feared Struck with a rope.

The First Seat's support is **conditional**, and that conditional support is the institutional weather. A live Tree is a quiet success no one writes down. A dead one is a name on a complaint. Success gets you the next hard road, not a better cart.

Who ends up on that road is the ranging, the Kept who will not sit a comfortable hearth, and the Wide-Hand Struck who still wants the Tree to *acknowledge* them. It is not a Kind-unit, and it is not the Seat's favourites.

The college will grant a grudging Cutting-leave at the end of a Road-hand's story, and it will keep the queue.

## Economy of the order

The economy is sketched on [[The Leaf-Mother]], and it is settled enough to play.

Towns house and feed the warden-hearth. The stipend is civic and in kind: bread, wood, and a place on the square. It is not a tax on Conditions.

Turning-Week is the festival share. There are markets, the reunion, and the year's namings, and the warden eats from the fair the way any other needed office does.

Cutting-leave has a fee. It is framed as the cost of carrying-earth and of a Road-hand's year, and at the edge it is experienced as the price of a future Tree. Lestrand will broker it. Vaethorn will call it an offering. Threnmaieth will want it on the roll. Historically, this is how the Seat took over a practice that began without a fee ([[The First Cut]]).

Pilgrimage keeps the Seat: offerings, lodging, and the devout who settle in the Motherwood. The college is rich in *proximity*, not in a Condition-tithe.

The Orenhael is **not** the Tithe-infrastructure sector. Venting-halls, greens, ranging-commons, and the safety-guilds live in [[Tithe-Infrastructure]], while the wardens *point* a new Given at a guild and do not licence the Stilled.

## In-world text

*This is a Cutting-leave, in a First Seat hand, carried north in the same earth as the scion. The verso is travel-stained. The recto is clean.*

> *The wood will take at Harrow's green. It will not take this year at the three hamlets past the ford — the soil is thin there, and the carrying-earth is needed for the first. Cut from a healthy hand, not from the First, and married with the watering-words. If the scion sickens, send to the Seat; do not cut a second without leave. The Mother does not reach twice by chance, and neither do we.*

The leave is competent and a little cold. It reuses the Tender's blessing as a shipping clause, the sentence in which the Mother does not reach twice by chance. The clerk writes that the soil is thin at the three hamlets, and the three hamlets will run Kept-heavy and call it weather.

## Minutes that do not agree

The college keeps copies, and the copies do not share a last line. A party that asks for "the Seat's position" can be handed any of these and told it is current.

*From a warden-hearth in an unnamed Hands-town, on the verso of a blight-slate.*

> *The wood is sound. The child is ten. I will Speak. I will not write the colour onto anyone's roll unless the family asks. The Tree witnessed. A tablet is a second, lesser seeing. If the Seat wants this as a policy, they may send a policy. I have a watering-can.*

*A Speaker's note from the Motherwood, the same summer, in a different hand.*

> *A colour unspoken to the realm is a colour that can be stolen. File the fall. The hearth that will not copy is doing piety as a gap. We are one order. We are not one breath.*

*From a Road-hand on the Heskoren front, written on the inside of a pot-wrap.*

> *The leave was for Harrow's. The three hamlets can see the canopy. I ate at the First Bowl first because the grove asked. The Seat may call that delay. The hill called it a meal. I am out of carrying-earth and out of argument. Send a second pot or send a better sentence than soil.*

*From a Remainder-clerk in the side-yard after a mixed fall. The Remainder is the desk that logs the scraps, and it does not Speak.*

> *Scraps logged. Colourless tissue boxed. The Speaking was storm-grey; the Watcher said amber at the serration. We do not Speak. We do not walk the box unless someone with leave takes the earth. Two accounts, one yard. Do not ask us which summer was true.*

*Unsigned, and filed under "Reckoned Hands, refused."*

> *Tarvae stood beside. I Spoke. I did not copy. The water was loud. If this is insubordination, the college may recall me. If this is the job, the tablet-hall already knows.*

A later hand has written *Rithim?* in the margin and then struck the question. The college has not decided whether the strike is horticulture or politics.

## GM Notes

**What this is.** They are the working clergy of the [[The Leaf-Mother|Motherfaith]], one order with offices, and not a god-list of paladin-chapters. They recruit by office, by practice, and sometimes by Condition, and never by [[Kinds of the Turning|Kind]].

**Canon.** Tree-wardens exist. They tend the Tree, they officiate the Leaf-Fall, and they hold the authoritative colour-reading. Grafts are cut by them. The faith's seat sits in the shadow of [[Turning Tree#The Awakening Tree|Thaeloren]]. Orthopraxy comes before orthodoxy. Recruits are not Kind-nations.

**Cutting-leave has a fee.** Historically, this is how the Seat captured a practice that began without it ([[The First Cut]]): the generation of argument, the paper that would not write who, the queue as a copy-right. Do not rebuild the office.

**They are right about the wood and incomplete about the hand.** Per [[Is the Leaf-Mother Real]], every scion *is* continuous tissue of the one Tree, and when any warden Speaks a colour they are naming a choice she made. Present the Orenhael as competent neighbours, not as people who secretly know. A skeptic town-warden is *good at the job* and wrong about the mind, the same two-sided schism, inside one office.

**The Cutting-leave.** Read by the GM, the clerk writes "the soil is thin there" and "The Mother does not reach twice by chance." The queue confesses itself there. Do not say so in player text.

**The queue is the injustice, not the Tree.** The Seat sees soil and carrying-earth. The hamlet past the ford sees another year of borrowed Turnings. Every Cutting-leave is a benefit paid somewhere else. *Which town gets the next graft* is a human rationing of reach. The queue is [[The First Cut|the Grafting]]'s child, copy-right captured in the generation of argument.

**Present-day fate-pressure is tabled, not rolled.** Peak Seat × long tenure of the queue; Road-hands overextended at the Heskoren front; waiting towns precarious. See [[The Years of Hands]]. Do not collapse the college here. Do not convert the Seat at the end of a Road-hand story.

**Skeptics at the hearth; believers at the Seat.** A town-warden may be a sincere skeptic and still a good warden, because the wood is mortal and the colours are folk-known. The Seat runs believer-heavy. A skeptic Speaker at Thaeloren is possible and uncomfortable. The call is the user's.

**Taste still open.**

1. **The name *Orenhael.*** Everyday *tree-wardens* is already in canon. The liturgical name is a coinage from existing roots. You may swap it or drop it.
2. **Skeptics at the hearth.** The other clean options are skeptics in every office, or a rule that Speakers must believe.
3. **A college, not a pope.** If you want a single First Speaker as a face, that is a later polish, and it is not a load-bearing throne.
4. **One order.** Revisit that only if play needs a Motherfaith civil war. The schism already supplies the intra-faith conflict.

**Threnmaieth must not capture the Seat in the first sentence.** Proximity is pressure, not ownership. A First Seat that *is* the Threnhael is a different, darker setting. Keep the college able to refuse the roll, and keep some Speakers who do not.

**Do not invent Kind-orders or a Kumbaan mission.** Road-hands do not "bring the Mother" across the storm-wall. [[The Open Table]] is the control case.

**Not rebuilt here.** [[The Watchers]], [[The Book-Hands]], [[The Door-Keepers]], and [[The Table-Keepers]] have their own notes. Tithe-infrastructure is [[Tithe-Infrastructure]]. The underdog there is [[The Intake]], not a Road-hand clone.

**Hooks.** A mixed fall two houses want to name. A Cutting-leave the party is asked to carry, or to lose. A skeptic warden and a Wanting Kept apprentice in the same hearth. A Road-hand circuit whose next Tree is already dying ([[The Mill-hold]]). A Speaker who will not write a colour onto the Threnhael ([[Maiethlir\|Rithim]] is the local incomplete version). Harrow's green getting the scion the three hamlets can see from the ford. A later planting in the Far Yield that still wants the meal ([[The First Bowl]]). Eolvaeth sending a cohort the gospel says should wait. An attic tenth-year in Eolthael. A hearth that has to Speak the Wrong Green a second time. A white-fire letter that goes to [[The Protectors]] and does not come back answered.
