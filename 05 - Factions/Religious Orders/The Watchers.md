# The Watchers

The Watchers are the working people of [[The Watching]] (*Haelthael*), the faith that keeps the Tree and withholds the Mother-name. They are not priests, and they have no mother-church, because the [[The Tree-Wardens|First Seat]] grew over the rooms that would have been one. What they still keep, in those rooms and under those chapels, is a vigil and a second voice. They are the neighbour who will sit up with the Tree the night before Turning-Week, and the second pair of eyes a family sends for when a leaf comes down strange. They describe what they saw, and they do not confirm it, because that confirmation, the civic word, stays with the warden. They have day jobs, and the house is a practice, not a wage.

A second reading that becomes a confirmation is the house breaking itself, and a Night Watch that becomes a hunt is the house breaking itself the other way. The old rooms sit under chapels that were built over them.

*(Everyday, everywhere they are known: **Watchers.** Liturgical: **the Nethoren** — NETH-or-en — [[The Old Tongue|neth]] "night" + [[The Old Tongue|oren]] "to witness": those who *keep the night and speak what was seen.* The Waiting Lands say **the night-kin**; the Ledger Coast barely has one (*vigil-folk*); the Tallied Crown files the house as **the Grown-Over** and its people as **the second column**.)*

## Shape of the order (at a glance)

They have no seat, and the archaeology is the theology. The Watching has no college in the Motherwood, because that seat was grown over by the First Seat. Their oldest rooms sit under older chapels, or in houses that never took a warden's mark. A Watcher who wants a mother-church has already become something else.

The house has two acts, and it does not keep four offices. The **Night Watch** is what the house *does*, and anyone of Haelthael may keep it. The **Second Reading** is a reputation, not an office. It is the person a town will ask to sit beside the warden when a fall is rare, mixed, or argued. There is no Speaker-equivalent with the last word, no Road-hand circuit, and no Cutting-leave desk. Those jobs belong to a faith that has a Tree to carry.

A Watcher is needed as a witness, and a town does not keep one as a civic utility the way it keeps a warden. The wood still wants watering, and that watering is the warden's. What a Watcher is *for* is accurate speech, and a night of matching the Tree's attention with their own. Lestrand can live without that. Threnmaieth, the Tallied Crown, cannot stop counting it.

They are a witness-religion's people, not a heresy-court's. They do not police the Mother-name. They withhold it. The pressure they meet is the recut beam, a door-beam turned so its older face sits against the stone, and the roll that wants a name for the withheld word, and that pressure is not a hunt.

## What they actually do

The faith already named the rites ([[The Watching#Practice]]). This page is the job: who knocks, what the hour looks like, and what they refuse.

### If you meet one

A Watcher looks like a neighbour: a baker, a weaver, a Long-Lived who copies texts, a Kept who sits a quiet hearth. They do not wear a warden's mark, because that mark is the warden's. You know them because someone said *ask the old room*, or *send for the one who sat last year's mixed fall*, or because you saw them under the Tree the night before Turning-Week with no lamp and no blessing.

They are useful the way a second opinion is useful. A town can live without them. A family that does not trust a single speaker on a strange leaf cannot.

### Who knocks, and when

| The family of a ten-year-old, after a rare or mixed fall | The warden Spoke one colour, the leaf looked like two, and the year will argue | They sit *beside* the warden, or after, in the old room, and **describe**: stem, serration, how the leaf turned, and whether the child flinched. ### An ordinary year (most of it is not holy)

Eleven months of the year they are not on duty. They bake, they copy, they range, and they keep a stall, and the house-work that still happens in those months is the teaching, the naming of what was seen, and the care of the old room.

They teach the withheld blessing at the hearth, the year a child turns nine. It has the same cadence as the Tender's line, and the Mother is not in it. This is how you can tell a Watcher household in a mixed street. The child can recite both, or will only recite one.

After any Turning they attend, they name what they saw. It is not a sermon. They sit with *their* child, or with a neighbour's if they were asked, and they make the fall into accurate speech: the colour, the way the leaf came, and what the year will ask. Devotion is the description. A sloppy Watcher is a bad Watcher.

They sweep the old room. If the house still has a recut beam, they keep the recut beam's hidden face from rotting. They hang the Night Watch coats. In Threnmaieth they answer the clerk who wants to be told that the room is still a room.

They do **not** water the Tree. That is the warden-hearth. A Watcher who starts blight-watching has started doing the Orenhael's job, and the Orenhael are the tree-wardens, whose work is the wood.

### The Night Watch (the one night the house is visible)

The night before [[The Reckoning of the Year|Turning-Week]] is the eve of [[The Reckoning of the Year|Eolthael]] in a town that keeps the clergy's year, and that is the night the Watchers keep a silent vigil at the town Tree. There is no blessing, no colour-reading, and no lamps if they can help it. The point is to be seen keeping the night.

This is not an office you are appointed to. A household that speaks the withheld blessing is expected to send someone. A town with three Watcher families may have six people under the boughs and no one in charge. A town with one old room under the chapel may have a single Long-Lived who has kept the same night for eighty years. Both are the order, such as it is. They count the *silences*, not the hours. No one will explain this to a guest. A first-time sitter who asks *how long* is told *until it has been seen*.

They do **not** officiate the Leaf-Fall the next day. The warden does that. They attend, because the practice is what binds them and because their children Turn, and they speak their own line when the Tender's blessing is said.

### The Second Reading (when someone pays for a second witness)

Where a town will have it, a Watcher may sit a **second reading** of a rare or mixed fall. It is not authoritative over the warden. It is a *witness alongside*. They describe what they saw, and they do not confirm it. Confirmation is civic, and it belongs to the Speaking, the warden's word that makes a colour the town's.

The hour runs like this.

1. The child is still under the boughs, or has just been walked to the warden-hearth, and the town is still there. The warden Speaks. *Storm-grey*, say.
2. If a household asked, or if the fall was mixed enough that the square muttered, the Watcher is already standing to the side, and they do not interrupt the Speaking.
3. They say what they saw, in the square or afterward in the old room: the stem, the serration, the turn, the flinch or the lack of one. They will use colour-words. They will resist Condition-names if the leaf was not one colour.
4. Most years the town nods at both accounts and goes to the fair. The child is called Two-Bodied because the warden Spoke that name, and also someone said *there was amber at the edge*, and that sentence lives in the family.

A Second-reader is just the Watcher three streets will send for when the fall is argumentative, and neither a diploma nor a confirmation from the Seat is what makes them one. Dual practice is common: a Watcher may describe the same fall while the warden Speaks. Two *authoritative* Speakers in one square is the fight the First Seat will not permit, and the Watching does not ask for it.

In [[Polity Archetypes|Threnmaieth]] they are a known column on the [[Naming in the Turning|Threnhael]], the crown's roll of persons. The second reading is tolerated as counted dissent, the night is tolerated as a festival-eve custom, and the withheld word is what the roll wants a name for.

### What they carry

They carry almost nothing that looks like clergy. A scrap for the description, plain paper and not a book, because a book would be a Settler's tool. A coat that lives in the old room and smells of last year's eve. In a Bare Tree household, where they will not say *she* at all, there is no Mother-mark on the door-frame. In a Masked one, where they will stand for the Tender's blessing and add the withheld line under it, the warden's mark is on the frame, and a second, older scratch sits underneath, where a clerk can miss it.

They have no stamp, no Cutting-leave, and no ticket, so they cannot licence a Stilled and they cannot plant a Tree.

### What they refuse (the job's edge)

They will not **Speak**, and if a town has no warden that year they will still only describe, because a civic confirmation from a Watcher is the house breaking itself. They will not take a warden's mark. Their oldest rooms are in houses that never took one, and under the chapels built over those rooms. They will not police the Mother-name in the street. They withhold it, and a hunt would be the house failing. They will not travel as a circuit, because a Watcher in another town is a cousin visiting.

### The old rooms (not a network)

There is no chapter-house circuit. The order meets, when it meets, because Turning-Week gathers people who already keep the same night.

What looks like organisation from the outside is architecture. It is the house that never took a warden's mark, the beam that was turned to the stone when the chapel was re-floored, and the room under the nave where the Night Watch leaves its coats. The First Seat knows those rooms exist. It grew over them. It does not inventory them, except in Threnmaieth, where inventory is the local weather.

## Who may keep the watch

**Not by Kind.** A [[Kitsune]] hearth-child and a human neighbour keep the same night if they will sit it and speak accurately. Kind, the people a person comes from, does not sort the watch, and Haelthael has no Fox-chapter, no ridge-watch, and no Kind-nation inside it.

**By practice first.** You are of the house when you keep the Night Watch and teach a child the withheld blessing. The name for that belonging is orthopraxy, and it is older than the Motherfaith's. The withheld word is the dissent, and the standing at the Tree is the belonging.

**The schism walks in.** A **Masked** Watcher will stand for the Tender's blessing and add the withheld line under it, and a mixed town barely notices them. A **Bare Tree** Watcher will not say *she* at all. Both may Second-read. They will not always Second-read *the same* fall the same way. That is the house arguing with itself in the square, which is allowed, and it is why they cannot present a single rival authority even if they wanted one.

**Condition leans, not Condition-gates.** A Condition, and the Tithe that comes with it or the lack of one, may lean a person toward the night or the second reading. It does not close the door.

| Act | Often | Why the Tithe (or the lack) fits | Also, and welcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Night Watch | anyone of the house | The night does not care what you carry. | The Kept, who were seen as whole; the Struck, who were seen at a harder door |
| Second Reading | [[Long-Lived]] | An incorruptible memory of how a rare fall looked *last time*. | A sharp Kept; a Stilled who will describe without flinching; a Wide-Hand Struck who has seen a threshold-colour from the other side |
| Keeping an old room | families that never took a warden's mark | The archaeology is inherited with the house. | A new household that refuses the warden's mark |

The feared-licensed ([[The Stilled]], heavy [[Far-Voiced]], [[Bound]]) are not barred. A Stilled Second-reader is a civic comfort, because the gaze holds a room still for accurate speech, and a pilgrimage fright. They are rarer at the sacred centre than in an old district that never stopped being Haelthael. A [[Phoenix]] is not clergy here either. They are a seeing no one in the square is ready to describe twice.

Struck Watchers must still be **vouched anew** like any Struck ([[Law and Citizenship]]). The old rooms are not a hiding-place from that seam.

## How they live in the three corners

It is the same house in each corner, and what changes is the name and the pressure. The lever is [[Naming in the Turning|the name reveals the stance]].

**[[Polity Archetypes|Vaethorn]] (the night-kin).** This is cousin-dissent in the Waiting Lands. The reach is thin and the faith is hot, and a Watcher household is unusual and not hunted. From a Hands-folk hearth the Night Watch looks like extra piety. The withheld Mother-name is the only sting, and a Guest-reading town has larger fights, the grove among them, than this one.

**[[Polity Archetypes|Lestrand]] (vigil-folk, when named).** They are rarely met. A Watcher on the docks is a scholar, or a migrant from the core. The signing-watch does not need a second reading, because the book is already the second reading. Faith is worn light enough that a withheld blessing is just another quay-sentence.

**[[Polity Archetypes|Threnmaieth]] (the second column).** This is the darkest pressure, and it is the house's real weather. They are a known column on the roll, the Grown-Over, counted and not pogromed. The Night Watch is a festival-eve custom the crown can schedule. The second reading is tolerated until it contradicts the Speaker the Threnhael has already named. [[Laevila]] is the Long-Lived who still has a copy of the beam from before it was recut. The pressure is a tolerated column on the roll, not a hunt.

## The recut beam (the order's injustice)

They remember a cost, and they have no institution that can keep a beam from being turned to the stone.

The First Seat did not steal a throne from them. There was no throne. What was lost is quieter: a beam recut, a room under a chapel that is now a vestry, a second reading that remains *alongside* because the civic word was given to the warden when the Mother-name grew over the Seeing. All of that can be described as history. It is also a choice, still being made, about whose speech counts in the square.

A second reading does not buy a seat, and the Grown-Over column stays a column. They have hold of the Before and the cost. They do not have the last word on a colour. A settled Mother-name would not promote them.

They do not walk a scion-queue, since that injustice is the Orenhael's, and the one they live with is the recut beam.

## Economy of the house

As an institution, the house has almost no economy, and that is the point. Households keep the night, and the bread comes from the same table. There is no stipend, no Cutting-leave, and no pilgrimage-share. A Second-reader eats as a neighbour. Some old districts leave a plate at the room under the chapel during Turning-Week, and that plate is courtesy, not a wage.

Threnmaieth has a tell. Being a *column* can become a living if a crown-clerk pays for a copied beam or a listed name. A Watcher who takes that coin has not left the house. They have let the roll furnish it. The Bare Tree branch will not eat with them. The Masked will, and argue after.

The Nethoren are **not** the Tithe-infrastructure sector, and they are **not** a safety-guild. They point no one at a licence, they describe a fall, and the tickets live in [[Tithe-Infrastructure]].

## In-world text

*This is a second-reading slip, written in a Watcher's own hand the morning after a mixed fall, and filed by a Threnmaieth Speaker under "alongside, not over." The Speaker's docket-mark is cleaner than the slip.*

> *Storm-grey at the stem, lamp-amber at the serration, and the child did not flinch when the leaf turned. I will not say Two-Bodied. I will not say Answered. I will say what I saw: the Tree looked twice and did not choose the easier colour. The warden Spoke storm-grey. I sat beside and said the amber. Both were seen. File me as the second column if you must. Do not file the amber as an error.*

The writer is a careful witness, a little stubborn, and useful to a clerk who needs a name for dissent. The slip's whole case is that seeing something is not the same as choosing it. The Speaker who filed it "alongside" was keeping the peace between the warden and the Watcher.

## GM Notes

**What this is.** The Watchers are not priests, and they have no mother-church, because the First Seat grew over the rooms that would have been one. The faith itself is on [[The Watching]]. This note does not rebuild it.

**Canon.** Watchers exist. They keep the Night Watch. They offer a second reading *alongside* the [[The Tree-Wardens|tree-warden]], not over that warden. They have no seat in Thaeloren's shadow. They recruit by faith, by practice, and by Condition-lean, and never by [[Kinds of the Turning|Kind]].

**They are the keystone's unexpected corroborators, and they will still miss.** Per [[Is the Leaf-Mother Real]], the seeing *is* hers. Per [[The Other Hands]], they have hold of a household before it was named Mother, and of a cost. Do not confirm their metaphysics, and do not give them a seat in a late reveal, because that would turn the heretics into a second Motherfaith. The warmth lands *through* them, not *as* them. The slip's last line reads, to the GM, as this: looking twice is still a hand, and the hand has a household the writer will not name. The roll that kept the slip was doing theology, and the Speaker who filed it was doing the peace.

**The injustice is the recut beam, not a hunt.** The oldest rooms sit under the chapel that grew over them. The Seat sees horticulture and a festival-eve custom. The Bare Tree sees a theft of speech. Each recut beam is a past choice the present household lives inside. The Watchers keep the Night Watch and the second reading so the pressure stays low (about 5% frightening). A hunt is that pressure failing.

**Do not clone the Orenhael.** Give them no First Seat, no Road-hand circuit, no Cutting-leave, and no four offices. Do not invent a travelling Watch. The Road-hands already occupy that clergy-shape. A Watcher's constraint is *no mandate anyone will fund.*

**Taste still open.**

1. **The name *Nethoren.*** Everyday *Watchers* is already in canon. The liturgical coinage is *neth* + *oren*. You may swap it or drop it. Do not reuse *Orenhael*'s roots in reverse (*Haeloren*).
2. **Second Reading is a reputation, not an office.** The other clean option is that any Watcher may describe, and that "Second-reader" is only what Threnmaieth writes on the roll.
3. **No coordinating body.** A quiet network of letters between old rooms is a later option if play needs one. Do not grow a hidden pope in the vestry.
4. **Masked and Bare Tree share a square.** Both may Second-read, and they will not always agree. That disagreement is why they cannot rival the Speaking. Keep it.

**Do not invent a Kumbaan Night Watch or a Kind-watch.** Do not make them a secret police.

**The Door-Keepers.** The old "do not let them" lock is lifted. The contested guest-grove, [[The First Bowl]], is the escalation room, and they will not agree. That disagreement is the fight. Do not invent a tidy synthesis that would close it.

**Names.** The liturgical form *Nethoren* — NETH-or-en — is [[The Old Tongue|neth]] "night" + [[The Old Tongue|oren]] "to witness": those who keep the night and speak what was seen. The Waiting Lands say *the night-kin*. The Ledger Coast barely has one (*vigil-folk*). The Tallied Crown files the house as *the Grown-Over* and its people as *the second column*.

**Hooks.** A mixed fall two houses want to name, the warden Speaking one colour while the Watcher describes the other. A recut beam whose hidden face a party is asked to copy. A Bare Tree Second-reader and a Masked household in the same old room. A Threnhael clerk who wants the night *scheduled*. A Night Watch in the First Bowl. [[Laevila]]'s copy, and [[Senithi]]'s marriage.
