# The Book-Hands

The Book-Hands are the working people of [[The Fair Hand]] (*Leddoren*), the faith that keeps an account fair enough that a person can walk away from it. They are notaries who are also theologians. In the morning they may stamp a hull, and in Turning-Week, the week a town holds the Leaf-Fall, they stand with a child's private book. When a Struck comes to the table — a person who Changed later, drowned, died, or taken, and who cannot live this year as the town is — they help that person find a payable year, which means a hall, a warrant, and someone who will stand. On oath-day they will recite a Bound's Terms, the year's obligations already written down, where a neighbour can hear them. They will not change those Terms.

They have no seat and many tables. On [[Polity Archetypes|Lestrand]], the Ledger Coast, they are often the same people as the commercial clerks, and the faith is the profession, worn lightly. The house does split on one question, which is whether anyone sits across the table when a Bound wants the clause itself changed. A Counterparty hand believes someone is there to be asked, and may sit and wait. A Book Alone hand does not believe the far side of the table is occupied, and will not sit that hour. Neither hand will rewrite the Terms, and neither will sell an easing.

*(Everyday: **Book-hands**, or **Settlers.** Liturgical: **the Leddhael** — LEDD-hael — [[The Old Tongue|ledd]] "to reckon" + [[The Old Tongue|hael]] "whole": those who *keep the account fair enough that you can walk.* The Waiting Lands say **Bargainers' clerks**; the Ledger Coast says **the Settling-hands**; the Tallied Crown files them as **the Uncounted Book**.)*

## Shape of the order (at a glance)

The Fair Hand keeps guild-rooms and quay-shrines, and it does not keep a college in anyone's sacred wood. A high house in a rich port is the oldest book-room, old because the copies have piled up, not because that room can give the next quay leave to stamp. The next quay has its own table, and it does not write to the first for permission.

The faith is the profession. A Book-hand witnesses a signing, teaches a child to keep a book, and helps a Struck settle a year that can actually be lived, which is a hall, a warrant, and a neighbour who will stand. On Lestrand the same person may stamp a hull before noon and recite a Bound's oath-day after. Doctrine is the ethic of the stamp, *fair terms, then walk*. They copy a Bound's Terms. They do not author them.

There is no membership roll. A roll of Book-hands would be a Threnhael, the crown's ledger of persons, sitting in a clerk's pocket, and the house will not make one. What makes you of the house is that you keep accounts honestly and that you will die to keep a crown-clerk out of a personal book. That last refusal is the house's one shared fury with every other faith.

They are a settling-religion's clerks, and the house does not field contract-paladins. They do not ride, and they do not hunt hidden terms as a knightly order. They sit at tables. The pressure they meet is the book that will not close, not a devil's advocate in vestments.

## What they actually do

The faith already named the rites ([[The Fair Hand#Marks you can see|The Fair Hand]]). This page is the desk: who sits down, what gets written, and what the hand at the table refuses.

### If you meet one

On [[Polity Archetypes|Lestrand]] a Book-hand looks like a clerk, ink-stained, with a small stamp on a cord and a table in a guild-room or in a quay-shrine the size of a pantry. Before noon they may witness a hull, and after that they may stand a Bound's oath-day, and the sentence is the same both times: *fair wind, fair term.* You would not know which hour was "church" unless you already kept Leddoren.

Off the coast they are rarer, and they look more obviously religious, because a pilgrim-town sends for them when a Struck cannot live this year — no hall, and no one to stand — while the Hands-folk of that town would rather walk the person to a Tree.

They are useful the way a notary is useful. A port cannot live without someone who will stamp a signing, and the faith holds that the stamp and the becoming use the same grammar. The becoming, in this house's book, is the colour and the Tithe written down beside what the year owes.

### An ordinary week on Lestrand (the faith *is* the profession)

**Morning — the commercial table.** The morning is hulls, marriages, copies, and a deathless house's instrument, paid by the stamp. A Kept clerk, Lestrand's *free* — no Tithe, no guild, the book a choice — may do only this hour and still be of the house, if they keep accounts honestly and will die to keep a crown-clerk out of a personal book.

**Turning-Week and oath-days — the signing-watch.** They attend the Leaf-Fall as witnesses, and they do not officiate. They stand at the edge of the square with a child's first book and wait for a colour they can enter. A Bound's oath-day is the high holy day. The Terms are recited in a voice the quay can hear, and the year's book is closed or marked owing. The Motherfaith calls this ostentation. The Fair Hand calls it the only honest worship.

**When someone Changed last week — civic settling, not a rewritten god-clause.** A drowning came back Far-Voiced in a town with no ael-hall. A death came back Returned, with a purpose the street will not house. The Book-hand finds a year that can be *paid* — passage, a warrant, a neighbour who will stand. If no one will stand, they write *owing*. The quay steps around. That is still the house's kindness and its cruelty. It is **not** a Bound's Terms being edited.

**A Bound's oath-day.** They recite what was already sworn, and they do not improve it. A Bound who wants the clause itself changed is asking the Counterparty, not the clerk. The honest house will witness the ask, or it will refuse to pretend that the clerk can answer. Anyone selling a new clause after the lamps is another table.

**They never write the word *gift*,** even as a loan. A first-year apprentice who copies *vael* into a personal book, in the line where *gift* would have stood, is made to strike it and write *grant*. No one calls this a rule. It is how the ink goes.

They do not cut grafts, tend Trees, or carry scions. A sickening Tree is a warden's crisis and a door-keeper's fight, and a Book-hand will only write the delay into a contract.

There is no Speaker-equivalent, no clerk whose reading cancels the other. If two Book-hands disagree on a clause, they write both readings and date them. That is not a schism. That is a book.

### How civic settling actually runs (the hour)

This hour is for a Struck whose *Tithe* cannot be lived in this town. It is not for a Bound's supernatural Terms.

1. The Struck sits, and the Book-hand names what is already true, in the words the house uses: *you carry a voice; you carry a purpose.*
2. They name what cannot be paid *here*, a hall that is not in this town, or a warmth no one will give.
3. They find a year a neighbour can see being paid, which may be passage to a release-house, a warrant, or a seventh day.
4. They find someone to **stand for** the person ([[Law and Citizenship]]). The Book-hand is not themselves a pillar unless they are Bound, Long-Lived, or Far-Voiced.
5. If that fails, the mark is *owing, not broken*. The civic book stays open, and the quay learns the name.

### How a Bound petition-watch runs (if they sit it)

1. The Bound speaks the ask, and the Book-hand writes the ask, not the answer.
2. They wait, and they do not invent a clause to fill the silence.
3. If nothing answers, they close the hour, the old Terms still stand, and the house was honest.
4. If something answers, they copy the new clause *as heard*. They do not improve the wording, and they do not take a fee for "getting a better deal."

A Book Alone hand stops at step 1 and sends the Bound home. They will not play at a table they believe is empty. That is also honest.

### What they carry

They carry a **stamp** that is personal, not a guild-licence. The ticket that lets a guild work is a different paper, and it belongs to [[The Stillers]], [[The Hall-Keepers]], and [[The Element-Guilds]]. They carry ink, and a cord-knife for striking a line. They sell, or give, the small **personal books** to children of the house. Those books are not the Threnhael, and a clerk who confuses the two has started a fight. On a Bound's oath-day they have the year's working contract in hand, and they read it aloud *as written*, not as improved.

A "high house" is a room with old copies and a better lamp. It is not a palace. The next quay does not write here for leave.

### What they refuse (the job's edge)

They will not show a personal book to the crown, and a campaign that subpoenas one is stealing a soul in *their* language. They will not call a Leaf-Fall a gift, and they will not retcon a Given child into a debtor. The book opens at the colour; it does not invent a storm. They will not ride out as contract-paladins, and they will not carry a scion. They sit. They will not keep a membership roll of Book-hands, because a roll would be a Threnhael. They will not rewrite a Bound's Terms, and they will not sell an easing of a Pledge. The clerk is not the Counterparty.

## Who may keep a table

**Not by Kind.** A [[Selkie]] quay-child and a human clerk hold the same stamp if they keep accounts honestly. Kind, the people a clerk comes from, is not the test, and Leddoren has no seal-chapter and no Kind-notary that would make one.

**By practice first.** You are taken as a hand when you are apprenticed at a table, and you are confirmed when a quay has watched you through a year's closings and at least one signing-watch. What you privately believe about whether anyone sits *across* the table, Counterparty or Book Alone, is the schism, and it is not the hiring test. Both branches refuse hidden terms. That is enough to share a desk.

**Condition leans, not Condition-gates.** A Condition, and the Tithe that comes with it or the lack of one, may lean a person toward the work. It does not lock the door.

| Novation of the Struck, which is the civic settling onto a year someone can pay | Bound; Wide-Hand Struck who settled | They have been on the far side of a bad term. | A Far-Voiced who can make a feeling unfakeable at the table ([[Law and Citizenship\|the three pillars]]) |

The feared-licensed are not barred. A Bound Book-hand is *ordinary* on Lestrand, and a theological problem in the Motherwood, where a written Term sitting next to gift-doctrine looks, to a Speaker, like this house leaking in. A [[The Stilled|Stilled]] at a table is rare and useful, because the room holds still for the clause. A [[Phoenix]] is not a clerk. There is no term that covers them.

Struck Book-hands must still be **vouched anew**. Civic settling is how the house *helps* that vouching, which is why the same kindness is also the lever. It still does not rewrite a Bound's clause.

## How they live in the three corners

**[[Polity Archetypes|Lestrand]] (the Settling-hands).** This is home weather, and here the faith *is* the profession. A high house is a proud old room on a rich quay, and it is still not a seat. When a Tree sickens, the delay can be written into a contract, and on this coast Cutting-leave can be brokered by the same people who close books. That brokering is Lestrand's clergy-sin, and it belongs to the *wardens'* fee, not to a Book-hand's stamp, except in the hour when a clerk "helps" a waiting town write the delay. Market-fade wears vestments here: the Tithe-poor Settler who cannot close the book is still a person the rich city steps around.

**[[Polity Archetypes|Vaethorn]] (Bargainers' clerks).** On the pilgrim edge of the Waiting Lands they are suspect, useful, and few. A Book-hand there is settling a Struck's year in a land that would rather take them to a Tree to be acknowledged as Given. The Hands-folk will eat with them if the year comes out kind. A Narrow-Hand preacher will not.

**[[Polity Archetypes|Threnmaieth]] (the Uncounted Book).** Private books offend the Tallied Crown. A personal ledger that is *not* the Threnhael is, to a Reckoned clerk, a second seeing the crown does not hold. Book-hands here work quietly, or not at all, or as commercial notaries who have learned not to say *Leddoren* on a docket. A campaign that subpoenas a Settler's book is stealing a soul in *their* language.

## The book that will not close (the order's injustice)

The house settles a person's year and closes the book. That is its kindness, and its cruelty. Every civic settling that saves a Struck who can pay is paid for, somewhere, by the Struck who cannot. The Book-hand sees one table. The quay sees a step-around. A settled Mother-name would not make the fading kind, and it would not turn a Bound's clause into a clerk's draft. The Fair Hand reads a cost as a price. The Book-Hands are that reading at a desk, helping, except where the desk has no right to write.

Their other exposed edge is the Bound they *cannot* help. A clerk who will not rewrite Terms will watch someone walk down the quay looking for a cellar that will lie to them. That cellar has a face now, which is [[The Holding Desk]]. The refusal to rewrite remains honest; it is no longer a reason the clerk cannot follow. The lived face of illicit Tithe-supply remains [[The Slide]]. Sold vouching is [[The Standing Trade]]. They do not stamp those books either. This is not a hunt and not a queue. The Road-hands already walk the scion they were given leave to plant.

## Economy of the house

**The stamp is the living.** On Lestrand a Book-hand is paid as a clerk is paid, by the signing, the copy, and the year's closing. The faith does not take a cut of the Tithe.

**High houses endow rooms.** Deathless money ([[Economy and the Tithe]]) likes a fair copy that will outlive a hull. That is proximity-wealth, not a Condition-tithe, and it is why the oldest rooms sit on the richest quays.

**Civic settling is badly paid.** The Struck who most need a hall and a warrant are the ones who can least stamp a fee. Some Book-hands stay until the candle dies. Some mark *owing* faster. Both are of the house.

They are **not** the Tithe-infrastructure sector, and they are **not** a safety-guild. They may write a term that *assumes* a venting-hall or a greens-share, and they do not furnish one. The door for that hall and that share is [[Tithe-Infrastructure]].

## In-world text

*This is a civic settling, written by a Lestrand quay-hand the week after a drowning came back Far-Voiced and could not pay a singer's Tithe in a town that had no ael-hall. The verso has the old storm-terms of *the year*, struck through — not a Bound's clause.*

> *Grant: the voice, already carried. Old term: a hall that is not here. New term: one honest outlet each seventh day at the release-house in Greyhull, passage stamped, warrant by M. Senn the hull-clerk. If the seventh day is missed, the book is owing, not broken. Walk, if you can walk. If you cannot, the quay will not call it a gift.*

The slip is the house's kindness: a storm-book rewritten so a person can live. "The quay will not call it a gift" is honest, and it is also how the quay steps around the person. Greyhull's release-house will take the seventh day. The eighth week the passage-money fails, the slip will still be fair, and the person will still owe a book that does not close.

## GM Notes

**What this is.** The Book-hands have no seat and many tables, and the faith itself is already on [[The Fair Hand]]. This note is the desk they sit at. It does not rebuild the faith.

**Canon.** Book-hands exist. They witness signings. Personal books are not the [[Naming in the Turning|Threnhael]]. They recruit by faith, by office, and by Condition-lean, and never by [[Kinds of the Turning|Kind]]. They do **not** rewrite a [[Bound]]'s Terms. That clause belongs to the Counterparty. They may *witness an ask*. They do not dictate one.

**The slip's last turn, read by the GM.** The house's whole case against the Mother is in the slip, and so is the fade confessing itself. "The quay will not call it a gift" is honest, and it is also the step-around, sacralized.

**Wrong about the verb, right about the ache — now wearing a stamp.** Per the keystone, Leaf-Fall is a gift, not a signing. Per [[The Other Hands]], *Orledd* answers sworn asks. The Fair Hand is right about that door and wrong that every becoming is a bargain. Do not let a Book-hand retcon a Given child into a debtor, and **do not let a Book-hand rewrite Orledd's clause.** A petition-watch is *keeping the door* (purchase). It is not acolyte-authority. Do not play them as devil-worship, as devil-acolytes, or as capitalism-cosplay. They are as sincere as the Orenhael.

**The Slide is the lived shadow house.** The Slide owns a Tithe-week, not a stamp. Do not give anyone a magic stamp that authors Terms, and do not fold sold-vouching or Bound-easing into either desk.

**Name collision to avoid.** *Orledd* (the Counterparty) is already *oren* + *ledd* in reverse. This house's liturgical name is **Leddhael**, not *Orenledd*. If you swap the coinage, do not land on Orledd's roots.

**The injustice is the closed book of the rich and the owing of the poor.** The Book-hand sees one table, the quay sees a step-around, and Lestrand's "the free" for the Kept is the same market wearing a theology. A Tithe-poor Settler is still a person the city steps around.

**Do not clone the Orenhael.** Give this house no seat, no four offices, no Road-hand circuit, and no graft-cart. Do not invent contract-paladins or Orledd-acolytes. Do not send Book-hands up the pilgrim road with a scion, and do not give them a travelling underdog. Their exposed edge is civic settling, and the Bound they cannot help. Tithe-infrastructure is [[Tithe-Infrastructure]]. The underdog there is [[The Intake]].

**Taste still open.**

1. **The name *Leddhael.*** Everyday *Book-hands* and *Settlers* are already in canon. You may swap the liturgical coinage, or drop it. Do not land on *Orenledd*.
2. **No membership roll.** A roll would be a Threnhael. The other clean option is that the oldest book-room in a port keeps a *table-list* of who may stamp that quay, and nowhere else.
3. **High house means age, not authority.** A first-among-equals, a yearly settling at the oldest room, is a later option if play needs a face. Do not grow a High Notary who sits like a pope.
4. **Civic settling is kindness and cruelty in one motion.** Bound Terms stay with the Counterparty.
5. **They are not Orledd's acolytes.** A petition-watch is the closest honest tie. Dictating terms would make them a devil-church, and it would flatten the Book Alone branch.

**Do not invent a Kumbaan counting-house or a Kind-notary.**

**Names.** The liturgical form *Leddhael* — LEDD-hael — is [[The Old Tongue|ledd]] "to reckon" + [[The Old Tongue|hael]] "whole": those who keep the account fair enough that you can walk. The Waiting Lands say *Bargainers' clerks*. The Ledger Coast says *the Settling-hands*. The Tallied Crown files them as *the Uncounted Book*.

**Hooks.** A personal book the crown wants copied. A civic settling the party is asked to warrant. A Bound who wants the clause changed and is sent down the quay. A petition-watch that answers, or does not. A high house that will not take Tithe-poor closings. Greyhull's seventh day coming due on the road.
