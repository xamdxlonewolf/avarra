# The Table-Keepers

The Table-Keepers are the working people of [[The Open Table]] (*Ndeyaan*), the hill-hosts on [[The Sundering Isle|Kumbaan]], and they are almost not clergy. When the faith travels at all, it travels as a Yumboe keeping an attic abroad, and that happens rarely. The storm-shore is a different job on the same isle, and [[The Shore-Sitters]] are the ones who keep it.

*(Mainland everyday: **table-keepers**, also **hill-hosts.** Own-name: **the Njaalo** — NJAH-lo — a [[Yumboe]] coinage in the night-keeping tongue, "the hall that is kept." Not Maiethren; this house owes the liturgy nothing. Sailors say **moon-hosts**. [[Polity Archetypes|Vaethorn]] has them as a sailor's tale. [[Polity Archetypes|Lestrand]] says **the Moon-Feast people** when a quay wants a story. [[Polity Archetypes|Threnmaieth]] does not file them — they are off the map.)*

**In one sentence:** they are the person who opens the hall, or the attic, and lays an extra place — on Kumbaan that is how a valley eats and how a stranger becomes kin, and on the mainland it is one room, one supper, and a sentence that will not travel past the door.

The faith's quarrel lives on [[The Open Table]]. It is two sittings set against a Turning-witness, which is belonging because a town watched you Turn, and it is also a tenth-year week that a host will not spend walking a child to a Tree. This note keeps the table itself: who is fed, what the host refuses, and what they carry.

## Shape of the order (at a glance)

Ndeyaan is almost not an order. It has no chapel, no college, no authority over colours, no graft, no book, and no door-walk that wants a title. The person whose hall the valley comes to on the bright nights is a table-keeper, and that is reputation rather than an office. When they die, the valley comes to the next hall that still sets the extra place.

There is one job, and there is no second office to be promoted into. The table-keeper keeps the table, lays the extra place, and on the second sitting says that no one is a stranger twice. That sentence is the whole of the work.

There is no seat across the sea, and there is no mission. A Yumboe who wants a hill-hall on [[Strandoren]] keeps Ndeyaan in whatever room they have, and they do not send for a scion, a stamp, or a Night Watch. The mainland does not send wardens to Kumbaan. The storm-wall is the boundary [[Faiths of the Turning]] already locked.

This is a hospitality-house. It is not a fairy-bargain, and it is not a guild of one Kind. The unseen hands fetch and pour as part of the hall, and you can see the cups move. They are not a geas, and sitting the table does not bind a guest into a debt. A human who has sat twice and will lay the place is of the table. A Yumboe who will not is just a neighbour who sleeps through the bright night. The house recruits by faith, not by Kind.

## What they actually do

The faith already named the rites ([[The Open Table#Practice]]). What follows is the table: who is fed, what the host's hands do, and what they refuse.

### If you meet one

On [[The Sundering Isle|Kumbaan]] a table-keeper looks like the person whose hill-hall the valley already knows. The playable hall, the one a group can actually use, is [[Ndenjoo]], and the host there is [[Njunda]]. They are small, pearl-pale, and silver-haired if they are Yumboe, and most are, because that is who lives there, and they open the door on the bright nights. By mainland noon they are often asleep. You would not call them a priest. You would go to their hall when the moon is up because that is where the food is and where a stranger is allowed to sit.

On the mainland they look like one person in a rented room who still sets a place no body will use. A sailor finds them by asking for the Moon-Feast. A clerk finds them by following a rumour and then files them as a guest. They cook, they pour, and on the second night they say the sentence.

They are useful the way a host is useful, which is to say they do not water Trees, stamp books, or walk drowning-stones. If you came for those jobs you have the wrong house.

### A bright night on Kumbaan (the job you can see)

At dusk the table-keeper opens the hill-hall, often because it was their grandmother's, and often because it was the only roof that still fitted the crowd. People arrive as the moon clears the Paps. The extra place is already set, slightly back and a little apart, and the unseen hands begin to fetch and pour, so that you can see the cups move. There are songs, and there is no reading of a colour and no reciting of terms. A Staying host says the dead's names when the dead fill that place. A Serving host does not say the names, and still fills the place.

In that hour the table-keeper opens, sets, and greets, keeps the extra place from being used as a spare stool, and sends a child for more bread if the hands have not already gone. If someone is sitting their second night, the host says the sentence. When the moon goes down the table-keeper sleeps, and daylight is not a fallen hour in this house. It is the time they keep for rest.

They do not confirm becoming, and there is no Leaf-Fall on the isle. They do not vouch a Struck in the mainland sense. Struck people are rare enough here to be a family matter, not a class ([[Yumboe]]). They do not licence anyone, they do not graft wood, and they do not file a person onto a roll.

### An ordinary supper (the job you can miss)

The extra place is not only furniture for the feast. A poor house lays it on a quiet night. To omit it is the one real impiety, not because the staying starve, since they do not, but because you have counted the household too small. The hands make this possible. That is not a metaphor they use. It is why a table-keeper can host beyond their means without becoming a Tithe-charity.

A child of the house learns the work by carrying dishes and by being told not to sit in the extra place. That is the apprenticeship. There is no Seat to confirm it.

### An attic on Lestrand (the same job, no hall)

A Yumboe on the mainland is a table-keeper **by necessity**. At morning, which is their night's end, they may work a quay kitchen, or mend, or copy, and they pay the rent in the local coin. In the evening they lay the extra place in a room that fits four. Someone climbs the stair. On the first night that person is a guest. On the second night the host says the sentence and means it. The quay will not file the guest as a citizen. The host will still set them a place on the third night.

This is how the faith travels, when it travels at all. It does not travel as a mission. There is no Njaalo chapter-house, no collection taken up for one, and no Road-hand of the moon. A second Yumboe in the same city may share a supper and not share a roof, and both of them are of the table. A mainlander who has sat twice and opened their own room is of the table too, if you take the faith-not-Kind lean.

### The second sitting (what "doing the job" *does* to a person)

The first night you are a guest, and a guest is already sacred. The second night you are of the table. On Kumbaan this is how a stranger becomes kin, nearly law, in all but parchment. On the mainland the same supper collides with [[Law and Citizenship|citizenship-by-witness]], because there you belong when your town watched you Turn, not because you ate twice.

A table-keeper who treats the second supper as belonging is doing their faith honestly. A warden who smiles and files them as a guest is doing theirs. Neither is a villain. The sentence does not cross the storm-wall intact. The host's job, abroad, is to keep the sentence inside the room and not pretend the clerk will honour it.

### What they carry

They carry an extra plate, the songs, and the sentence. On the isle they have a hall, inherited, or simply the one that still fits the crowd. On the mainland they have the rent and whatever the room will hold. They do not carry a stamp, a token from a land-door, a colour-scrap, or Cutting-leave.

The extra place is set slightly back from the board, not at the end of it. A mainlander who puts the plate at the foot, "for a servant," is corrected without a sermon, and the plate is moved.

### What they refuse (the job's edge)

They will not read a colour, tend a Tree, or keep a Night Watch. They will not turn the sentence into a Fair Hand term or into a geas, because the words are *eat, and be the household*, not *sign, and owe*. They will not test a guest. The storm-wall already did. They will not send a mission, collect for a hall across the water, or plant a Moon-Feast chapter in Vaethorn. They will not omit the extra place in order to look poorer, or to look more devout. That omission is the one impiety.

## Who may keep a table

**Not by Kind.** This is the order's load-bearing courtesy, and it is also already the faith. A mainlander who sits once is a guest, and a mainlander who sits twice is kin. A human, a [[Selkie]], or a [[Kitsune]] who will lay the extra place is of the Njaalo. A Yumboe who will not is not.

**By practice first.** You are a table-keeper when the valley, or the attic stair, comes to you and you set the place. No Seat confirms an apprenticeship. A child who has carried dishes on the bright nights is being watched, and a mainland convert who has sat twice and opened their own room is being watched by whoever they fed.

**The schism does the dishes.** A **Staying** host will say the dead's names. A **Serving** host will not, and will call the hands the hill's own courtesy. Both of them set the extra place, and both of them pass the same cup. The argument is for after the meal.

**Condition leans, not Condition-gates — and on Kumbaan, barely a lean.**

| Act | Often | Why | Also, and welcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hill-hall on the isle | whoever inherited the hall | There are no Given Conditions on Kumbaan, and the Kind is Yumboe because that is who lives there, not because the order requires it. | A rare Struck person who still hosts, and a mainlander who stayed after the second sitting. |
| Attic on the mainland | a Yumboe abroad, by necessity | They are still keeping Ndeyaan with whatever table they can make. | A mainland convert who sat twice, and a Kept clerk who likes the night better than the Tree. |
| Laying the extra place | anyone of the table | The one impiety is omitting it. | The poor house, as much as the deep-hill hall. |

The feared-licensed are not barred, and they almost never arise on the isle. A [[Phoenix]] is not a host the Njaalo have a rite for. A Bound person who sits twice is kin, and their Terms are not a grace said over the plate. The Open Table does not use the Fair Hand's terms and debts.

Struck table-keepers on the mainland must still be **vouched anew** if they want to *stay* in a town. The second supper will not satisfy a Watchful clerk. That collision is the weather of the house abroad, the ordinary condition of keeping this table off the isle, and it is not a failure of the hospitality.

## How they live in the three corners (and off the map)

The three corners barely reach this house.

**[[The Sundering Isle|Kumbaan]] (hill-hosts).** This is home. The Njaalo is simply whoever opened the hall. They keep the moon, not the solstice, and wholeness is the default, the way people already are when nobody is waiting to be chosen. There is no queue, no roll, no book, and no second reading of a person.

**[[Polity Archetypes|Lestrand]] (the Moon-Feast people).** On this coast the house is a quay-story, and an attic-host is a curiosity who also sets a good supper. Sailors print "no one is a stranger twice" as a proverb on a card. The Settling will not enforce that card. The attic-host will enforce the sentence inside the room, and cannot enforce it past the door.

**[[Polity Archetypes|Vaethorn]] (a sailor's tale).** The faith here runs hot and the reach of the Trees runs thin, so a table-keeper is a wonder if they ever arrive, and a test of how a Guest-reading household treats a guest. They will be fed. They will be asked about the Mother. They will not understand why a people need to be *chosen* in order to be wanted.

**[[Polity Archetypes|Threnmaieth]] (off the map).** They are not on the roll. A Yumboe in the Sacred Core is a diplomatic incident of manners, not a column the crown can fill. The crown cannot file a people who were never at a Tree. That is a relief and a slight at once.

## The sentence that will not travel (the order's injustice)

On Kumbaan, the second sitting is how you belong, and on the mainland you belong because a town watched you Turn.

The quarrel is played on [[The Open Table]]. The host who treats the second supper as kin-making is keeping Ndeyaan, and the warden or the clerk who files that same person as a guest is keeping a different grammar of belonging. The sharper clock is Turning-Week. A child of the table is in it, the host will not walk them to a Tree, a neighbour will, and a door shuts on the last day of Eolthael.

Romanticizing the Moon-Feast is itself a slight. They have no scion-queue and no mission, and an attic-host is one person at an ordinary supper, with no authority outside the room. That is enough.

## Economy of the house

On Kumbaan the hall is the living. A table-keeper is fed by the same feast they host, and the unseen hands are what make a poor house's extra place possible. There is no stipend, no Cutting-leave, and no share of a pilgrimage.

An attic pays its rent in the local coin. A Yumboe abroad works as anyone works. They are not a charity-order, and they are not part of the Tithe-infrastructure, so they may cook for a quay and still will not furnish a venting-hall.

There is no collection for a hall across the water. That collection would be a mission, and there isn't one.

## In-world text

*A place-card, badly copied by a Strandor sailor who did come back, and sold on a Lestrand quay as a dinner-grace. The verso is the sailor's own hand: "for luck at second sitting."*

> *We have counted you. Sit. If you have a name we have not learned it yet; if you have a colour we have no tree to read it. The moon is enough light. Eat, and be the household. — and if you sit again, you are not a stranger. This is not a proverb. Do not sell it as one.*

The card is a hospitality blessing, and the quay's copy has already half-ruined it. The sailor thought it was a grace. On the isle the same words are how you belong, a legal sentence in all but parchment. The last two lines were added by the table-keeper when she saw him copy the blessing, and he sold them anyway. Lestrand files the Njaalo as a Moon-Feast story, lucky if you eat twice. The woman in the attic still sets the place.

## GM Notes

**Moved from the body.** These limits used to be easy to lose, so they stay here in one place. Do not make a class out of the rare Struck on the isle, do not turn the Open Table into the Fair Hand's grammar of terms and debts, and do not invent a Night Watch on the isle. Do not turn the sentence into a term, and do not fold the isle into a grove.

**What this is.** The Table-Keepers are almost not clergy. The faith itself lives on [[The Open Table]], and this note does not rebuild it.

**Canon.** The rite is supper, there is no chapel, and there is no Kumbaan mission. This is not an order of one Kind only. On the isle the hosts are Yumboe because that is who lives there, and the names of the house are not settled.

**The card, read by the GM.** The house's whole case is that being wanted was never a gift, and the sailor who sold the card could not afford to see that. He thought it was a grace.

**Not of her household, and not a Kind-order.** [[The Other Hands]] is where the staying-kin stay what they are. They are not *Threnvaeth*, and they are not anyone else. Do not absorb Kumbaan. [[Kinds of the Turning]] already forbids a good-people-only order, a moon-chapter of any other Kind, and a clergy that only Yumboe may join, even though almost every hill-host on the isle is Yumboe, because that is who lives there. The convert in the attic is the proof that the rule holds.

**Not ghosts, not a geas, and not a mission.** The unseen hands are part of the house's ordinary work, the fetching and the pouring, and they are not a bargain that closes on the guest. "No one is a stranger twice" is a courtesy with legal weight on the isle, and it is a proverb on the quay. Do not turn the Open Table into a trap-cult or into a Fair Hand contract. Do not send table-keepers to plant a hall in Vaethorn. Do not send Road-hands to Kumbaan.

**The injustice is the sentence that will not travel.** Belonging by supper stands against belonging by witness, and a tenth-year week can close while both offices mean well. The keystone does not give them a Tree, and it does not make their wholeness into a missed gift. Play the quarrel on [[The Open Table]]. Do not invent a scion-queue of the moon.

**One job, and no offices, no seat, and no travelling circuit.** An attic-host's constraints are enough: one room, a plain supper, and no authority outside the door.

**Taste still open.**

1. **The name *Njaalo.*** Everyday speech already has *table-keepers* and *hill-hosts* in canon. The own-name is a Yumboe coinage, with a pre-nasal and open vowels, and it is not a collision with a given name on the *20260826* list. You may swap it or drop it. It is not Maiethren, and it is not *Ndeyaan* reused as the house's name.
2. **Mainlanders who sat twice may keep a table.** The working lean is faith, not Kind. The other clean option is that only isle-born hosts keep a table, and mainlanders remain kin without becoming hosts. That option leans toward a Kind-gate. Take it only if you want the order narrower than the faith.
3. **One job.** Do not add a "moon-speaker" office in a later polish.
4. **No mission.** Revisit that only if a campaign is the crossing itself.

**Names.** In mainland everyday speech they are *table-keepers*, and also *hill-hosts*. The own-name is *the Njaalo* (NJAH-lo), a [[Yumboe]] coinage in the night-keeping tongue meaning "the hall that is kept," and it is not Maiethren, because this house owes the liturgy nothing. Sailors say *moon-hosts*. Vaethorn has them as a sailor's tale, Lestrand says *the Moon-Feast people*, and Threnmaieth does not file them at all.

**Hooks.** You can start from Mbela's second sitting, which Sorel will not file, or from a tenth-year child on the landing in Eolthael, or from a sailor's place-card sold as luck. A mainland convert may open a room that a Yumboe will not share. A warden may be a guest on Kumbaan with no Tree to tend, and a rare Struck person on the isle is treated as family rather than as a class. If the session is on the isle, use [[Ndenjoo]], and with it Nolas, Soonke, and Saalo.
