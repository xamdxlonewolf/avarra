# The Pourers

People call them **the Pourers**, or **the Cup Circle**. Neighbours call the practice **the five-year cup**, because five years is what the circle asks of a member in all, and they smile when they say it, the way people smile about a rich uncle's hobby. The Motherfaith is content to call the whole thing a private devotion. [[The Fair Hand]] will not put a stamp on it, because a stamp needs someone on the other side of the deal and the Hand cannot see anyone there. [[The Watching]] counts it as one more cost of a long life and says it has nothing to do with the Mother. [[The Old Ways]] do not treat the cup as a sacred place or as a door, and [[The Open Table]] has no use for it.

The Pourers say they made a god, and they call it [[The Unspent|the Poured God]]. That claim is the centre of the practice. The years go into the cup, and the god, as they tell it, is the presence they made on the other side of the gift.

None of this is a Turning: no leaf falls, no warden stands up to Speak a colour, and the practice is not one of the five lived faiths.

## If you meet one

You meet a [[Long-Lived]] person with a small, ordinary cup. They have given some of their years of memory away, one year at a time, up to five, and they are missing those years, though they are still recognizably the same person. They will tell you they invented the god on the other side of the cup, and they will describe how the circle pictures it this decade, as a librarian, a quiet guest, or a child who never left home. The picture they use may change from one decade to the next, and the rule does not. Members give years, the cup keeps what they gave, and the cup cannot Give a Condition or save anyone from death.

**Molevad** *(mo-LEV-ad)* keeps the minutes of the best-known circle, on a long-house shelf in [[Polity Archetypes|Lestrand]]. Molevad comes from an old quay that the sea has worn down, is the oldest member in the room, and keeps both the book and the key to the shelf. Molevad is not a priest, and is not a Hand.

A second, quieter circle meets in [[Orenbren]], in an upper room of a lodging-house that once slept travellers. It keeps the same practice. It does not ask Molevad's circle for permission, and the two circles do not write to each other.

## The ask

Every [[Long-Lived]] person is asked once to join the Pourers, and the asking is done in person by a member. At the long-house shelf in [[Polity Archetypes|Lestrand]], a member of Molevad's circle asks. In the upper room in [[Orenbren]], a member of that circle asks. A Long-Lived person who lives in another town is still asked once, by a member who can reach them, and a person who has already been asked says so. That answer stands.

The invitation is a real one. The member asks because the circle will accept the person who says yes, and a yes is what makes them a member. From then on the member gives one year into the cup at a time, and five years is what the circle asks of a member in all.

A refusal is not punished. The person keeps their standing, people still consult them, and the work a court, a guild, or an archive already gave them stays theirs. A long-house will still seat a person who refused the invitation. Most Long-Lived people are not members, and every Long-Lived person still knows that the circle exists.

The members keep a library on the long-house shelf in Lestrand, and that shelf is the deathless library on the Ledger Coast. They copy [[The Old Tongue]], and they copy the common speech people already use. The copies stay on the shelf, beside the papers members have given with their years, and Molevad keeps the minute-book on the same shelf. The circle in the upper room in Orenbren keeps the records of its own meetings. People use the books and the copies, and the work of copying and keeping them is real.

They do not take over medicine. That work stays with [[The Stilled]], who stop a bleeding wound, hold a patient still through the surgery, and can stop a fight before someone is killed. Towns already call on the Stilled as surgeons, menders, and peacekeepers, and [[The Stillers]] licence that work and keep the infirmary.

## What they do

**The gift.** A member gives **one year** into the cup. Each year is its own night, with its own minutes, and the member may stop before the next one. Five years is what the circle asks of a member in all. Members say they give "the memories that year held, and the year itself." For each year given, that means three things.

**The memory goes into the cup.** The member puts one drop of their own blood in the cup, because a Long-Lived person already reads memory from blood, and afterward the member no longer has the memories of that year. The member still needs to keep learning, as every Long-Lived person does. What changes is that they no longer carry that particular year of other people's funerals.

**The papers go onto the shelf.** The member also gives the long-house shelf everything they wrote or kept in that year, the letters, the account books, the diaries, and the court notes. The papers are not put in the cup. They are filed on the shelf under the member's name, where other members can read them, and where a visitor Molevad allows can read them too. That filing is one reason the shelf in Lestrand holds so much. Letters name other people, and those people were not asked before their letters went onto the shelf.

**The circle counts the member one year younger.** At a long-house that keeps the circle, a Long-Lived person's age and standing are counted only in the years they still remember, so for each year given the circle counts the member one year younger. A member who has given three years is seated as if they were three years younger. Among the Long-Lived, age decides who speaks first, who judges a hard case, and who keeps a key, which means every year given costs a member rank inside those long-houses. Courts, guilds, banks, and Long-Lived people who are not members keep counting the ordinary way. The same person can be a bank's most senior clerk and still sit below a younger member at the long-house.

**The shared store.** Members can read the cup the way a Long-Lived person reads a drop of blood. What they read is true, and it is also mixed, so one reading can hold memories of the same year from more than one member. The circle values that mix. They say the knowledge goes to the god they made. In practice the cup is where the memories are kept, and the circle is the group that reads them.

**The minutes.** There are no priests. The oldest member writes down who gave which year, how the circle described the god that decade, and whether anyone asked to stop, and there is one member who has kept the book for so long that the circle cannot imagine meeting without them.

**Leaving.** A member may stop before the next year, or leave the circle. The years already given stay in the cup. The circle gives back the papers from those years and does not give back the memory, and members call the papers a **thinner copy** of what was given. The real cost is social. The circle treats the person as someone who weakened the god, so other Pourers will not read blood with them, and Long-Lived houses that pay for the long-house will not give them a seat. They are free to go, and they will be alone among the other people who do not age.

| Who | Why they give | What the circle will not do |
|---|---|---|
| A Long-Lived who has buried too many people | The ordinary Tithe is the need to keep learning, and the heavier cost is outliving everyone they love. | It will not make anyone Long-Lived, because they already are. |
| A younger member who wants the shared store | They want to read memories of years they did not live. | It will not take years from someone who is not Long-Lived. The cup is only for the deathless. |

| A neighbour who wants a god | They come from curiosity, from pity, or for a good story. | It will not set the cup up in a public square, and it will not ask a warden to Speak it. |

## What they refuse

They will not try to Give a Condition, and they will not Strike someone into one, because the cup cannot do either. They will not assign work by Condition. They are not a crown, and they are not [[The Walled Book]]. They say every gift is freely given. They will not become a sixth faith on [[Faiths of the Turning]]. The practice stays in the household, and it stays small.

## How they reach a party

A Long-Lived patron has given years away and wants company who will not ask which years are missing. A player character may be invited to watch a night's gift, and they cannot give unless they are Long-Lived. A member who wants to leave needs someone to sit with them in a long-house that will not seat them. Molevad hires a runner to fetch a member who missed the night of their next year.

| [[The Protectors]] | A hospice cares for the dying, and the Pourers give their own memory. | The two have nothing to do with each other. A hospice is not a cup. |
| [[The Walled Book]] | A Closed Heir who is Long-Lived may join, and the Book does not care about cups. | Ranking people is not the same thing as giving away your own memory. |

In **Lestrand**, the Pourers are an ordinary hobby that happens to have a library key. In **Vaethorn**, a preacher who needs a sin may call the cup an insult to the Mother's gift, and most households will not notice it at all. **Threnmaieth** wants the minutes on its roll. Molevad's circle files nothing the crown could use.

## In-world text

*From the minutes on the Lestrand long-house shelf, in Molevad's hand. Someone drew a face in the margin and scratched it out twice.*

> *One year from S. into the Cup We Made. Face this decade: the quiet guest. Blood as record. The circle read a year that was not only S.'s. We do not write which. If S. asks the years back, the return is thinner and the copy stays. If S. asks the cup for a leaf, the cup keeps what it already has. We made the listener. We will not stand it in a square. We will not say Mother. We will say the shelf.*

These are the circle's own words. They record a member, S., giving one year, and they say that a reading of the cup held memories of a year that belonged to more than one person. They also say what happens if S. asks for the years back: the papers come back, and the memory stays in the cup. The scratched-out face in the margin is the circle changing its mind about how to picture the god.

## GM Notes

### The taking

Inside the Pourers there is an **inner circle**, and most members do not know it exists. It does not work through Molevad's minute-book.

The inner circle chooses a [[Long-Lived]] person for the knowledge that defines their life. That might be a judge with three hundred years of rulings, or the last person who can read a dead hand. The person does not have to be a member, and someone who refused the invitation can still be chosen.

The inner circle takes the person, holds them, and keeps them until they give into a cup. The means are confinement, isolation, and threats. Shutting a Long-Lived person away from anything new is already a known cruelty, and the inner circle uses it on purpose. Some victims are deceived instead. A member is told that this is an ordinary one-year gift, and the inner circle chooses what goes in. No new power is involved. The gift works the way every gift works, and the difference is that the person did not choose it.

What is taken is personal memory and the understanding the person spent years earning: the cases a judge learned from, the reasons a scholar trusts one source over another, and the people they loved and why. The person keeps their speech and their ordinary skills, can still recognize the world around them, and knows what is gone well enough to say so. They are not starving in the Tithe sense, and the rules on the [[Long-Lived]] card still apply to them as written. Each victim loses something different, so no two of them are the same afterward.

The taken memory does not go into the shared store. It goes into a second cup, the one on the shelf nobody will talk about. The **founder** reads that cup. The founder is a Long-Lived person who started the Pourers and is still alive. Do not name the founder yet. A Long-Lived person forgets nothing they take in, so the founder keeps all of it.

The founder believes that with enough of this knowledge they will become a god. The founder also believes the attempt could ruin the very things they want to keep safe, and has accepted that risk. The notes record that belief. They do not say the founder can become a god, and they do not say the world could end. Player-facing text never calls the founder a god, a forgotten deity, or a threat that will end the world.

Ordinary members still give a year at a time of their own accord, and that is still what most of the Pourers do. The line "They say every gift is freely given" in the player section is the circle's claim. It is true of the ordinary members.

### The papers and the count

Each year given has three parts. The memory goes into the cup, the papers go onto the shelf, and the circle counts the member one year younger. The papers are not in the cup, and they are not part of [[The Unspent]]. They are ordinary paper on an ordinary shelf.

The papers are how a party can learn about a gift without reading the cup. A member's letters, account books, and court notes from the years they gave are filed under their name, and Molevad can let a visitor read them. A person named in one of those letters may want it back. The circle's answer is that the letter was the member's to give, and that argument is a real one a party can be hired into.

The taking is different. It still does not go through Molevad's minute-book, and the papers of a person the inner circle took from do not go onto the open shelf. Where those papers go is not written.

The count is a long-house custom, not a power. It changes where a member sits and who speaks first. It does not change their body, their Tithe, or anything on the [[Long-Lived]] card.

### How a party meets it

The harm a party can meet is one person, someone Long-Lived who has lost the knowledge that defined their life and can tell you so, though they may not know who took it. Run that meeting at about five percent frightening. Daily life with the Pourers is courtesy and real scholarship, and the inner circle is the one hidden thing underneath it.

### Keep these apart

Keep the three groups apart, because they do not share a job. The Pourers are the people who give away their own memory, [[The Walled Book]] is the house that ranks people, and [[The Protectors]] are the house that catches Phoenix fire and hides it behind worship. Do not merge any two of them into one description.

The Pourers will not take [[Phoenix]] fire. Another house does that, the Protectors, and the Pourers do not know its name. Do not merge the cup with a Protector sanctuary. A Walled Book clerk who ranks people is not a Pourer either.

A single drop of blood still lets a Long-Lived person read a true fragment, and it still does not harm the person who gave it. The taking is not that drop.

The Pourers are not part of [[The Isolated Fall]], and they have nothing to do with [[Harrow's Green]] or the Phoenix.

[[The Unspent]] stores what it is given and does not make plans. It cannot Give a Condition or Strike someone into one, and it does not feed on people. The founder is a person, not this presence.

### Other notes

The Pourers' faith is built on experience, on the funerals and on the fade, and on what they do, which is the gift, rather than on a creed. Each circle governs itself, and there is no head of the faith. They give memory away so they can keep living. The argument inside the house is about whether a member may stop. It is not about doctrine.

Play them sad and a little vain. Do not play them as a blood cult, or as the origin of vampires.

The library is the shelf already in the long-house in Lestrand, and the other circle meets in the upper room in Orenbren. Do not add a library, a city, or a medical house. Medicine stays with the Stilled.

The Pourers are not a sixth door of [[The Other Hands]]. Do not give a date or a month for the nights of giving.

Molevad comes from seed `20260832`, on the Worn list, toward the mid-bottom. The circle has no name of its own. People name the long-house instead.

[[Reimaethe]] is a seed. They have given four years, one at a time, and want to stop before the fifth. They stay offstage in [[The Isolated Fall]]. Do not plot them here.

The hooks you can reach for are the fifth year coming due, the second cup, a reading that holds more than one person's memories, a long-house that will not seat a member who left, Threnmaieth asking for the minutes, and a Long-Lived person who has lost the knowledge that defined their life.
