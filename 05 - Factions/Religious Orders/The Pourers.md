# The Pourers

People call them **the Pourers**, or **the Cup Circle**. Neighbours call the practice **the five-year cup**, and they smile when they say it, the way people smile about a rich uncle's hobby. The Motherfaith calls it a private devotion. [[The Fair Hand]] will not stamp it, because they cannot see anyone on the other side of the deal. [[The Watching]] counts it as one more cost of a long life, and says it has nothing to do with the Mother. [[The Old Ways]] do not treat it as a sacred place or a door. [[The Open Table]] has no use for it.

The Pourers say they made a god, and they call it [[The Unspent|the Poured God]]. That claim is the centre of the practice.

It is not a Turning. No leaf falls, and no warden Speaks a colour. It is not one of the five lived faiths.

## If you meet one

You meet a [[Long-Lived]] person with a small, ordinary cup. They have given five years of their memory away. They are missing those years, and they are still recognizably the same person. They will tell you they invented the god on the other side of the cup. They will describe how the circle pictures it this decade: as a librarian, a quiet guest, or a child who never left home. The picture changes. The rule does not. Members give years. The cup keeps what they gave. The cup cannot Give a Condition, and it cannot save anyone from death.

**Molevad** *(mo-LEV-ad)* keeps the minutes of the best-known circle, on a long-house shelf in [[Polity Archetypes|Lestrand]]. Molevad comes from an old quay that the sea has worn down, and is the oldest member in the room. Molevad keeps the book and the key to the shelf. Molevad is not a priest and is not a Hand.

A second, quieter circle meets in [[Orenbren]], in an upper room of a lodging-house that once slept travellers. It keeps the same practice. It does not ask Molevad's circle for permission, and the two circles do not write to each other.

## The ask

Every [[Long-Lived]] person is asked once to join the Pourers. A member asks them in person. At the long-house shelf in [[Polity Archetypes|Lestrand]], a member of Molevad's circle asks. In the upper room in [[Orenbren]], a member of that circle asks. A Long-Lived person who lives in another town is still asked once, by a member who can reach them. A person who has already been asked says so, and that answer stands.

The invitation is real. The member asks because the circle will accept the person who says yes. A yes makes them a member.

A refusal is not punished. The person keeps their standing. People still consult them, and the work a court, a guild, or an archive already gave them stays theirs. A long-house still seats a person who refused the invitation. Most Long-Lived people are not members. Every Long-Lived person knows the circle exists.

Members keep the library on the long-house shelf in Lestrand. That shelf is the deathless library on the Ledger Coast. They copy [[The Old Tongue]], and they copy the common speech people already use. The copies stay on the shelf. Molevad keeps the minute-book on the same shelf. The circle in the upper room in Orenbren keeps the records of its own meetings. People use the books and the copies. The work is real.

They do not take over medicine. That work stays with [[The Stilled]]. One of the Stilled stops a bleeding wound, holds a patient still through the surgery, and can stop a fight before someone is killed. Towns already call on the Stilled as surgeons, menders, and peacekeepers. [[The Stillers]] licence that work and keep the infirmary.

## What they do

**The gift.** A member gives **five years** into the cup: the memories those years held, and the years themselves. The member puts one drop of their own blood in the cup, because Long-Lived already read memory from blood. Afterward the member no longer has those memories. The member still needs to keep learning, as every Long-Lived does. What changes is that they no longer carry five years of other people's funerals.

**The shared store.** Members can read the cup the way a Long-Lived reads a drop of blood. What they read is true. It is also mixed: one reading can hold memories of the same year from more than one member. The circle values that. They say the knowledge goes to the god they made. In practice, the cup is where the memories are kept, and the circle is the group that reads them.

**The minutes.** The oldest member writes down who gave, how the circle described the god that decade, and whether anyone asked to stop. There are no priests. There is one member who has kept the book for so long that the circle cannot imagine meeting without them.

**Leaving.** A member may ask for their years back. They receive a **thinner copy** of what they gave, not the years themselves, and a copy stays in the cup. The real cost is social. The circle treats the person as someone who weakened the god. Other Pourers will not read blood with them. Long-Lived houses that pay for the long-house will not give them a seat. They are free to go, and they will be alone among the other people who do not age.

| Who | Why they give | What the circle will not do |
|---|---|---|
| A Long-Lived who has buried too many people | The ordinary Tithe is the need to keep learning. The heavier cost is outliving everyone they love. | It will not make anyone Long-Lived. They already are. |
| A younger member who wants the shared store | To read memories of years they did not live | It will not take years from someone who is not Long-Lived. The cup is only for the deathless. |
| A member who wants out | Their next five-year gift is coming due | It will not lock the door. It will make leaving cost them their friends and their seat. |
| A neighbour who wants a god | Curiosity, pity, or a good story | It will not set the cup up in a public square. It will not ask a warden to Speak it. |

## What they refuse

- They will not try to Give a Condition or Strike someone into one. The cup cannot do either.
- They will not take [[Phoenix]] fire. Another house does that, and the Pourers do not know its name.
- They will not assign work by Condition. They are not a crown, and they are not [[The Walled Book]].
- They say every gift is freely given.
- They will not become a sixth faith on [[Faiths of the Turning]]. It is a household practice, and a small one.

## How they reach a party

A Long-Lived patron has given five years away and wants company who will not ask which years are missing. A player character is invited to watch a gift. They cannot give unless they are Long-Lived. A member who wants to leave needs someone to sit with them in a long-house that will not seat them. Molevad hires a runner to fetch a member who missed their fifth year.

In **Lestrand**, the Pourers are an ordinary hobby that happens to have a library key. In **Vaethorn**, a preacher who needs a sin may call it an insult to the Mother's gift; most households will not notice. **Threnmaieth** wants the minutes on its roll. Molevad's circle files nothing the crown could use.

## In-world text

*From the minutes on the Lestrand long-house shelf, in Molevad's hand. Someone drew a face in the margin and scratched it out twice.*

> *Five years from S. into the Cup We Made. Face this decade: the quiet guest. Blood as record. The circle read a winter that was not only S. We do not write which. If S. asks the years back, the return is thinner and the copy stays. If S. asks the cup for a leaf, the cup keeps what it already has. We made the listener. We will not stand it in a square. We will not say Mother. We will say the shelf.*

These are the circle's own words. They record a member, S., giving five years. They say that a reading of the cup held memories of a year that belonged to more than one person. They say what happens if S. asks for the years back. The scratched-out face in the margin is the circle changing its mind about how to picture the god.

## GM Notes

### The taking

Inside the Pourers there is an **inner circle**. Most members do not know it exists. It does not work through Molevad's minute-book.

The inner circle chooses a [[Long-Lived]] person for the knowledge that defines their life. That might be a judge with three hundred years of rulings, or the last person who can read a dead hand. The person does not have to be a member. Someone who refused the invitation can be chosen.

The inner circle takes the person, holds them, and keeps them until they give into a cup. They use confinement, isolation, and threats. Shutting a Long-Lived away from anything new is already a known cruelty, and the inner circle uses it on purpose. Some victims are deceived instead. A member is told this is an ordinary five-year gift, and the inner circle chooses what goes in. No new power is involved. The gift works the way every gift works. The difference is that the person did not choose it.

What is taken is personal memory and the understanding the person spent years earning: the cases a judge learned from, the reasons a scholar trusts one source over another, the people they loved and why. The person keeps their speech and their ordinary skills. They can still recognize the world around them. They know what is gone, and they can say so. They are not starving in the Tithe sense, and the rules on the [[Long-Lived]] card still apply to them as written. Each victim loses something different, so no two are the same afterward.

The taken memory does not go into the shared store. It goes into a second cup, the one on the shelf nobody will talk about. The **founder** reads that cup. The founder is a Long-Lived person who started the Pourers and is still alive. Do not name the founder yet. A Long-Lived forgets nothing they take in, so the founder keeps all of it.

The founder believes that with enough of this knowledge, they will become a god. The founder also believes the attempt could ruin the very things they want to keep safe, and has accepted that risk. The notes record that belief. They do not say the founder can become a god, and they do not say the world could end. Player-facing text never calls the founder a god, a forgotten deity, or a threat that will end the world.

Ordinary members still give five years of their own accord. That is still what most of the Pourers do. The line "They say every gift is freely given" in the player section is the circle's claim. It is true of the ordinary members.

### How a party meets it

The harm a party can meet is one person. They are Long-Lived, they have lost the knowledge that defined their life, and they can tell you so. They may not know who took it. Run it at about five percent frightening. Daily life with the Pourers is courtesy and real scholarship. The inner circle is one hidden thing underneath.

### Keep these apart

- **Three groups.** The Pourers give away their own memory. [[The Walled Book]] ranks people. [[The Protectors]] catch Phoenix fire and hide it behind worship. Do not merge any two of them.
- **The drop of blood.** A single drop of blood still lets a Long-Lived read a true fragment, and it still does not harm the person who gave it. The taking is not that drop.
- **Not the opening.** The Pourers are not part of [[The Isolated Fall]] and have nothing to do with [[Harrow's Green]] or the Phoenix.
- **The presence.** [[The Unspent]] stores what it is given and does not make plans. It cannot Give or Strike. It does not feed on people. The founder is a person, not this presence.

### Other notes

- **Belief.** The Pourers' faith is built on experience (the funerals, the fade) and on what they do (the gift), not on a creed. Each circle governs itself, and there is no head of the faith. They give memory away so they can keep living. The argument inside the house is about whether a member may stop. It is not about doctrine.
- **Tone.** Sad and a little vain. Do not play them as a blood cult or as the origin of vampires.
- **The ask.** The library is the shelf already in the long-house in Lestrand. The other circle meets in the upper room in Orenbren. Do not add a library, a city, or a medical house. Medicine stays with the Stilled.
- **Names.** Molevad — seed `20260832`, Worn list, mid-bottom. The circle has no name. People name the long-house instead.
- **[[Reimaethe]] is a seed.** They have given four years and want to stop before the fifth. They stay offstage in [[The Isolated Fall]]. Do not plot them here.
- **Hooks.** The fifth year coming due; the second cup; a reading that holds more than one person's memories; a long-house that will not seat a member who left; Threnmaieth asking for the minutes; a Long-Lived who has lost their life's knowledge.
