# The Holding Desk

When the honest stamp has already closed and a person still has clauses to sign, the everyday name for the people who will write the next one is **the Holding Desk**, and the people themselves are **hold-clerks**. They keep a back room under an honest table, and the oldest chair is on [[Orentel]]'s lower slip.

A [[Bound]] is a person who signed a contract and is still living under its **Terms**, the clauses that outlast a single year. The civic year is the honest account of that year, and a Book-hand can close it upstairs, but the desk opens when the year is payable and the Terms are not. In [[Polity Archetypes|Lestrand]], the Ledger Coast, people say *the Holding Desk* the way they say *extension*, as if a second term were ordinary business. In [[Polity Archetypes|Vaethorn]], the Waiting Lands, a preacher has no clean word for it and calls it **the second signing** when they want a sin. [[Polity Archetypes|Threnmaieth]], the Tallied Crown, files nothing, because a private Term that is not the [[Naming in the Turning|Threnhael]], the crown's written roll of persons, is already a problem, and this desk is that problem with a chair.

They are not [[The Slide]], the people who rent a week of green, an hour, or a page when someone's upkeep is about to fail in public. The desk works in **clauses**, and it does not rent that week.

## If you meet one

You would know them by the ink, and by a stool that does not face the quay. They look like a junior Settler, a Book-hand who settles a year at the honest table, who lost the front room. You would find them if you asked *who still writes after the book is closed.*

**Mutelo of the Lower Slip** *(moo-TEL-o)* keeps the oldest chair on [[Orentel]] and speaks the worn-down speech of the quay. They will tell you they kept a person walking, and the book on the shelf is labelled *extensions*. The last page is not.

## What they actually do

A [[Bound]] finishes a civic year at an honest table, and the year is payable while the **Terms** are not. Fear of the Pledge, the payment the contract still holds over them, is what starts the next ask. The hold-clerk does not invent that fear. They name a smaller Term that covers the larger one for a season, and they call that an extension.

That pipeline is how the desk works: each signing eats more of the person, until the last signing, which is the [[Bound|Given-Over]], the person the guilds call the Forfeit. The law has never decided when the person on the stool stopped being one ([[Law and Citizenship]]).

| Who comes | What the desk does | The limit |
|---|---|---|
| A Bound behind on a Pledge | They write a covering Term, name a day, and extend. | They will not recite the old Terms as if they were gone. The honest table still has them. |
| A family who will pay to keep a sibling "working" | They hold the person as an asset that still signs. | They will not call this kinship, and they will not take them to a door-keeper to be named *kin*. |
| A creditor who wants a walkable instrument | They match a Counterparty who will accept the next signing. | They will not stamp a civic year. That is a Book-hand. |
| A Struck Bound, raw from the signing that made them | Sometimes, if the ask is already live. | They will not sit [[The Intake]], the shed for a raw Struck. That is the wrong shed. The Whisper a Bound hears when they fall behind cannot command, and this is still a table. |

They **extend first**. That is what they believe about themselves. The withhold is the next clause, measured, never quite the last one until it is.

## What they refuse

They will not rent a Tithe-week, a week of the upkeep the Slide sells, whether that week is dirt, a Cry, or a page. They will not sell *I watched you Turn.* That trade belongs to [[The Standing Trade]], which sells a witness rather than a clause. They will not steal a scion, a living cutting of wood, and they will not Speak a colour, which is a warden's naming and not a clerk's. They will not rewrite a Term the Counterparty has already named. They add a Term, and that distinction is how they sleep. They will not work a wreck on [[The Sundering Isle|Kumbaan]], because there is no Bound table on the sand.

A dead Bound is a ruined book, and the desk does not want one. They still walk people toward the Forfeit.

## How they reach a party

A Bound friend is being refinanced, which is the word they use for the next covering Term. A Book-hand's *owing* walks downstairs and does not come back up. The party is asked to witness a signing that is "only a season." A Given-Over case has no settled law, and it needs a witness who is not the creditor's. Mutelo will hire runners. They pay in extensions.

## How they live with the other houses

| House | Everyday | The fight |
|---|---|---|
| [[The Book-Hands]] | They work upstairs and downstairs of each other. A Settler who marked *owing* has made a customer. | It is the same Bound in the same week, and the clerk who follows them down has started a case. |
| [[The Slide]] | The product is different, and a Bound may be on both books. | Money is not a clause. A week-keeper who starts writing Terms has changed houses. |
| [[The Standing Trade]] | A Forfeit who needs to cross a border may buy a witness. | A false childhood will not pay a Pledge. |
| [[The Tree-Wardens]] | There is almost no contact. A quiet receiving-rite does not close a Term. | Wardens carry grafts, living wood for a Tree, and they do not carry Terms. |
| [[The Reckoned Offices]] | A private Term that Threnmaieth cannot file is a leak in the crown's count. | Menirein wants the name on a roll. The desk wants the name off any roll that is not theirs. |

**Lestrand** is home weather for the desk, the coast where an extension already sounds like business. **Vaethorn** calls it a sin and still uses it when a Bound would otherwise become a rope-story. **Threnmaieth** cannot file the desk without admitting the channel failed, so the hold-clerk is left looking like any other clerk.

## In-world text

*An extension-slip, Orentel lower slip, pinned under a stool. The verso is last season's slip, the date scratched once.*

> *Covering Term, one season. The upstairs year is closed. This is not that year. Come before the Pledge-day. If you cannot come, send no one — a clause is not a child. We do not call this a gift. We do not call it a sale. If you have already signed, come anyway. The next Term is the same Term until it is not.*

The writer sounds like a clerk being practical. What the desk sells is *not paying yet*, and the slip never uses the word *Forfeit*. "If you have already signed, come anyway" is the house's whole case: becoming a Bound did not save anyone from needing them.

## GM Notes

They think they are in a refinancing story. Play them that way, and avoid the stock devil's advocate, the soul-seller, and a night-clone of the Book-Hands. The form stays clerk and creditor. What they know, what they want, and the role they fill do not match that form, because they want the book to continue. A Given-Over is a failed extension they will still file as competence.

The moral parallax sits at two distances: a causal one, the sentence "I kept them from the Pledge," and an informational one, the quay being glad not to see the stair. The engine is accumulation, because each covering Term seems small, and the comfort that keeps the town complicit is the word *extension*. Orledd does not receive the spiral ([[Bound]]; [[The Other Hands]], where Orledd's receiving is kept).

Opposition can act. They will push a person toward the Forfeit when the book requires it. They prefer a walking instrument, and that preference is not a lock. A desperate hold-clerk will write the last Term.

They are not the Slide, and they are not the unused Back Table. The Back Table stayed unused as a *Slide pick*. This desk is a later face for the pipeline the economy already named. Do not merge it with The Standing Trade, and do not let a Road-hand become a hold-cart.

Mutelo of the Lower Slip comes from seed `20260831`, off the eroded list, picked mid-bottom. Avoid a median name like "Cassian Vale."

Seed, and do not plot, a Bound who wants the next Term refused, and a hold-clerk who will not let the book end.

The hooks already on the table are the twice-dated slip, a sister at the stair, a personhood case with no rule, a Book-hand and a hold-clerk in one room, and a Counterparty who starves on unread extras and wants *one* kept Term.
