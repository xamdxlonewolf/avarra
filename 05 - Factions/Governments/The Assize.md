# The Assize

> **The players' body.** A travelling commission of inquiry — a circuit court that rides province to province, holds a sitting, settles what the local machinery couldn't, and rides on. It is one of the three separated powers in [[The Shape of the Empire]] (Governors hold, Circuits judge, the Rolls count), and it is the reason "the courts are fair, even to the conquered" is *true* rather than propaganda: the judge has no local stake and does not stay to profit from the verdict. This is the campaign's week-to-week engine. **Name is provisional** (working label — the naming pass owns the final call); role titles below are provisional too and flagged.

## What it is, and where its power stops

The Assize is a small, self-contained court on the move. It carries the centre's warrant to hear and determine — but nothing else. It is deliberately a *scalpel, not a standing authority*: it renders judgment and then leaves, which is precisely what makes its judgment trusted.

| The Assize **can** | The Assize **cannot** |
|---|---|
| Hear and rule on cases referred up or petitioned to it | **Govern.** No taxes, no garrison, no public works, no appointments — that is the Governor. |
| Summon witnesses, compel testimony, and examine sites during its sitting | **Keep the count.** It may *consult* the [[The Shape of the Empire\|Rolls]], but the ledgers belong to the scribal service. It can flag an error; it cannot amend one. |
| Overturn or confirm a local magistrate's ruling on appeal | **Legislate.** It applies imperial law and weighs it against local custom; it does not make law. |
| Judge the Governor's own officers — the cases no local court has standing to touch | **Rule the province after it leaves.** Ongoing enforcement reverts to the Governor. Its authority is *present-tense only.* |
| Issue writs and execute them through its own small detail (or a requisitioned one) | **Reach beyond its sitting.** Once the court rides on, it is just travellers on the road again. |

That last line is the whole point. A Governor is powerful and permanent and *local*; the Assize is powerful and momentary and *from away*. Neither can become the other, and each is the check the other cannot corrupt.

## How a case reaches the circuit

Most disputes never do — they are settled by town magistrates and the Governor's administration, as they should be. What kicks *up* to the Assize is the residue the local machinery cannot cleanly handle:

- **Conflict of standing** — the Governor, his officers, or the local court are themselves a party. No one may judge his own house; it waits for the circuit.
- **Cross-jurisdiction** — parties from different provinces or under different customs, where no local court has clean standing.
- **Weight** — capital matters, or disputes large enough to threaten public order.
- **Deadlock or doubt** — a local court that split, stalled, or is suspected of a thumb on the scale.
- **Petition** — and this is the famous one: *the Assize will hear anyone.* A single farmer may lay a grievance against the richest notable in the province, and the court is bound to hear it. This open door, more than any statute, is why the conquered trust it.

Ahead of the court's arrival, the Governor's clerk assembles a **docket** — the season's stored-up matters, sworn petitions and referred cases both. The court arrives to a province that has been *saving up its trouble.* That backlog is the case-of-the-arc.

## The commission (structure & roles)

An Assize rides as a **mixed body by deliberate policy**: a court that must judge every people fairly is staffed from every people, so no party can claim it is stacked against them. That policy is the in-world reason the party looks the way a player party looks — and it is the "job, not identity" frame made concrete. Each PC takes a **seat** (a function); *who* fills it, and whether they privately love or despise the Empire, is entirely player-authored.

| Seat (provisional) | Function | Maps naturally to |
|---|---|---|
| **Presiding Justice** ("the Justice") | Holds the warrant; runs the sitting; signs the verdict. The commission's head. *(Default: an NPC — see Fork.)* | the party's patron / anchor |
| **Examiners** (assessors) | The field arm — gather facts, question witnesses, walk the ground, make arrests. *Several seats.* This is where most PCs sit. | fighters, rogues, rangers, scouts, a physician-examiner |
| **Intercessor** (liaison) | Bridges tongue and custom — translates, explains local law, negotiates with notables. Sees both truths at once. | a face/social PC, ideally from a conquered people |
| **Serjeant** ("the arm") | The court's own muscle — bailiff, protection, executes writs and summonses during the sitting. Commands a small detail. | a warrior, guardian, ex-soldier |
| **Recorder** (clerk of the commission) | Keeps the record, drafts the writings, files the verdict with the centre, and *reads the Rolls.* | a scholar, a knowledge PC |

Specialists attach to the docket as needed (a physician for a suspicious death, a surveyor for a boundary, a scribe seconded from the Rolls) — so any Daggerheart party finds a real seat without forcing. The **argument is meant to live inside the commission**: a true-believer Justice's-clerk and an ex-rebel examiner at the same table means the moral parallax is a party conversation, not a PC-vs-NPC lecture.

## The riding season & the route

The Assize does not ride year-round. There is a **riding season** keyed to the passable months of the river-and-road spine (see [[Climate & Terrain]]) — the court sets out from the Aurenne after the roads open, works down the Vaur and its branches in a known order, holds one sitting per province, and returns to the centre before the season closes. Off-season it winters at the capital, where the Justice reports and next year's dockets take shape.

That rhythm *is* the campaign structure. The known route down the ring turns the map into a season:

- **[[The Aurenne]]** — placid, prosperous, low-stakes. The tutorial province: teaches the procedure while the peace still feels total.
- **[[The Halden]]** — proud and content, but where hill-custom and imperial statute grind. The verdict is easy; the *feelings* are not.
- **[[Kessad]]** — raw, recently taken, contested gratitude. The moral heat: cases where the just ruling still wounds, and the rebellion recruits from the losers.
- **[[The Outmark]]** — the eerie cold edge. Garrison matters, the last milestones, and the quiet that shouldn't be so quiet.

## Running an arc

Each province is a self-contained arc built on one rule: **the case resolves satisfyingly, and leaves exactly one thing that doesn't add up.**

1. **The docket lands.** The court arrives; the clerk lays out the stored-up matter.
2. **The examiners work.** Testimony, sites, the Rolls — real investigation, real stakes, real people.
3. **The commission deliberates and the Justice rules.** *Genuine justice is done* — this is non-negotiable (see the design contract below). The verdict should feel *earned and fair*, even when it costs someone.
4. **One seed is left un-explained.** A deed marked "reconciled." A name the Rolls can't account for. A fort that reconciles something on a schedule. Never explain the seed in the arc that plants it — let it *rhyme* with the next one, sessions later.

| Province | A case that fits | The seed it can leave |
|---|---|---|
| Aurenne | inheritance / water-rights / merchant fraud — teaches the tools | an old deed struck through and marked *reconciled* |
| Halden | hill-custom vs. new statute; a blood-debt the law won't honour | the young who are Halden-proud and no longer Empire-grateful |
| Kessad | which court, and *whose memory*, the law will honour | two of everything — and who quietly benefits from the imperial answer |
| Outmark | a garrison discipline case; something missing at the line | a ledger that "reconciles" the forts on a fixed calendar |

## Session zero

The GM imposes exactly one fact: **you all ride with this commission.** Everything else is the table's.

1. **Pick a seat** from the roster above — party composition maps straight onto real functions. Fill the seats you have players for; the rest are NPCs (the Justice usually is).
2. **Author the person.** *Why are you here, and how do you feel about the Empire?* Translator from a conquered people, ex-rebel on amnesty, careerist true-believer, paid foreign contractor, disgraced noble's child — the stub's roster is a menu, not a cast.
3. **Open in the Aurenne** with a small, winnable case that teaches the procedure while the world still feels comfortable. The seam waits.

The pitch to players is one line: *you are the fair court that rides in when no one local can be trusted to judge — and you are very good at your job.*

## GM Notes

`visibility: gm` — walled off; this note is player-safe.

- **Naming decision (2026-08-12, preserved):** the euphemism **"reconciliation"** is deliberately kept *out* of the players' job title, to avoid burning the word before [[The Cost]]'s mechanism (task 2) defines what it means. The court keeps a plain, mundane name (**"the Assize,"** provisional). "Reconciliation" is reserved for its quiet home — frontier garrison ledgers, old deeds, and the scribal "reconcile the rolls" (see [[The Outmark]], [[The Shape of the Empire]]) — where it stays a rare, creepy seed rather than everyday PC vocabulary.
- **This is `positional-revelation` bait, and its integrity is the trap.** The commission's ordinary duties walk it *straight up the seam* — it adjudicates "whose memory the law honours" in [[Kessad]], it consults the Rolls, it visits the silent forts of the [[The Outmark]] — and it never knows. **Keep the court genuinely, admirably fair.** The horror in [[The Cost]] is not that the players run a kangaroo court; it is that they dispense *real* justice, beautifully, for a system whose foundation they cannot yet see. Complicity is the water (see [[Themes]]). A corrupt Assize would collapse the whole premise — do not do it.
- **The Recorder is the natural first eye on the seam.** The seat that *reads the Rolls* is where a struck name or a scheduled "reconciliation" first snags — a slow, deniable thread, never confirmed for a long time.
- **The referral path is a future conduit, but not yet.** How "reconciliation" matters might one day move *through* a court like this is task 2's to build (the selection mechanism). Seed only; do not invent the mechanism here.
- **Forks flagged for the user** (defaults chosen to keep moving):
  - *Is the Presiding Justice a PC or an NPC?* — **Default: NPC** (the party's anchor, questgiver, and moral mirror; matches the stub's roster, which lists *staff*, not a judge). A table may hand the seat to a PC, but then rule the *verdict is the commission's, the signature ceremonial*, so no one player lords over the others.
  - *Does the Assize have teeth of its own?* — **Default: a small personal detail** (the Serjeant's arm) for protection and executing its writs; anything larger must be *requisitioned from the Governor.* Recommended to keep — the muscle the court may need to investigate a Governor belongs to that Governor. Excellent friction.
  - *Prestige track or dumping-ground?* — **Default: both.** Circuit service is genuinely prestigious (you wield the Empire's fairness) *and* a hardship posting (always moving, never home, judging strangers). That duality is exactly what staffs it with careerists *and* misfits — which is what we want.
- This note is `player`-safe as written; nothing above the GM Notes should let a clever player conclude the Empire is secretly evil. The machinery is genuinely good. That is the point.
