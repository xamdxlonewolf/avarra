# Leddvael

> **The Reckoned Gift.** *(formal *Leddvael*, LEDD-vael · [[The Old Tongue|ledd]] "to reckon" + [[The Old Tongue|vael]] "gift.")* The name has eroded the way names erode on this coast. It keeps the doctrine in the writing: a gift is a bargain that has been priced. **They do not rewrite Bound Terms.**

People say **the Reckoned Gift** and **Gift-clerks**, while charters and learned maps write *Leddvael*. Devotion to the Motherfaith is slight on this coast, and what they wear in its place, as civil law, is [[The Fair Hand]].

## Overview (public)

Leddvael is a signing-coast on [[Strandoren]] that took [[The Fair Hand]] off the dock, where it had been a theology of terms, and made it a **civic rite**. Leaf-Fall, the festival when a child stands at the town's Tree, is also a signing-watch, with clerks there to see the term opened. The Tithe, the upkeep a person needs in order to carry what they have become, is treated as a term to be serviced. The [[Bound]], people who swore a contract and are held to it, are model citizens here — not feared, *employed* as the shape of honesty. Governance sits in the middle: the book works as a kind of statute, and it stops short of becoming a census of souls. They will not copy [[Polity Archetypes|Threnmaieth]]'s list, and they will file a contract.

**Site.** This is a different stretch of indented coast from Lestrand, with enough water to have grown its own houses. It is a salt quay with a signing-watch, and Fair Hand clerks and Motherfaith tree-tenders work in the same wind. The held prestige berth stays on the Ledger Coast.

**Seat.** [[Naenor]] is the port where the signing-watch sits, slightly smaller than [[Orentel]]. It competes with the Ledger Coast, and it wins the term and loses the berth: the signing happens here, and the prestigious dock does not. Towns and villages along the rest of that coast stay unnamed. The [[The Book-Hands|Book-Hands]] still have no seat of their own, and they still do not rewrite Terms. Naenor is not a second Orentel.

**How it formed.** Fortune-and-contract practice thickened until a charter-coast found it cheaper to *establish* the Settling, the Fair Hand's signed close, than to argue hymns at every dock. After [[The Grey Summer]] the witnesses had died, and a Bound's word still closed a bargain, so the civic rite is that year made into a seal. No prophet founded it. A habit became a statute.

**What they remember.** A Given child, one the Tree marked at Leaf-Fall, is a term opened. In the Grey Summer, honesty was in short supply, and the Bound were the people who kept it. The chronicle is [[The Other Count]].

## Structure & Membership

A charter-council sits over the book-tables, where the [[The Book-Hands|Book-Hands]] stamp the signings and recite the Terms. They have **no seat**, and they **do not rewrite Terms**. The coast does not recruit by Kind, the ancestry a person is born with. Gift-reading families live here, the households that still hear a colour as a gift rather than a price, and they are the minority that sounds devout and looks like a credit risk.

## Goals & Methods

They keep the signings clear, and they vouch a Struck person briskly, reading that later change — someone who came into it after Leaf-Fall, not at the festival — as a later contract. Motherfaith talk of gifts is allowed through the town. It is unpriced language, and unpriced language is not the language they prefer.

**Its injustice.** The harm is **bargain-theology as statute**. A Given child is a term opened, and a household that insists the colour was a gift is a bad debtor in a signing-town. The Bound are safe here, and they are *over-read*: the person the world already uses as a contract-pillar is asked to be the world's whole person. There is no villain-faith in that, only a sincere ledger laid over real Conditions, the changes people actually carry.

## GM Notes

Leave the districts unfleshed, and do not clone the [[The Book-Hands]] as a state church. The seat is [[Naenor]], so do not move the signing-watch onto Orentel, do not give the Book-Hands a seat, and do not census the rest of that coast.

**The Fair Hand is not villain-theology** ([[Strandoren]] GM Notes). Leddvael is that reading made civic: wrong about *who*, because no bargain was struck, and sincere, useful, and crushing in the specific case.

**Do not rewrite Bound Terms**, because the book-hands' lock travels with the polity. On the theology knob, Motherfaith gift-devotion stays low while the rival practice stays high, and that split is not a reason to invent a fourth axis for the Fair Hand.

**Hooks.** A gift-reading family can stand at a signing-watch, a Bound's Terms can be recited in the square as civic theatre, a Motherfaith warden and a Book-hand can share a Leaf-Fall, and someone can be vouched as a contract who wanted a blessing.
