# The Hall-Keepers

> **What this is.** The lived face of **official outlets** from [[Economy and the Tithe]] and the **Far-Voiced ticket** from [[Law and Citizenship]] — one lintel, because the seam Condition *is* the building. The ael-hall is a concert house until it is a release-valve. They furnish *enough* voice that a person can walk until tomorrow. They are not [[The Slide]]. Built with `economic-systems`, `governance-systems`, and `moral-parallax` for [[Roadmap|Epic 5, Story 5.3]].

> **Canon status.** 🟡 **Proposed** (the liturgical name, one lintel vs two). 🔒 **Locked upstream:** Far-Voiced sit on the self-paying / provided-for seam; a Cry is a real bystander hazard; licences are guild-issued; scheduled outlets are how the Tithe is paid in public. Recruits by practice / office / Condition-lean, **not Kind**.

*(Everyday: **hall-keepers**, or **ael-hands.** Liturgical, 🟡: **the Aeloren** — ayl-OR-en — [[The Old Tongue|ael]] "to air; to let feeling out" + [[The Old Tongue|oren]] "to witness": those who *hear you vent and will say you did.* [[Polity Archetypes|Vaethorn]]'s second word is **gift-hall folk**; [[Polity Archetypes|Lestrand]]'s is **release-hands** (ordinary, like saying *box-office*); [[Polity Archetypes|Threnmaieth]] files them as **counted-hall clerks**.)*

**In one sentence:** they sell you an hour in which you may feel in earnest — and they will not open after the lamps.

## Shape of the house (at a glance)

- **One building, two jobs.** The hall *furnishes* the outlet (Tithe-infra) and *stamps* the Voice-ticket (safety-guild). Splitting them would put a college on a playhouse. The same mouth that says *your hour is the third* says *you may stand in a crowded room.*
- **The hour is enough.** A scheduled vent — song, weeping, a relay-call, a laugh that empties the chest. The person should leave emptied and able to work. [[The Slide]]'s shut room is one outlet that is never quite enough. This house wants you gone until tomorrow.
- **They think they are in a playhouse story.** Bills, benches, who carries the high note. They do not call themselves a police of feeling. The ~5% dial stays at the off-hour Cry — not at a hunt for loud people.
- **No high hall with authority.** The oldest playhouse on a Lestrand quay is old. The next town's hall does not write to it for leave.

## What they actually do

### If you meet one

Ink on a finger, or chalk. A slate of hours. A hall that smells like sweat and lamp-oil and last night's joy. You would buy a bench and not know which hour was "church." You would find them if you asked *where a voice can be loud without becoming a Cry in the market.*

They are useful the way a well is useful, and the way a master's ticket is useful. A town with Far-Voiced and no hall has a Book-hand writing *owing* and a watch waiting for the market.

### Who comes, and when

| Who | Why they come | What the hall-keeper does | What they will not do |
|---|---|---|---|
| A Given [[Far-Voiced]] child, after the colour | The voice must be trained before it is a hazard | Apprentice them: breath, projection, venting on purpose. The ticket comes when the town can stand next to them. | Will not Speak the colour. The warden did that. Will not call the voice a gift on Lestrand. |
| A working Far-Voiced (performer, crier, witness) | The vocation *is* the vent — self-paying | Give them the stage, the relay-post, the stand. Stamp the year if the ticket is due. | Will not invent a crisis so they can vent off-slate. |
| A Far-Voiced who lost the honest stage | Isolated posting, dull labor; the Undertow is close | Sell or grant a **scheduled hour**. Empty them. Send them out. | Will not open after the lamps. Will not keep them half-full so they return tonight. |
| A Struck Far-Voiced, raw (the drowning-door) | The voice came back untrained | Hand them to [[The Intake]]. Sit the first hour *with* Intake if the Cry is already in the room. | Will not stamp a ticket on a raw throat. Will not leave them in the market. |
| A Book-hand with a settling | The term *assumes* a seventh-day hour | Honour it if the slate has a hole. Mark *full* if it does not. | Will not pretend Greyhull has a bench when Greyhull is full. |
| Anyone who came for a play | The hall is also the playhouse | Sell a bench. Take the coin. Let the working voices pay their Tithe *as* the show. | Will not stop a performance to process a ticket. The show *is* the Tithe for the people on the boards. |

### An ordinary week (the job you can miss)

**Morning — the slate.** Hours named. Relay-posts listed. A child's breath-lesson in the cold hall. The ticket-desk is a stool by the door.

**Evening — the house.** Feeling in earnest, on purpose, where a neighbour paid to hear it. This is the pleasant version of the Tithe, and it is also the town's entertainment. No one calls that a coincidence.

**The unswept bench.** One seat is never swept. *That's the first Cry's seat.* No one will explain which Cry. A first-year who sits there is moved without a sermon.

When a voice misses its hour, the keeper does not open the side door. They know a room that still shuts. They do not say the room's name.

### What they carry

A slate. A stamp (the Voice-ticket — competence, not a census). Chalk. A key. No book of weeks. No colour-scrap.

### What they refuse (the job's edge)

- They will not open after the lamps for a missed hour. That is the gate, in felt form.
- They will not keep you half-vented. Enough means empty.
- They will not stamp a raw Struck. That is [[The Intake]].
- They will not rewrite a Bound's Terms because a voice was used to swear them. Different table.
- They will not run a mission-hall on [[The Sundering Isle|Kumbaan]].

## Who may keep a hall

**Not by Kind.** A [[Selkie]] crier and a human neighbour hold the same stool if they can keep a slate and hear when a voice is about to break.

**By practice first.** You are of the house when the town still comes to your hour, and when you have pulled a child off a Cry before it landed.

**Condition leans, not Condition-gates.**

| Act | Often | Why | Also, and welcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| The boards, the relay, the stand | [[Far-Voiced]] whose vocation *is* the vent | self-paying | a Kept who can keep the slate without needing the hour |
| The ticket-stool | a Given Far-Voiced who trained young | they know what competence feels like in the throat | a sharp Kept; a Long-Lived who remembers last century's Cry |
| The first hour with a raw throat | whoever will sit it | Intake borrows this room | a Struck who remembers the drowning and will not flinch at the wet sound |

A [[The Stilled|Stilled]] on the door is rare and useful (a Cry held a beat). Do not make them the face. A Bound who stamps tickets is money-adjacent; keep them off the stool unless the town is Lestrand and already treats the faith as a profession.

## How they live in the three corners (and off the map)

- **[[Polity Archetypes|Vaethorn]] (gift-hall folk).** A barn, a festival, a blessing said over the first note. Almost no ticket-stool. A Struck throat is a late gift and a hazard the valley will stand around, singing, until someone who has been to a real hall arrives. Soft injustice: you were given the hour, you will spend the voice as the valley asks — crier, witness, hymn.
- **[[Polity Archetypes|Lestrand]] (release-hands).** The playhouse *is* the hall. Posted hours. Guild-ticket as master's papers. A voice without coin for a bench still has a labour-hour at the back if they will sweep. A voice without even that goes to week-rent.
- **[[Polity Archetypes|Threnmaieth]] (counted-hall clerks).** The crown furnishes the counted hall and can withhold the hour. Tickets are crown-warrants in all but the stamp. A Cry off-slate is a column. The hall looks like a festival. The leash is the hour.
- **[[The Sundering Isle|Kumbaan]].** No. A bright night is not an ael-hall.

## The scheduled hour (the house's injustice)

The hall sells (or grants) the right to feel in public *on time*. Off-time is a ticket problem. A performer who lost the honest stage still has the hour — if the slate has a hole, if the season-fee was paid, if the crown still lists them.

The town is glad. An emptied voice is not a Cry in the market. The Warm can say the gift was aired. The quay can say the bill was posted. The crown can say the channel held.

Confirming she is real does **not** make the eighth week kind. She gave a voice that must vent. She did not build the slate. R2 is the whole point.

They have no scion-queue. They have no gate-of-dirt. They have no Grey-clock (that is the Stillers). Do not clone those. This house's constraint is the hour — analog benches, one slate, no authority after the lamps.

The first Cry's bench stays unswept. That is not a theology. It is how the hall remembers.

## Economy of the house

- **The bench and the stamp are the living.** Ticket-fees, playhouse coin, a civic stipend for the free hour (Warm), a crown channel (Watchful).
- **Self-paying voices subsidize provided-for ones** when the polity lets them — the show *is* some people's Tithe and other people's night out. Lestrand calls this the market. Vaethorn calls this tending. Both can be true in one barn.
- **They are not the Slide.** A shut room after close is another house.

## In-world text

*An hour-slate, Lestrand release-house, chalked on the door beside tonight's bill. The verso is yesterday's slate, not washed. `oblique-worldbuilding`.*

> *Third hour: the boards (paid). Fifth: relay-drill, apprentices. Sixth: open hour, two benches, no coin if you sweep. After the lamps: the house is shut. A missed hour is a missed hour. We do not keep a throat half-full. If you have already cried in the street, this is still the house — come at open, not now. If you cannot wait, we do not know a room.*

On its face: a playhouse being practical, almost kind (the sweep-hour). Underneath: the slate confessing the gate, and the same lie the greens tell — "we do not know a room." The first Cry's bench is not on the slate. Everyone knows which one it is.

## GM Notes
*(Stripped on player-safe export. Keyed to the keystone via `reveals: [leaf-mother-is-real]`.)*

- **They do not know what story they are in.** Defaults: magic-police of loud people, opera-church, nice Slide. Rotate. Form stays playhouse + ticket-stool (load-bearing). They want a town that does not Cry in the market, and a bill that fills. They do not want a client who comes back tonight.
- **One lintel is the point.** Far-Voiced are the seam Condition. The building that empties them is the building that stamps them. Split only if play proves the ticket needs a different mouth — and even then, do not grow four offices.
- **Moral parallax.** Distances: temporal (the hour) and informational ("we do not know a room"). Engine: exchange — every open hour is a bench someone else waits for. Comfort: the word *hour*. R2: she did not build the slate.
- **The unswept bench is inscrutable.** Do not explain which Cry. Moving a PC off it is local manners.
- **Do not clone the Slide or the Road-hands.** The injustice is the scheduled hour.
- **Name collision.** *Aeloren* sits near *Vaeloren* / *Leddoren*. Everyday *hall-keepers* is already in canon. Swap or drop the coinage if it blurs at the table. Not *Aelledd* (Slide-adjacent). Not *Aelbren* (collides with Vaelbren).
- **No Kind-gate. No Kumbaan export. No high-hall pope.**
- **Hooks.** A seventh-day term on a full slate; a raw drowning in the market and no Intake for three days; a counted hall late on purpose; a sweep-hour that is the only hour a PC can afford; the unswept bench after a Cry no one will name; a junior who started opening after the lamps.
