# The Back Table

> **What this is.** A **rogue mainland house** — not a fifth faith, not clergy, not Kind-bound. They do the work the honest tables will not: sold vouching, illicit Tithe-supply, off-book Bound brokerage. Seeded in [[Economy and the Tithe#The shadow economy]]; pulled forward as a lived faction because [[The Book-Hands]] do not rewrite a Bound's Terms, and [[The Table-Keepers]] are not a mainland order. Built with `moral-parallax` and `cliche-transcendence` for [[Roadmap|Epic 5, Story 5.2]]. 🟡 **Proposed** — names and how ugly the Bound-brokerage gets are taste-open.

> **Canon status.** 🟡 New. 🔒 **Locked upstream:** the shadow economy exists (illicit Tithe, off-book contracts, sold vouching); Bound Terms belong to the Counterparty; recruits by practice / Condition-lean / desperation, **not Kind**; ~5% scary (ordinary people after the lamps, a real obstacle underneath). They are **not** a devil-church and **not** [[The Open Table]] relocated.

*(Everyday, when named at all: **the Back Table.** Also **the Quiet Stamp.** Liturgical / cant, 🟡: **Nethledd** — neth-LED — [[The Old Tongue|neth]] "night" + [[The Old Tongue|ledd]] "to reckon": the night-account. Honest Book-hands call them **the other hour.** The crown, when it catches a name, files **off-book** and does not print a theology.)*

**In one sentence:** they are the clerks and brokers who stay after the honest stamp is put away — they will sell you a warrant the square did not watch, a Tithe the hall will not furnish, and (they say) an easing of a Bound's clause that no Book-hand will write.

## If you meet one

By day they often *are* a clerk. Same ink, same quay, sometimes the same guild-room. After the lamps they are in the room behind, or the cellar, or a boat that does not take a passenger list. You find them by being told *not that table — the one after.* A Tithe-poor Taken-In finds them because the greens are gated. An unvouched Struck finds them because no Long-Lived will stand. A Bound finds them because the Book-hand recited the Terms and would not change a word.

They do not wear a mark. A mark would be a roll. They look like someone who will take your coin and your name and not ask who watched you Turn.

## What they actually do (the three trades)

**Sold vouching.** Citizenship is witnessed, not recorded. If no one watched you Change, you need someone to stand. The Back Table sells a stander. Most of what they sell is thin — a clerk who will say they were there, a Bound whose word was bought, a Far-Voiced paid to *feel* a belonging that is not true. The one thing they almost cannot fake is a Long-Lived's memory. When they do, a town has been lied to for a century.

**Illicit Tithe.** The scary edge of [[Economy and the Tithe]]: you are sliding, the hall is closed, someone in the cellar has green or an outlet or a book you should not read. Lucrative. Cruel. The customer is already at the Tithe-poor line.

**Off-book Bound work.** This is the trade the honest house refused. A Bound wants the clause changed. The Back Table will take the coin.

- **Most of the time it is a lie.** They write a softer sentence on a scrap and tell the Bound the Counterparty accepted it. The Terms do not move. The Whisper still comes. The Bound has paid to be fooled.
- **Sometimes they run a real petition in the ugly hours** — blood, a worse Pledge offered, an ask spoken where the honest table will not sit. If something answers, the new clause is still not theirs. They only kept a door no one should have kept that way.
- They do **not** walk around with the authority to dictate Terms. That would make them the Counterparty. They are not.

They are not a Night Watch, a grove-walk, or a moon-feast. They are not a Kumbaan export.

## Shape (why they are not a church)

- **No seat, on purpose.** A seat can be counted. Threnmaieth would love a column. They do not give it one.
- **No liturgy they will admit.** The Fair Hand's *fair wind, fair term* is said here as a joke, or not said.
- **Recruits by the work.** Tithe-poor clerks, unvouched Struck, Bound who already broke a Term, a Kept who likes the free life a little too off-book. **Not by Kind.** No Fox-cell, no Yumboe-cell.
- **The ~5% dial.** Most members want rent and not to slide. A few enjoy the leverage. Play them as neighbours who stayed late, not as a cackling cult. The obstacle is real: you can buy a belonging that was never witnessed, and someone else paid.

## How they live in the three corners

- **[[Polity Archetypes|Lestrand]].** Home weather. The richest shadow, because the honest table is busiest here. Market-fade upstairs; the Back Table downstairs selling the fade a delay.
- **[[Polity Archetypes|Vaethorn]].** Thinner, meaner. A sold warrant in a town that lives by watching you Turn is a deep sin. They still exist — the unvouched still pay.
- **[[Polity Archetypes|Threnmaieth]].** The crown wants this on the roll and cannot keep it there. Off-book is a hanging word. The trade goes quieter and more expensive.

They have almost no weather on [[The Sundering Isle|Kumbaan]]. Do not invent a Back Table under a hill-hall. The isle's belonging-rule is supper, not a bought warrant.

## The sold belonging (the house's injustice)

Every warrant they sell is a hole in citizenship-by-witness. Every illicit Tithe is a hall denied to someone who waited in the day. Every fake easing is a Bound who will fall further behind, sure they already paid.

Confirming the Leaf-Mother is kind does not make a bought voucher true. Confirming the Counterparty is real does not make a cellar-stamp into a new clause. R2 holds.

## In-world text

*A scrap passed under a Lestrand cup, no stamp, no name. The verso is blank on purpose. `oblique-worldbuilding`.*

> *The front table recited and would not write. Come after the second lamp. Bring coin, not a colour. If you want the clause kinder, say so. If you want a face who will stand in the square, say so. Do not bring a crown-clerk. Do not bring a warden. We are not a church. We are the hour they close.*

On its face: a broker's card, a little proud. Underneath: the honest house's refused job, for sale — and a blindness the scrap cannot afford. "If you want the clause kinder" is the lie most buyers need. The hour they close is also the hour the Whisper still keeps.

## GM Notes
*(Stripped on player-safe export. Keyed to the keystone via `reveals: [leaf-mother-is-real]`.)*

- **They are not Orledd's ordained acolytes.** Per [[The Other Hands]], *Orledd* answers sworn asks and wears the face the Bound gave. Purchase is higher where the oath-door is kept. An ugly petition-hour can be *kept*. That is not a licence to dictate Terms, and it is not a player-facing devil-church. Most Back Table "easings" are fraud. The rare real answer is still Orledd's clause, copied in a cellar. Do not give them a magic stamp.
- **Do not make them the campaign's Dark Brotherhood.** ~5%. Ordinary people, late lamps, a real hole in witness. A few enjoy it. Most are sliding.
- **Do not fold this into [[The Open Table]].** Hospitality is not a geas and not a sold warrant. No Kumbaan chapter.
- **Do not make them a Kind-order or a fifth faith.** They are the shadow of institutions that already exist.
- **Taste still open.**
  1. **The name *the Back Table* / *Nethledd.*** Swap or drop.
  2. **How often a cellar-petition is real.** Lean: rare. If you want them scarier, raise the hit rate — you will also raise the "are they a church?" risk.
  3. **Whether a corrupt Long-Lived voucher exists in play.** Economy already says their corrupt vouch is catastrophic. Use sparingly.
  4. **Whether this waits for Story 5.3.** Tithe-infra will want this underside. Having a face for it now is useful; do not let 5.3 clone them as the official guilds.
- **Hooks.** A Bound who bought an easing that did not take; a sold warrant the party is asked to trust; a Book-hand who recognizes their own apprentice in the cellar; a Long-Lived whose "I remember you Turned" is a lie a century deep; a Tithe-poor Taken-In whose smuggled green was cut from someone else's commons.
