# The Two Papers

> The Two Papers are what happened when a hymn-country would not share a page. Twenty-nine summers after the Retreat, the old gift-country of the Sacred Core tried to write its people down, and half of it refused. Everyday speech calls the split **the Two Papers**. [[Polity Archetypes|Threnmaieth]] calls it **the year the reckoning stood**. [[Maiethvael]] calls it **the year they would not be named**. It is a succession of desks, not of blood. The chronicle of the summer sits in [[The Other Count]].


## What happened (as believed)

After [[The Closing]], the grove had a college sitting beside it and lodging-hearths gathered around it. The wider hymn-country, dense with trees and still talking about gifts, with no Closed Seat left on the gate, went on singing the same hymns. It did not yet have two flags.

A reckoning-desk opened in a river-town that would later be counted as part of [[Maiethlir]]'s country. **[[Limrae]]** *(lim-RAY)* wrote persons down because, as they said, a gift you cannot find again is a gift you have already failed to tend. The Closed Seat had ranked people. Limrae wanted to *keep* them. It is the same hunger for ink, and it claims the opposite kindness, if you believe the recto, the front of the page.

**Manril** *(MAN-ril)* kept the older proof, in the plenty-country that would not take the new name. You belong because you were seen to Turn, and you stay whole because your neighbours furnish what you were Given. To write a person onto a roll is to say the three pillars failed, the seeing and the furnishing a town was supposed to manage without a list. Manril would not sign the roll. They would not be named to it.

There was no battle worth a banner. There were two desks, two refusals, and a summer after which the same hymn was sung over two different papers. Households chose a desk the way they now choose a stance. Some cousins still share a Turning-Week, and will not share a clerk.

Threnmaieth dates a **crown-count** from that summer. A devout clerk may write Cut-year 387 and Crown-year 320 on the same slate, and the difference between those numbers is sixty-seven. That difference *is* the split.

Maiethvael dates nothing of the kind, and they will tell you the year is a river-habit.

## What each paper inherited

| | [[Polity Archetypes\|Threnmaieth]] | [[Maiethvael]] |
|---|---|---|
| The hymn | They kept it. | They kept it. |
| The people | They wrote them down. | They witnessed them, and did not write them down. |
| The Closed Seat's hunger | They turned it into a care-count: everyone kept on the roll, and nobody ranked out for having a gift. | They turned it into a refusal: no list at all. |
| The injustice | The care became a sanctified surveillance. | The piety works as a social tax. |
| The claim | We are the older tending. | We are the older proof. |

Both claims are true of the person speaking them. Neither claim is a dynasty of blood. A later crown that wants a founding-ancestor will try to make Limrae a first king. The river-town knows they were a clerk.

[[Orenbren]] was not in the room. They house people, and they do not sing this argument. The [[The Walled Book|Inner Close]] watched two lists be born that were not the Book, and filed both of them as other people's ink.

[[The Watching]] had already refused to count years from the Cut. A second count did not tempt them.

## Visibility markers

- A debt in Threnmaieth carries two numbers, an Eoloren year and a crown year. A neighbour from Maiethvael will convert neither of them.
- Cousins who share a Tree and will not share a clerk. It is the old split household of the Hands years, done with paper instead of with a walk.
- Limrae's name on the preface of a roll, and Manril's name on no roll at all, repeated in the minutes of guilds as *the one who would not be named.*
- An heir of the Close who says, correctly, that Threnmaieth's list is not their Book, and who is still insulted when Menirein, the registrar ([[The Reckoned Offices]]), writes them unmarked.

## In-world text

*This is a guild-minute from the plenty-country, in Manril's summer. Later clerks of Threnmaieth file it as Crown-year 0, and they will not read the last line aloud.*

> *Limrae will write the colour and the person in one breath. We will furnish the colour and leave the person to the square that saw them. If this is a slight, it is a slight against a desk, not against the Mother. Do not send their tablets. We are not unmarked. We are unlisted. Those are not the same hunger, and we will not feed theirs.*

Both desks can hold the same hymn.

## Consequences

- **The hymn-country of Maiethorn is two powers.** They share the *maieth*, and they split gift from reckoning, which is the pair of names [[Powers of the Turning]] already set side by side. The pair now has a year.
- **Threnmaieth's crown-count has a beginning.** Which date a clerk writes is itself a stance, and it shows inside Threnmaieth's own house: a clerk who leaves the crown-year off a slate is doing a quiet Maiethvael.
- **The First Seat stays uncaptured.** Neither paper owns Thaeloren. The college would not let itself be named onto a roll either, and it would not refuse in Manril's voice.
- **The flavour of a dynasty, without a dynasty.** Play can meet a house that claims Limrae, or a house that claims Manril. The claim is kinship on paper, not a right of blood.

### Production notes (P2.1)

Moved from the player-facing body during residual export polish:

- > **Canon status.** 🟡 **Lived split (Story R.9).** 🔒 **Does not capture the First Seat.** 🔒 **Does not make Maiethvael the kind foil.** Names *Limrae / Manril* 🟡 (seed `20260830`). Crown-count start **Cut-year 67** — present **Crown-year 320**.

This was not a civil war, and it is not a foil made out of a Kind. The split is desks. Maiethvael's refusal is a real warmth and a real bill. Threnmaieth's care-count is real care and a real cage. Confirming that she is kind does not wash either of those clean.

Do not fold the Book into the Threnhael. There are three lists, and they are not the same list. The Close ranks, Threnmaieth keeps, and Maiethvael will not write. A campaign that steals all three will not get the same child three times.

Manril cannot see that "we will furnish" is already a bill. Limrae cannot see that "tend" and "find again" are how a river learns to take a census.

The kinship on offer is paper-kin. If a later story wants a tomb for Limrae or for Manril, that is a taste call, not a fact this summer locked.

If the table needs a thread: a pair of cousins and only one clerk; a crown-year that a Maiethvael court will not find on any paper it recognizes; Limrae's preface used as a founding-charter; the Close insulted by being written down as unmarked; a Speaker asked to convert a Crown-year into a Cut-year, and refusing to start history at a desk.
