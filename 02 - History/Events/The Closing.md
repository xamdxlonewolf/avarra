# The Closing

> The Closing is the war that followed the copy. Someone had already cut living wood from Thaeloren, the one Tree at the origin, and a town on the Near Mile had already Turned a child under a canopy that was not Thaeloren's, standing that child under a local tree at Turning-Week so the origin was no longer the only door. The class that sat on the grove tried to make the copy untrue, and they could not. Everyday speech calls the war **the Closing**, because what they closed in the end was their walls. Folk who watched the riders call it **the Uprooting**. The remnant that still keeps the book says **the Retreat**. The people who sat the grove are **the Closed Seat**, and in ordinary talk they are the **Grove-Sitters**. The cut in the wood that the war could not undo is [[The First Cut]]. Who held the knife is still unwritten. The dated chronicle of the war sits in [[The Other Count]].


## What happened (as believed)

In [[The Walking Years]], when the origin was the only place a child could be Turned, that origin was a gate. The Closed Seat were untithed houses, houses that carried no Tithe, the owing that comes with a Gift, and they held a wall-right older than any Cutting-leave, the paper that would later say who may cut living wood. They decided who might stand under Thaeloren. A family could arrive with shoes and a note and still be turned away at the gate, because distance was not the only thing that sorted a child. **Leave** was, the paper you had to be carrying when you reached the wall.

Cut-year 0, where the clerical count of years begins, is a wound in the wood. Cut-year 19 is a ceremony the gate did not authorize. The nameless green, the first copied grove whose town history will not name, held a Turning-Week, and a town that was not the origin Turned its own child ([[The First Cut#The generation of argument (how a heresy grew a desk)|the generation of argument]]). After that summer the Closed Seat's monopoly was no longer a fact on the ground. It was a claim they still meant to enforce.

They did what a gate does when the road grows a second door. They tried to **uproot** the copies.

They rode out a day's earth with carrying-sacks and one sentence they were willing to say in every square: the copy is a theft of the one Tree, so bring the wood home, or burn the pot it is growing in. Some pots died under that sentence. Some towns hid a slip, a small cutting of the living wood, in a well. Some lodging-hearths, the squares that would become [[Orenbren]], would not starve their own beds to save a gate. The early [[The Tree-Wardens|First Seat]], the college of tree-wardens at the origin, wanted *authorization*, the copies made legitimate rather than burned, and did not want ash. The hidden slips, the hearths that would not starve a bed, and the college that would not take ash are three different refusals, and together they are why an origin-empire cannot police a day's walk in a world without a printing press, where an order dies with the person who was supposed to carry it.

By Cut-year 38 the Closed Seat's rule at the grove was over. **[[Hildal]]** *(HIL-dal)*, the last person to speak for the Closed Seat that anyone will still name, ordered the Retreat. They did not save the grove, and what they saved was a **book**, the record of who owes. The walls they closed around that book are the [[The Walled Book|Inner Close]]. The grove stayed where it was. The college sat *beside* it, and the college was not a throne. The beds stayed civic, a town's lodging and not the Seat's to grant.

No one agrees which pot was the first they failed to pull. Naming that town would pick a hearth, and a hearth would pick a hand, and history does not.

## The Closed Seat (what they thought they were)

They were not a dark lord, and they were not a first king. They were a class that had grown a country around a job they believed someone had to do, because the one Tree cannot stand a world at once, and so someone must choose the road. A child turned away at the gate is UnTurned, never taken through the rite, and that is a different reason from lacking the trip. Both reasons filled the far lands. [[Polity Archetypes|Vaethorn]]'s later guilt wants the people who were denied to have been unworthy, and the songs do not agree.

What they lived by was plainer than the sermon.

**Grove-leave** was the paper you carried to the gate. A house rich enough for the Salt Walk, the road that buys a ship's berth, could pay for that berth and still fail the paper. Columns on the Long Mile, the long road in from the far country, learned to arrive in numbers the gate could not turn away without a riot, and the gate still turned some of them.

**The untithed sat.** That is how folk remember it, and it is also how the remnant still ranks its own. A Given child of the Closed Seat, a child the fall has marked with a colour and a Gift, loses the heir-line *now* ([[The Walled Book]]). The rule is older than the Retreat. The war did not invent it. The war moved it off the grove and put it behind a wall.

**They fought the copy, not the knife.** A named cutter would have been a gift to them, a face to hang the theft on, and they never received one. The five attributions, the five stories of whose hand it was, stayed five. The Closed Seat's sermons called the nameless green a blight. The folk called the riders thieves of a theft.

Rationing the one Tree, choosing who may stand under it, is the same hunger as rationing the next scion, choosing which town receives the next cutting. The sentence that justified the ration changed owners, and the hunger did not.

## Who would not help them

| Who | What they did | What they were not doing |
|---|---|---|
| Near-Mile towns that had planted | They hid slips of the wood, watered them at night, and sang the thief-songs with *we* in them. | They were not a rebel army under a banner. They were a planting kept alive under a rider. |
| Lodging-hearths | They kept the extra beds open, and they refused to starve a pilgrim-season to please a gate. | They were not a new crown. They federated later, as [[Orenbren]]. |
| The origin-college | They forbade cuts that had no sanction, and they also forbade burning a Hand, a local tree, that had already spoken. | They were not the Closed Seat's clergy. The Seat captured *how* a cut is made, not *who* had made the first one, and not the grove. |
| Salt-Walk houses | They kept writing their notes. A war at the origin was a delay to them, not a theology. | They were not provinces in revolt. They had never been a province. |
| Far hearths on the Long Mile | They walked anyway, or they did not. The collapse of the gate did not make the road any shorter. | They were not liberated subjects. The Closed Seat had never held them. |

[[The Watching]] grafted ordinary pears through the war-years and refused to start one. [[The Old Ways]] were not in this fight, because the guests they count as the beginning had not yet reached [[Heskoren]]. [[The Fair Hand]] filed the cargo and did not ride. [[The Open Table]] owes the Closing nothing, and was not a party to it.

## Four monopolies from one fall

The Closed Seat's rule over the grove ended, and the job they had been doing split. The split was not freedom.

| What they had held | Who holds the piece now |
|---|---|
| Who may *stand under the origin* | No one holds it as a gate. The extra mile is devotion, prestige, or necessity, wherever a graft has not yet spoken ([[The Years of Hands]]). [[Orenbren]] houses the people who still walk it. |
| Who may *cut next* | [[The Tree-Wardens\|Cutting-leave]] and the scion-queue, the line of towns waiting on the next young tree. The college absorbed a heresy and called it horticulture. |
| Who *slept near the grove* | [[Orenbren]]'s lodging-compact. The hospitality is how the rent is paid. |
| Who *owes* besides the Tithe they already carry | The [[The Walled Book\|Book of Tithes]], inside the walls. It classifies who owes. It is not a church. |

[[Polity Archetypes|Threnmaieth]]'s list, the roll that hymn-country keeps and calls care, is a later paper ([[The Two Papers]], Cut-year 67). It is not the Closing's child in a straight line. It is the same hunger, carried into a country of hymns that decided reckoning was a way of caring for people.

The Closed Seat's fall is already in the past. The Seat reached too far, because you cannot uproot a day's walk in every direction, and what it left is a remnant.

## Seat narration vs folk memory

The Closed Heirs tell one summer, and the folk, the lodging, and the college tell another. They are arguing about the same riders.

| | Closed Heirs / Book | Folk / lodging / Seat college |
|---|---|---|
| The war | We closed the walls so the Book would live. | They rode out to pull pots, and they could not. |
| The grove | We sat it because someone had to. | They sat it because they could. |
| The Retreat | It was the stewardship of a house. | It was a gate that chose a wall. |
| What was saved | Rank. | Beds, a college, and the copies. |
| The knife | A theft we never named. | It is still nameless, and it is nameless on purpose. |

A [[Long-Lived]] who walked to the grove as a child in Cut-year 10, and a Closed Heir who keeps Hildal's last page, will fight about the same summer. Memory is perfect. Meaning is not.

## Visibility markers

- In a Near-Mile house you can find a kitchen-knife kept on a ribbon and, in the same yard, a scorch on the ring where a pot once sat, the unnamed cutter and the burned copy still sharing one place.
- The stone of the Inner Close is one colour, and the stone of [[The Low Wall]] is another. People who tell you the two are the same wall are making a claim.
- Hildal's name is in the Book, and it is on no stone at the grove. The college will not write it there. The Close will not loan the page.
- A Salt-Walk house will skip the years 19–38 in its own count and call the gap an "origin delay," and it will still collect on the notes.
- A child in Eolvaeth may be told that the old untithed sat on the grove, which is true as folk tell it, and incomplete if you try to use it as a map.

## In-world text

*This is Hildal's last leaf, kept in the Inner Close and filed as the Retreat. Later clerks of the Book keep it under the heir-roll. The verso, the back of the leaf, is a list of pots they failed to bring home.*

> *We will not write the knife. We will write the house. The copy has spoken and we cannot unspeak a colour. Carry the Book behind the wall. Rank who owes. The grove will keep its college; the college was never ours to sit. A gift is a gift. A gift is not a seat. If the next fall is a colour, strike heir. We have always struck heir. We are only closing the door we used to open.*

Folk who keep the nameless knife read this page as a confession. The Close reads it as the same housekeeping as Talnin's line, the rule that strikes an heir when a colour falls. Both of them can hold the same copy and mean a different thing by it.

## Consequences

- **A gate no longer grants the right to Turn.** In the squares that kept their wood, a town witnessing its own child became the ordinary way belonging worked, as if the weather had changed, and nobody had to pass a law to make it so. The walk to the origin remains, and it has new jobs where a local tree is missing, or where someone still wants the old road.
- **[[Orenbren]] federates from hearths that would not starve.** The guest-compact with the Close starts here, beds outside the wall and the Book inside it. They share a country. They do not share a page.
- **The First Seat does not capture the grove as a throne.** It captures the copy-right, the power to say who may cut next, and that capture was already underway before the Retreat. The Closing is why a college could sit *beside* a grove after the Closed Seat had left it.
- **Maiethorn's later six are successors, not ancient kingdoms.** Gift, reckoning, hinge, thaw, dry sowing, and lodging each inherit a wreck, not a crown that was always there. [[The Two Papers]] splits the hymn-country twenty-nine summers later.
- **The monopoly changed hands.** As the trees spread, the question moved with them, from who may stand under the origin to who decides where the next door will be planted.
- After the Retreat, the Closed Heirs kept the same manners wherever the Book went: a Kept person at the head of the table, a Kept person's word taken first, the keys in Kept hands, and a Given heir struck from the roll.

## GM Notes

### What actually happened

Do not name the cutter in order to explain the war. The Closed Seat never learned a face, and the table should play that ignorance rather than solve it. If a later story wants a tomb for a cutter, that is still a taste call, and it belongs to [[The First Cut]], not to a clarification of this war.

Hildal is a clerk, not a villain-king. The name comes from seed `20260830`. They chose a wall over a grove they could no longer hold, and the injustice, then and now, is the Book.

This was not a world-empire. Strandoren paid, Heskoren waited, and Kumbaan is off the clocks. A campaign that sets out to liberate the continents from the Grove-Sitters is writing the wrong map.

Do not let the GM's voice agree that the Closed Seat *deserved* to sit the grove, and do not let it agree that the copies *civilized* the origin. When anything was saved, what was saved was a walk that some children could now skip, and a rank that some children still cannot escape.

The Low Wall is not the Close. The stone is different, and nobody has counted the wall. The Close will claim it anyway, the Watchers will not put a date on it, and the party should walk both and hear both claims.

The split of the old job is not freedom, and this note should not collapse the Close, the Seat, and Orenbren into one house. The fall was already in the past by the time anyone tells it: the Seat overreached, because you cannot uproot a day's walk in every direction, and it left a remnant.

Hildal cannot see that "we have always struck heir" is the Closed Seat's rule still going. On the page it feels like housekeeping.

If the table needs a thread, any of these will hold: the verso of Hildal's leaf, the pots they failed to bring home; a Near-Mile slip that survived a rider; a Salt-Walk note that still charges an "origin delay"; Threnmaieth clerks treating a Retreat as if it had been a census; a pilgrim ranked as labour because the Book does not know their hug.
