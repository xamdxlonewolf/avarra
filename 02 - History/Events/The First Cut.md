# The First Cut

> The First Cut is the hinge as people lived it. Someone cut living wood from Thaeloren, the one Tree at the origin, or from a branch that came away in a hand, carried it a day's earth, married it to new ground, and it *took*. A town on the [[The Walking Years|Near Mile]], the road in to that origin, Turned a generation later under a canopy that was not the origin's, which is to say the town stood its own child under a local tree. Everyday speech calls it **the First Cut.** The liturgical name is **Eoloren** *(EOL-or-en)*, "the witnessed hinge." The clerical count of years starts here ([[The Ages of the Turning]]), so that this taking is Cut-year 0 and the present year is Cut-year 387. This note is the event and the wave that followed it. What the wave has left in the present is written in [[The Years of Hands]].


## What Happened (as believed)

Everyone agrees that a first town-graft succeeded. It happened within a day's living earth of Thaeloren, close enough that a cut could still be green when it met the new ground. That much is horticulture, the keeping of living wood, and not a theology. No two houses agree whose story the cut is, and this account does not pick a winner for them.

The first green is not named. Folk who need an object to point at call it **the nameless green**. Naming the town would pick a hearth, and a hearth would pick a hand.

## What the Cut broke (the Closed Seat)

In [[The Walking Years]] the origin was not an open grove. A class of the untithed, people who carried no Tithe, the owing that comes with a Gift, sat on Thaeloren and decided who might walk in to it. Everyday speech calls them **the Closed Seat**, and folk call them **the Grove-Sitters**. Distance was one way they sorted a child, and **leave**, the paper that got you through the gate, was the other. They were a gate at the origin that had grown a country around itself, not a flag planted on three continents.

A local Tree meant you did not need their gate. That is the monopoly the Cut broke, and it is not a sixth story about whose hand held the knife. The five stories still disagree about the hand, and they can agree about what the copy *did*. The Closed Seat tried to uproot the copies, and they could not. The war, the collapse, and the retreat behind the walls that survived are [[The Closing]]. Who cut stays unwritten there too.

The riders fought a pot, and they never learned a face.

A freshly planted scion, the young tree cut from a living one, still takes **a generation** to hold a Leaf-Fall ([[Turning Tree]]), the rite in which a child stands and a colour or a hug comes down. So Cut-year 0 is a wound in wood, and the first *local* Turning, a town Turning its own child, is about twenty summers later. "A graft arrived" and "this town Turns under its own canopy" are never the same year. The wave is slow because each Hand, each local tree once it can do the work, has to grow up before it can be a source for the next cut.

## The five attributions (lived)

The five stories are not speeches someone stands up and recites. They are jobs a room is already doing, and which job the room is doing is a faster read than any creed.

### Folk — the stolen branch

A parent who would not watch another child stay [[Law and Citizenship|Kept]], without a Gift, for want of a trip the family could not make, or who would not bury another child on the [[The Walking Years|Near Mile]], took wood without leave. The mercy and the theft are the same act.

**The nameless knife** is the object, not a saint ([[The One Knife]]). Some hearths keep a kitchen-knife on a ribbon, the way children of the Hands years climb the mile-shrines along the road, and they cannot say whether they are honouring a theft or a gift.

**Thief-songs** on the Near Mile are walking-songs that make the cutter *we*. Versions from the Salt Walk, the sea crossing, turn it into a joke on the quay, *the first unpaid berth*. Versions from the Long Mile, the long road from the far country, are not funny. Vaethorn's later guilt wants this parent to have been *unworthy*. The songs do not agree.

**Folk-right cutting** did not vanish when the Seat wrote a leave. A Taken-In with a sick neighbour and no year to spare will still take a slip, a small cutting. Most of those slips die. The ones that take are how the queue, the line of towns waiting their turn, learns that it is not the only horticulture in the world. [[The Tree-Wardens|Road-hands]], the people who walk the wood for the Seat, call a stolen slip a problem. Towns that lived through a stolen year call it a year they Turned.

The later [[The Tree-Wardens|Cutting-leave]] fee is, in this telling, **a fine that forgot it was a fine.** Lestrand will broker that fee without blushing. That is not a different story. That is this story with a clerk.

This telling leaves out that a stolen Hand still left a town behind that did not get the next one.

### Clergy — the warden ahead of the college

A tree-warden of a Near-Mile hearth cut with a leave that did not yet exist. The wood took. The hearth at the origin spent **the generation of argument**, from Cut-year 0 into the first local Leaf-Fall, trying to decide whether the taking was blight, theft, or rite. The [[The Tree-Wardens|First Seat]] as a desk that issues Cutting-leave is what that argument left sitting on the ground. The [[The Tree-Wardens|Road-hands]] are the scandal moved into an office. They are the people who walk what used to be done without sanction.

**Speakers who will not say *first*** are part of how the office lives. A Speaker, a voice of the Seat, will say *the witnessed hinge*. They will date a note. They will not point at a grave. Some of them are skeptics who will grant the horticulture and refuse the saint. Some of them are devout, and they think that naming a cutter would steal the offering from the Tree. It is the same silence, doing two jobs.

**The quiet years at the origin** came after the first copy, when the Motherwood, the wood around the one Tree, tried to forbid any further cut. Wood died in pots anyway, and neighbours cut anyway. Forbidding a copy that had already taken is how a college learns that it does not own the Tree's tissue, the living wood itself. It owns only the *authorization*, the right to say which next cut is allowed.

**Road-hands are the heresy once it has been made regular.** The office is the weather of the Hands years ([[The Tree-Wardens]]). History adds one sentence to that. They were not designed as a church that travels. They are the generation of argument, given a cart.

This telling leaves out that "we will write how, not who" is how a heresy becomes a monopoly.

### Devout — the Tree offered

A branch came away in a hand. There was no theft in it, and no genius, only a Hand giving a Hand, the Tree offering a tree. The Mother, or the Tree if you will not say Mother, *meant* the wood to travel. This is the version the Seat prefers when it preaches, because it makes the [[The Tree-Wardens|scion-queue]], the line of towns waiting for the next young tree, sound like stewardship rather than like a ration.

**Eoloren sermons** at Turning-Week in the Motherwood say that she sent her hands outward so the hearths could stay. On a good day the graves of the Walking Years are named in the same breath. On a bad day they are not.

**The watering-words** are offered as proof. They are the words said when the wood is married to new ground, and the devout ask: if that rite were only horticulture, why would the wood listen? A skeptic's answer is that you watered it. Both of those people can stand under the same canopy.

**Cutting-leave, in this telling, is a blessing.** Vaethorn calls it *the wood's blessing*, and the devout need that name. Without it the queue is only a list of who waits.

This telling leaves out that "she meant the wood to travel" narrates a wave that went to paying coasts before it went to the poor far. The thing saved, when anything was, was a walk, and it was not everyone's walk.

### [[The Watching]] — horticulture, then an overlay

People had always been able to cut living wood. They grafted fruit, kept kin in orchards, and set a slip in a pot. The Motherfaith, the faith that calls the Tree the Mother, later *named* one of those cuts the First and started a count from it, which is what an overlay does: a later name laid on a thing people were already doing. The Tree was already seeing. A date does not make a mother.

**They refuse the Cut-count.** A Watcher clerk will date a debt from a house-year, a year counted from a house's own start, or from a drowning-year, or from a night they kept. They will convert the date into the Eoloren-count if a court makes them, and they will not call that count history.

**Pear-grafts are the tell.** Watcher hearths on Maiethorn still graft ordinary fruit in the same week a Road-hand passes with carrying-earth, the living earth a cutting has to travel in. They do not think the pear is a joke. They think the pear is the whole argument, done with hands.

**The withheld *first*** has the same cadence as the blessing they withhold. *What was cut, was seen. What took, was seen. The Tree does not reach twice by chance.* There is no Mother in it, and no epoch in it.

This telling leaves out that refusing the date does not refuse the wave, because children still died on the road until the copies took. The overlay is a name. The graves were not.

### [[The Old Ways]] — the land received a guest

No one "cut" in the sense the Seat means. What happened, in this telling, is that a grove took kin in. The Hands, the years of local trees, are when guests began arriving from the east, and host-rights start there, not in a warrant from a college.

**The first meal** for a new graft is food left at the planting, the way you feed a guest before you ask their business. [[The Door-Keepers]] still do this. A Road-hand who eats is doing the courtesy of a host, whether or not the Seat wrote the meal down. A Road-hand who plants and leaves without sitting is making a claim.

**Guest-reading and First-reading** start the story in the same place and then split. A Guest-reading town will make the pilgrimage and keep the meal. A First-reading district will tend the same wood as a guest in their house, and will still refuse the Tender's blessing, the line a warden says over a child at the fall. Both of them start at a receiving, not at a knife. The Cut is a mainland word for what they call an arrival.

**They do not start history at Cut-year 0.** On [[Heskoren]] the guests *arrive* in the Cut-year 200s. Calling that arrival "late" is already the Motherfaith talking. Calling it "the First Cut, delayed" is a map drawn by the Seat. The land was already making children at the old doors.

This telling leaves out that a guest who takes a generation to speak, which is what the first local Leaf-Fall is, will still be called a Tree by the children who Turn under it. Host-rights do not stop a town from becoming a town of the Hands years.

All five stories are still told. Skeptics will grant the horticulture, because you can see it, and will deny the conclusion someone else draws from it, that a mind chose the travelling. [[The Fair Hand]] is not a sixth story about the *cut*. They come into it when the wood boards a ship, which is below. [[The Open Table]] is not in this story.

## The generation of argument (how a heresy grew a desk)

Cut-year 0 is a cut. The next twenty summers are copies, dead wood, a college that cannot un-cut what has taken, and a first local Leaf-Fall that ends the argument without settling it.

**What people actually did** was smaller than the sermons, and more stubborn.

Near-Mile towns cut because the first green was *visible from the next square*. They had living tissue, a day's earth, a pot, and a Taken-In who knew soil. That is why the towns at the origin filled first: not worthiness, but the distance a sapling can survive.

Most of the early copies **died**. The wood was mortal, or the season was wrong, or a pot dried on the Near Mile in Hale-month, or a well-meant slip went into the ground with no carrying-earth under it. Those deaths are why the Seat could tell itself, for a while, that the first taking had been a fluke.

The hearth at the origin tried **forbidding** the next cut. Speakers named it theft, blight, a wound in the First Hand, and neighbours cut anyway. A prohibition that has no riders cannot police a day's walk in a world without a printing press, where the order dies with the person sent to carry it.

**The proof arrived as a ceremony.** When the nameless green held its first Turning-Week, a colour fell, or a hug fell. The tellings do not agree which, and the disagreement does not change the paper. A town that was not the origin had Turned its own child. Belonging by a local witness, the town itself seeing the child, came into that square the way weather does, a generation after the knife, without anyone passing a law to create it. The Walk stopped being the only door. The Seat can argue doctrine until the leaves fall. It cannot argue the leaves back onto Thaeloren.

**What the Seat captured** was not the tissue, the living wood itself, which the college could not own. It could own **who may cut next**, and that ownership is the copy-right. [[The Tree-Wardens|Cutting-leave]] is the paper. The [[The Tree-Wardens|scion-queue]] is what a copy-right becomes when the demand runs ahead of a generation of wood.

The fee is framed as the cost of carrying-earth and of a Road-hand's year, and it is the same coin in four stances ([[Naming in the Turning]]). Folk hear a fine in it, Lestrand hears a licence, Threnmaieth wants it written on a roll, and Vaethorn calls it an offering. Historically it is how an institution absorbed a heresy and called the heresy horticulture. Cutting that had no sanction became cutting that had a permit. The practice survived by changing owners, and the queue is old.

Road-hands are the people you send when you have claimed the knife and you still need the wood to walk, and they are not a second order of their own.

**The second canopy made a queue.** Once a town had its own tree, the new injustice was *which town next*, and that injustice is paid in twenty more years of borrowed Turnings, twenty years in which children still stand under someone else's tree while theirs grows up.

## How the wave moved

This is how the wood moved, inside the dates the Ages note already gives.

A scion still takes a generation before it can Turn a child. A healthy town Tree may supply the next cut, acting for the origin ([[The Tree-Wardens]]). So the wave is a chain of Hands that have to grow up, not a crate of miracles you could open on any shore in the same summer. The sea can jump a link in that chain. A cart on the Long Mile cannot, not until carrying-earth will last the trip, and not until there are rest-hearths whose job is the wood rather than the pilgrims.

### [[Maiethorn]] — Cut-year 0–80 (origin-towns first; Rain-Shadow slower)

The Near Mile stops being a *requirement* in the core, town by town, as the canopies come of age. The squares at the origin go first, a day's earth, then two, then a valley. The pilgrimage does not disappear. It remains as devotion at Thaeloren, and as necessity wherever a graft has not yet spoken.

**The Rain-Shadow is slower, and the slowness is in the ground.** It is the same continent, with thin soil, wells, and sand-country. A cut that takes in the Sacred Core, where the soil will hold it, dies in the dry hills unless a Taken-In and a lucky year agree. This is not unworthiness and not a different gospel, because it is dirt. Hearths of the Fox of the Sands still walk west toward Thaeloren after the squares on the coast have already stopped. The wave runs at two speeds on one continent.

The Seat's narration of those years is *her hands filled the Motherland.* Folk memory says *we stopped burying children on the Near Mile where the wood took, and we did not stop where it didn't.* Both sentences can be said in the same market.

By Cut-year 80 the Motherland is **old Hands** in the core, towns whose trees have been ordinary for a long time, and **still waiting** in pockets. That waiting is not Heskoren's story yet. It is why a hamlet on Maiethorn can still send this year's ten-year-olds to a neighbour, and not be play-acting a history that has closed.

### [[Strandoren]] — Cut-year 40–160, by sea (wealthy coasts first, interior later)

[[Named Ground|The Old Crossing]] already knew how to move a child. Moving a Hand is the same berth with worse instructions on it: living earth, do not stow the pot below, water it at dawn, and a Taken-In or a Road-hand sleeps beside it.

**The sea starts while Maiethorn is still filling, in Cut-year 40, because a ship is faster than filling every inland hamlet.** A wealthy coast can pay a Cutting-leave and a Salt-Walk crew in the same summer that a Near-Mile village is still sitting on the Seat's list. The wave went where the ships and the fees went.

**The coasts take first.** Port-cities take, and the deep interior waits on river-days and a cart, which is a short version of the Long Mile. Strandoren's later combination of a high reach and a worn faith is not a paradox. The wood arrived as cargo, and cargo does not require a mother. [[The Fair Hand]] dates the carrying as a fact of a supply line. They will attend a planting as witnesses to a signing. They will not start history at a wound in wood. *The first carrying* is their name for Eoloren, and what they mean by it is a bill of lading, the paper that says what the ship had aboard.

**What the Salt Walk becomes,** once a coast Turns under its own canopy, is no longer a necessity of a child's tenth year. The berth becomes a luxury, a hinge in a contract, a devout extra. Road-houses on the inland days lose a season of boots and keep the notes. That shift in fortune is already part of the larger history. What belongs on this page is the quay that noticed the pots had replaced the children.

By Cut-year 160 the Shore-lands are **mature Hands** on the water, and still patchy inland. Lestrand files the whole motion as *the carrying*. They are not wrong about the ships.

### The gap (why [[Heskoren]] waits until Cut-year 200)

The wait is not unworthiness, and three frictions are stacked under it.

1. **Wood dies on the Long Mile.** The trip is weeks to months. A day's earth is a Near-Mile problem, and a ship's hold is a Salt-Walk problem someone has already solved, if the pot is watered. A cart between hearths in thaw-mud is how scions become sticks. Until carrying-earth will last, and until there are **rest-hearths whose job is the wood and not the pilgrims**, Heskoren cannot be a circuit a living tree survives. Road-hands had to exist as a standing office before the far country could be a route, rather than a funeral of saplings.
2. **The chain has to grow up.** You cut from the origin, or you cut from a healthy town Tree. Maiethorn's first generation of sources comes of age in the Cut-year 20s–40s. Strandoren's coastal Hands are not ready to be sources until later still. You cannot skip ahead to the far reach without a mature link, unless you spend origin-wood on a march that kills it. The Seat, mostly, did not. That is horticulture, and it is also a choice about who is next.
3. **The queue prefers a next town that can pay.** After the coasts, the carrying-earth is committed to interiors, to replacements, and to sick Trees, the quiet work that does not look like a wave. Heskoren is poor, far, and already accused, by a guilt that gets its theology later, of having waited because it deserved to wait. The job of the history is to refuse that sentence. They waited because the wood was slow, the road was long, and the list was written in the Motherwood.

Vaethorn will preach that *we wait because we are unworthy.* The gap is the evidence that sermon uses.

### [[Heskoren]] — Cut-year 200–387 (still incomplete)

The frontier coast faces back across the sea toward Strandoren, and that facing is the lifeline new grafts come in on. The luckiest towns have had a local Turning for a few generations, a scion that arrived in the 200s and spoke in the 220s, or later than that. Whole districts still send their children down the road, to a neighbour's new canopy, or, still, along the Long Mile, if what the neighbour has is a stick in a pot.

**This is the wave in the present tense.** It is not a re-enactment of Brenvaeth, the old name for the years when walking was the only way. Some hamlets are in the Walking Years *this year, for this cohort*. Some are already in the Hands years, one valley over. Both can be true of the same road. The waiting [[Polity Archetypes|Vaethorn]] lives in is the front of the wave, not a metaphor for a history that has finished.

Host-rights of the Old Ways start *here* as a collision people are living, not as a sermon preached on Maiethorn. A graft set in an old Taken-In green is a guest, a Cutting-leave is a paper from the mainland, and the meal comes first, or else the planting is a claim. The people on both sides of that argument love the wood ([[The Door-Keepers]]).

The Seat's narration says *her will is still travelling.* Folk memory says *the cart came late, the soil was called thin, and the three hamlets past the ford can see Harrow's green from here.* That Harrow-sentence is already how new towns talk ([[Turning Tree]]). On Heskoren it is not nostalgia, because it is the decade they are in.

### [[The Sundering Isle|Kumbaan]] — never

No graft crossed the storm-wall, the drowning weather that closes the isle, and Road-hands do not carry a Tree over that sea. Sailors tell of pots that went into the water, and dead wood is not a Tree, and no old scion has ever been found on the Isle. [[The Open Table]] owes Eoloren nothing, and counts its time in moons, not in Cut-years.

## Seat narration vs folk memory

The Seat's account and the folk memory sit side by side, the way the Walking Years kept the romance and the graves in the same sentence.

| | Seat / devout sermon | Folk memory (and the other houses) |
|---|---|---|
| The knife | She offered a Hand. | Someone took one. |
| The paper | It is stewardship, and it is horticulture. | It is a fine that forgot what it was, or a licence, or a warrant. |
| The order of the wave | It is her will, travelling. | It is ships and fees, and then the far. |
| Heskoren's wait | It is soil, timing, and the Mother's pace. | It is the list, the dead wood, and the cart that came late. |
| What was saved | The world received her. | Children stopped dying on *some* roads. |

A [[Long-Lived]] who can say *I walked*, because they did, and a Speaker who keeps the Eoloren-count, will fight about the same summer. A Long-Lived who *planted*, a Road-hand who held the work for a long tenure, or an innkeeper who watched the pots replace the children, will not always tell you which taking they think was holy. Memory is perfect. Meaning is not.

As the trees spread, they moved the place where a child is expected to have been seen at ten. A town of the Hands years can still meet a feared Struck person, whose change did not come from being seen at ten, with a rope. A canopy of the town's own does not stop the rope.

## Visibility markers (what a character notices)

- A kitchen-knife on a ribbon in a Near-Mile house that will not name a saint for it.
- Watchers grafting pears in the same week a Road-hand waters carrying-earth.
- A bowl from the first meal, set at a planting and licked clean by the dogs, which the Seat never wrote down.
- A ship's instruction that is still being copied: *one living hand — do not stow below — water at dawn.* The clerk who copies it files the line as cargo.
- Three hamlets that can see a neighbour's canopy and are still on the queue. They have learned not to say *queue.*
- A child of the Rain-Shadow still on the west-road after the core of the country has stopped walking.
- Empty berths of the Salt Walk in Eolthael, the season when those berths used to fill, and a hold that still smells of wet earth out of season.
- A Long-Lived who planted, and who will not say *I walked*. It is a different job, and the same quiet.

## In-world text

*This is a minute of the First Seat, attributed to the Speaker [[Rithnali]], from the summer the nameless green held its first Turning-Week. Later clerks file it as Cut-year 19. The verso, the back of the leaf, is a list of pots that died, and no one read that list aloud.*

> *The wood that took within a day's earth of the First was cut without leave. We will not write who. We will write how, so the next cut does not kill what it carries.*
>
> *Carry in living earth. Marry with the watering-words. Do not cut a second without the Seat. If the scion sickens, send word; do not take another wound in the First to hide the first.*
>
> *The Tree does not reach twice by chance, and neither do we. From this summer a cut is a leave, or it is a theft we have decided not to chase. The towns that wait will wait on horticulture. The soil is thin in more places than it is not.*

Folk who keep the nameless knife think this paper is a confession, the Seat thinks it is competence, and both of them can hold the same copy.

## Folklore

The teaching-story of the Cut is [[The Branch That Came Away]], five tellings of one taking, and a last line that will not choose whose hand it was. A child on the Near Mile hears a thief-song in it, a Speaker hears a rite, a Watcher hears a pear, the Old Ways hear a guest, and a child of the Hands years hears why the neighbour has a Tree while they still have a road.

## Consequences

- **The wave starts.** The Grafting, as a wave, is wood carried outward, a generation to mature, Maiethorn first, Strandoren by sea, Heskoren last and still live, and Kumbaan never.
- **Where the wood has taken, a town Tree becomes the ordinary way.** Children Turn under the town canopy. The pilgrimage remains, with a new job, devotion or prestige, or necessity when a graft fails.
- **The Seat gains a bottleneck.** Cutting-leave and the scion-queue are how an institution took a heresy in and called the result horticulture.
- **The clerical count of years begins.** Other people do not have to use it, and the count a person does use shows the stance ([[The Ages of the Turning#How years are numbered (the name-reveals-stance lever, on history itself)|dating reveals stance]]).
- **Language and money were already bent by the road, and the bend eases where the trees stand.** Local trees let daughters grow up in place, so the road's pull toward the older speech loosens ([[The Old Tongue]]). Road-houses in the core lose their pilgrim-seasons and keep the notes ([[Economy and the Tithe]]). What both of those changes left behind is in [[The Years of Hands]], the two fates of the road-house and the three jobs the walk still has.

## GM Notes

### What actually happened

Do not confirm a cutter. A named first Road-hand, a named parent, or a named saint is a taste call, and only if play needs a tomb. A face turns the history into a great person's history, and the engine of the event is wood and waiting. The five attributions are the play. If the GM's voice collapses them into one, that voice has accidentally written a gospel.

Rithnali's minute says *Tree*. The later Cutting-leave says *Mother*. The early paper has not finished laying the later name over the older one. A household that keeps both practices, an old Speaker, or a college still arguing the name are all playable, and none of them is a trap to spring on the table. Do not "correct" the minute.

On the keystone: every scion *is* continuous tissue of the one Tree, so the First Cut really did move a Hand. In player-facing speech, that continuity is doctrine. The way her reach grows as the grafts spread is the quiet argument that a mind is in it, and it is not a licence to narrate her standing over the first knife. Skeptics still win the mundane reading, which is horticulture, ships, and soil, and both readings share the same wave.

The nature of the limit stays open. That she *could* reach further once a Hand stood nearer is already locked, as a pattern. *Why* she could not simply reach without the wood is still unwritten. Do not smuggle "finite by nature," or a wound, or a ration in as the reason the first cut worked. Lock the ache, and do not lock the cause.

The devout sentence, "she meant the wood to travel," is one belief among five. Do not let the GM's voice agree that the far country needed saving. When anything was saved, what was saved was a walk. Heskoren still lives the wave as *waiting*, the Old Ways live it as guests who arrived late, and Lestrand lives it as cargo. Keep all three of those in play.

The gap carries weight. Cut-year 160–200 is not empty time, and it is not a punishment. It is dead wood on the Long Mile, a chain that had to grow up, and a queue that preferred a next town that could pay. [[The Grey Summer]] stacked a fourth friction on that wait, and it did not become the gap.

The Closed Seat is not a sixth attribution. The Cut broke a monopoly, the Closing is the war, and you should not name the cutter in order to explain either one.

Do not date the Tree. [[Before the Walk]] has no start, and Watchers will fight any date that makes the wood begin at a knife.

Kumbaan is the control on the whole account. A wrecked pot in a sailor's story is allowed, and a taking is not.

The shift in fate has already happened, and it is not a proposal sitting in front of the table. Unsanctioned cutting became Cutting-leave and the queue, an absorption. The pressure on fate in the present day is tabled in [[The Years of Hands]], noted there and not rolled here.
