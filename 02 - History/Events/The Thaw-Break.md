# The Thaw-Break

> The Thaw-Break was snowmelt that arrived in one week. In Cut-year 233 [[Named Ground|the Rain-Wall]] did not thaw the way it usually thaws. Water that should have taken a month took seven days, the valleys drank it as flood, and the passes lost the stones that had marked them. Everyday speech calls it **the Thaw-Break**. On the ridges it is **the one-week melt**. The chronicle of the year sits in [[The Other Count]].


## What happened (as believed)

[[Lirorn]] lives by water that runs again after the winter. The lowlands drink the spring, and the ridges are the ones who notice when the spring is wrong. In an ordinary Liren, the month when the thaw is supposed to take its time, the melt is a season. Shelves drip, then they run, and the high road is mud before it is a road again.

In 233 the high snow went all at once. Sky-couriers saw the white break across a single week, and the valley charters saw everything that followed. A mile-shrine in a thaw-square stood in water up to the notch where travelers tie a ribbon. The name cut in the stone survived. The road to the stone did not, for two summers.

People died, and so did animals, stores, and one well-town on the leeward side, whose cistern took silt instead of snowmelt. [[Saelthael]]'s west-road, the walk that had gone on after the core of the country stopped needing it, was cut. Children who would have gone west in 233 and in 234 went later, or went to a neighbour, or missed the year. The Given-door, the one week in which a child can stand to be Given, does not open a second time because a flood ate the road. A flood is not a second-chance year.

**[[Narol of the Pass]]** *(NAH-rol)* took the thaw-toll when the old pass-holder's stone was under the water-line. What Lirorn can levy, in the middle of its country, is the thaw, the pass, and the high road. After the Break, the argument was *which* pass a traveler was on. Narol's house still levies the toll. The valley still drinks. The ridge still pays for that drink by being cut off. The injustice did not start in 233. The year made it visible, a water-line standing over a ribbon.

## What the water moved (not a sermon)

**[[Saelthael]] kept walking, two years late.** The core of the country called the delay soil. The west-road called it mud. [[Ornsael]] is the type of place that learned the lesson, a well-town that still sends its children on, and the lesson was that weather can put a square of the Motherland back on a neighbour's week, under some other town's Tree, without the town's own Tree having sickened.

**A graft that [[Ornled]] had been waiting on died in the pot.** The cart was late to the quay because the range had become a lake. **Vathne** *(VATH-neh)* wrote a slate on the wall: *we will not wait with a hymn for a stick that drowned in someone else's thaw.* Vaethorn heard godlessness in the sentence. Ornled heard a luxury they had just watched die. The secular frontier is older than this one pot, because Ornled had already declined to treat scarcity as a problem of the soul, but this is the year a specific cluster can point at.

**[[Saelvaeth]]'s neighbour's week had to climb worse mud.** In the luckiest squares the live front of the wave, the towns whose trees had only just begun to speak, was about a dozen years into that speaking. A pot that arrives late is a childhood of wood delayed, a tree that will be ready to Turn children later than it should have been. The queue did not become fair because the road was worse. The Harrow-sentence, the one new towns already say about hamlets that can see a neighbour's green and still do not have one, had more weather in it that year.

**[[Vaelhesk]] fed first meals to people the water had moved.** Host-rights do not ask what Cut-year a flood belongs to. A guest who arrives dripping is still a guest, or still a claiming, depending on whether they sat down to the meal. Some Road-hands sat. Some did not.

**[[Threnmaieth]] wanted the high road counted.** A pass that moves is a row on a list that moved without a clerk's permission. Lirorn would not be named as a people. They would let the passes be named, if the namers could manage it. After 233 the new pass was named, and Lirorn charged them for the water anyway.

## What it was not

The melt was not hers, not a watching, not a bargain, and not the Closed Seat. A later preacher who needs a moral will find one, and the melt does not owe it. The flood is not the reason Heskoren is poor in trees. Thin reach, the wood still arriving and still sparse, was already the front of the wave. It was not a disaster that picked out a Kind, one of the peoples a person is born as. Tengu shelves and Snows-Fox dens flooded with everyone else.

## Visibility markers

- A pass-stone with a water-line over the old ribbon-notch. Children rub it for luck. Ridge-folk rub it because the water was real.
- Mile-shrines on the west-road that skip two summers of names and then take the names up again.
- A slate in Ornled whose first line is still Vathne's, copied over, on wood newer than the sentence.
- A Lirorn toll that quotes a "Break-rate" in a kind year, and will not explain the quote to a lowlander.
- A bowl for the first meal, set at a high door, with silt still in the glaze.

## In-world text

*This is a sky-courier's strip from a high shelf in Lirorn, written on the third day of the melt. It was later filed as a note about the weather. The back of the strip is a list of passes that were roads on the first day and water on the seventh.*

> *The white is going together. Tell the valley to move the grain up, not the people down. The old stone at the noon-pass will not be a stone by dark. I can see three roofs that were shelves. I cannot see the well-town. If you are a warden, this is not a colour. If you are a clerk, this is not a row. If you are Narol, the toll is going to be a different notch. Water the wood if you can reach it. I am turning back. The air is too heavy to be a road.*

## Consequences

- **The 387 years of the count contain a flood.** The spread of the trees does not get to be the only weather in them.
- **Lirorn's power over the watershed has a year attached to it.** A toll that feels like geography feels like 233, if you were on the shelf when the snow went.
- **Saelthael, Ornled, and Saelvaeth inherit delays, not destinies.** The west-road and the live front lost the same two summers to one melt.
- **The ground this year named is written on [[Named Ground]].** The courier's noon-pass is the Noon Pass. Narol's new road is the Shelf-gate. The river on the west face is the Core-thaw. The seasonal water on the leeward side is the Well-wash.
