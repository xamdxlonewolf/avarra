# The Grey Summer

> The Grey Summer was a fever, and it was not a Tithe. In Cut-year 171 the quays of the Old Crossing, [[The Hinge Shore]] and the Ledger Coast that faces it, went grey for a summer. People died who already had a local Tree, a guild-ticket that let them work, and their hymns said. The wood did not fail first, and the watering-hands, the people who kept the young trees watered, did. Everyday speech calls it **the Grey Summer**. On the docks it is **the Salt-Grey**. Deathless clerks, the clerks of the old credit houses, still write **Grey + 1** on the back of a note. The chronicle of the year sits in [[The Other Count]].


## What happened (as believed)

It began in the holds, a cough in a packed berth that became a street, and then a quay-gang that could not stand long enough to work a hearth. The [[The Stillers]], who sit the hours with the sick, did what they do in a year like that, and some of the sick lived. The Grey did not care whether a person was Given a colour at the Tree, Struck into a change later, or Kept without a Gift. That is the year the devout learned, again, that a Hand in the square, a town's own Tree, is not an infirmary.

Ships stayed tied up. [[Named Ground|The Old Crossing]], which had been the world's highway since before anyone numbered a Cut-year, went quiet enough that a Far-Voiced on [[The Hinge Shore]], posted where a call has to cross the water, could hear the next quay's bells and could hear no cargo moving between them.

The Deathless houses did not die, and their debtors did, so the notes outlived the people who had signed them. That is why a trusted note still holds, and why a grandchild in Cut-year 387 can still owe a Grey-summer board, the price of a bed from that fever. [[The White Note House]] was in its ninety-first house-year, counting on its own clock rather than from the Cut. Ledan would later learn to convert one count of years into another. In 171 the only conversion was this: the person is gone, and the paper is not.

Grafts went unwatered in the squares where the Taken-In who knew the soil were already in the ground. A town that has a Hand can lose it, and a few coastal squares had to borrow a neighbour's week the following Eolthael, the month when a town stands its children, sending those children to another town's Tree for the rite. The Seat called that loss thin soil, and the quays called it this year.

## What the fever wrote (second-order, not a sermon)

**[[Trenledd]] began writing persons down.** The machinery that already ticketed work and taxed it had columns for hazard and for debt. **[[Dirrol]]** *(DEER-rol)* added a column for who was still alive to be found. There was no hymn in the column. There was a desk that had lost too many living witnesses, and that did not trust the three pillars, the witnesses a town relies on when it will not keep a roll, to restock themselves. The Worn Count, which is what people call Trenledd's roll, is this summer's child. Threnmaieth at least claims that a list keeps you hale. Trenledd learned, in a plague-year, that it wanted you *findable*.

**[[Brenledd]] piled its charters into one compact.** In Cut-year 174 the lesser hearths that had been priced one at a time through the fever, charged berth-fees in a summer when no ship used a berth, and called to pay notes early because a house was frightened, put those charters in one pile. **Nidtol** *(NID-tol)* is the hearth-name the compact still recites when Lestrand offers to simplify them into something easier to buy. The league is not a founding-king. It is a refusal that learned how to speak as one voice.

**[[Leddvael]] made a signing into a civic rite.** The witnesses had died, and a Bound person's word still closed a bargain when the witnesses could not. The Fair Hand had always been a dockside theology, a faith of the closed account. After the Grey, a stretch of water found it cheaper to establish the Settling, the public form of that faith, than to argue which square still remembered you. Leddvael began witnessing signings in public, and it trusted a Bound person's word because so many of the old witnesses were dead.

**[[Netstrand]]'s west trade thinned for a decade.** Hulls that should have gone on toward [[Heskoren]] stayed home, or stayed buried. The supply line of new trees already preferred the next town that could pay. The Grey is one more reason the far country waited. It is not a punishment, and it is not the whole of the gap.

**[[The Hinge Shore]] kept saying the blessing.** The pious shore watched the Ledger Coast put a price on survival, and it kept saying the blessing, so that it never had to call what it was watching a change of faith.

## What it was not

The fever was not a Gift, and no colour fell with it. A later preacher who calls it hers, or calls it a watching, or calls it a bargain, is laying a story over a cough. It was not the Closed Seat taking revenge. The Close ranked the dead they already ranked, and they did not send the cough. It is not why [[Heskoren]] waited until Cut-year 200. Dead wood on the long road, a chain of trees that had to grow up before the next cut could come from them, and a list that preferred a paying next town were already the reasons for that wait. The Grey stacked a fourth trouble on a list that was already unfair.

## Visibility markers

- A house-note with **Grey + 1** on the verso, the back, and a Cut-year on the recto, the front.
- Stones in Trenledd with numbers worn smooth on the back, the column Dirrol added, still a row a person can find.
- A Brenledd charter that begins *after the Salt-Grey, we will not be priced one hearth at a time.*
- A signing-watch in Leddvael that still opens Turning-Week with a Bound person's word before anyone says a blessing.
- Empty berths in Eolthael that the older people call *the Grey's habit*, and not the prestige-walk. Both accounts can be true of the same stretch of wood and water.

## In-world text

*This is a stiller's hour-slate from a quay on the Hinge Shore, in the Grey Summer, the second month of the cough. The guild later copied it as a page for training, and struck the last line.*

> *Morning: four. Midday: the quay-gang from the third hold — I will not write how many; the Voice-ticket does not have a box for a street. Evening: a Taken-In from the lot, no Tithe in it, just fever. The Tree in the square Held a child last Eolthael. The child is not here. I am not a warden. I am an hour. If you are reading this in a kind year, water the green. The wood did not fail us. We failed the watering.*

Dirrol, working inland, will later invent the box the stiller could not find on the ticket. The stiller was asking for water.

## Consequences

- **The count of 387 years contains a plague.** The spread of the trees does not get to be the only weather inside those years.
- **Trenledd, Brenledd, and Leddvael have causes.** A roll of persons, a compact of hearths, and a coast that signs in public each come out of this summer. They are not hats a country put on, and they are not eternal facts that were always waiting.
- **Deathless credit shows what it can do.** The note that outlives a cough is the same kind of note that outlives a walk, a debt that stays when the person who signed it does not.
- **Heskoren's wait gains a stacked reason, and does not gain a new theology.**
