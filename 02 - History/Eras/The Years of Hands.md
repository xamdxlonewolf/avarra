# The Years of Hands

> **The ordinary way to Turn now, and the present. Not post-history.** Where the wood has taken, a town [[Turning Tree]] is the ordinary way to Turn, a child standing under a Tree in their own town rather than walking to the one grove. People call these years **the Years of Hands**, and also *Since the Hands*. The liturgical name is **Ornthael** *(ORN-thael)* — "Tree-land." The Grafting is **not** a previous age you exit. It is a wave of living wood that is still moving. The spine of the ages is [[The Ages of the Turning]]. How you used to walk is [[The Walking Years]]. How the wave moved is [[The First Cut]]. This note is what the Walking left on the ground, and what this year still is. Settlement types the Walk left behind are [[Settlement Seeds]].


## Overview

This is the era the other notes were written in, often without stopping to name it. Where a graft has taken — a living copy of the origin Tree, carried out and planted — a town has its own Tree, so the Leaf-Fall, a child's tenth-year standing under that Tree, can happen in the square. Citizenship can rest on *this* town having watched you become, and the pilgrimage to Thaeloren is an extra rather than the only door.

The clerical count opens the era at [[The First Cut]], Cut-year 0, and the present, where those grafts have taken, is Cut-year 387. That count is not a finished map. Read the [[The World Frame|reach-gradient]] — how far the living wood has reached — as a date rather than as a chart of a completed age. Two clocks sit in it: how a child Turned, by the road or under a town Tree, and where the wood has taken. They overlap, and neither one has closed.

**[[Maiethorn]]** is old Hands, and the grafts there are dense and mature, so the Walking is biography for the deathless, the [[Long-Lived]] who still remember those roads, and a ribbon on a mile-shrine for everyone else. The exceptions are the Rain-Shadow, where the waiting went on after the core stopped, and any place where a Tree has sickened. Old Hands can un-Hands. That is weather, not a fall of the age.

**[[Strandoren]]** is mature Hands, and the faith there is worn. The road-houses finished becoming banks on this coast. What is left of the walk is a luxury, a hinge in a contract, and a hold that still smells of earth when the passenger is a pot of living wood.

**[[Heskoren]]** is the Grafting *this decade.* The luckiest towns have had a local canopy for a few generations, and whole districts still send their children down the road. [[Polity Archetypes|Vaethorn]]'s waiting is not a metaphor for history. It is the wave's front.

**[[The Sundering Isle|Kumbaan]]** never came onto either clock. No graft of this era reaches it, and no extra mile does either.

The pilgrimage **did not vanish.** It changed jobs. Institutions that survive by changing function are the texture of this era. The First Seat still authorizes cuts, and what used to be a decision is now a queue. Deathless houses still remember the road, and what used to be a bed is now credit. Mile-shrines still stand, and what used to be a grave is now folklore, an argument, and a thing children climb. The romance of the old mile is a Hands feeling. The graves are Walking facts.

Ornthael is not "the modern age after the Grafting." The wave is still moving. A child on the Long Mile this summer is not doing history.

Those same 387 summers also held a war, a split of paper, a fever, a flood, and a hush. Those years sit beside the two clocks in [[The Other Count]]. They do not replace them.

## The walk's three jobs (it changed work, it did not vanish)

Brenvaeth is the name for [[The Walking Years]], when a child's tenth summer meant the road. In Brenvaeth the walk was *how you Turned.* In Ornthael it is three jobs that share roads, inns, and the one-week door, and do not share a reason. Stance-variants live in [[Naming in the Turning]].

| Job | Who still walks | What they are buying | What they are not |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Devotion — the extra mile** | People who already have a local Tree and still want Thaeloren | an origin-fall; a ribbon at the First Hand; the Motherwood in Eolthael | necessity. They could have Turned at home. |
| **Prestige — the First-Hand year** | [[Polity Archetypes\|Lestrand]] money, a Bound signing-watch, a guild-ticket hinge, a wedding extra | being *seen at the origin* when you did not need to be; a held-over winter as a product | piety, unless it is also that. The Fair Hand will attend as witnesses to a signing. |
| **Necessity — the neighbour's week** (still *the old mile* when the neighbour is a continent) | Tree-poor, Tree-sick, Tree-young; a missed fall this year; [[Heskoren]] this cohort; the Rain-Shadow after the core stopped | a Given-door that is not at home | folklore. They are not re-enacting Brenvaeth. They are making the week. |

People did not say *Ornthael* on the road. They said *we are going to the Thallow*, the worn everyday name for Thaeloren, or *we Turn at Harrow's*, or *the berth is for the child*, and the later filing still depends on who is speaking: [[Polity Archetypes|Vaethorn]] calls almost any walk *the waiting-road*, [[Polity Archetypes|Lestrand]] files the extra as *the First-Hand year* and the need as *the borrowed week*, and [[Polity Archetypes|Threnmaieth]] wants the miles *on a roll*.

**They share the Near Mile in Hale-month**, the month when the road still fills. A devotion-column, a prestige household with too much luggage, a necessity-family who sold a year to make the trip, and a [[The Tree-Wardens|Road-hand]] going the other way with a pot of wood, can sleep in the same upper room. The innkeeper can tell who is which. They will not always tell you. The Far-Voiced ridge-cry still works, a voice that carries *the ford is wrong*, and some of the people who answer are pilgrims, while some are carts of wood.

**The inversion is lived on the road, not only in the names.** [[The Old Tongue|Thaelvaeth]] is wood carried *out*, the Tree going to the hearths. [[The Old Tongue|Brenvaeth]] was hearths walking *in*, people going to the Tree. In the Years of Hands both directions are traffic. A Road-hand and a tenth-year pass on a bank and do not owe each other a sermon. That passing *is* the two clocks in one afternoon.

**Hands can un-Hands.** A town Tree that sickens, or that fails a fall, puts a Hands-town back on the neighbour's week. A sick Tree is not the age ending, even in the core. The Seat will call it thin soil, and the town will call it this year.

The one-week door did not loosen. Miss Eolthael, the month that holds that one week, and the Given-door has shut, whether you missed it at home or at the origin. The Given-door is that week under a Tree, Struck remains the later mercy, and there is still no second-chance year. The held-over nine-year-old is still the expensive mercy — and on the prestige-walk it is also a winter you can buy.

[[The Sundering Isle|Kumbaan]] is not on this table. No extra mile crosses the storm-wall.

## What the Walk left behind

What the Walking built, the Hands still sleep in. The reuse is adaptive, not a field of ruins, and a thing the Walk left that a town treats as only a ruin is a Hands-town telling itself the road is over.

### Mile-shrines (the stone in the square)

A **mile-shrine** is still a stone at about a day's walk, with water, a flat top, and often a name and a ribbon. In a Hands-town the last one is frequently **in the square**, because the town grew around a graft planted beside the road, and the road's destination moved closer until the stone was in the middle of the life that replaced the walk.

The stone is several things at once, and the town does not agree which one it is allowed to be. It is a grave, a Walking fact, and a Long-Lived who says *I walked* can still tell you which ford the name belongs to. It is a waymark, because necessity-walkers still drink there and Road-hands still aim for the water. It is folklore, because devotion leaves a ribbon, Hands-children climb it, and the teaching-story of that climb is [[The Child Who Climbed the Stone]]. And it is an argument: a warden wants it moved because it is in the way of the Leaf-Fall crowd, a house that still dates its notes from a founding-summer wants it left, and a neighbour who still walks wants it left for a different reason, the water and the mile, not the memory.

The stone does not know which story it is in. A ribbon is still not a leaf-colour, not the colour that named what a Tree Gave. Travellers who have walked once know this. Travellers who have only heard the romance do not.

Hands-towns that **pull a stone down** are doing theology with a hammer. They are not wrong that the road no longer has to happen there. They are wrong if they think the graves went with the requirement. The next necessity-column will miss the water.

### The upper room (empty pilgrim-beds)

An old inn's upper room has too many beds for the present trade. In Brenvaeth those beds were the two crowded months. In Ornthael the same room is put to three uses, sometimes in the same fortnight.

Merchants take the beds, which is the White Note's conversion, occupancy treated as proof the house is competent. Eolthael still opens some of them, because devotion and prestige swell a Near-Mile house for a fortnight. And some stay **held**. A family said they would come, and they did not, so the innkeeper keeps the bed a year, then two, then as a habit that looks like hospitality and functions as a shrine and as proof the house remembers. [[The Walking Years|Thilim]] still makes Seine's. Houses that became banks mostly stopped making those beds, except the one they will not let, which is how a clerk writes *house custom* on a slate and means a grave.

The romance of the empty bed is a Hands feeling. The name written for the held bed is a Walking fact.

### Roads that end at a Tree

The pilgrim-road was often the main road. A graft took beside it, the town grew around the canopy, and **the road now ends at the Tree** — the last holy mile is a square. Beyond the boughs the old way is a cart-track to timber, a neighbour, or a ford. Children are told the road goes to the Motherwood, the origin grove at Thaeloren. It goes to the mill. Both can be true if you keep walking, which most Hands-children do not.

A road that "ends" is what a Tree-town says, while the necessity-family from the next valley still uses those same stones.

### Other things the Walk left that a character notices

What a character can still see, without anyone announcing an era, is the walk's leftovers still doing a job or refusing to stop.

Empty Salt-Walk berths sit in Eolthael, except the expensive ones and the holds that smell of wet earth out of season, because a pot of wood is the passenger and the earth comes with it.

A Far-Voiced ridge-cry still goes up in the old pattern, *the ford is wrong.* No column answers. A Road-hand does. Birds do.

A pair of siblings will not walk the same side of a road, one of them Given and the other UnTurned, and folk still call the second one Kept. They are a split household's grandchildren, keeping a courtesy they cannot date. Markets still swell at an old mile-mark in Hale-month and then vanish, even when no column comes, because the habit has outlasted the road. In a Near-Mile house a kitchen-knife hangs on a ribbon, and the house will not name a saint for it. That knife is [[The First Cut|the nameless knife]], and it is still not a museum piece.

## Deathless houses' road-past as present credit

Many [[Economy and the Tithe|deathless houses]] began as **road-houses**, inns on the mile kept by the Long-Lived. That change of fortune is already in the past. The inns became banks, the same Long-Lived are still in the house, and the fortune is a different one.

**The founding-summer is the credit.** "We kept you on the mile" is an origin-story that still collects. A century-loan written from a house-year, counted from the summer the house was founded rather than from the First Cut, is not only a quirk of dating ([[The Ages of the Turning|Ledan's query]]). It is a claim that the porridge was real, the note was real, and the house is still the same house. No mortal court can audit a [[Long-Lived]]'s memory of a walk two hundred summers back. That is why the trusted note holds. It is also why a house can call a debt whose grandchildren do not remember the ford.

Two fates of that same origin are still standing, and they do not agree about what success was.

**[[The White Note House]]** is the reformation. The beds are let to merchants and the paper is on the quay, so Ledan's **200th-summer** query, written in Cut-year 280, is already a clerk's problem in a house now in its **307th** summer. The Held names are in the founding-book, most of those beds are let, and one is not, which is how a bank proves it used to be an inn.

**[[The Third Hearth]]** is the house that stayed a hearth. Thilim still keeps it, still makes Seine's bed, and still knows which guests will be here at dark. The notes are smaller and the shrine is larger. Lestrand calls this a bed-house that failed to become a bank. Thilim does not call it a failure.

The long-house of books is still the deathless paying their own Tithe. The road-past is why anyone trusted them to endow a library in the first place. A house that remembered every debtor on the mile is a house you let steer the shelves.

**The prestige-walk is now a product some houses sell.** A held-over winter at the origin, once the frightened mercy of a far family who could not miss the week, is a First-Hand year you can book. It is one commodity, on three Coast desks, plus the beds at the origin, and the desks do not all sell the same piece of it.

**[[Netstrand]]** supplies the **berths**: hulls, crews, and West Water capacity chartered *in* to the Old Crossing. What the Walk left them is the far crossing, not this winter at the grove.

**[[The White Note House]]** writes the **terms**, credit for a year you do not need. They noticed the upper room was empty in Nethael and full of the wrong people in Eolthael, and they made a term for it. They did not invent the hold.

**[[Orentel]]** **holds** the departure. Sorim's first-quay occupancy is the business, empty Eolthael berths included, in the city that stages people toward Maiethorn.

**[[Orenbren]]** **houses** the year at the origin, beds within a week's walk of the grove. It is not a fourth seller of the berth.

Necessity-families still sign the old kind of note: eat now, pay on the home-walk, or when the child is grown, or when a sibling walks next year. It is the same paper doing two jobs. A clerk who files both the necessity-note and the prestige-note as occupancy is not telling them apart.

Vaethorn will preach that the houses *abandoned the waiting-road*, and the history is shorter than that sermon. The walk stopped being required in the core, the memory did not stop, and the paper outlived the season. That is a reformation, not a betrayal, and it still collects.

## Heskoren as the live front

**This is the wave in the present tense.** It is not a re-enactment of Brenvaeth. Some hamlets are in the Walking Years *this year, for this cohort*, still sending children the long way because no nearer Tree can take them. Some are Hands-era one valley over, with a canopy of their own. Both can be true on the same road.

**[[Harrow's Green]]** is the luckiest kind of Heskoren town. A scion arrived in the Cut-year 200s, spoke a generation later, and has now Turned local children for a few generations. It is a neighbour's canopy, and it is also a queue-confession you can see from the ford. The Harrow-sentence, *we Turned at Harrow's Tree before we had our own*, is how new towns talk everywhere. On Heskoren it is the decade, not a proverb about the past.

**[[The Three Hamlets Past the Ford]]** are the waiting kind. The Seat's Cutting-leave, the paper that says a town may cut and plant, already named them as thin soil. They send this year's ten-year-olds to Harrow's, or further if Harrow's year is too full, or still the Long Mile if the neighbour is only a stick in a pot. They have learned not to say *queue.* They can see the canopy from where they stand. Cutting by folk-right, with a kitchen-knife and without that paper, is a temptation. Most slips die. The ones that take are how the queue learns it is not the only horticulture in the world.

**Old Ways host-rights start here as a collision, not a sermon.** A graft set into an old Taken-In green is a guest in that green. A Cutting-leave is a mainland paper. The first meal comes first, or the planting is a claiming. [[The Door-Keepers]] and the Road-hands can both love the wood and still fight about the bowl.

**Borrowed Turnings are the front's ordinary year.** Citizenship-by-local-witness, belonging to the town that saw you Turn, is Hands-era weather, and the town that saw you may be Harrow's rather than the hamlet that raised you. Vouching-at-a-distance was invented to carry an *origin*-fall home, someone who had been under Thaeloren standing for a child in a town that had not seen it. It now carries a *neighbour*-fall home instead, the same three pillars, over a shorter road. The unvouched Struck are still older than the grafts. A local canopy did not dissolve them.

The Seat's narration is that her will is still travelling. Folk memory is that the cart came late, the soil was called thin, and Harrow's green is visible from here. Vaethorn will preach *we wait because we are unworthy.* History does not agree. They wait because the wood is slow, the road is long, and the list of who is next was written in the Motherwood.

The Rain-Shadow on [[Maiethorn]] is the same pattern on an older continent: waiting that went on after the core stopped. [[Ornsael]] is a town where this is happening, and the Hand, that town's Tree, Speaks most years even while the well is dropping and the west-road is water. A west-road child, after the Sacred Core has stopped walking, is the same waiting on the Motherland, which is how you can tell Ornthael is not a finished age.

## What is strained (the present tense of pressure)

This is not a chronicle of collapse. The wheel has not been rolled, and what a traveller can already see is pressure, not an ending.

The **First Seat** is old in the queue. It has authorized cuts since Cut-year 19, about three hundred and sixty-eight summers, and towns that can see a neighbour's canopy have learned the horticulture-sentence by heart. Folk-right slips still happen, and [[Polity Archetypes|Threnmaieth]] still wants the college on the roll. The Seat is not falling. It is *heavy.*

The **Road-hands** are thin on the Heskoren front. They carry living earth and mortal wood on a skeleton circuit, and their authority expires a week out from the Motherwood. A live Tree is a quiet success no one writes down. A dead one is a name on a complaint. They plant what they were given leave to plant, and they sleep in the town that was not chosen.

**Waiting towns** hang on a single scion, or on a neighbour's week. That is a civic crisis wearing a child's summer.

**Deathless houses** are richer in paper than in beds. The road-past is the credit, and the upper room is the tell.

Play may later ask what gives, and this note leaves the pressure on the table, with the cautions in the GM Notes.

## Settlement seeds

The catalog of what the Walk left, as types a town can be, is [[Settlement Seeds]]. The towns where this note's present is already happening are these.

[[Harrow's Green]] is a live-front Tree-town, the neighbour's canopy a waiting hamlet can see. [[The Three Hamlets Past the Ford]] are the waiting cluster, the queue made visible from the ford. [[The Third Hearth]] is a road-house that stayed a hearth. [[Ornsael]] is the Rain-Shadow walk-hold, the same waiting on the Motherland. [[The White Note House]] is a road-house that became a desk.

The Tree-at-the-centre grammar in [[Daily Life]] still holds. What the Walk left layers onto it: the stone in the square, the upper room, and the road that ends at the boughs.

## Visibility markers (what a character notices)

- Someone at a table says *I walked*, and the room sorts itself by who goes quiet, then sorts again when a Heskoren cousin says the same words and means last summer.
- A stone in a square has climbing-scuffs and a ribbon on it, and a Hands-child looking at the stone does not know it was a grave.
- An upper room is let to a wool-merchant, and the name of a held bed is written where no one reads it aloud.
- A Road-hand and a tenth-year pass on a bank, wood going out toward the towns and a hearth's child going in toward the grove.
- One debt carries three dates for the same summer: a Cut-year, a house-year, and a "year the graft took."
- A [[Heskoren]] cohort is on the neighbour's week while a neighbouring canopy is one summer from its first fall, or ten summers past that first fall and still not theirs.
- The Salt-Walk berths are empty, except one hold that smells of earth.
- Someone names a Phoenix as if the rebirth marked an era, and no one at the table agrees which summer that was.

## Folklore

The teaching-story of what the Walk left is [[The Child Who Climbed the Stone]]. A Hands-child and a waiting-child share the stone, and the last line will not choose whether the road is over. Tree-town children hear it as why they climb. Waiting children hear it as why they can see the canopy and still not be under it. Innkeepers hear it as the upper room. Speakers hear it as a stone in the way of a crowd.

### What the player body leaves out

Do not smuggle a graft, or an extra mile, across the storm-wall to Kumbaan.

Do not narrate the Years of Hands as "the modern age after the Grafting." The wave is still moving, and a child on a road this summer is inside the era, not commemorating it.

Do not treat a sick Tree as the age ending. It puts a Hands-town back on the neighbour's week, which is the old door opening again for one cohort.

Do not invent a second banking-order, and do not give a novelty a new clergy. Do not let the three Coast desks — Netstrand, the White Note, and Orentel — all claim they invented the First-Hand year.

Do not design the three archetype capitals in this note. The Tree-at-the-centre grammar in [[Daily Life]] holds, and what the Walk left layers onto it: the stone in the square, the upper room, and the road that ends at the boughs.

Do not dump [[Ornsael]] onto Heskoren, and do not forget it. A west-road child, after the Sacred Core has stopped walking, is the same waiting on the Motherland.

Do not name historical Phoenixes until a story needs a fishbowl that old.

Mataero cannot see that "house custom" is the credit, or that letting the loft to wool is how a road-house finishes becoming a bank in front of a junior who needs the conversion to look like competence. The founding-name they will not copy is the held bed kept as a bank-token. Thilim, inland, still makes the bed. The wool will still come due.

The clerk who files the necessity-note and the prestige-note both as occupancy does not see the difference.
