# The Walking Years

> **The old default way to Turn, now lived.** Once there was only the [[Turning Tree|Awakening Tree]], and a child's tenth-year Turning — standing under that Tree, where they might be Given what they would become — meant a *walk* to it. Families who could not make the trip **stayed [[Law and Citizenship|UnTurned]] for want of it**, never Given, and folk called them Kept, so the far reaches ran heavy on the UnTurned and the [[The Premise|Struck]], people who became something later at a threshold, as the *rule*, not an edge-case. Everyday speech calls the time **the Walking Years.** The liturgical name is **Brenvaeth** *(BREN-vaeth)* — "the hearth gone to the waiting." It grows out of [[Before the Walk]] with no dated start, and the spine and the two clocks are [[The Ages of the Turning]]. This note is the road as a life.


## Overview

In these years the Leaf-Fall, a child's tenth summer under the Tree, happened in one place: one grove, on [[Maiethorn]], under Thaeloren, that same Awakening Tree. A tenth summer was a journey or it was no Leaf-Fall at all, because you were not there.

The far-flung who stayed home were not "skipped by a goddess" in any way a town could prove. They were **children who did not walk.** Some were later Struck at a drowning, an oath, a death — a becoming with no leaf and no crowd, unwitnessed, because the only crowd that counted was a continent away. Some stayed UnTurned for life, wanted or not, because want-of-a-trip is not a theology, it is a distance.

This is the historical origin of today's pattern, stated mundanely: **thin reach used to mean far from the one Tree.** After [[The First Cut]], the dated hinge at Cut-year 0, when grafts began and wood from that Tree was carried out to make others, thin reach came to mean *Tree-poor places* — young, sick, or few grafts — which is why a hamlet next-door to a dying scion can be "far" in the old sense without being far on a map.

The pilgrimage **did not vanish** when grafts began. It changed jobs. In [[The Years of Hands]] the devout still walk to the origin; the poor and the Tree-bereaved still walk to a neighbour. The Walking Years are the time when *everyone who Turned as Given had walked, or had been carried.*

The later romance of the old mile is a Hands-era feeling laid on Walking-era graves.

## The walk (what it actually was)

### The door is one week

[[The Reckoning of the Year|Turning-Week]] is the week bracketing the High Solstice in **Eolthael**, the month that holds that solstice. Every child who has turned ten since the last High Solstice Turns that week, under Thaeloren, or they do not Turn as Given at all.

Miss the week and the door has shut. An eleven-year-old at the origin the following summer is a visitor, because the Tree's Given-door is the tenth-year cohort, not a standing appointment. [[The Premise|Struck]] doors still open later, elsewhere, with no leaf and no crowd. That is a different mercy, and it was always the far lands' other path.

This is why families from any distance **left early**, and why the richest or most frightened far households spent a *year* at the origin: a nine-year-old wintered in a road-house so they could not miss. The held-over child is the expensive mercy. Everyone else gambled one summer.

Hands-era towns with a local Tree did not grow a second door. A fever in your own square in Eolthael is the same shut week as a late Long-Mile arrival. Procedure for misses, refusals, and the folk habit of calling every unGifted child Kept: [[Turning Tree#Questions a warden gets asked]].

### Three walks, not one road

Named in the common tongue, because the walk was logistics before it was liturgy. Stance-variants live in [[Naming in the Turning]].

| Walk | Who used it | How long | What it cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **the Near Mile** | [[Maiethorn]] inland, anyone already on the Motherland | days to a couple of weeks (**3–12 days**) | a harvest-timing; almost everyone who meant to come, came |
| **the Salt Walk** | [[Strandoren]] — ship [[Named Ground\|the Old Crossing]], then the Near Mile | a sailing plus the inland days (**3–5 days' sail** + inland; typically 1–3 weeks) | a berth in fair weather; miss the last crossing and you miss the year |
| **the Long Mile** | [[Heskoren]], and the Strandoren interior before the coasts were rich | weeks to months (**6–12 weeks** for a coast-to-grove attempt) | a year's surplus, or a road-house note, or you did not go |

People did not say *Brenvaeth* on the road. They said *we are on the Long Mile*, or *we took the salt*, and the later names still sort by who is speaking: [[Polity Archetypes|Vaethorn]] calls the whole past *the waiting-road*, [[Polity Archetypes|Lestrand]] files it as *the road-years*, and [[Polity Archetypes|Threnmaieth]] wants the miles *on a roll*.

**Timing against the year.** The names in this timing are months. Near-Mile families left in **Haelren**, which people also call Hale-month, or in early **Eolthael**, and were home by **Vaelorn**. Salt-Walk households watched the Old Crossing from **Saelorn**, and a late storm in Hale-month left an UnTurned child. Long-Mile columns left in **Liren** or **Saelorn** — the thaw, the first green — and hoped the High Solstice had not happened without them. Some arrived in **Leddorn**, which people call Ledger-month, with a ten-year-old who had already missed. The road-houses knew that silence. They did not charge extra for it. They also did not refund the note.

[[The Sundering Isle|Kumbaan]] is not on this table, because no walk crossed the storm-wall.

### Who could afford it

The Near Mile was a civic season. Whole towns moved a short way together, and a poor child walked in a neighbour's dust. Cost was real (lost labour, a week of food) and rarely decisive.

The Salt Walk sorted by **cash and weather.** Coastal wealth on [[Strandoren]] bought berths on the crossing. Interior Strandoren was already "far" in these years, even though the coasts would later be Tree-rich. A family might send the Turning-child and one adult, and leave the rest. Guilds sometimes advanced a berth against a future colour — the leaf that would name a child Given — so that a Given Answered or Far-Voiced was worth the loan, while a hug, the Tree keeping the child and giving no colour, was a debt with no new trade in it. The Fair Hand would later call this a term. In Brenvaeth it was just the quay.

The Long Mile sorted by **whether you could lose a year.** You needed food, shoes, a second adult if you had one, and the note you signed at the first road-house that would take it. Many could not afford that. Those who could often could not afford it for *every* child.

**The split household** is the era's ordinary cruelty. A family with three children near ten could sometimes walk one of them, so the one who walked might come home Given, and the ones who stayed were UnTurned, or were Struck later at a local door, and grew up in the same kitchen as a sibling the origin had seen. No one needed a theology for this. Distance did it. [[Polity Archetypes|Vaethorn]]'s later guilt (*we wait because we are unworthy*) is a Hands-era reading of that fact. History does not agree with the guilt.

[[The Old Ways]] households on Heskoren often **refused the walk** on purpose. Their children were already the land's. Motherfaith neighbours, counting a child by whether the trip had been made, read those children as UnTurned for want of the trip, which is a misreading the walk itself cannot correct. The same child can be claimed as already belonging and pitied as passed over; the walk offers no neutral verdict.

### Who was let through

Distance and coin were not the only sorters. A class of the untithed, people carrying no Tithe for anyone to feed, sat on Thaeloren and decided who might stand under it. Folk called them **the Closed Seat**, and also **the Grove-Sitters**. A family could have shoes and a Salt-Walk berth and still be turned away at the gate. **Grove-leave** was the paper you carried to that gate if you hoped to be let through.

Far lands ran heavy on the UnTurned because some children did not walk, and because some children walked and were turned away. Both of those fill a kitchen. The later romance of the old mile prefers the children who never started, and the folk songs remember the ones who arrived and were turned from the gate.

The Closed Seat was an *origin-gate*, not a world-empire. Strandoren paid the crossing and was never a province of that gate, while Heskoren lay beyond a gate's ride. When a Near-Mile town Turned under a copy, a Tree grown from wood carried out from the origin, the gate was no longer the only seeing. The war that tried to make that untrue is [[The Closing]]. The road was already a life. The gate was a third cruelty on it.

### Who died

Children died on the road, and so did the adults who carried them. They died at river-crossings in the thaw, of a fever in a packed Near-Mile inn, in a late squall on the Old Crossing, of hunger on a Long-Mile stretch between hearths, and in a birth that would not wait for the grove. This is not a slaughter-age and not a hymn. It is a logistics problem that killed people, which is what long roads do.

The graves are mostly unmarked at first. Then someone sets a stone at a day's walk, because the next column needs to know the water, and because a name should sit somewhere. A ribbon goes on the stone the year after, and a later column uses the stone as a mile-mark. The romance and the graves are the same object seen at two hours of the day.

A child who dies on the way to be witnessed may Strike as [[Returned]] on the road — a becoming with no crowd, on the way to the only crowd that counted. It is rare, and it is remembered.

The origin also filled with **children who arrived and had no one to walk home with.** Road-houses took some in, and a bed became a life. Origin-towns took some. A Given child with no one to carry the witness home is the other half of the grave: they lived, and belonging still had to be invented for them.

## Far reaches as the rule

In Brenvaeth, a land far from Thaeloren was **UnTurned-heavy and Struck-heavy because most ten-year-olds never stood under the canopy.** That was not a frontier curiosity. It was how the world worked anywhere the walk was too far or too dear.

The **Given** were the ones who had been *there*, a minority that shrank as the miles piled up. They came home as specialists the town could not otherwise grow, and as people the town had not watched become.

The **UnTurned** were the default far childhood, because they never stood under the Tree. They were whole, untithed, and unwitnessed as Given, and folk called them Kept. The few who walked and were hugged, kept by the Tree with no colour, were Kept in the strict sense. Some of the UnTurned were wanted that way. Some were the price of a summer the family could not raise. Some were Old Ways children the Motherfaith had misfiled.

The **Struck** were the far lands' other door, and they always had been. A drowning, an oath, or a death made them, with no leaf and no origin-crowd. The unease that follows the Struck is older than the grafts. The walk only made it louder, because the Given in the same town *had* been seen, a continent away.

When the wood later travelled, this pattern *relocated*. A Tree-poor pocket next to a dying scion reads the same on a census and is not far on a map. Some of it still *is* Brenvaeth, this year, for this cohort, and some of it is a neighbour's canopy and a queue. Both can be true on the same road.

## Institutions of the road

There was no travelling church with offices. [[The Tree-Wardens]] as an order that carries grafts is Hands-era weather, not the weather of this road. In Brenvaeth the **origin-hearth** was the only Speaking that counted, the only place where a speaker under Thaeloren could name what a child had become and have that naming travel. Far towns had no Tree to tend, so what the road had was inns, stones, and summer traffic.

### Mile-shrines

A **mile-shrine** is a stone at about a day's walk, with water if the ground allows, a flat top where a pack can sit, and often a name and a ribbon.

They begin as graves and waymarks in the same act. You bury someone where the column had to stop, and the next year the stop is the place you aim for. The devout later leave ribbons for the Tree, children later climb the stones, and Hands-towns later argue about pulling them down, because the road now ends at a local canopy and the stone is "in the way." The stone does not know which story it is in. What the road left, and where those arguments live now, is in [[The Years of Hands]], in the stone in the square and the upper room.

A ribbon is not a leaf-colour, not the colour of leaf that names what the Tree Gave someone. People tie what they have, so a copper-green scrap on a Long-Mile stone does not mean a [[The Taken-In|Taken-In]] died there, even when a traveller who has only heard the romance reads the green that way. Travellers who have walked once know the difference. Travellers who have only heard the romance do not.

### Road-houses (the deathless inn in the act of being an inn)

Many [[Economy and the Tithe|deathless houses]], houses kept by the [[Long-Lived]] past any mortal lifetime, began on this road. In Brenvaeth they were **road-houses**: beds, porridge, a dry room, and credit for a walk you could not miss twice.

The everyday name is **road-house.** The cant is **brenhael** *(BREN-hael)* — *bren* + *hael*, "the hearth kept whole." The Waiting Lands say *waiting-hearth*. The Ledger Coast says *the bed-house* (and still means the bank). The Tallied Crown would like them *on the roll*.

What a road-house actually sold was ordinary, and several later institutions are that ordinary thing with a different job.

**A bed in the two crowded months**, and a quieter bed in the other ten. Held-over nine-year-olds were the winter trade. An empty pilgrim-bed in a Hands-era upper room used to be this bed, in the season when the road was full.

**A note.** You eat now, and you pay at the origin, or on the way home, or when the child is grown, or when a sibling walks next year. The Long-Lived innkeeper **forgets none of it**, because that memory does not fade. The memory is why the paper will outlive the pilgrimage-season and become the trusted note. In the act, it is an innkeeper who will still know your name in fifty years, which is a kindness and a leash.

**A little Tithe-furnishing**, improvised, and not a guild. A green-lot behind the house was there for a Taken-In parent, and a shed was there so a Far-Voiced could cry without taking the dormitory with them. The road itself is novelty enough that a Long-Lived innkeeper's own Tithe stays fed, which is why the deathless *stayed* in the trade, and why the same people later endow libraries: the hunger that kept them on the mile is the hunger that built the archive. The greens-keepers and hall-keepers are Hands-era gates, and Brenvaeth had a pot and a shed.

**A held bed.** A family said they would come. They did not. The innkeeper keeps the bed a year, then two, then as a habit that looks like hospitality and functions as a shrine. The slate below is one of those beds, still being made.

The White Note's founding-summer is a house-year, not a Cut-year. In the Walking Years that house was a hearth with too many boots by the door. This is the same fortune, earlier.

### Summer traffic

For two months the Near Mile is a moving town, columns and dust, with Far-Voiced cries carrying from ridge to ridge: *the road is full; the ford is wrong; a child is down*. The Two-Bodied range the edges, because the ranging *is* the Tithe and the column is a herd. Markets grow at the mile-shrines and vanish when Eolthael ends. Then the road is empty and local, and the road-house counts the notes.

There were no pilgrim-knights and no official convoy-guild. People walked with whoever left the same week. A Bound's word might hold a column together for a fortnight, and a Bound cannot cheaply lie about what they swear. A Kept adult with no Tithe to manage was, then as now, the person who could carry the extra pack.

## Witness at the origin, or not at all

[[Law and Citizenship|Citizenship-by-local-witness]], belonging because your own town saw you Turn, is Hands-era weather. In Brenvaeth the town that *saw you Turn* was the crowd under Thaeloren, or it was no one.

Going home did not bring that crowd with you. Your village had not watched. Belonging at home required someone who *had* been under the boughs to stand for you: a parent, a neighbour from the column, or a Long-Lived who keeps origin-falls the way they later keep town-falls. **Vouching-at-a-distance was invented to carry the origin's witness home.** The three pillars of that vouch — Long-Lived memory, a Bound's word, and a Far-Voiced feeling — are older than the grafts. The *job* of carrying a far Turning is what belongs to the Walking Years. Hands-era travelers still move on that job, under the names **road-word**, company-vouch, and guest-right ([[Law and Citizenship#How a traveler stays vouched]]).

A Given child with no surviving witness is legally thin in the place they grew up. Origin-orphans, road-house children, and a Bound stranger who will swear the colour they did not see, and who cannot cheaply lie, are all versions of that thinness. The unvouched are older than the stigma that later attached to the Struck, and they include people the Tree *did* Give.

Someone Struck on the road carries a double unease: an unwitnessed becoming, and that becoming happening *on the way* to the witnessing. A Returned child may walk the last Near-Mile days beside the parent who has not yet forgiven the threshold.

Staying home meant you were never Given-witnessed. You were still of your town, because your town had watched you be a child. An UnTurned child was not "unmarked on a roll." Folk called such children Kept, though no Tree had hugged them.

The origin-crowd was enormous in Eolthael and ordinary the rest of the year. Speakers of the origin-hearth named colours until their voices went. The colours were already folk-known. The warden's word made them *count* for people who would have to carry that word home.

## Long-Lived who still say *I walked*

A [[Long-Lived]] who says *I walked* is saying when they were young. Memory is perfect. **Meaning is not.**

At present, Cut-year 387, the sentence does at least four jobs, and the people who say it argue about which job is being done.

**I walked as a child, under Thaeloren, before my town had wood.** A Maiethorn deathless Given in the late Walking — the last cohorts in the core, before Maiethorn filled in during the first Cut-years — is speaking of a summer most of the room has only as a ribbon. A Heskoren deathless may mean *last decade*, the same words on a different clock.

**I walked the mile as the inn.** A road-house Long-Lived may never have stood under the canopy as a pilgrim. They walked the same stretch every summer for two hundred years. They remember every debtor. They will not always tell you which children came back.

**I remember the graves and I will not call them holy.** These are the skeptics among the deathless. The road, they say, was biology plus bad fords. They are sincere. They still tear up at a ribbon, which they will not explain.

**I remember the graves and I will not call them logistics.** These are the devout among the deathless. Every stone, they say, was a waiting. They are sincere. They still know which ford killed which name, and they will tell you whether you asked or not.

A Blood-Read will give you a true fragment of a walk. It will not tell you which of the four jobs the living speaker is doing. That fight is play.

They also disagree with Seat chronicles that start history at [[The First Cut]]. A Long-Lived who walked as a child, and a clerk who counts Cut-years, will fight about the same summer. Both can be honest. Only one of them was there.

## Visibility markers (what a character notices)

- In Hale-month the Near Mile is dusty with people on the move, and in Ledger-month that same road has no dust on it, because the walking season is over.
- A mile-shrine carries a ribbon and the scuffs of someone climbing it, and a Hands-era child looking at the stone does not know it was a grave.
- An inn's upper room has more beds than the present trade can fill, and the house still dates its notes from a founding-summer when those beds were full.
- A Held bed has not been let in anyone's living mortal memory. The innkeeper can tell you the name on it. They will not tell you whether they think the child is dead, or Kept, or Old Ways, or was never born.
- A pair of siblings will not walk the same side of a road. One of them is Given, and the other never stood under a Tree, so that second child is UnTurned, though folk still call them Kept.
- A Far-Voiced cry goes out along a ridge in the old pattern, *the ford is wrong.* No column answers. Birds do.
- Someone at a table says *I walked*, and the room sorts itself by who goes quiet.

## In-world text

*A Turning-Week slate from the third hearth on the Near Mile, kept by Thilim, who is Long-Lived and has kept this house since before the Cut, as later clerks would say. The verso is knife-scored with tally-marks that do not match the recto.*

> *Eolthael, the summer the ford ran high.*
>
> *Beds: forty-and-two spoken for. Notes out: Tora of the long cape, two people, pay on the home-walk. Valein of the salt, one person, paid at the quay. The Held: Seine of the far cape — tenth summer, not come. Hold the bed. The mother said next year. Next year was last year.*
>
> *Porridge: enough. Shed: the blue-leaf girl from the second column cried the rafters quiet; no charge; we needed the quiet. Green-lot: used. The Taken-In man from the long cape left a cutting. I put it in the pot. It is not a Tree.*
>
> *I do not ask which summer was holy. I ask which people will be here at dark. The Held bed is made. If Seine comes I will not have to remember I made it for nothing.*

The cutting in the pot is not a First Cut. It is a Taken-In man who missed his green, and Thilim put the cutting in the pot and wrote that it was not a Tree. The knife that was the First Cut is a different thing, and it is [[The First Cut]].

## Folklore

The teaching-story of the road is [[The Child Who Counted Stones]]. It holds the romance and the graves in the same count, and its last line will not choose between them. Near-Mile children hear it as a walking-song, Long-Mile children hear it as a warning, and Hands-era children hear it as the old mile, which is how a grave becomes a rhyme.

## Key Events

The era has no dated start, because it grows out of [[Before the Walk]] when the trip becomes *the* way to Turn. The dated hinge is [[The First Cut]], at Cut-year 0. After that the Walking Years cease to be the *default* in lands the wood has reached, and they remain the default in lands it has not. [[Heskoren]] is still, in places, *in* this era. That is not a metaphor.

Do not add a walk that crosses the storm-wall to Kumbaan.

Do not pick a cutter, and do not date the Tree.
