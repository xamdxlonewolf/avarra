# The Ages of the Turning

This note is how people in the Turning divide the past, and it is the spine the other history notes are written from. It is not a chronicle of kings, and it does not pile the world into four ages. Once there was only the [[Turning Tree|Awakening Tree]], and a child's tenth-year Turning meant a walk to that Tree. Turning is what the Tree does for the child in that year: a Condition can be Given, and a child who never arrives stays UnTurned. Then grafts travelled, living cuttings of the same Tree carried out and planted, and the devout say the Mother's reach travelled with the wood. This note names the ages, says how the years are counted, and says what that change did to law, money, language, and the map.

[[The Walking Years]] follows the life on the road, [[The First Cut]] follows the cut and the spread, and [[The Years of Hands]] together with [[Settlement Seeds]] follows the present and what the walk left behind. The dated years that were not about wood are [[The Other Count]], and [[A History of the Turning]] tells the whole history in order on one page.

## The organizing idea: two clocks, not four stacked ages

People in this world do not live in a Fourth Age. They keep two clocks at the same time, and the one a person reaches for tells you where that person stands.

The first clock is personal, and a family keeps it the way it keeps a grandparent's stories. It counts how you were Turned. Did you walk to [[Maiethorn|Thaeloren]], the Awakening Tree, or to a neighbour who had already made that walk, or did you stand under a town graft, a cutting of that Tree planted at home? An ordinary family will tell you they are a Hands-house, and that grandmother still talks of the road. A [[Long-Lived]] person who says "I walked" is saying when they were young, because the Long-Lived live long enough that the walk can have been their own childhood.

The second clock is geographic, and it has not finished. The Grafting is the carrying of living wood outward from the origin, and it is not a golden age that opened and then closed. The wood started at the Awakening Tree, filled [[Maiethorn]], crossed the sea to [[Strandoren]], and is still arriving on [[Heskoren]]. [[The Sundering Isle|Kumbaan]] stands entirely off that spread. Where the wood is still thin, that thinness is not a scar history left behind. It is the front of a process that has not finished.

Both clocks sit on a preface that was never given an age-name, [[Before the Walk]]. The Tree was already there, and no count that is being honest begins with it.

This was not a civilizing mission, and the Walking Years were not a golden age of pilgrimage that the grafts then improved. The Walking Years were the work of getting a child to one Tree in time, across weeks of road and water, and that work killed children. The Grafting was horticulture, the cutting and planting of living wood, and people sanctified it afterward. The First Seat, the tree-wardens who keep the count, now tells both the walk and the grafts as the Mother's will travelling. Folk memory is less tidy, and holds the romance and the graves in the same sentence.

The pair of liturgical names is a deliberate inversion of the name the [[The Tree-Wardens|Road-hands']] use for themselves, and the Road-hands are [[The Tree-Wardens]]. In [[The Old Tongue]], **[[The Old Tongue|Thaelvaeth]]** means "the Tree gone to the waiting," which is wood carried out to the hearths. **Brenvaeth** *(BREN-vaeth)* means "the hearth gone to the waiting," which is people walking in to the Tree. **Eoloren** *(EOL-or-en)* is the liturgical name of the cut, and **Ornthael** *(ORN-thael)* is the liturgical name of the years when a town has its own Tree. The Grafting is the moment the world stopped sending hearths to the Tree and started sending the Tree to hearths.

## How years are numbered (the name-reveals-stance lever, on history itself)

There is no year-zero that everyone shares. The calendar that these counts sit on has the same twelve months, and it has three different days for the new year, which [[The Reckoning of the Year]] sets out. The past is the same past. There are seven honest ways to count it, and the number a clerk writes on a debt is a theology, because the year you choose is the place you are willing to start the story from.

| Who | What they count from | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| [[The Tree-Wardens\|First Seat]] / devout clerks | **the First Cut** — **Cut-years** (in the liturgy, the *Eoloren-count*) | "the 387th Cut-year" |
| [[Polity Archetypes\|Threnmaieth]] | Cut-years *and* a **crown-count** from [[The Two Papers]] (Cut-year 67; the present is **Crown-year 320**) | a number that wants to be a roll |
| [[Polity Archetypes\|Lestrand]] / [[Economy and the Tithe\|deathless houses]] | a **house-founding**, the issue of a note, or the term of a contract | "the 307th summer of the White Note," which is the present; the archival conversion is the **200th**, Ledan's query in Cut-year 280 |
| [[Polity Archetypes\|Vaethorn]] | **the year our graft took**, or the last actual Turning-Week | a child "turns the year" when the local Tree last managed a fall, and that is already how Vaethorn starts its calendar |
| [[The Watching]] | they **refuse the Cut-count** | a count laid over the Tree is not an epoch, because the Tree was already seeing |
| [[The Old Ways]] | a closed door, a drowning-year, or a guest that took | the Years of Hands are when guests arrived, not when history began |
| [[The Sundering Isle\|Kumbaan]] | **moons** | the mainland ages are a sailor's chart, and Kumbaan does not owe that chart a count |

The present is **Cut-year 387**, the 387th summer since the First Cut.

What nobody can date is older than that number. The [[Turning Tree|Awakening Tree]] is older than any reliable dating, and [[Before the Walk]] has no start. Who made the First Cut is contested, and [[The First Cut]] keeps the argument without settling it. Believers ask why her Trees reached the far lands late, and skeptics say there is no Leaf-Mother. This note does not choose between them.

Cut-year 0 is a graft. It is the first witnessed copy of living wood, and it is not the first Condition, not the first Giving, and not the Tree's birthday. The Giving, and the walk that carried children to it, sit in [[Before the Walk]] and [[The Walking Years]] with no start year written on this shelf. Becoming at a door, a Condition arriving at a threshold away from the Tree, is older than the walk. Being born a Kind, one of the peoples a person is from birth, is older than both.

The bands below are enough to keep the geography honest. They are not a campaign almanac.

| **Cut-year 0–80** | [[Maiethorn]] filling. The origin-towns, nearest the Awakening Tree, came first, and the Rain-Shadow was slower. |
| **Cut-year 40–160** | [[Strandoren]] by sea. The wealthy coasts came first, and the deep interior later. |
| **Cut-year 200–387** | [[Heskoren]], still incomplete. The luckiest towns have had a local Turning for a few generations, and whole districts still wait. |
| **never** | [[The Sundering Isle\|Kumbaan]]. The wood never crossed. |

A freshly planted scion, the cutting kept alive on the road, still takes a generation to hold a Leaf-Fall ([[Turning Tree]]). So the year a graft arrives and the year a town Turns its own children under its own canopy are twenty years apart. Heskoren's waiting is not only the queue, the list of towns waiting for a cutting. It is the queue, and then the childhood of the wood.

## What the two clocks did (spine consequences)

These are the lasting effects of the two clocks on law, money, the clergy, language, the map, and faith.

**Law.** [[Law and Citizenship|Citizenship-by-local-witness]], citizenship because someone local saw you Turned, is the default of the Years of Hands. In the Walking Years the town that saw you Turn was the crowd under Thaeloren, or it was nobody, if you never arrived. Children in the far lands who stayed UnTurned were not simply missing from a roll. They were unwitnessed as Given, because they did not make the trip, and a Giving is a Condition from the Tree. People invented ways to carry that original witness home across the distance: a [[Long-Lived]] person who remembers the fall at the origin, the word of someone [[Bound]], and the feeling of someone [[Far-Voiced]]. That vouching at a distance was made so the origin's witness could travel. The Struck who were never vouched for are older than the grafts, because a becoming on a far threshold was always unwitnessed. The Grafting did not invent that unease. It localized who is expected to have been seen, and it put that expectation in the town where the person lives.

**Money.** Many of the [[Economy and the Tithe|deathless houses]], the houses of the Long-Lived, began as road-houses. They were inns, they held endowments, and they extended credit across a century to families who had to walk. When the walk stopped being required in the core, the lands nearest the Awakening Tree, those houses still had a Long-Lived memory of every debtor on the road, and they had paper that outlived the pilgrimage season. They became banks. The endow-not-inherit loop, in which a house is endowed rather than passed to an heir, is older than the Years of Hands. The trusted note, the paper a house will still honor years later, is an instrument from the Walking Years, and it outlived the road it was written for. The same houses are still here, with a different business, and the banking people use now is old.

**Clergy.** [[The Tree-Wardens|Cutting-leave]] is how the First Seat captured a practice that had begun without the Seat's permission. People argued the first successful cut as theft, as mercy, as the Tree offering a branch, and as old horticulture that the Motherfaith, the faith that speaks of the Mother, later gave a name. The Seat won the authorization, the right to say which cutting may be made. The [[The Tree-Wardens|scion-queue]] is the Grafting's child. It is the waiting list that appears, and the bottleneck with it, when someone has the power to decide which town is next.

**Language.** The pilgrimage kept the old speech from drifting. Once a year, people from far away walked to the same grove and heard the same liturgy, so [[The Old Tongue]] stayed nearer to itself along the road. When a town had its own Tree, its children grew up where they were born, and the speech grew there with them. That is why the drift in the Old Tongue follows how thin the Trees are, and why [[Polity Archetypes|Lestrand]] moved so far from the old speech, and so quickly. Lestrand had trade, it had a canopy of its own, and nobody there needed to walk to the Motherwood, the grove of the Awakening Tree. The Old Tongue split into three daughter-drifts. Those three are changes that belong to the Years of Hands, sitting on a root the Walking Years had held nearer to itself.

**Geography.** The [[The World Frame|reach-gradient]], the graded reach of the Trees across the map, is the Grafting as it stands in Cut-year 387. [[Maiethorn]] is old in the Years of Hands, and its towns have had their own Trees for a long time. [[Strandoren]] is mature in those same years, and the commerce that carried the grafts is still in the way the country works. [[Heskoren]] is the Grafting in this decade, the place the wood is arriving now. [[The Sundering Isle|Kumbaan]] never entered either clock. To someone living in the world, this reads as distance and as history, and that reading is true as far as it goes.

**Faith.** [[The Watching]] remember [[Before the Walk]] as the Before, a seeing before anyone had called the Tree a mother, and they will not start history at a Cut. [[The Old Ways]] were already becoming, already taking a Condition at their own doors, before any pilgrim road existed, and for them the Years of Hands are when guests arrived late. [[The Fair Hand]] dates a contract from the day it was signed, not from the First Cut in the wood, and it treats the Grafting as a fact about the supply of wood. [[The Open Table]] owes these ages nothing. It does not count its life from them.

## Visibility markers (what a character notices)

- A [[Long-Lived]] person says "I walked," and the room goes quiet. That is the personal clock, how you were Turned, spoken as a life.
- A mile-shrine stands on a road that now ends at a town Tree. The stone was set for a Turning that no longer has to happen on that road. The devout still leave a ribbon on it, and the town's children climb it.
- An old inn lets its upper room, once full of pilgrim-beds, to merchants, and the beds are empty of pilgrims. The deathless house that owns the inn still dates its notes from the founding-summer, the summer those beds were full.
- One debt carries three dates for the same summer: a Cut-year, a house-year, and the year the graft took. A fourth date appears if the note survived [[The Grey Summer]]. The clerk's query below is archival, from Cut-year 280, and Ledan still writes for the house.
- A hamlet on [[Heskoren]] still sends its ten-year-olds to walk to a neighbour. They are not doing history. They are doing this year.
- Someone names a [[Phoenix]] as a way to mark an era, "the Phoenix of the last Walking summer," and nobody in the room agrees which summer that was.

## In-world text

What follows is a query from a clerk of the White Note to a Speaker of the First Seat, written in **Cut-year 280**, the house's 200th summer. It is filed in the founding-book, and the desk still reaches for it when two counts have to be set side by side. Ledan, the clerk, is [[Long-Lived]]. Mataero, in Cut-year 387, is still junior to Ledan. The verso, the back of the sheet, has been folded around a second number.

> *Ledan of the White Note, to the Speaker who keeps the Eoloren-count.*
>
> *The house writes a century-loan from our founding-summer, which we call the 200th of the White Note. The debtor in Wetorn writes the same summer as the year their graft took. Your college will write it as the 280th Cut-year if I have the conversion right, and as the 279th if I have it wrong.*
>
> *Which number should stand on the note so that in fifty years the same person still owes us? We do not ask which summer was holy. We ask which summer a court will still be able to find.*

In Cut-year 387 the house is in its 307th summer, and Ledan still asks which number a court can find.

## What this history does not say

This history does not say who made the First Cut. Folk, clergy, the devout, the Watching, and the Old Ways each tell it in their own way, and the tellings are kept on [[The First Cut]]. This note picks none of them.

It also does not say when the Tree first stood. Nobody has counted that year, and [[The Watching]] will fight any date anyone proposes.

### What the player body leaves out

The player-facing pages leave the following out, and a later edit should keep that material out of them.

The nature of the Leaf-Mother's limit is not in the history. It is locked that she exists and that her reach has a limit ([[Is the Leaf-Mother Real]]). What the limit is stays open. That her reach grew with the grafts is the quiet evidence of a hand. It does not explain the wound, the finiteness, or the rationing. Do not put a cause into the history.

The two-clock spine, the unnamed preface, and the names Brenvaeth, Eoloren, and Ornthael were locked on 2026-08-24, with the present at Cut-year 387. The spread was locked the same day: Maiethorn first, Strandoren by sea, Heskoren last and still going, Kumbaan never. Still open, and still to be left open, are who made the First Cut and what limits the Leaf-Mother.

Pick the cutter only if play needs a tomb. The spine does not pick a face.

Do not date the Tree, and do not slide the present year. Cut-year 387 stays the present.

Ledan cannot see that the three numbers on the query are three theologies. The Speaker who answers, if they answer, will name a wood. The Vaethorn debtor will name a taking. The loan still comes due. The unanswered query is still on the desk in Cut-year 387.

The law, money, clergy, language, and geography paragraphs were traced from the two clocks with `systemic-worldbuilding`. The three daughter-drifts of the Old Tongue are Hands-era weather on a Walking-era root, changes from the years of town Trees sitting on speech the Walking Years had held closer. Deep grammar is not decided.

Several reformations are already in the past, and they are not proposals waiting to be rolled. Road-houses became deathless banks. Unsanctioned cutting became Cutting-leave and the queue. Required pilgrimage became optional. The Closed Seat became the Inner Close. The hymn-country became two papers. Quay-fever became Trenledd's roll and Brenledd's compact. The Hinge War became the Hush-rate. Present-day fate-pressure is in [[The Years of Hands]], noted and not rolled. Do not collapse any of these without a later call.

No creation myth is written, no first king, and no war of the ages. The Closed Seat was a gate that grew a country, not a first king ([[The Closing]]). Dated events that are not about the Tree live in [[The Other Count]], and they do not become a fourth age.

### The two clocks protect the keystone without confirming it

Clock 2, the spread of the wood, is the player-facing face of "reach grew with the grafts." It is the quiet argument that a hand, and not a climate, sits behind the thin reach. Skeptics still win the ordinary reading: young Trees, sick Trees, few grafts, distance, and history. Both readings share the same map. Do not let a player note assert the mind.

The Grafting is not a redemption arc. Local Trees changed who gets a local witness and who waits on a queue. The Seat's phrase, *the Mother's will travelling*, is a claim about the wood, not a verdict on the list.

Do not run the colonial version of this history. "We brought Trees to the benighted far" is the Seat's sermon after the fact, and it is also Lestrand's story of a supply line. What happened is shorter and worse. Children died on the road, someone cut a branch, the branch took, people copied it, and the Seat claimed the right to authorize the copies. [[Heskoren]] still lives the spread as waiting, not as being given a civilization. [[The Old Ways]] live it as guests arriving late. Keep all three of those tellings.

Long-Lived memory is the era-marker the calendar refused to be. At Cut-year 387, many of the Long-Lived remember the late Walking Years as their own lives, and they do not agree on what those years meant. A Long-Lived person who walked as a child, and a Seat chronicle that starts at the Cut, will fight about the same summer. That fight is play. Do not give the table "the" Long-Lived account.

Kumbaan is the control on the whole spine. If a later note finds an old graft on the Isle, it breaks the isolation and it breaks the Open Table. Road-hands do not bring the Mother across the storm-wall.

A table can pick up any of these. A debt with three dates, or four if the note survived the Grey Summer. A Long-Lived person who walked, and a town that wants that memory to stand as citizenship. A mile-shrine a Hands-town wants to pull down. A Speaker asked to turn a house-year into a Cut-year for a court. A cohort on Heskoren still walking, while a neighbouring canopy is one summer from its first fall. Ledan's unanswered query from Cut-year 280.
