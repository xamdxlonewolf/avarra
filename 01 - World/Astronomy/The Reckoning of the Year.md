# The Reckoning of the Year

The Turning keeps time by a calendar, by the sky, and by the solstice the whole world turns on. The [[Turning Tree|Leaf-Fall]], when the Trees turn their colour and choose, is timed to that solstice, so the reckoning of the year is also how people know when children Turn, when the great festival falls, and how a world with mechanical clocks and no printing press counts its days.

## The sky

A single sun and a single moon, on a familiar ~monthly cycle, are enough to give the world its nights, its tides, and the moonlit calendar the [[Yumboe|Yumboes']] keep. There are four true seasons, and the winters are real, in the temperate register of the [[The World Frame|Old World]]. The fact that matters is the solstice, the sun's turning-point, because that is when the [[Turning Tree|Trees]] turn.

The Leaf-Fall happens at the solstice because that is when the [[Turning Tree|Trees]] turn their colour and choose. The sun reaches its turning, and so do the children. Believers read the shared date as the [[The Leaf-Mother|Mother]] timing her choosing to the year's own hinge, and skeptics read it as ordinary seasonal biology, the Trees responding to light the way any tree does. Both are content with the same calendar. What that noon and that long night look like to a person standing in them, and what Kumbaan's moons look like from a hill, is [[The Sky from the Ground]].

## The Turning Year

The year hinges on two solstices, and those two dates are the fixed astronomical anchors everyone can see.

The **Deep Solstice**, at midwinter, is the longest night, the low of the year and its turning point. The season around it belongs to the hearth, to endowment-giving, and to the [[Long-Lived|deathless houses']] closing of their accounts.

The **High Solstice**, at midsummer, is the longest day, and it is the Turning, the great one: the solstice when the [[Turning Tree|Trees]] turn and the year's ten-year-olds meet the Leaf-Fall.

The High Solstice is warm, safe to travel and to gather, and full of light. A child's Turning is a joyous festival, so midsummer suits it far better than the midwinter dark, and that same warmth makes the pilgrimage to [[Maiethorn|Thaeloren]] a summer road.

### Turning-Week

The Leaf-Fall is not a single day but **Turning-Week**, the week that brackets the High Solstice, when the [[Turning Tree|Trees]] hold their colour and the choosing is done. Every child who has turned ten since the last High Solstice Turns during this week. The town gathers, the tree-wardens officiate, and the week is at once the holiest rite and the biggest fair of the year, part christening, part graduation, and part festival, which [[Turning Tree]] sets out in full. The year of a person's Turning becomes a lifelong marker of age and of cohort, which is what people mean when they say *"we Turned the same summer."*

### Months & weeks

The calendar is plainly countable. Twelve months follow the moon's cycle, arranged as four seasons of three months each, hinged on the two solstices, with the two equinoxes between them kept as the lesser markers. The week is the working unit of the guild towns: guild-weeks, market-days, and the mechanical clock-towers of the trade cities striking the hours. There is no printing press, so the calendars are kept by clergy and by clerks rather than printed for everyone, which is one more reason the [[Turning Tree|tree-wardens]] and the [[Polity Archetypes|Tallied Crown's]] clerks hold a quiet power, since they are the ones who keep the dates.

#### The twelve months

The liturgical names are [[The Old Tongue|Maiethren]], and the worn form, the common tongue and the speech of [[Polity Archetypes|Lestrand]], sits in the column beside them. The lever is the same one [[Naming in the Turning]] describes: the name a person uses shows the stance they are taking. The table is counted from the Deep Solstice, as an astronomical order, and the argument about when the year actually starts follows the table. The newer roots, *neth, eol, bren, lir, sael*, live in [[The Old Tongue#Notable words & roots]].

The devout and the clergy begin the year at **Eolthael**, because for them the Turning is the hinge that matters. [[Polity Archetypes|Lestrand]] and the deathless houses begin it at **Nethael**, because midwinter is when the books close. [[Polity Archetypes|Vaethorn]] follows the clergy when they are in the pulpit, and follows the last *actual* Turning-Week when they are in the fields, so a child "turns the year" when the local Tree last managed a fall. The twelve names do not change, but there are three new-year's days, and any of the three can open a dispute over what year it is.

#### Kumbaan's moons

[[The Sundering Isle|Kumbaan]] does not use this table. The [[Yumboe]] keep twelve named moons, a lunar count that owes the solstice nothing. The names are **Yaaba · Ndibi · Sambiyo · Waandi · Lunji · Saalo · Soonke · Nkoosa · Ndenjoo · Yendo · Njili · Mbela**. A mainlander will try to line *Saalo* up with Seed-month and be cheerfully, consistently wrong.

## How years are numbered

Months name a season inside the year, and years have no universal number. It is the same lever as the three new-year's days, turned now toward history, and the count you write on a debt is a theology. The full spine of those counts is [[The Ages of the Turning]].

The [[The Tree-Wardens|First Seat]] and the devout clerks count **Cut-years** from [[The First Cut]]. In the liturgy that count is the *Eoloren-count*, and the present year is **Cut-year 387**.

[[Polity Archetypes|Lestrand]] and the [[Economy and the Tithe|deathless houses]] count from a house-founding or from the issue of a note. In the present they will say "the 307th summer of the White Note." The famous problem of converting one count into the other is the archival **200th**, Ledan's query of Cut-year 280, which [[The Ages of the Turning]] keeps.

[[Polity Archetypes|Threnmaieth]] writes the Cut-years and also a crown-count taken from [[The Two Papers]]. The papers fall in Cut-year 67, and the present crown-count is **Crown-year 320**.

[[Polity Archetypes|Vaethorn]] counts from "the year our graft took," which is the same local-fall logic as the way they start the year.

[[The Watching]] refuse the Cut-count entirely, and [[The Sundering Isle|Kumbaan]] keeps its moons and owes the ages nothing.

The [[Turning Tree|Awakening Tree]] is old beyond reliable dating. There is no year the world began, and no year she appeared.

## How it varies across the world

One sun, one moon, and the two solstices make the reckoning universal in principle. People live it differently along the [[The World Frame|reach-gradient]], from a country where Turning-Week is an institution to the one country that has no Trees.

In [[Maiethorn]], where the reach is full, Turning-Week is a near-certainty and a vast institution. The [[Turning Tree|Trees]] stand dense and healthy, the core runs higher on the Given than the world does as a whole, more of its people carrying the gift the Leaf-Fall gives, and the pilgrimage to [[Maiethorn|Thaeloren]] crests at the High Solstice. The lived shape of that pilgrimage, in the years when the walk was required, is [[The Walking Years]] (the Near Mile).

In [[Strandoren]], where the reach is high, the solstice is kept, and it is as much a commercial hinge as a sacred one: the fairs, the contract-terms, and the [[Long-Lived|deathless houses']] own reckoning all turn on it. The Salt Walk was the berth that made Eolthael; miss the last crossing and you missed the year.

In [[Heskoren]], where the reach is thin, Turning-Week is a hard-won event of a decade rather than a certainty. A child may travel days to the nearest turning Tree, or wait years for a graft to take. The solstice still turns; the gift may not fall, and whole districts are still on the Long Mile.

On [[The Sundering Isle|Kumbaan]], where there is no reach, the [[Yumboe|Yumboes]] keep the moon and not the solstice. Their reckoning is nocturnal and lunar, and it owes the Trees nothing, because they have none. They are the one people in the world whose calendar has no Leaf-Fall in it.

## GM Notes
*(Stripped on player-safe export. Keyed to the keystone via `reveals: [keystone-adjacent]`.)*

- **Status and build.** Built with `systemic-worldbuilding` for Epic 3 ([[Roadmap]]); the astronomy is kept light on purpose, enough to run the rite and date events. The one fixed point is that the Leaf-Fall is solstice-timed ([[The Premise]] / [[Turning Tree]]); the High Solstice is settled and is not reopened. The months table and the moons are locked (2026-08-23); the moon names came from hearth-register seed `20260826`.
- **Which reading of the solstice is right** is keystone-gated; see below.

- **The solstice-timing is a two-reading fact, like everything keystone-adjacent.** Believers hold that the [[The Leaf-Mother|Mother]] times her choosing to the year's hinge. Skeptics hold that the [[Turning Tree|Trees]] are photoperiod-sensitive plants that colour with the light, and that the "choosing" is biology. Per [[Is the Leaf-Mother Real|the keystone]], she *is* real, so the believers are right about *who* and the skeptics are right about the *mechanism*: she works through the Trees' real nature, not against it. Don't resolve it in player text; the calendar works identically either way.
- **Kumbaan's lunar calendar is the sharpest small tell in the world.** The [[Yumboe|Yumboes]] keep the moon and not the solstice because the Trees never reached them — a whole people whose reckoning of time has no Leaf-Fall in it at all. In the world that reads as cultural difference, an isolated island keeping its own calendar. It is also the keystone experiment ([[The Sundering Isle]]): the one calendar the Mother's Trees demonstrably never shaped. Reserve the significance, and let it look like colour.
- **Whoever keeps the dates holds power.** There is no printing press, so calendars are hand-kept by clergy and by clerks. The [[Turning Tree|tree-wardens]] set Turning-Week, and the [[Polity Archetypes|Tallied Crown's]] clerks record it. A campaign can turn on a disputed date, a miscounted cohort, or a child Turned a year "late" because someone kept the calendar wrong (or made them). Cut-years set against house-years set against "the year the graft took" is that same lever aimed at history ([[The Ages of the Turning]]).
- **Astronomy is deliberately shallow.** One sun, one moon, and two solstices are enough to run the rite and to date an event. If a later story wants eclipses, comets, or a second moon as material for an omen, that is open space and not a contradiction, because nothing above depends on the sky being simple beyond the solstice.
