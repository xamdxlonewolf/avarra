# Brine and Rain

On the Shore-lands a leaf-lot can be burned by brine and drenched by estuary rain in the same month, and from the lot itself that is what the month looks like. A west wind lays salt on leaves that were green at dawn. A Chart-run rise, or a squall off the Old Crossing, then soaks those same leaves with fresh water until the paths flood, and the plants taste the salt and the fresh water together. A [[The Taken-In|Taken-In]] who rests in that lot feels the scald and the rot as one week, while the granary uphill still has bread. The Tithe is the living green they came there to rest in, and the staple is the bread, so the two have come apart. That split is ordinary ecology, not a curse.

[[Orentel]]'s rise has its own salt, on roots the tide does not reach, and nobody on the rise explains it. Brine and rain are the estuary's weather at the scale of a lot: the working shore, the rented green, the path that is mud by afternoon. [[Denlad]]'s Hand sits above its inlet and is not this lot, while a city's leaf-lots, downwind of the cranes and in reach of both the wind and the river, are.

People argue about which of the two damages to count. Guilds count the flood, because a flood has a witness, and the salt-burn is quieter and often the one that takes the green. A Hall-keeper schedules the day around the wet paths, and a Book-hand does not put the salt in a Term unless someone makes them look at a leaf.

Sometimes a lot salts on a windless morning, when the estuary is flat and nobody filed a gale. The tender washes the leaves and will not say where the salt was in the air.
