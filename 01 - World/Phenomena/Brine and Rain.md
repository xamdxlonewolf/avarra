# Brine and Rain

On the Shore-lands a leaf-lot can be burned by brine and drenched by estuary rain in the same month. [[Climate of the Turning]] already says so. This is what it looks like from the lot.

A west wind lays salt on leaves that were green at dawn. A Chart-run rise, or a squall off the Old Crossing, then soaks the same leaves with fresh water until the paths flood. The plants taste both. A [[The Taken-In|Taken-In]] who rests in that lot feels the scald and the rot as one week, while the granary uphill still has bread. Tithe and staple come apart. That split is ordinary ecology, not a curse.

[[Orentel]]'s rise has its own salt, on roots the tide does not reach. This note does not explain that. Brine and rain are the estuary's weather at lot-level: the working shore, the rented green, the path that is mud by afternoon. [[Denlad]]'s Hand sits above its inlet and is not this lot. A city's leaf-lots, downwind of cranes and in reach of both wind and river, are.

People argue about which damage to count. Guilds count the flood, because a flood has a witness. The salt-burn is quieter and often the one that takes the green. A Hall-keeper schedules around the wet paths. A Book-hand does not put the salt in a Term unless someone makes them look at a leaf.

Sometimes a lot salts on a windless morning. The estuary is flat. Nobody filed a gale. The tender washes the leaves and will not say where the salt was in the air.
