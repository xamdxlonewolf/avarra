# Ecology of the Turning

The land is worked, grazed, cut, flooded, manured, and tended, and there is no untouched green that feeds people without that labour. About three people in four carry a Condition and its Tithe, often in the same shape as the work they already do. The Condition is that unusual labour, and it is the only advance this world has on any other late-medieval one: a person may coax growth, range in another body, hold a wound still, carry a warning through a storm, or work where death has already entered. The Tithe is the upkeep the labour demands in return. What follows is enough ecology to feed a week, to threaten a harvest, and to know who a town needs. Weather by month and by band is on [[Climate of the Turning]], and journey lengths and the named rivers remain on [[Named Ground]].

## Five rules of living ground

1. **Motherwood is mortal hardwood.** A town's [[Turning Tree]] is living grafted wood of that same hardwood, so the tree can thirst, drown, split, sicken, and die.
2. **Food is still food.** Grain, beans, roots, greens, orchard fruit, herds, and fish are what feed the world. Provisioning a Tithe is important civic work, and it is not a second staple crop set beside the grain.
3. **Work and upkeep often coincide.** The labour a town already depends on is often the same labour that meets the Tithe. The [[The Taken-In|Taken-In]], who coax growth, rest among living plants while they do it, and the [[The Stilled|Stilled]], who can hold a wound still, work the Grey of that stillness back out through exertion. A [[Far-Voiced]] person can carry a warning through a storm and still needs an honest outlet for the voice. The [[Returned]] work where death has already entered, and they need a Purpose that is actually theirs, so a useful task done for someone else does not meet the Tithe. The [[Two-Bodied]] need time in the other self, and that other self is a person in a real animal body, the body they range in. The [[The Answered|Answered]] must use what answered them, and the [[Long-Lived]] need genuine learning rather than a new cover on an old account.
4. **Disease follows bodies and water.** Quays, camp-streets, wells, flood silt, winter rooms, and armies decide who sickens, and they matter more than any curse. [[The Grey Summer]] was a fever, not a Condition, not a punishment, and not the Grey the Stilled work back out by exertion.
5. **Beasts are livelihoods before they are encounters.** A goat is milk, dung, hide, and a drinker at the well, and a pack pony is a road made possible, while the adversaries of the wild road stay on [[Dangers of the Turning]].

## The ordinary larder

The rows below are defaults for each named ground, not an exclusive list of what that ground may eat. A market can import what its own weather will not grow.

| Ground | Staple crops and stores | Gathered or caught nearby | The lean-week meal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mother-core | Rye and wheat bread, oats, peas, beans, cabbage, turnips, apples, pears, flax | Hazel nuts, berries, nettles, mushrooms, river fish, honey | Oat pottage with dried peas; cabbage if the pit stayed dry |
| Rain-Wall shelves | Oats, barley, rye on lower slopes, peas, cabbage, turnips, hay | Bilberries, sorrel, mushrooms, stream fish | Barley broth, hard cheese, and a boiled root |
| Rain-Shadow terraces | Barley, millet, lentils, peas, onions, hardy greens, grapes where a slope holds warmth | Wild herbs, small fruit after rain, snails, well-water fish only where deliberately stocked | Millet or barley porridge with lentils; goat cheese if the herd came in |
| Crossing and Shore-lands | Wheat and rye, oats, beans, peas, cabbage, turnips, flax, apples and pears | Eels, herring, mussels, cockles, seaweed, marsh greens, berries | Bread softened in fish broth; beans when the fleet stays tied |
| Sundered upland | Oats, rye, barley, peas, cabbage, turnips, sheltered apples, hay | Berries, hazel nuts, nettles, sorrel, mushrooms, river and shore fish | Oat mash, dried peas, and smoked fish or cheese |
| Kumbaan terraces and shallows | Oats, barley, beans, peas, onions, cabbage, turnips | Mussels, limpets, seaweed, fish, berries, mushrooms, hazel nuts | Barley broth and shellfish; goat milk when the wall closes the shallows |

When a Yumboe tale says "corn," the word is the common one for grain, and it is not proof of a crop this table has missed. Kumbaan's table looks generous because hands gather, carry, prepare, and serve. The food still has to grow, or it has to be caught.

## Forage by season

Forage will supplement a larder, show what the ground is doing, or bridge a difficult week, and it will not feed a marching company forever.

| Season | Reliable finds | What can go wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Winter | Stored roots in an abandoned shed, shellfish on a safe low tide, evergreen browse for animals, a few hardy greens | The ground is frozen, a storm has closed the shore, the pits have spoiled, or someone has taken a household's survival store |
| Spring | Nettles, sorrel, young greens, eggs, river fish, shellfish, last nuts in dry caches | Floodwater fouls the greens, or hungry people have already gathered the easy ground |
| Summer | Berries, mushrooms after rain, shore food, herbs, honey, fresh peas near worked land | Heat spoils the meat and the fish, or a "wild" patch may be somebody's Taken-In planting |
| Autumn | Nuts, apples and pears gone wild, mushrooms, berries, gleaned grain, late fish runs | The gleaning right belongs to a hearth, a guild, or a poor-house, and the weather can close the harvest in a day |

A Taken-In can find and coax forage beyond these defaults. That skill is Condition-labour, not proof that every hedge is a pantry. Before anyone eats from it, ask whose ground it is, what has already eaten, and whether the water is sound.

## Trees make towns tend watersheds

A graft wants living earth and a drink, and that need runs out through the life of a settlement.

| Order | Consequence | What characters see |
|---|---|---|
| First | Roots need water, air, soil, and protection from salt, grazing, and hard wind. | Someone takes a cistern turn for the Tree, wicker goes around a young trunk, and a ditch is cut before the rain. |
| Second | The Tree competes with gardens, animals, washing, and people during scarcity. | A well-share names the planting and the Hand separately, and someone takes a night-bucket. |
| Second | Flood and sickness can remove the people who know how to tend it. | A sound canopy goes thirsty while the town lies fevered, as happened in the Grey Summer. |
| Third | A sick Tree changes law, travel, beds, and witness. | A cohort is sent to a neighbour in the same Turning-Week, an upper room is opened, and a Far-Voiced is asked to carry the stand, the week's witness, home. The seated example is [[The Mill-hold]] walking *in* to Brenthael. |
| Third | A dead Tree changes a generation. | Fresh living wood has to be carried and established, and children walk while it matures, because dead wood does not Turn. |

The country with the most Trees therefore has the deepest watering custom, not the lightest. Old success leaves old drains, old arguments, and offices that can conceal neglect behind competence.

## Tithe-ecology

The same weather can leave the grain secure and a person's Tithe exposed, since keeping the staple and meeting the Tithe are not the same labour.

| Need | Where the land supplies it | Where society supplies it | Ecological failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Living green** — [[The Taken-In]] | Farms, woods, orchards, terraces, grazing commons | City leaf-lots, river-gardens, potted greens, harvest work | Brine, frost, drought, blight, flood rot, imprisonment, or a gate on the lot takes the green |
| **Exertion** — [[The Stilled]] | Field work, steep roads, herding, storm repair | Surgery, patrol, drill, demolition, hauling | Injury or enforced idleness after a crisis leaves the exertion unmet, and there are too few Stilled where the disease is worst |
| **An outlet** — [[Far-Voiced]] | Work-song, open ranging, warning posts | Quays, courts, stages, worship, release-houses | Isolation, silence orders, packed shelters, or a priced hall cuts the outlet off |
| **Purpose** — [[Returned]] | Recovery, plague work, tending the dead when the Purpose fits | A named duty, witness, or continuing search | A useful task that does not serve this person's Purpose, or a quarantine from what holds them, leaves the Tithe unmet |
| **The other self** — [[Two-Bodied]] | Ranging, herding, scouting, water-work, courier paths | Beast-yards and scheduled leave in dense cities | Confinement, unsafe streets, or treating the animal body as hired stock keeps them from the other self |
| **Earnest elemental use** — [[The Answered]] | Field drains, mills, kilns, boats, walls, firebreaks | Guild craft, firefighting, harbour and building work | Being benched after the emergency, a monopoly on a workshop, or a lack of material to work leaves the element unused |
| **Genuine novelty** — [[Long-Lived]] | Travel, changing seasons, first-hand natural observation | Courts, archives, correspondence, teaching, new craft | Isolation and repetition. A new cover on an old account is not new knowledge. |

This is why provisioning a Tithe behaves like keeping wells and keeping roads. Whoever controls the green, the hall, the workshop, the route, or the archive can help a person stay whole, or can turn that need into leverage. The fuller structure of that economy is [[Economy and the Tithe]].

Urban Taken-In include city-Given neighbours, people whose Condition was Given in the city, and with them the lot workers, the river-gardeners, and the harvest-hands who wintered or stayed. Their green is a lot, an orchard, a pot, or a season of work — not a hidden forest inside the walls. A gate on that green can keep someone in the city as surely as a locked road.

### Ecology changes the workday

A flood produces surgery for the Stilled and recovery work for the Returned, and then, when the emergency is over, it takes the exertion and the Purpose away again.

A harvest puts the Taken-In and the Two-Bodied at the exact work that keeps them well, and it can then strand both of them in a stone quay through the winter, away from the green and from the other self.

A storm gives the Far-Voiced real warning work and gives the Answered urgent craft, and what follows is a silence in which the ordinary outlets have gone.

A new pest, or a soil sickness, feeds a Long-Lived's novelty once. The second ruined year is the same lesson over again, and repetition is not provision.

A quarantine can preserve a town and still cut a person off from green, from Purpose, from kin, from range, or from an outlet, and good disease practice still has a Tithe cost.

## Blight and disease

The Turning has no single supernatural disease running underneath the ordinary ones. Most sickness is familiar and local, and it gets worse where people are crowded together or the water is bad.

| Illness or harm | Where it thrives | What people do | Who the town needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Flux** | Fouled wells, flood silt, crowded camp-streets, bad ship water | They separate drinking water from washing, boil when the fuel allows, and dig the waste downhill and downwind | Taken-In who can read the ground; a Tide-Answered or an Ember-Answered, who improve the water and the heat; ordinary well-keepers |
| **Quay cough and hold fever** | Packed berths, damp lofts, ships kept closed in bad weather | They air the rooms, isolate the hearth that is coughing, mark the contacts by witness, and tie the suspect hulls | The Stilled, to pause the harm and to operate; the Returned, for the plague and for the dead; a Far-Voiced, to warn without sending a runner |
| **Winter lung** | Peat smoke, wet bedding, cold halls, long indoor seasons | They dry the bedding, clear the smoke, and open the rooms when the weather permits | Hearth-keepers, roofers, and the Stilled when breathing fails |
| **Wound-rot** | Field cuts, animal bites, surgery without clean water, long wet journeys | They wash the wound, cut away the dead tissue, dress it with honey or with clean dressings, and make the person rest | Stilled surgeons, and the Returned where the wound or the place is killing the living |
| **Marsh ague** | Warm standing water, wet lowlands, bog-edge camps | They move the beds, drain the ground near the dwellings, cover the water jars, and burn smoke against the biting insects | Taken-In who can read the ground, and ordinary ditch crews |
| **Root blight** | Waterlogged soil, salt, damaged bark, crowded roots, dirty tools | They drain, prune, isolate the cuttings, clean the knives, and stop carrying the sick wood | Tree-wardens and Taken-In tenders, and a Root-Answered for the drains, not a miraculous cure. The seated example of a sick town Tree is [[The Mill-hold]] |

[[The Grey Summer]] in Cut-year 171 remains the epidemic everyone cites on the Old Crossing. It began as a cough in packed holds and on tied ships. It killed across the Given, the Struck, and the Kept — a Condition the Tree had already given, a Condition that came later, and no Condition at all — and it left the grafts unwatered when their tenders fell. Later fevers may resemble it, and they are not another Grey Summer unless people are making a fearful comparison.

### A disease week at the table

1. Pick the route the sickness is traveling, which will be the water, the breath, a wound, the smoke, or the insects.
2. Show one ordinary marker of it: a covered well, bedding put out on a roof, a hull kept tied, chalk at a door, or a watering turn that stands empty.
3. Name the labour that is missing, whether that is no stiller, no clean fuel, no Returned willing to take this particular work, or no runner who can beat the weather.
4. Make the choice a civic one: isolate the quay or keep the food moving, water the Tree or water the camp-street, and spend the hall on a warning or on grief.
5. Keep it a disease, with no colour, no curse, and no monster required to make the week true.

## Beasts people live beside

The animals below are the ones a household already lives beside, and each of them is a livelihood before it is a problem on the road.

| Beast | Livelihood | Where it is common | Scene use |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[The Cart-Ox\|Ox]] | Ploughing, cart haulage, heavy timber | Core farms, Chart-run plain, broad Heskoren vales | A cart has stalled, a breeding animal is too precious to lose, or a ford is too deep for the ox |
| Horse or pony | Riding, courier work, pack and light haulage | Roads everywhere; smaller hardy ponies in uplands | A mount goes lame, the stable is crowded, or the weather outruns the rider |
| Mule | Sure-footed pack work | Rain-Wall passes, dry hills, long roads | The load has to be divided when the pass narrows |
| Sheep | Wool, milk, meat, manure | Rain-Wall shelves, Shore-land meadows, Heskoren uplands, Kumbaan hills | People are shearing or lambing, or a flock has cut the road into mud |
| [[The Terrace Goat\|Goat]] | Milk, meat, hide, manure, scrub browsing | Rain-Shadow, Heskoren rough ground, Kumbaan terraces | A well-share turns into an argument, or a terrace wall is knocked loose |
| Cattle | Milk, meat, hide, manure | Wetter lowlands and sheltered frontier vales | The winter fodder is running short, or a flood forces the herd to move |
| Pig | Meat and woodland mast | Mother-core, Strandoren interior, Heskoren woods | A mast right is in dispute, or the pigs are loose in a green |
| Chicken or goose | Eggs, meat, alarm, market income | Nearly every settled hearth | The birds are a sickness marker, a supper has been stolen, or they make noise in a hiding place |
| Bee | Honey, wax, orchard work | Core orchards, Shore-lands, sheltered Heskoren pockets | A blossom has failed, wax is owed to a hall, or the hives must be moved before a flood |
| [[The Path Dog\|Dog]] or cat | Herding, guarding, vermin control, companionship | Farms, quays, halls, and road-houses | A dog refuses a flooded path, or a granary cat marks hidden spoilage |

A Two-Bodied person's other self is a person in another real animal body, never livestock, never breeding stock, and never a guild-owned beast. Their work can overlap with the animals in this table without erasing that difference.

Kind-hearths follow familiar ground, and the habit does not harden into a border. Fox-born hearths keep to the woods, the snow, and the dry hills, Selkie hearths keep to the coasts, Tengu hearths keep to the ridges, and Yumboe hearths keep to Kumbaan and to nowhere else. A dense hearth is a family's livelihood on that ground, and it is not a state of that Kind.

On Kumbaan the unseen hands can fetch eggs, lift a net, carry kindling, gather fallen nuts, or bring the baskets in before the rain. They do not conjure food, they do not harvest a valley at once, they do not cross the storm-wall, and they do not make a lean year generous by themselves.

## GM Notes

- **Disease does not become a secret taxonomy.** The Grey Summer is the dated epidemic. Other sicknesses stay ordinary routes with local names, not a second historical plague and not a disguised Condition.
- **Reach clues stay botanical.** Tree health can correlate with distance, age, soil, and care in the player body. Do not let blight announce the bounded hand behind the wood.
- **Livelihood before encounter.** If a working beast becomes a hazard, begin with ownership, hunger, injury, or weather. Use [[Dangers of the Turning]] when the scene needs an adversary.
