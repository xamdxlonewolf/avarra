# Ecology of the Turning

The land is worked, grazed, cut, flooded, manured, and tended. There is no untouched green that feeds everyone without labour. About three people in four carry a Condition and its Tithe, often in the same shape as their work. What differs from another late-medieval world is that Condition-labour is the only unusual advance: some people can coax growth, range in another body, hold a wound still, carry a warning through a storm, or work where death has already entered.

This is enough ecology to feed a week, threaten a harvest, and know who a town needs. Weather by month and band is on [[Climate of the Turning]]. Journey lengths and named rivers remain on [[Named Ground]].

## Five rules of living ground

1. **Motherwood is mortal hardwood.** A town's [[Turning Tree]] is living grafted wood. It can thirst, drown, split, sicken, and die.
2. **Food is still food.** Grain, beans, roots, greens, orchard fruit, herds, and fish feed the world. Tithe-provision is important civic work, not a second staple crop.
3. **Work and upkeep often coincide.** The Taken-In rest among living plants; the Stilled work Grey out through exertion; Far-Voiced need an honest outlet; Returned need Purpose; Two-Bodied need time in the other self; the Answered must use what answered; Long-Lived need genuine learning.
4. **Disease follows bodies and water.** Quays, camp-streets, wells, flood silt, winter rooms, and armies matter more than curses. [[The Grey Summer]] was a fever, not a Condition and not a punishment.
5. **Beasts are livelihoods before they are encounters.** A goat is milk, dung, hide, and a mouth at the well. A pack pony is a road made possible. Wilderness adversaries stay on [[Dangers of the Turning]].

## The ordinary larder

These are defaults, not exclusive regional lists. A market can import what its own weather will not grow.

| Ground | Staple crops and stores | Gathered or caught nearby | The lean-week meal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mother-core | Rye and wheat bread, oats, peas, beans, cabbage, turnips, apples, pears, flax | Hazel nuts, berries, nettles, mushrooms, river fish, honey | Oat pottage with dried peas; cabbage if the pit stayed dry |
| Rain-Wall shelves | Oats, barley, rye on lower slopes, peas, cabbage, turnips, hay | Bilberries, sorrel, mushrooms, stream fish | Barley broth, hard cheese, and a boiled root |
| Rain-Shadow terraces | Barley, millet, lentils, peas, onions, hardy greens, grapes where a slope holds warmth | Wild herbs, small fruit after rain, snails, well-water fish only where deliberately stocked | Millet or barley porridge with lentils; goat cheese if the herd came in |
| Crossing and Shore-lands | Wheat and rye, oats, beans, peas, cabbage, turnips, flax, apples and pears | Eels, herring, mussels, cockles, seaweed, marsh greens, berries | Bread softened in fish broth; beans when the fleet stays tied |
| Sundered upland | Oats, rye, barley, peas, cabbage, turnips, sheltered apples, hay | Berries, hazel nuts, nettles, sorrel, mushrooms, river and shore fish | Oat mash, dried peas, and smoked fish or cheese |
| Kumbaan terraces and shallows | Oats, barley, beans, peas, onions, cabbage, turnips | Mussels, limpets, seaweed, fish, berries, mushrooms, hazel nuts | Barley broth and shellfish; goat milk when the wall closes the shallows |

Do not treat "corn" in a Yumboe tale as proof of a new crop. It is a common word for grain. Kumbaan's table is generous because hands gather, carry, prepare, and serve; food still has to grow or be caught.

## Forage by season

Forage is a supplement, a clue, or a difficult week's bridge. It does not feed a marching company forever.

| Season | Reliable finds | What can go wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Winter | Stored roots in an abandoned shed, shellfish on a safe low tide, evergreen browse for animals, a few hardy greens | Frozen ground, storm-closed shore, spoiled pits, or taking a household's survival store |
| Spring | Nettles, sorrel, young greens, eggs, river fish, shellfish, last nuts in dry caches | Floodwater fouls greens; hungry people already gathered the easy ground |
| Summer | Berries, mushrooms after rain, shore food, herbs, honey, fresh peas near worked land | Heat spoils meat and fish; a "wild" patch may be somebody's Taken-In planting |
| Autumn | Nuts, apples and pears gone wild, mushrooms, berries, gleaned grain, late fish runs | The gleaning right belongs to a hearth, guild, or poor-house; weather can close the harvest in a day |

A Taken-In can find and coax forage beyond these defaults. That is skilled Condition-labour, not proof that every hedge is a pantry. Ask whose ground it is, what already ate, and whether the water is sound.

## Trees make towns tend watersheds

A graft wants living earth and a drink. That simple need cascades through settlement life.

| Order | Consequence | What characters see |
|---|---|---|
| First | Roots need water, air, soil, and protection from salt, grazing, and hard wind. | A cistern turn for the Tree; wicker around a young trunk; a ditch cut before rain. |
| Second | The Tree competes with gardens, animals, washing, and people during scarcity. | A well-share that names the planting and the Hand separately; someone taking a night-bucket. |
| Second | Flood and sickness can remove the people who know how to tend it. | A sound canopy going thirsty while the town lies fevered, as happened in the Grey Summer. |
| Third | A sick Tree changes law, travel, beds, and witness. | A cohort sent to a neighbour in the same Turning-Week; an upper room opened; a Far-Voiced asked to carry the stand home. Seated example: [[The Mill-hold]] walking *in* to Brenthael. |
| Third | A dead Tree changes a generation. | Fresh living wood must be carried and established; children walk while it matures. Dead wood does not Turn. |

The most Tree-dense country therefore has the deepest watering custom, not the least. Old success produces old drains, old arguments, and offices that can conceal neglect behind competence.

## Tithe-ecology

The same weather can leave grain secure and a person's Tithe exposed.

| Need | Where the land supplies it | Where society supplies it | Ecological failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Living green** — [[The Taken-In]] | Farms, woods, orchards, terraces, grazing commons | City leaf-lots, river-gardens, potted greens, harvest work | Brine, frost, drought, blight, flood rot, imprisonment, or a gate on the lot |
| **Exertion** — [[The Stilled]] | Field work, steep roads, herding, storm repair | Surgery, patrol, drill, demolition, hauling | Injury or enforced idleness after a crisis; too few Stilled where disease is worst |
| **An outlet** — [[Far-Voiced]] | Work-song, open ranging, warning posts | Quays, courts, stages, worship, release-houses | Isolation, silence orders, packed shelters, or a priced hall |
| **Purpose** — [[Returned]] | Recovery, plague work, tending the dead when the Purpose fits | A named duty, witness, or continuing search | A useful task that does not serve this person's Purpose; quarantine from what holds them |
| **The other self** — [[Two-Bodied]] | Ranging, herding, scouting, water-work, courier paths | Beast-yards and scheduled leave in dense cities | Confinement, unsafe streets, or treating an animal body as hired stock |
| **Earnest elemental use** — [[The Answered]] | Field drains, mills, kilns, boats, walls, firebreaks | Guild craft, firefighting, harbour and building work | Being benched after the emergency; monopoly on a workshop; lack of material to work |
| **Genuine novelty** — [[Long-Lived]] | Travel, changing seasons, first-hand natural observation | Courts, archives, correspondence, teaching, new craft | Isolation and repetition. A new cover on an old account is not new knowledge. |

This is why Tithe-provision behaves like wells and roads. Control the green, hall, workshop, route, or archive and you can help a person stay whole—or make their need into leverage. Fuller structure: [[Economy and the Tithe]].

Urban Taken-In include city-Given neighbours, lot workers, river-gardeners, and harvest-hands who wintered or stayed. Their green is a lot, orchard, pot, or season of work—not a hidden forest inside the walls. A gate on that green can keep someone in the city as surely as a locked road.

### Ecology changes the workday

- A flood produces Stilled surgery and Returned recovery work, then removes exertion and Purpose when the emergency ends.
- A harvest employs Taken-In and Two-Bodied at the exact work that keeps them well, then can strand both in a stone quay through winter.
- A storm gives Far-Voiced warning work and the Answered urgent craft, then leaves a silence in which their ordinary outlets have gone.
- A new pest or soil sickness feeds a Long-Lived's novelty once. The second ruined year is repetition, not provision.
- A quarantine preserves a town and can cut a person off from green, Purpose, kin, range, or an outlet. Good disease practice still has a Tithe cost.

## Blight and disease

The Turning has no single supernatural disease ecology. Most sickness is familiar, local, and made worse by crowding or bad water.

| Illness or harm | Where it thrives | What people do | Who the town needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Flux** | Fouled wells, flood silt, crowded camp-streets, bad ship water | Separate drinking water from washing, boil when fuel allows, dig waste downhill and downwind | Taken-In who read the ground; Tide- or Ember-Answered who improve water and heat; ordinary well-keepers |
| **Quay cough and hold fever** | Packed berths, damp lofts, ships kept closed in bad weather | Air rooms, isolate the coughing hearth, mark contacts by witness, tie suspect hulls | Stilled to pause harm and operate; Returned for plague and dead work; Far-Voiced to warn without sending a runner |
| **Winter lung** | Peat smoke, wet bedding, cold halls, long indoor seasons | Dry bedding, clear smoke, open rooms when weather permits | Hearth-keepers, roofers, Stilled when breathing fails |
| **Wound-rot** | Field cuts, animal bites, surgery without clean water, long wet journeys | Wash, cut away dead tissue, honey or clean dressings, rest | Stilled surgeons; Returned where the wound or place kills the living |
| **Marsh ague** | Warm standing water, wet lowlands, bog-edge camps | Move beds, drain near dwellings, cover water jars, burn smoke against biting insects | Taken-In ground-readers and ordinary ditch crews |
| **Root blight** | Waterlogged soil, salt, damaged bark, crowded roots, dirty tools | Drain, prune, isolate cuttings, clean knives, stop carrying sick wood | Tree-wardens and Taken-In tenders; Root-Answered for drains, not a miraculous cure. Playable Hands-un-Hands: [[The Mill-hold]] |

[[The Grey Summer]] in C.Y. 171 remains the epidemic everyone cites on the Old Crossing. It began as a cough in packed holds, tied ships, killed across Given, Struck, and Kept, and left grafts unwatered when their tenders fell. Later fevers may resemble it. They are not "another Grey Summer" unless people are making a fearful comparison.

### A disease week at the table

1. Pick the route: **water, breath, wound, smoke, or insects**.
2. Show one ordinary marker: a covered well, bedding on a roof, tied hull, chalk at a door, empty watering turn.
3. Name the missing labour: no stiller, no clean fuel, no Returned willing to take this work, no runner who can beat the weather.
4. Make the choice civic: isolate the quay or keep food moving; water the Tree or the camp-street; spend the hall on warning or grief.
5. Keep it disease. No colour, curse, or monster is required.

## Beasts people live beside

| Beast | Livelihood | Where it is common | Scene use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ox | Ploughing, cart haulage, heavy timber | Core farms, Chart-run plain, broad Heskoren vales | A stalled cart, a precious breeding animal, a ford too deep |
| Horse or pony | Riding, courier work, pack and light haulage | Roads everywhere; smaller hardy ponies in uplands | A lamed mount, a crowded stable, weather outrunning a rider |
| Mule | Sure-footed pack work | Rain-Wall passes, dry hills, long roads | A load that must be divided when the pass narrows |
| Sheep | Wool, milk, meat, manure | Rain-Wall shelves, Shore-land meadows, Heskoren uplands, Kumbaan hills | Shearing, lambing, a flock cutting a road into mud |
| Goat | Milk, meat, hide, manure, scrub browsing | Rain-Shadow, Heskoren rough ground, Kumbaan terraces | A well-share argument; a terrace wall knocked loose |
| Cattle | Milk, meat, hide, manure | Wetter lowlands and sheltered frontier vales | Winter fodder running short; flood evacuation |
| Pig | Meat and woodland mast | Mother-core, Strandoren interior, Heskoren woods | A disputed mast right; animals loose in a green |
| Chicken or goose | Eggs, meat, alarm, market income | Nearly every settled hearth | A sickness marker, a stolen supper, noise in a hiding place |
| Bee | Honey, wax, orchard work | Core orchards, Shore-lands, sheltered Heskoren pockets | A failed blossom, wax owed to a hall, hives moved before flood |
| Dog or cat | Herding, guarding, vermin control, companionship | Farms, quays, halls, and road-houses | A dog refuses a flooded path; a granary cat marks hidden spoilage |

A Two-Bodied person's other self is a person in another real animal body, never livestock, breeding stock, or a guild-owned beast. Their work can overlap with these animals without erasing the difference.

Kind-hearths follow familiar ground without becoming borders: fox-born hearths in woods, snow, and dry hills; Selkie hearths along coasts; Tengu hearths on ridges; Yumboe hearths on Kumbaan only. A dense hearth is family and livelihood ecology, never a Kind-state.

Kumbaan's unseen hands can fetch eggs, lift a net, carry kindling, gather fallen nuts, or bring baskets in before rain. They do not conjure food, harvest a valley at once, cross the storm-wall, or make a lean year generous by themselves.

## GM Notes

- **Disease does not become a secret taxonomy.** The Grey Summer is the dated epidemic. Other sicknesses are ordinary routes and local names, not a second historical plague or disguised Condition.
- **Reach clues stay botanical.** Tree health can correlate with distance, age, soil, and care in the player body. Do not let blight announce the bounded hand behind the wood.
- **Livelihood before encounter.** If a working beast becomes a hazard, begin with ownership, hunger, injury, or weather. Use [[Dangers of the Turning]] when the scene needs an adversary.
