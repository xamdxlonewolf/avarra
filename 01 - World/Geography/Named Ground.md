# Named Ground

> **What this is.** The physical names the settlements and the Other Count were already using as common nouns: the water between the Old World pair, the range that makes the dry east, four rivers a GM can point at, and a travel table built from the walks that already existed. ## The Old Crossing

The oldest trade route in the world. Narrow enough that a fair-weather hull does it in a handful of days; busy enough that a late storm in Hale-month is still a Kept child. [[Eolstrand]] is the Motherland face. [[Orentel]] is the Shore-lands face. The Salt Walk was this water, then the Near Mile.

It is not "inner" because a cartographer said so. It is **old** and **close**. The two continents are the Old World pair because this water made them one argument.

What happened on it, dated: [[The Grey Summer]] (C.Y. 171) tied the quays. [[The Hinge Hush]] (C.Y. 304) is the peace still charged as the **Hush-rate**.

## The West Water

Wider than the Crossing. This is the Long Mile's sea-leg, the graft-pot's worse berth, [[Netstrand]]'s leftover. Heskoren's frontier coast faces it back toward Strandoren. Beyond the last capes, the storm-wall. The Hush's west-water clause lives here: Netstrand quotes the far; Saelvaeth may take a pot without a waiting-due; Vaethorn may bless and may not levy; Ornled is a beach-fee.

Do not book [[The Sundering Isle|Kumbaan]] on this water. A wrecked pot in a sailor's story is allowed. A taking is not.

## The Rain-Wall (Lirorn: the Thaw-Wall)

A highland spine down [[Maiethorn]]'s middle. West-face weather is wet and four-seasoned; the leeward **east** is the Rain-Shadow. The range is why [[Saelthael]] sows at wells. The range is why [[Lirorn]] can levy a thaw.

Fox-of-the-Snows and [[Tengu]] hearths sit densest on the high snow. That is terrain, not a flag ([[Kinds of the Turning]]).

Two passes a table needs:

- **the Noon Pass** — the old high road. [[The Thaw-Break]] put a water-line over the ribbon-notch. Children rub it. The road to it was gone for two summers.
- **the Shelf-gate** — the road that was left. Narol of the Pass took the toll when the old stone sat under the water-line. Threnmaieth wanted the new one counted. Lirorn charged them for the water anyway.

Heskoren's highland spines are a *different* weather. Do not hang the Rain-Wall on the frontier.

## Four rivers

### the Core-thaw

Snowmelt off the Rain-Wall's west face. Runs toward the Old Crossing. [[Maiethlir]] sat down where the water slowed enough to hold a Hand — a day's living-earth from the Motherwood, on the *river road*, not [[Orenbren]]'s Near Mile.

The extra mile used to come through with this thaw. Clerks file the spring rise as snowmelt. **Inscrutable, on purpose (already on the seat):** the Core-thaw runs louder the week before Leaf-Fall. Watchers do not file it. Rithim Speaks over it.

In a high Liren the lodging-road meets a near branch of the same water. Thilim's slate — *the summer the ford ran high* — is this river in a bad year, not a fifth name.

[[The Thaw-Break]] (C.Y. 233) is what happens when the Rain-Wall does not thaw as it usually thaws. The Core-thaw took a month in seven days.

### the Well-wash

East of the Rain-Wall: a seasonal run that is a river in a kind Liren and a silt-line in a cruel one. Towns cluster at wells because this water cannot be a civic certainty. [[Ornsael]] drinks a well, not this wash; the wash is why the well matters. The well-town the Break silted is of this weather. [[The Dry Stair]] climbs above a different well-town and is not a river-shrine.

### the Rise-water

A low stream off Harrow's rise. Downstream it is the ford the Seat used as a geography because the Seat will not learn Brenod, Vaelun, Ornath. In clear weather the hamlets can see the canopy from the water. No stone on the waiting side. The cup still goes on the ford-rock.

### the Chart-run

[[Strandoren]]'s interior river-plain, running to [[Orentel]]'s estuary. The First Cut's "river-days" are this water: coasts took by sea; inland waited on a cart *and* this run. [[Trenledd]] sits on the wealthy stretch that got filed. Food on the Salt Quay is this plain, the Crossing's catch, and whatever the hinge pays.

## Travel times

The unit is a **day's walk** — the interval a mile-shrine already marks ([[The Walking Years]]). A horse does not invent a different world; it buys you the same road with fewer blisters. A ship buys you the water the walks already named.

The three walks, converted:

| Walk | What it was | Fair-weather band |
|---|---|---|
| **Near Mile** | Maiethorn inland to Thaeloren | **3–12 days** (a couple of weeks at the long end) |
| **Salt Walk** | Old Crossing, then the Near Mile | **3–5 days' sail** + the inland days → typically **1–3 weeks** |
| **Long Mile** | West Water, then Heskoren inland | **weeks to months** — figure **6–12 weeks** for a coast-to-grove attempt |

[[The Sundering Isle|Kumbaan]] is not on this table. No walk crossed the storm-wall.

## The remnant stays where the sitting was

The surviving Kept regime is **not** a sixteenth flag and **not** a replacement of [[Eolstrand]].

The Closed Seat was an origin-gate on the grove ([[The Closing]]). Hildal's Retreat closed walls **one day's walk from the Motherwood**, inside [[Orenbren]] lodging-country. That is the [[The Walled Book|Inner Close]]. A shore-flag cannot carry a remnant that sat the *grove*. Eolstrand is the hinge of a different war ([[The Hinge Hush]]). Leave both jobs where they are.
