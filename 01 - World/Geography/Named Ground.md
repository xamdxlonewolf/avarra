# Named Ground

> **What this is.** These are the physical names that settlements, and the chronicle called [[The Other Count]], use as ordinary nouns: the water between the Old World pair, the range that makes the dry east, four rivers a GM can point at, and a travel table built from the old walks. The labelled picture of the same ground is [[The Known Map]].

## How to say it at the table

Speak the common-tongue name. The liturgical reconstruction, if anyone bothers, is a later clerk's hobby, a rebuilt old spelling and not the word you say out loud. Stance still shows in the mouth. A devout speaker keeps the *th* in the land-names gathered around the name, and a dock worker does not.

| Everyday | What it is | Who says something else |
|---|---|---|

Vaethorn calls it *the waiting-water*. Sailors say *the Crossing*. Threnmaieth wants the name on a roll. |
| **the West Water** | The wider sea between [[Strandoren]] and [[Heskoren]]. | This is already the Night Shore's word, and the Hush's west clause lives on this water. |

**the Noon Pass** and **the West Water** were already in everyday use before this list was written. The list puts them beside the rest, so a table has one place to look.

## The Old Crossing

The Old Crossing is the oldest trade route in the world. It is narrow enough that a fair-weather hull makes the crossing in a handful of days, and busy enough that a late storm in Hale-month can hold a family past Turning-Week, the solstice week when a child of ten is supposed to stand under a Tree. Miss that week, and the child stays UnTurned. [[The Hinge Shore]] is the face of it on the Motherland. [[Orentel]] is the face of it on the Shore-lands. The Salt Walk, the old way a Shore-lands child reached a Turning, was this water first and the Near Mile inland after that.

People do not call the water "inner" because a cartographer said so. They call the two continents the Old World pair because the water is old and close, and because it made Maiethorn and Strandoren into one argument.

What happened on it has dates: [[The Grey Summer]], in Cut-year 171, tied the quays, and [[The Hinge Hush]], in Cut-year 304, is the peace still charged as the **Hush-rate**.

## The West Water

The West Water is wider than the Crossing. It is the sea-leg of the Long Mile, the long way on to Heskoren, and it is the worse berth for a graft-pot, a pot of living wood riding in a hull. [[Netstrand]] quotes the far crossing on this water. [[Braetu]] is the harbour where that crossing is quoted. Heskoren's frontier coast faces the same sea back toward Strandoren, and beyond the last capes stands the storm-wall.

The Hush's west-water clause lives here, and the clause is particular: Netstrand quotes the far crossing, Saelvaeth may take a pot without a waiting-due, Vaethorn may bless and may not levy, and Ornled is a beach-fee.

[[The Sundering Isle|Kumbaan]] is not on this water. A wrecked pot in a sailor's story is allowed, and a taking is not.

## The Rain-Wall (Lirorn: the Thaw-Wall)

The Rain-Wall is a highland spine down the middle of [[Maiethorn]]. Weather on the west face is wet and runs through four seasons. The leeward east is the Rain-Shadow, which is why [[Saelthael]] sows its fields at wells, and why [[Lirorn]] can levy a thaw as if the melt were a tax. The Fox-of-the-Snows and the [[Tengu]] hearths sit densest on the high snow. That is terrain, not a flag, which is the distinction [[Kinds of the Turning]] keeps, and a table needs two passes across the spine.

The **Noon Pass** is the old high road. [[The Thaw-Break]] put a water-line over the ribbon-notch of the pass, children rub that line, and the road up to the pass was gone for two summers.

The **Shelf-gate** is the road that was left once the old one sat under that line. Narol of the Pass took the toll when the old stone sat under the water-line. Threnmaieth wanted the new pass counted, and Lirorn charged them for the water anyway.

Heskoren's highland spines are a different weather, and they are not the Rain-Wall.

## Four rivers

### the Core-thaw

The Core-thaw is snowmelt off the west face of the Rain-Wall, and it runs toward the Old Crossing. [[Maiethlir]] sat down where the water had slowed enough to hold a Hand, a town's Turning Tree, a day's living-earth from the Motherwood, which is as far as living wood could be carried in a day and still be planted. The town is on the river road, not on [[Orenbren]]'s Near Mile.

Clerks file the spring rise as snowmelt, but the Core-thaw runs louder the week before Leaf-Fall, and [[Maiethlir]] is where that louder week is known. Watchers do not file it, and at Maiethlir the Speaker, [[Rithim]], raises their voice over the river.

In a high Liren, the thaw-month when the water is already up, the lodging-road meets a near branch of the same river. The line in [[The Walking Years]], "Eolthael, the summer the ford ran high," is this river in a bad year, and it is not a fifth river. Below [[Maiethlir]], the same water is [[The Down-Bank]], the days a traveller is already going to spend on the way to [[The Hinge Shore]], where the shore inspects the hull. It is not a second river.

[[The Thaw-Break]], in Cut-year 233, is what the records call the time the Rain-Wall did not thaw as it usually thaws. The Core-thaw took a month in seven days.

### the Well-wash

East of the Rain-Wall, the Well-wash is a seasonal run. In a kind Liren, a gentle thaw, it is a river, and in a cruel one it is a line of silt. Towns cluster at wells because this water cannot be a civic certainty, something a town could budget a year on. [[Ornsael]] drinks a well, not this wash, and the wash is the reason the well matters. The well-town that the Break silted is of this same weather. [[The Dry Stair]] climbs above a different well-town, and it is not a river-shrine.

### the Rise-water

The Rise-water is a low stream off Harrow's rise. Downstream it is the ford the Seat used as a geography, because the Seat will not learn the names Brenod, Vaelun, and Ornath. In clear weather the hamlets can see the canopy, the spread of Harrow's Tree, from the water. There is no stone on the waiting side, and the cup still goes on the ford-rock.

### the Chart-run

The Chart-run is [[Strandoren]]'s interior river-plain, running out to [[Orentel]]'s estuary. The "river-days" in [[The First Cut]] are this water. The coasts took their Trees by sea, and the inland country waited on a cart and on this run together. [[Trenledd]] sits on the wealthy stretch that got filed. [[Lunbra]] is the city on that stretch, and it is where the roll is kept. Food sold on the Salt Quay comes from this plain, from the catch of the Crossing, and from whatever the hinge pays to have brought across.

Week-by-week weather inside these bands is [[Climate of the Turning]]. Forage, crops, disease, Tithe-ecology, the living requirements of a gift's upkeep, and the beasts people work are [[Ecology of the Turning]].

| Band | Where | Weather |
|---|---|---|
| Crossing-shore | both faces of the Old Crossing | Mild and wet-temperate, and a Hale-month storm can decide a Salt Walk. |

| Rain-Wall / Thaw-Wall | [[Lirorn]] | Snow and shelves, and a thaw that the towns keep as a civic year. |

| Storm-wall / Kumbaan | [[The Sundering Isle]] | Mild and wet under the cloud, with bright nights, and the wall itself is the climate. |

## Travel times

The unit is a **day's walk**, the interval a mile-shrine on the old roads already marks ([[The Walking Years]]). A horse does not invent a different world; it buys you the same road with fewer blisters. A ship buys you the water the walks already named.

The three walks, converted in the table, are the bands a fair-weather journey actually takes. The Near Mile is the inland walk on Maiethorn to Thaeloren, the Salt Walk is the Old Crossing and then that inland walk, and the Long Mile is the West Water and then the inland roads of Heskoren.

| Walk | What it was | Fair-weather band |
|---|---|---|
| **Near Mile** | Maiethorn inland to Thaeloren | **3–12 days** (a couple of weeks at the long end) |
| **Salt Walk** | Old Crossing, then the Near Mile | **3–5 days' sail** + the inland days → typically **1–3 weeks** |
| **Long Mile** | West Water, then Heskoren inland | **weeks to months** — figure **6–12 weeks** for a coast-to-grove attempt |

[[The Sundering Isle|Kumbaan]] is not on this table, because no walk has crossed the storm-wall.

A Hale-month storm on the Old Crossing can turn a Salt Walk into a missed year. A thaw-week on the Rain-Wall can turn the Shelf-gate into a lake, and [[The Thaw-Break]] closed roads for **two summers**. The table is weather, not a promise.

## Rothallo is in Orenbren, not on the Hinge Shore

[[Rothallo]] and [[The Walled Book]] are not a sixteenth flag, and they do not stand in for [[The Hinge Shore]] or replace it.

The Closed Seat was an origin-gate on the grove, which is the matter of [[The Closing]]. Hildal's Retreat closed its walls **one day's walk from the Motherwood**, inside the lodging-country of [[Orenbren]]. That place is [[Rothallo]], and people still call it the Inner Close. The book kept there is [[The Walled Book]]. The Walled Book belongs to the grove's country, not to the shore. The Hinge Shore is the hinge of a different war, the one settled in [[The Hinge Hush]].
