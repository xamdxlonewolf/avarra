# The World Frame

> **What this is.** This is the top-level geography of the Turning. It sets down the four continents, how they sit relative to one another, and the single idea that organizes the whole map. That idea is a reach-gradient: the Trees are dense at the sacred origin, thinner as you travel out through the trading middle and the frontier, and absent on the storm-walled isle beyond. Every region, every polity, and every settlement hangs off that gradient. Fifteen great powers sit across the three large continents. The three [[Polity Archetypes|worked polities]], the ones built out as full examples, each have a place on it, and they do not sit on the same continent.

## The organizing idea: a reach-gradient

The map is not a scatter of lands that happen to be neighbours. It is a gradient of how far the [[Turning Tree|Trees]] have reached, and if you read it outward you can tell the story of the world in one pass:

**sacred-dense origin → mercantile middle → thin frontier → storm-walled isle beyond**

There is one [[Turning Tree|Awakening Tree]], and its name is Thaeloren. Every town Tree is a living graft carried outward from it, a piece of that first Tree kept alive on the road and planted in a new square. The farther a land sits from the deep, old, densely grafted core, the thinner its reach tends to run. The Trees are fewer, younger, and more fragile. More children are hugged and left without a colour, which is what the [[Law and Citizenship|Kept]] are. More children miss a Tree entirely and stay UnTurned. More people are [[Law and Citizenship|Struck]] later, alone, with no one there to see the change.

The [[The Old Tongue|Old Tongue]], the liturgical language the mainland still speaks in worn forms, drifts along the same line. It is conservative in the core, worn on the frontier, and eroded on the trade coast, where the old sounds have been rubbed down by the docks.

Seasons, and the week-by-week weather across all four, are [[Climate of the Turning]].

The three [[Polity Archetypes|archetype polities]] were seated on three different continents on purpose, so that they are not neighbours and a traveller cannot meet all three by walking one road. Each of the three large continents also carries several of the ~15 great powers, named in [[Powers of the Turning]]. The archetype is only the most fully worked example on that continent, not the only power living there.

## How they sit (relative geography)

Read the world roughly from east to west, which is also the direction of the reach-gradient, from full to empty.

[[Maiethorn]] anchors the east. It is the old heart, the land that has been settled longest, and the [[Turning Tree|Awakening Tree]] still stands there, which is why everything the world calls "the center" is on this continent.

[[Strandoren]] lies west of that heart, across the [[Named Ground|Old Crossing]]. The crossing is close enough for constant, ancient trade, which is why Maiethorn and Strandoren are spoken of as the Old World pair, Strandoren's long indented coasts face the water, and that water is the busiest in the world.

[[Heskoren]] sprawls farther west and south, beyond Strandoren's far coast. It was reached late and grafted late, and it is the edge of the settled world. Between Strandoren and Heskoren, [[Named Ground|the West Water]] widens, the reach thins, and "the frontier" is the name people give to what begins on the far shore.

[[The Sundering Isle]] sits alone in the far west ocean, or in the deep south, past Heskoren's last capes, and the compass is not the part that is fixed. A band of killing weather, currents, and reef has wrecked most of the people who tried to cross, so the isle is off the edge of the reach-gradient entirely. It has no Tree, the grafts never crossed to it, and its people are mythic-rare on all three of the other continents, known mostly as a sailor's story.

> **East and west.** These are the directions used on [[The Known Map]]. What has to hold, if a later map turns the page, are the relative positions and the distances: Maiethorn and Strandoren stay close and old, Heskoren stays far and thin, and the Isle stays sundered beyond the last capes.

## The reach-gradient, continent by continent

[[Maiethorn]] is full reach, the oldest and densest grafting in the world. The Trees are mature, numerous, and healthy. The core runs higher on the Given, the children a coloured leaf fell on, than the world as a whole, and the shares are kept on [[Conditions]]. The [[Law and Citizenship|three pillars]] are thick on the ground: the memory of the [[Long-Lived]], the sworn word of the [[Bound]], and the feeling the [[Far-Voiced]] carry in the voice. This is the baseline the [[Polity Archetypes|universal social physics]] was written for, the ordinary rules of the society set down as if this fullness were normal everywhere.

[[Strandoren]] is high reach, with grafts that are mature and many, especially along the wealthy coasts. The Given are dense, and the Condition-economy, the work and trade that runs on what people became, is in full commercial flower. The faith has been worn thin by trade, and the Trees have not. That split is why [[Economy and the Tithe|deathless banking]] and the public works built by Condition-labor come to their peak here, on a coast that can pay for them.

[[Heskoren]] is thin reach, where the grafts are young, scattered, and prone to sickening, and whole districts wait on a Tree that has not taken, so the continent runs high on the [[Law and Citizenship|Kept and the Struck]] and has many UnTurned children. The frontier's social character follows from that thinness: custom outruns written law, vouching is stretched thin, and the unvouched Struck, the people who changed with nobody to stand for them, are a constant problem.

[[The Sundering Isle]] is near-none, with no [[Turning Tree|Tree]] there. The grafts never crossed the storm-wall, and [[The First Cut]] is strict about that absence: dead wood is not a Tree, and sailors still tell of pots that went into the water. The Isle's people acquire their nature by their own path, outside the Leaf-Fall entirely, which is the matter of the Isle note and of the [[Yumboe]] ancestry.

## Rival faiths on the map

The [[The Leaf-Mother|Motherfaith]] is one faith among several. The others are built in [[Faiths of the Turning]], and they are woven into the large continents, where they rub against the Motherfaith and make friction, instead of being left on the far isle alone.

On [[Maiethorn]], the rival is [[The Watching]], whose own name is *Haelthael*. It sits in the sacred core, an older Tree-faith that the Motherfaith grew over. The Tree sees, in their telling, and it does not owe you a mother's name. It may remember things the Motherfaith would rather forget.

On [[Strandoren]], the rival is [[The Fair Hand]], whose own name is *Leddoren*. It is a mercantile theology. It reads a Condition, the change a person carries, not as a gift given but as a bargain struck, and it is at home among the [[Bound]].

On [[Heskoren]], the rival is [[The Old Ways]], whose own name is *Vaeloren*. It is an animist and ancestor faith that predates the grafts, and its claim is that the Mother's reach is new here. It reveres the Struck, and the [[The Taken-In|Taken-In]], people the wild took partway into itself, as the land's own children.

On [[The Sundering Isle]], the faith is [[The Open Table]], whose own name is *Ndeyaan*. It grew in isolation, it owes nothing to the Leaf-Mother, and what it keeps is hospitality, the moon, and the staying-kin.

The named waters, the range, and the travel times are [[Named Ground]], and the labelled picture is [[The Known Map]].

## GM Notes
*(Stripped on player-safe export. Keyed to the keystone via `reveals: [keystone-adjacent, the-other-hands]`.)*

**Why this note exists.** It fixes the frame [[The Premise]] set: about fifteen great powers across three large continents and one small one, with the worked polities on three different continents as the corners of an axis-space. Names and relative positions can still be refined as regions get built. The note was built under [[Roadmap]] Epic 3, Story R.10. The reach edge is [[Is the Leaf-Mother Real]].

**The thinning.** The true cause of the thinning is GM-side. In the world, people read it as plain distance and history.

**The Isle.** Do not find an old scion on the Isle. The isle is the sharpest edge of the [[Is the Leaf-Mother Real|keystone question]] in the whole world.

**The compass.** East and west are not cosmology. When the map is drawn, it may be flipped freely.

**The map is the keystone edge, drawn.** The reach-gradient, from the dense core to the thin frontier to the Treeless isle, is the ground-level face of [[Is the Leaf-Mother Real|the Mother's bounded reach]]. In the world it reads as plain distance and history. [[The Ages of the Turning]] explains today's thin edges as Tree-poor places, young or sick or simply few grafts, and as the front of a wave that is still moving on [[Heskoren]]. The skeptics take that account as the whole truth. It is most of the truth. The part that stays on the GM side is why the grafts thin with distance from Thaeloren: her reach itself has an edge. Never let a player-facing region note assert that deeper cause. Let the player-facing note read as geography.

**The Sundering Isle is the sharpest test.** A land with no Tree, whose people become what they become without the Leaf-Fall, is the strongest natural experiment in the world. Per [[The Other Hands]], there are no Gifts, because reach does not cross. Struck are rare, because the household has little purchase. Their table is not hers. Reserve the confirming beat. It is still late, and it is not early colour.

**The heartland heresy remembers.** [[The Watching]] is placed at the sacred center on purpose. An old religion that the Motherfaith grew over is the natural keeper of pre-Motherfaith memory, what the world believed before the Trees were understood as hers. The faith is built in [[Faiths of the Turning]], and it still points here.

**East and west are not locked cosmology.** Only the relative positions matter, as the orientation note above already says. If a later map or a later campaign wants the gradient to run from north to south, or along some other axis, that is free. The gradient is the canon. The compass is not.
