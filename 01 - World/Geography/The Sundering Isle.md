# The Sundering Isle

> **What this is.** The fourth and smallest continent of [[The World Frame|the world]] — a **remote, storm-walled land** alone in the far ocean beyond [[Heskoren]]'s last capes, sealed off by a killing band of weather, current, and reef. Homeland of the **Yumboes** — the world's fourth custom ancestry — whose people are **mythic-rare** everywhere else: most folk of the known world have only *heard stories.* There is no Tree on the Isle, and a graft never crossed to it (its reach label is **near-none**). It has its own faith and its own tongue, owing nothing to the Leaf-Mother.

*(Common tongue — the sailors' name: **"The Sundering Isle"** or **"the Sunder,"** for the sundering water that walls it off and the ships it has sundered. In the Yumboes' own tongue: **Kumbaan** — koom-BAHN — "the hidden home." The two names are the whole relationship in miniature: outsiders name the *barrier*, the Yumboes name the *hearth.*)*

## Overview

The Sundering Isle is **off the edge of the reach-gradient entirely** — the one land the grafts never reached. Dead wood is not a Tree. Where [[Heskoren]] is thin, the Isle is *empty* of the [[Turning Tree|Trees]] the rest of the world is built around, and its people become what they are by **their own path**, outside the [[Turning Tree|Leaf-Fall]] altogether (how they become it is left open in the [[Yumboe]] note). It is walled off by a permanent band of storm, current, and reef — **the storm-wall** — that has wrecked most who tried the crossing, so that in [[Maiethorn]], [[Strandoren]], and [[Heskoren]] alike a Yumboe is a figure from a sailor's tale: pearly-skinned, silver-haired, *"the good people from across the drowning sea."* Some of the stories are even true.

Kumbaan is an ordinary *home*, warm and hospitable, that happens to be nearly impossible to reach. The Yumboes are not strange to themselves, only to a world that has never met them. They are a hill-people who love a feast, served by hands you cannot see, and the rest of the world thinks they are a legend.

## Geography & Climate

- **The storm-wall.** A near-permanent girdle of violent weather, treacherous currents, and reef rings the Isle — the reason it stays sundered. Ships that try the crossing are wrecked, turned back, or simply never return. There may be a *season*, or a *way*, the wall thins. Nobody knows.
- **The Paps — the hill country.** The Isle's defining landform: rolling green **hills honeycombed beneath** with the Yumboes' true dwellings. They live *within* the hills, coming out by moonlight; the surface is pasture, terrace, and standing-stone, and the life is below.
- **Mild, wet, moonlit.** A temperate maritime island climate under the storm-wall's cloud — but the Isle is famous (in its own tellings) for its **bright nights**: the people are nocturnal, and moonlight is their daylight.
- **Small.** The one small continent of the four — an island-continent, not a sprawl; walkable, known-in-full by its people, the opposite of Heskoren's trackless vastness.

Playable week-weather, crops, sickness, and livelihood ecology: [[Climate of Kumbaan]].

## Reach

**Near-none.** There is no [[Turning Tree|Tree]] on the Isle, and there never was a graft. The grafts never crossed the storm-wall, and [[The First Cut]] is explicit: **never.** Sailors tell of pots that went into the water. Dead wood is not a Tree. The Yumboes do not Turn at a Tree; whatever they become, they become otherwise.

## People — the Yumboes

The Isle is the homeland of the **Yumboes** ("Bakhna Rakhna," *the good people*) — pearly-white-skinned, silver-haired, hill-dwelling, moonlit, and famously **hospitable to strangers**, native and foreigner alike. Full ancestry build (three Daggerheart features, folklore, naming register) lives in the Yumboe ancestry note (`03 - Cultures/Peoples/`), built to the same depth as [[Kitsune]], [[Selkie]], and [[Tengu]]. In brief for the geography frame:

- They live **beneath the hills**, come out **by moonlight**, and hold **great feasts served by hands and feet you can see but bodies you cannot** — the Isle's signature uncanny image.
- They are **native to Kumbaan and rare beyond it** — the storm-wall keeps them home, so the few who *do* cross become the source of every legend the mainland tells.
- Their **tongue and faith are their own** — non-Maiethren, owing nothing to the Leaf-Mother.

## Faith

**[[The Open Table]]** (*Ndeyaan*) — the Yumboes' own cosmology, **grown in isolation** and owing nothing to the [[The Leaf-Mother|Motherfaith]]. Their old accounts bind the good people up with **the ancestors and the dead**, and the mainland tales call them "the spirits of the dead." They are a living people with a faith of hill, moon, hospitality, and the staying-kin. They are not ghosts. ## How a crossing works

The storm-wall is not shut for good, and it is not a ferry route. The [[Named Ground|West Water]] is named; a thinning is still a moon, not a timetable.

**You do not book Kumbaan.** A [[Netstrand]] hull may *try*. That coast quotes the far crossing, and there is no timetable. Shore-sitters do not swim out. They do not keep a rescue-boat. Most attempts wreck, turn back, or never return.

**If you live:**

1. You are on the sand or you are not.
2. A [[The Shore-Sitters|shore-sitter]] decides *alive*. Water, a blanket, no questions about Kind or colour. Dead go above the tide.
3. The living are walked **uphill**. First night as *guest* starts at a hall, not on the sand.
4. A [[The Table-Keepers|table-keeper]] decides *kin*. At the second meal the guest is told: *no one is a stranger twice.* That belonging is **on the isle**.
5. Being called kin does not travel back to a mainland watch. [[Law and Citizenship|Road-word]] still works on a [[Strandoren]] quay *before* you sail. Here the three questions are not asked.

**A thinning** is a moon the sitters know — wreck-moons, the weeks after a crossing rumour. It is not a scheduled door.

**Return** is the same wall. Sitters do not send anyone back. You wait for a thinning, or you take a hull that will try. A Yumboe who wants off sits the same sand from the other want.

**What a mainlander owes, if they eat:**

- Eat. Sleep the day if you can. Do not plant a Tree. Do not file the hill. Do not swim the wall. Do not repeat *no one is a stranger twice* at home as a saying. It is said to a guest at the table. It is not a pass.
- Guest-right on the isle is the table, not a week-token you carry home.

A hall to start from: [[Ndenjoo]] (the Feeding Hill). Shore on that stretch: Soonke. Hall: [[Njunda]].

## Story hooks

- **The crossing itself** — an expedition to try the wall; who has done it, what they brought back, whether this moon is a thinning.
- A **lone Yumboe on the mainland** — a walking legend, treated as myth-made-flesh: a wondrous stranger, or a lonely person no one believes is real.
- **[[Ndenjoo]]** — a hall that has to seat a wreck, a kin who will not leave, a sibling who wants out.
- A mainland scholar (or heretic — cf. [[The Watching]], [[The Old Ways]]) who believes Kumbaan holds the answer to what the Trees really are.

## GM Notes
*(Stripped on player-safe export. Keyed to the keystone via `reveals: [keystone-adjacent, the-other-hands]`.)*

- **Status and build.** Built with `systemic-worldbuilding` for Epic 3 ([[Roadmap]]). The name Kumbaan is committed for play. Locked bones from [[The World Frame]] and [[The Premise]]: remote and storm-walled; near-none reach; no Tree and no graft; the Yumboe homeland; its own faith and tongue.
- **Do not find an old scion here in a later story.** That breaks the isolate and [[The Open Table]]. Dead wood is not a Tree.
- **The Isle as a test of the keystone.** A whole people acquiring their nature with no Tree is the sharpest test in the world of [[Is the Leaf-Mother Real|the keystone question]]. Reserved as a late reveal; the mechanism stays open in the ancestry note. The scholar hook is not the first night.
- **Folklore sources.** The Yumboes' hill-dwelling follows the old "Paps hills" accounts, and "spirits of the dead" is the source myth's phrase (folklore-true in the source, not literal here).

- **The Isle is the strongest keystone experiment — guard the reveal.** A land with no Tree whose people become what they become *without* the Leaf-Fall is the cleanest natural test of whether the Mother is behind *any* of it. Per [[The Other Hands]]: **no Gifts** (no graft has crossed; her reach does not get there); **Struck are rare** (the household has little purchase where it is unknown and the doors are not kept); their Kind and their table are *their own*, not a subtler Tree. Don't resolve a lone Yumboe's Struck-or-not in session one — it's still a late beat, just no longer an empty question.
- **Three old faiths, three edges, one buried truth.** [[The Open Table]] joins [[The Watching]] and [[The Old Ways]] as the world's three keystone-adjacent religions — each grown far from (or before) the Motherfaith's account, each possibly preserving a fragment of what the Trees really are. The Isle's is the *purest* control: a cosmology with zero Motherfaith contamination. If a campaign triangulates the keystone, these three are the corners. See [[Faiths of the Turning]].
- **"Spirits of the dead" is folklore, not literal here.** The source myth calls Yumboes ghosts/ancestral spirits. We reinvent them as a *living* people (they're a playable Kind), but the ancestral flavour is deliberately kept as their *faith's* self-image — a people who venerate hill, moon, and lineage and speak of themselves in the old ancestral idiom. Don't make them literally undead (that's the [[Returned]]'s lane).
- **The storm-wall is a dial, not a lock, and not a ward.** The GM picks when a hull can try. A permanent absolute wall kills the expedition hook; a weekly packet kills the isolate. Thinning, wreck, or a Kind/Condition that can survive a night on the reef (a [[Selkie]]? a [[Far-Voiced]]?) stays a lever. Procedure is above; do not invent a moon-warden or a Kumbaan mission. Do not explain the girdle as a larger power blocking the household. The cause of her limit stays open. See [[The Other Hands]].
- **Play [[Ndenjoo]] as a home.** Confirmation that the isle is the control stays late. First nights are dishes, wrecks, and guests.
