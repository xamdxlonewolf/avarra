# The Sundering Isle

> **What this is.** This is the fourth continent of [[The World Frame|the world]], and the smallest. It is a remote, storm-walled land alone in the far ocean beyond the last capes of [[Heskoren]], sealed off by a killing band of weather, current, and reef. It is the homeland of the Yumboes, the world's fourth custom ancestry, a people born to this isle. Everywhere else they are mythic-rare. Most folk of the known world have only heard stories. There is no Tree on the Isle, a graft never crossed to it, and the reach label is near-none. The Isle has its own faith and its own tongue, and it owes nothing to the Leaf-Mother.

*(In the common tongue, the sailors' name is **"the Sundering Isle,"** or **"the Sunder,"** for the sundering water that walls it off and for the ships that water has broken. In the Yumboes' own tongue the name is **Kumbaan** (koom-BAHN), "the hidden home." The two names are the whole relationship in miniature. Outsiders name the barrier. The Yumboes name the hearth.)*

## Overview

The Sundering Isle sits off the edge of the reach-gradient entirely. It is the one land the grafts never reached, and dead wood is not a Tree. Where [[Heskoren]] is thin, the Isle is empty of the [[Turning Tree|Trees]] the rest of the world is built around, and its people become what they are by their own path, outside the [[Turning Tree|Leaf-Fall]] altogether. Nobody on the mainland knows how that becoming works. A permanent band of storm, current, and reef, called the storm-wall, has wrecked most of the people who tried the crossing, so that in [[Maiethorn]], [[Strandoren]], and [[Heskoren]] alike a Yumboe is a figure from a sailor's tale: pearly-skinned, silver-haired, "the good people from across the drowning sea." Some of the stories are even true.

Kumbaan is an ordinary home, warm and hospitable, that happens to be nearly impossible to reach. The Yumboes are not strange to themselves, only to a world that has never met them. They are a hill-people who love a feast, served by hands you cannot see, and the rest of the world thinks they are a legend.

## Geography & Climate

The storm-wall is a near-permanent girdle of violent weather, treacherous currents, and reef, and it is the reason the Isle stays sundered. Ships that try the crossing are wrecked, turned back, or simply never heard from again. There may be a season, or a way, in which the wall thins, and nobody knows which, or whether.

The defining landform is the Paps, the hill country. The Isle is rolling green hills, honeycombed beneath with the dwellings the Yumboes actually live in. They live within the hills and come out by moonlight. What you see in the day is pasture, terrace, and standing-stone, and the life of the place is below.

The climate is mild, wet, and moonlit, a temperate maritime island under the storm-wall's cloud. In the Isle's own tellings it is famous for its bright nights, because the people are nocturnal and moonlight is the light they do the day by.

The Isle is small, the one small continent of the four, an island-continent rather than a sprawl, walkable, and known in full by its own people, which is the opposite of Heskoren's trackless vastness.

Week-by-week weather, crops, sickness, and livelihood ecology are in [[Climate of Kumbaan]].

## Reach

The reach is near-none. There is no [[Turning Tree|Tree]] on the Isle, and there never was a graft, because the grafts never crossed the storm-wall, and [[The First Cut]] is explicit that they never did. Sailors tell of pots that went into the water, and dead wood is not a Tree. The Yumboes do not Turn at a Tree, and whatever they become, they become otherwise.

## People — the Yumboes

The Isle is the homeland of the Yumboes, who call themselves *Bakhna Rakhna*, "the good people." They are pearly-white-skinned, silver-haired, hill-dwelling, and moonlit, and they are famously hospitable to strangers, whether the stranger was born on the Isle or came off a wreck. The full ancestry, with its three Daggerheart features, its folklore, and its naming register, lives in the Yumboe note in `03 - Cultures/Peoples/`, built to the same depth as [[Kitsune]], [[Selkie]], and [[Tengu]].

They live beneath the hills, they come out by moonlight, and they hold great feasts served by hands and feet you can see but bodies you cannot. That service at the table is the image the mainland repeats whenever it tells the isle. They are native to Kumbaan and rare beyond it. The storm-wall keeps them home, so the few who do cross become the source of every legend the mainland tells. Their tongue and their faith are their own, not inherited from Maiethorn, and they owe nothing to the Leaf-Mother.

## Faith

The faith is **[[The Open Table]]**, and its own name is *Ndeyaan*. It is the Yumboes' cosmology, grown in isolation, and it owes nothing to the [[The Leaf-Mother|Motherfaith]]. Their old accounts bind the good people up with the ancestors and the dead, and the tales told on the mainland call them "the spirits of the dead." They are a living people, with a faith of hill, moon, hospitality, and the staying-kin, which is what they call the ancestors and the dead kept with the living, and they are not ghosts. The full account is [[The Open Table]], the people are [[Yumboe]], the hall is [[The Table-Keepers]], the sand is [[The Shore-Sitters]], and the map of the faiths is [[Faiths of the Turning]].

## How a crossing works

The storm-wall is not shut for good, and it is not a ferry route. The [[Named Ground|West Water]] is a named sea, and a thinning of the wall is still a moon, not a timetable.

You do not book Kumbaan. A [[Netstrand]] hull may try, because that coast is where the far crossing gets quoted, and quoting a crossing is not the same thing as having a timetable. The shore-sitters do not swim out and do not keep a rescue-boat, and most attempts wreck, turn back, or never return.

If you live, the landing goes in this order, and it does not skip.

1. You are on the sand, or you are not, and a wreck that never makes the beach has not landed.
2. A [[The Shore-Sitters|shore-sitter]] decides whether you are alive. The living get water and a blanket, and they are not asked their Kind or their colour, and the dead go above the tide.
3. The living are walked uphill, and the first night you spend as a guest begins at a hall, not on the sand.
4. A [[The Table-Keepers|table-keeper]] decides whether you are kin. At the second meal the guest is told, "no one is a stranger twice," and that belonging holds on the isle.
5. Being called kin does not travel back to a mainland watch. [[Law and Citizenship|Road-word]], the name of someone at your last hearth who will stand for you, still works on a [[Strandoren]] quay before you sail. On the isle the watch's three questions, your name, your last hearth, and who stands for you, are not asked.

A thinning is a moon the sitters know, the wreck-moons and the weeks after a rumour of a crossing, and it is not a door that anyone schedules.

The way back is the same wall, and the sitters do not send anyone home. You wait for a thinning, or you take a hull that will try. A Yumboe who wants off the isle sits the same sand from the other want.

If a mainlander eats, there are things the meal does not license. Eat what is set in front of you, and sleep the day if you can. Do not plant a Tree, do not file the hill in a clerk's book, and do not swim the wall. Do not repeat "no one is a stranger twice" at home as a saying you can spend. It is said to a guest at the table, and it is not a pass. Guest-right on the isle is the table, not a week-token you carry home to show a watch.

A hall to start from is [[Ndenjoo]], the Feeding Hill. The shore on that stretch is called Soonke, and the hall there is [[Njunda]].

## Story hooks

The isle is hard to reach, and that difficulty is already the story.

- The crossing itself is an expedition: who tries the wall, who has done it, what they brought back, and whether this moon is a thinning.
- A lone Yumboe on the mainland is a walking legend, treated as myth made flesh, either a wondrous stranger or a lonely person no one believes is real.
- [[Ndenjoo]] is a hall that has to seat a wreck, or a kin who will not leave, or a sibling who wants out.
- A mainland scholar, or a heretic of [[The Watching]] or of [[The Old Ways]], believes Kumbaan holds the answer to what the Trees really are.

## GM Notes
*(Stripped on player-safe export. Keyed to the keystone via `reveals: [keystone-adjacent, the-other-hands]`.)*

**Status and build.** This note was built with `systemic-worldbuilding` for Epic 3, tracked on [[Roadmap]]. The name Kumbaan is committed for play. The locked bones, from [[The World Frame]] and [[The Premise]], are these: the isle is remote and storm-walled, the reach is near-none, there is no Tree and no graft, it is the Yumboe homeland, and it has its own faith and its own tongue.

**Do not find an old scion here in a later story.** An old cutting that took would break the isolate, and it would break [[The Open Table]]. Dead wood is not a Tree.

**The Isle is a test of the keystone.** A whole people acquiring their nature with no Tree is the sharpest test in the world of [[Is the Leaf-Mother Real|the keystone question]]. Keep it reserved as a late reveal. The mechanism stays open in the ancestry note. The scholar hook is not the first night.

**Folklore sources.** The Yumboes' hill-dwelling follows the old accounts of the Paps hills, and "spirits of the dead" is the source myth's phrase. That phrase is folklore-true in the source. It is not literal here.

**The Isle is the strongest keystone experiment, so guard the reveal.** A land with no Tree, whose people become what they become without the Leaf-Fall, is the cleanest natural test of whether the Mother is behind any of it. Per [[The Other Hands]], there are no Gifts here, because no graft has crossed and her reach does not get there. Struck are rare, because the household has little purchase where it is unknown and the doors are not kept. Their Kind and their table are their own, not a subtler Tree. Do not resolve whether a lone Yumboe is Struck or not in session one. That beat is still late. It is no longer an empty question, and it is not an early one.

**Three old faiths, three edges, one buried truth.** [[The Open Table]] joins [[The Watching]] and [[The Old Ways]] as the world's three keystone-adjacent religions. Each grew far from the Motherfaith's account, or before it, and each may be preserving a fragment of what the Trees really are. The Isle's faith is the purest control, a cosmology with no Motherfaith contamination in it. If a campaign triangulates the keystone, these three are the corners. See [[Faiths of the Turning]].

**"Spirits of the dead" is folklore, not a literal claim about the people.** The source myth calls Yumboes ghosts, or ancestral spirits. This world reinvents them as a living people, because they are a playable Kind, and it keeps the ancestral flavour on purpose as the faith's picture of itself: a people who venerate hill, moon, and lineage, and who speak of themselves in the old ancestral idiom. Do not make them literally undead. That is the [[Returned]]'s lane.

**The storm-wall is a dial, not a lock, and not a ward.** The GM picks when a hull can try. A wall that never thins kills the expedition hook, and a weekly packet kills the isolate. A thinning, a wreck, or a Kind or a Condition that might survive a night on the reef — a [[Selkie]], maybe, or a [[Far-Voiced]] — stays a lever, and that maybe stays open. The procedure is in the player section above. Do not invent a moon-warden, and do not invent a Kumbaan mission. Do not explain the girdle as a larger power blocking the household. The cause of her limit stays open. See [[The Other Hands]].

**Play [[Ndenjoo]] as a home.** Confirmation that the isle is the control stays late. The first nights are dishes, wrecks, and guests.
