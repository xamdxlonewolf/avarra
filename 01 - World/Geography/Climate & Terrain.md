# Climate & Terrain

> The physical stage the Empire stands on — and the reason it can feed, move, and keep itself. Geography does real work here: the Empire is prosperous *because of where it is*, not only how it is run. For how that land is administered, see [[The Shape of the Empire]].

## The one-sentence shape

A great temperate river-basin at the heart of a contiguous continent, ringed by uplands and reached by a long coast — a landmass generous enough to grow a surplus, and connected enough to move it before anyone starves.

## The bones

- **The interior heights (the Reave, proposed).** A high snow-holding range along the continent's spine. It is the water tower of everything below: its melt feeds the rivers that feed the basin.
- **The great basin (the Aurenne, proposed).** A broad, low, well-watered lowland — deep soils, a long growing season, reliable rain off the heights. This is the Empire's cradle and breadbasket. See [[The Aurenne]].
- **The trunk river (the Vaur, proposed) and its canal spine.** The Vaur is navigable for most of its length; generations of engineering have laced it to neighbouring valleys with cut canals and locks. Grain, stone, timber, and law travel by water where they can and by road where they can't.
- **The uplands.** Cooler, harder country shouldering the basin — pasture, ore, hardy grain, tough people. The long-integrated [[The Halden]] sits here.
- **The warm lowlands.** Rich, hot, southerly plains and deltas — the most fertile ground in the Empire and the most recently taken. [[Kessad]] lies here.
- **The coast.** A mild maritime edge of harbours and fisheries where the rivers meet the sea; the Empire's window on trade beyond itself (the Sarne Coast, proposed).
- **The marches.** The thin, cold, marginal edge where the network runs out. Beyond it, wilderness and other peoples. See [[The Outmark]].

## Why the prosperity is plausible

Three facts, stacked:

1. **A large temperate breadbasket** grows more food than it needs in a normal year.
2. **Rivers and canals** carry that surplus cheaply and far.
3. **Because food can move, a bad harvest is a local problem, not a death.** A failed year in the uplands is covered by the basin; a flooded delta is fed from upriver. Famine, the ordinary terror of the pre-modern world, is here a *rare* event — and the machinery that makes it rare is visible to everyone. People know exactly why they are not hungry.

The same logic runs through everything the Empire is admired for: the network that moves grain also moves post, judges, soldiers, and coin. Prosperity is not decreed; it is *plumbed*.

## Climate, briefly

Temperate and forgiving in the heartland; colder and wetter in the uplands; hot and lush in the southern lowlands; mild and salt-blown on the coast; harsh and thin in the marches. Seasons are pronounced enough to structure the calendar (planting, the grain-fleets, the courts' riding season) but rarely violent enough to break it.

> *Something is odd, if you count the milestones.* The imperial roads are measured from a marker called the **Zero Stone** — except no living surveyor can say where the Zero Stone actually is, and the count on the oldest roads runs higher than the Empire's own founding records allow. The land, it seems, was being measured before there was anyone here to measure it.
