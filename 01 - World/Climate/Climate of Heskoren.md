# Climate of Heskoren

Heskoren's single broad band is the Sundered upland: cool coast, rough moor, wooded vale, high spine, and long river country with weather changing between them. Winters are harder than on the Old World pair. The kind season is shorter. A clear day is not background; it is when roofs, hay, carts, witnesses, and graft-tenders all move at once.

The continent is not empty wilderness. It is a patchwork of worked pockets around surviving Trees and older hearths that predate them, separated by country too wet, steep, cold, or simply distant to hold densely.

## At the table

| Ask | Use this |
|---|---|
| **Weather this week** | Cold rain in the vale, sleet on the ridge, one brilliant dry day, then a hard frost. On the west coast, add a long swell and wind that keeps the next hull outside. |
| **What you can eat** | Oat mash, rye bread, peas, cabbage, turnips, hard cheese, smoked fish; berries, hazel nuts, nettles, sorrel, and mushrooms in season. |
| **What can enter a scene** | A Long Mile cart taking shelter; hay cut before the next rain; a wind-burned young graft; a camp fever at Eolvaeth; a pony mired between mile-shrines; a neighbour cohort with one dry day left. |

## Reading the short kind season

| Months in common speech | Coast and lower rivers | Vales and live-front pockets | Moor and high spine |
|---|---|---|---|
| **the Deep · Hearth-month · the Thaw / Liren** | Cold rain, sleet, gales, river ice in sheltered bends | Snow and frozen mud; roofs and fodder under pressure; thaw arrives unevenly | Deep snow, scoured ridges, paths hidden; Liren can bring avalanche and bog together |
| **Seed-month · Hale-month · Turning-month / Eolthael** | Wet fields open first; roads remain soft; coast hulls return | Sowing, lambing, roof repair, then crowded neighbour roads | Snow persists in shade; passes open late; a Hale-month frost can reset spring |
| **Gift-month · Ledger-month · Harvest** | Best fishing and coasting; hay and grain race the rain | Gardens, oats, rye, roots, berries, and the brief surplus season | Grazing and repair; nights sharpen by Ledger-month |
| **the Tally · the Wait · Dark-month** | Storms increase; fish and peat stores become public concern | Harvest ends fast; animals come close; first frost exposes weak roofs | Snow closes tracks from the top down; the next mile-shrine becomes uncertain |

Heskoren still uses the mainland months. The same date covers several seasons because elevation and exposure matter more here than a clerk's expectation.

## The frontier coast

The coast faces the West Water back toward [[Strandoren]]. It is the entry for iron, salt, craft goods, specialists, letters, and living grafts—and the exit for timber, wool, hides, fish, and raw staples.

### A playable coast week

| Day-shape | Weather | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | Low cloud, steady wind, workable surf | Fish comes in. Cargo waits on whether the inland road has dried. |
| Middle | One clear cold day | Every roof, net, cart, and drying rack is in use. A Stilled visitor gets three requests before breakfast. |
| Turn | West rain and rising swell | The next hull stands off. The quay runs out of covered space. |
| After | Wind falls; river remains high | Ships can land before carts can leave. Food, graft earth, and passengers compete for dry storage. |

Coastal ground grows oats, rye, barley, cabbage, turnips, peas, and hay where salt wind is broken. Sheep and cattle use rough pasture. Fish, mussels, limpets, seaweed, and eels supplement grain. Peat and managed woodland provide fuel.

Storms make ordinary shortages stack. A hull may carry a stiller, a page a Long-Lived has not read, seed grain, and a Road-hand's living pot. Losing it is not one kind of loss.

## Vales and river pockets

Sheltered vales carry the continent's deeper fields: rye, oats, barley, peas, cabbage, turnips, hay, and apples on warm slopes. Cattle and oxen fit broad bottoms; sheep and goats climb; pigs use woodland mast under local rights.

### A playable vale week

| Season | Weather turn | Put it on |
|---|---|---|
| Winter | Snow becomes rain, then freezes at night | Roofs, fodder, firewood, and the path to a living evergreen |
| Spring | River rises under a clear local sky | A ford, seed already in low soil, waste pits, a neighbour road |
| Summer | Three dry days end in heavy rain | Hay half-stacked, berries ripe, a camp-street ditch |
| Autumn | First hard frost comes before harvest is complete | Roots still in ground, apples on boughs, a Tree-tender choosing what to cover |

Floodwater brings silt and can bring flux. The right response is clean drinking water, drainage, separation of waste, and food. A Tide-Answered can improve flow; a Root-Answered can cut a drain; neither makes fouled water safe by declaration.

## Moor and spine

The high country is wind, peat, short grass, heather, rock, and snow holding late in shade. This is not Maiethorn's Rain-Wall. It has no Lirorn thaw-levy and no Shelf-gate.

### A playable ridge week

| Watch | Weather | Road fact |
|---|---|---|
| Dawn | Frost or sleet, strong crosswind | Footing is firm until it is not; exposed travellers lose warmth fast. |
| Midday | Sun reaches one slope | Snow releases from the warm face; everyone chooses whether to cross before it refreezes. |
| Evening | Cloud lowers | The next ridge vanishes; a known cairn matters more than speed. |
| Night | Hard freeze or cold rain | Packs, boots, and animal bedding decide the next day. |

Lower moors graze sheep, goats, and hardy ponies. Oats and barley grow in sheltered folds. Bilberries, sorrel, mushrooms, and stream fish are seasonal supplements. Peat is fuel and wet ground is a trap for cart wheels, hooves, and cellars.

Tengu and Fox-of-the-Snows hearths are denser on heights because of terrain and history, not because any ridge is a Kind-state. Mixed hearths use the same winter stores.

## Live-front Tree pockets

A Heskoren town often begins where a mortal graft survived. That survival requires work.

- The planting needs living earth kept alive through the Long Mile.
- A young union needs shelter from wind and grazing.
- Wet ground must drain without drying the roots.
- Snow must be lifted from vulnerable boughs.
- Bark damage must be cleaned before rot enters.
- A dry summer needs a cistern share even in a wet continent.

[[Harrow's Green]] is the fortunate version: a Hand mature enough to Turn, with neighbour hamlets still using its week. [[Eolvaeth]] is the waiting version: an older graft that never became certainty. Both are Heskoren ecology. Neither proves worth.

### How a weather loss becomes a generation

1. Wind or root-rot sickens a young Tree.
2. Wardens cannot safely hold that year's standing there.
3. A cohort must reach another sound Tree during the same Turning-Week.
4. Flood, mud, or a closed ford spends part of the week.
5. Those who miss are unTurned; those who arrive still need a witness to walk home.
6. The town must tend, replace, or wait another generation for wood that can Turn.

This is why the map follows surviving Trees. It is also why roads, guest-right, and weather knowledge are part of Tree care.

## Long Mile ecology

Exact times stay on [[Named Ground]]. On the ground, a Long Mile week consumes food, fodder, dry socks, living green, and information.

| Need | Ordinary source | When weather removes it |
|---|---|---|
| Food | Oats, hard bread, dried peas, cheese, smoked fish | A bridge loss separates travellers from the next market |
| Fodder | Hay, grazing, oat feed | Snow covers grass; wet hay heats and spoils |
| Water | Streams, springs, carried water | Flood makes water plentiful and unsafe |
| Fuel | Peat, deadfall where permitted, carried charcoal | Rain soaks fuel; a treeless ridge offers none |
| Shelter | Road-house, farm guest-right, tent, lee wall | A roof is already full with a neighbour cohort |
| Living plant | Field edge, potted herb, sheltered green, forest | Snow, stone lodging, quarantine, or a dead pot leaves a Taken-In exposed |
| News | Far-Voiced post, traveller, local guide | A warning crosses the ridge while the guide cannot |

For road teeth, use [[Dangers of the Turning]]. Gap-cats are adversaries, not the explanation for every missing sheep or late traveller.

## Food and forage

### What households plant

- **Oats** tolerate cool wet ground and feed people and animals.
- **Rye** makes bread on poorer soils.
- **Barley** fits shorter seasons and brewing.
- **Peas** enrich fields and dry well.
- **Cabbage and turnips** stand between a household and the end of winter.
- **Hay** is not human food and still decides whether milk, wool, and traction survive to spring.
- **Apples** grow only in sheltered vales and are therefore a local pride, not a continent-wide default.

### What travellers can gather

- Spring: nettles and sorrel near sound water; eggs where taking them is permitted.
- Summer: berries, mushrooms after rain, fish, shellfish on the coast.
- Autumn: hazel nuts, late berries, mushrooms, fallen apples near old steadings.
- Winter: very little beyond safe shore food and whatever was stored.

The frontier is land-rich, not automatically food-rich. A wet summer can grow grass and ruin hay. A wide forest can belong to a winter mast-right. Forage should reveal who uses ground, not erase the need for roads.

## Disease and care

| Sickness | Where it enters | Local practice | Condition-labour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Camp fever | Turning-month camp-streets, crowded gift-halls, road-houses | Air bedding, separate hearths, carry food to the sick | Far-Voiced warn; Returned tend plague and dead; a Stilled visitor becomes precious |
| Flux | Flooded wells, river camps, waste uphill of water | Close the source, boil if fuel allows, move waste | Taken-In read ground; Answered improve drains and heat |
| Winter lung | Peat smoke, wet rooms, long cold season | Clear smoke, dry bedding, open rooms on the one fair day | Stilled hold crisis; ordinary healers do the long tending |
| Wound-rot | Axe cuts, animal injuries, wet feet, long travel | Wash, drain, dress, rest | Stilled surgeons when one can be reached; Returned where the work is deadly |
| Marsh ague | Bog-edge camps and standing summer water | Move beds, cover jars, drain near houses | Ditch crews, Taken-In, Root- and Tide-Answered where present |
| Root blight | Wind wounds, standing water, dirty cutting tools | Isolate, drain, clean tools, remove dead tissue | Tree-wardens and Taken-In; no guaranteed cure |

The [[The Grey Summer|Grey Summer]] happened on the Old Crossing, not here. Heskoren inherited caution through delayed hulls, travellers, and stories: isolate a coughing berth, do not let one witness carry a fever through every neighbour's week. A local outbreak may be deadly without becoming a second named Grey.

There are fewer Stilled surgeons and fewer specialist infirmaries than in the Old World pair. A travelling stiller is a summer's luck. Returned do the plague and body work people still need. Neither group should become an excuse to skip ordinary care or the journey to reach them.

## Tithe-ecology at thin reach

| Condition | Heskoren fit | Hard edge |
|---|---|---|
| [[The Taken-In]] | Living land is abundant; farming, forestry, and tending fit the Tithe | Winter, quarantine, bog loss, or a dead planting can isolate someone from living green |
| [[The Stilled]] | Herding, cutting, repair, surgery, and road work provide exertion | Training and surgical support are scarce; injury can enforce dangerous stillness |
| [[Far-Voiced]] | Warning posts, worship, work-song, and open country provide outlets | An isolated posting or full sleeping hall leaves nowhere safe to vent |
| [[Returned]] | Recovery, plague, mines, flood, and tending dead are needed | Useful danger may not serve the person's Purpose; frontier need does not own them |
| [[Two-Bodied]] | Ranging, scouting, herding, courier, and rescue are central livelihoods | Towns may treat family range as public property; confinement spends the other self |
| [[The Answered]] | Drains, walls, kilns, ferries, roofs, and firebreaks need earnest craft | Few guilds mean little backup; after the work, an idle Answered can be stranded |
| [[Long-Lived]] | Every new valley, custom, graft, and traveller can teach | Isolation turns discovery into repetition; novelty cannot be issued from a winter store |

Frontier provision is often a neighbour's field, a gift-hall hour, a place by the fire, or permission to range. It is warm and real. It also runs out.

## Beasts as livelihood

- **Hardy ponies and mules** carry Long Mile loads where carts fail.
- **Oxen** work broader vales and timber roads.
- **Sheep and goats** turn rough slopes into wool, milk, meat, hide, and manure.
- **Cattle** use sheltered grazing and make winter hay a public concern.
- **Pigs** use woodland mast near established hearths.
- **Chickens and geese** fit small yards and camp-streets.
- **Dogs** herd, guard, and find people in bad weather.
- **Bees** survive in sheltered pockets; a failed blossom is read before the grain harvest.

A Two-Bodied courier in a pony, hawk, wolf, otter, or other real animal body is still a person, not one of these animals pressed into use. Their other self can make a route possible and still argue against it.

## The one thing the frost does not explain

After the first hard snow around some young Heskoren Trees, a narrow melt runs back along the road by which the graft first arrived. It can lie on the shaded side while the open square stays white. People call it the old-road melt only when they need to give directions.

Packed earth, drainage, buried stone, or a tender's morning walk can explain any one example. No explanation accounts for enough of them to end the conversation. It does not point toward the next town and does not promise a Tree will take there.

## GM Notes

- **The player-facing cause remains distance and history expressed through horticulture:** young, sick, or scarce grafts; long carrying chains; wind, soil, water, and delayed care. The real edge stays unconfirmed.
- **The old-road melt is the region's single inscrutable.** It remembers where wood came from, not where it should go. Do not turn it into a compass, a prophecy, or a voice.
- **Kind-hearth discipline:** foxes in woods and snow, crows on ridges, seals on coasts. Terrain can make a hearth dense. It never makes a Kind-government.
