# Climate of Strandoren

Strandoren is maritime country: indented coast, tidal estuary, wet lowland, and a west face that watches open water. The sea softens the cold and refuses predictability in return. A week can begin with three working tides, lose two days to fog, and end with every crane tied down.

The coast and the [[Named Ground|Chart-run]] carry food, people, living grafts, disease, and news through the same narrow mouths. High reach and mature Trees do not make the shore ecologically safe. They make it busy.

## At the table

| Ask | Use this |
|---|---|
| **Weather this week** | Salt mist and mild rain; one clear unloading day; estuary water rising after inland rain. On the Night Shore, a west front gives hard rain, a false lull, and a second wind. |
| **What you can eat** | Rye or wheat bread, beans, cabbage, apples, eel or herring; mussels and cockles at a safe tide; oat pottage when fleets stay tied. |
| **What can enter a scene** | A late Chart-run barge; brine in a rented leaf-lot; a coughing hull held outside the quay; a fish market closed by red water; a Far-Voiced warning with no ship behind it. |

## Reading the year

| Months in common speech | Shore-lands and Chart-run | Night Shore | Old Crossing face |
|---|---|---|---|
| **the Deep · Hearth-month · the Thaw / Liren** | Cold rain, occasional frost, muddy fields, river floods | Long gales, wet cold, high surf, few reliable sailing days | Cold drizzle and working tides between squalls |
| **Seed-month · Hale-month · Turning-month / Eolthael** | Early sowing on drained ground; blossom; warm rain and long fair evenings | Fog, hard bright days, then sudden west-weather | Hale-month storms decide departures; Turning-Week fills every berth and road |
| **Gift-month · Ledger-month · Harvest** | Hay, grain, fruit, flax, and busiest river traffic | Best coasting weather, still interrupted by fronts | Fish, passengers, and market cargo compete for holds |
| **the Tally · the Wait · Dark-month** | Wet harvest endings, estuary fog, first field frost | Gales return; missing hulls become the season's public count | Rain and wind shorten the crossing before winter closes inland roads |

Hard frost is less reliable than in Maiethorn's core. That makes winter wet rather than kind: rot, mud, smoke, and bad bedding do more harm than deep snow.

## Shore-lands

Most settlements lie within reach of tide or estuary. Saltwater is road, food, waste-carrier, and threat. Inland rain can flood a quay under a clear local sky because the Chart-run brings another district's weather downstream.

### A playable week

| Day-shape | Weather | What moves |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | Mist, light rain, gentle tide | Fishing boats work; carts reach the quay; washing does not dry. |
| Middle | A bright day with a steady breeze | Every crane, drying rack, and hayfield works at once. Labour and berths are scarce. |
| Turn | Warm rain inland or a coastal squall | The Chart-run rises; low warehouses sandbag; fresh water pushes silt into berths. |
| After | Clear salt air over standing water | Grain may be dry in the field and trapped behind a flooded road. |

Drainage is wealth. Wealthy river fields hold ditches maintained by Root- and Tide-Answered craft alongside ordinary labour. Poorer ground floods longer. The advance is skilled work, not command of rain.

### What grows

- Wheat and rye make the best bread; oats and barley carry wet or poorer ground.
- Peas and beans restore and fill fields; cabbage and turnips keep through winter.
- Flax grows in low country and feeds rope, sailcloth, and linen work.
- Apples and pears thrive behind windbreaks.
- Hay meadows support cattle, sheep, horses, and oxen.
- Marsh edges provide reeds, grazing, eels, and biting insects.

Seaweed is gathered for food in lean weeks and for manure where custom permits. It can enrich a field and salt-burn a young green if piled carelessly.

### What can be gathered

Herring, eel, mussel, cockle, limpet, seaweed, marsh greens, berries, hazel nuts, nettles, sorrel, and mushrooms cover the ordinary range. Safe gathering depends on tide, ownership, and water quality.

A clean-looking shore after a storm can hold fouled shellfish. A generous mussel bed can already belong to a quay hearth. A Taken-In may read an estuary better than most, but no Condition makes spoiled water wholesome by wishing.

## The Old Crossing face

The eastern harbours face [[Maiethorn]] across the oldest, busiest water in the world. The distance is short enough to tempt bad decisions and long enough for one Hale-month storm to close the Given door.

### Salt Walk weather

| Sign | Harbour reading | Family's problem |
|---|---|---|
| High thin cloud, falling air, gulls inland | A blow is coming before the next tide | Leave early, lose cargo, or wait with the ten-year-old |
| Fog holding after noon | Wind may be light; navigation is not | A Far-Voiced can be heard and still cannot show the shoal |
| Warm rain and a backing wind | Fast crossing possible, rough landing likely | Berth and inland road may not be open together |
| Sudden clear after squall | The harbour opens all at once | Pilgrims, grain, fish, and graft earth compete for the same labour |

Far-Voiced are infrastructure here because warning and berth-calls carry through weather. They do not steer ships or make a safe channel. Tide-Answered improve hull work and read flow; they do not cancel a gale.

Turning-Week traffic also crowds lodging, wells, green lots, and release-houses. The prosperous coast can furnish all four and still price a poor family out of them.

## The Chart-run plain

The river interior feeds the ports. Its weather arrives twice: first in the field, then days later as water at the estuary.

### Field week

| Season | Field condition | Quay consequence later |
|---|---|---|
| Spring | Saturated soil delays seed and carts | Grain stores rise in price before anyone knows the yield |
| Summer | Warm rain swells crops and river | Barges move well until debris reaches the lower run |
| Harvest | Three dry days followed by rain | Every household cuts at once; wet sheaves and crowded mills |
| Winter | Mud and bank erosion | Roads fail while the river remains navigable, shifting power to boat owners |

Oxen work heavy soils. Horses and mules move lighter loads. Barges carry bulk. Two-Bodied in suitable animal forms scout banks, carry messages, herd, or work water, but an animal self is not a replacement for a barge crew with hands.

## The Night Shore

The western face meets the open West Water. Here "weather" often means a hull overdue beyond the last forecast anyone trusted.

### A west-weather week

| Watch | Sky and sea | Life ashore |
|---|---|---|
| First | Long low swell, wind steady from the west | Nets come in; roofers are hired; late ships are named but not feared yet. |
| Second | Dark front, hard rain | Coasting stops. Far-Voiced warning posts work. Stores and people crowd under cover. |
| False lull | Rain eases; pressure stays wrong | One factor tries to sail. Families argue whether the lull counts. |
| Second wind | Stronger gusts, crossed sea | A berth breaks, a roof goes, or wreckage arrives without a crew. |
| Clearing | Sharp light, debris line | Chart-crews and rightful owners meet at what the water brought. |

Fish, oats, rye, sheep, and hardy orchard fruit support the shore. Peat supplements driftwood and imported fuel. Salt meadows graze sheep and cattle when storm surge has not covered them.

Missing-hull culture is an ecological adaptation before it is a mood: duplicate stores, shore watches, salvage rights, widows' shares, and factors who never count cargo as arrived before the line is tied.

## Mortal Trees at saltwater

Strandoren's Trees are mature and many, especially on wealthy coasts. Their hazards are ordinary and persistent.

1. Salt spray and storm surge burn leaves and soil.
2. Waterlogged roots invite rot even in a land that calls rain prosperity.
3. Gale damage opens bark to blight.
4. Quay expansion compacts living ground and narrows root space.
5. Polluted runoff follows streets downhill toward old squares.

Towns answer with raised greens, fresh-water cisterns, shelter walls, drains, and restrictions on what may be stored uphill of a Tree. These are civic works built by ordinary hands with Answered craft where available.

A mature graft surviving for generations becomes a financial fact. Houses lend against nearby property, guilds schedule Turning-Week around it, and leaf-lots cluster in the same protected drainage. One storm can therefore threaten sacred wood, Tithe provision, and rents together.

## Food and Tithe ecology

| Place | Food system | Tithe fit | Failure in a bad week |
|---|---|---|---|
| River farm | Grain, beans, roots, hay, cattle | Taken-In green and Two-Bodied work are close to free | Flood isolates the household and drowns living green |
| Fishing quay | Fish, shellfish, imported grain | Far-Voiced, Tide-Answered, Returned salvage, physical Stilled work | Tied fleet removes income and outlet; saltwater enters lots |
| Stone port | Imported food, paid wells, rented green | Deep markets in lots, halls, archives, and licensed medicine | The poor stay hungry for green or outlet while food still reaches richer streets |
| Night Shore town | Fish, sheep, oats, stored cargo | Warning posts, repair work, ranging | A missing hull carries food, novelty, witnesses, and a person's Purpose at once |

Urban Taken-In are a working minority on orchards, river-gardens, quay greens, and rented leaf-lots. They were Given here, wintered here, work here, or cannot afford to leave. Brine in a lot is therefore a public utility failure, not decorative damage.

Long-Lived thrive on the flow of testimony, scholarship, and cargo knowledge—until a house controls which pages or travellers they may see. Novelty can arrive by ship. It is not a commodity made genuine by stamping it "new."

The Stilled have the continent's best licensed surgery and enough physical work to clear Grey. The danger comes after injury, quarantine, or dismissal, when the body ordered to rest is also the body that must keep moving.

Returned can align a Purpose with plague wards, drowned-body recovery, or wreck work. The job remains deadly and useful. It does not become every Returned person's Purpose by civic demand.

## Disease on connected water

The same network that makes Strandoren rich makes infection fast.

| Route | First marker | Ordinary response | Tithe complication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Packed hold | Coughing in one berth, wet bedding, fever by night | Tie the hull, air people ashore by hearth, mark who stood near | Quarantine cuts people from green, range, Purpose, and paid work |
| Fouled well | Several streets with flux after flood | Close the rope, boil water, draw elsewhere, dig waste lower | Fuel and clean water are priced; city lots still need a drink |
| Bad shellfish | One market sick after a calm red or foul tide | Close that shore, discard catch, use stores | A tied food trade removes Far-Voiced and Two-Bodied livelihoods |
| Wound-rot | Rope burns, fish cuts, crane crushes | Wash, dress, operate when needed | A licensed Stilled may be available only to a chartered household |
| Marsh ague | Repeating fever near standing water | Drain near beds, cover jars, move camp | The poorest live on the low ground being drained last |

[[The Grey Summer]] in C.Y. 171 is the named epidemic: a hold cough, then streets and quay-gangs; no respect for Given, Struck, or Kept; tied ships; dead tenders; notes that outlived debtors. A later fever is not a second Grey Summer. People call it "Grey" when they are frightened, and clerks decide whether the comparison changes a rate.

Stilled surgeons hold harm and operate. Returned do plague and dead work. Far-Voiced warn without carrying their bodies street to street. Tide- and Ember-Answered improve water and boiling. None of them replaces clean bedding, separation, food, or trust.

## Beasts as livelihood

- **Oxen** pull ploughs and heavy quay carts from the Chart-run.
- **Horses and mules** carry lighter goods between river landings and indented coasts.
- **Sheep** graze salt meadow and higher dry ground; wool travels even when meat spoils.
- **Cattle** use wet meadows and provide milk, hide, manure, and traction.
- **Pigs** feed on woodland mast inland and quay scraps under strict ownership.
- **Geese and chickens** fit small port yards; their sudden sickness is noticed quickly.
- **Dogs** herd, guard, and work boats; **cats** protect grain stores.
- **Bees** serve orchard country and supply wax to halls and houses.

These animals enter a scene as property, food, labour, affection, or loss. If the coast needs teeth, link to [[Dangers of the Turning]] rather than inventing a sea-monster for every fog.

## The one thing the tide does not explain

Some Turning-month mornings, salt lies on the roots of mature coastal Trees above any storm line. [[Orentel]]'s Tree on the rise is the best-known case. The tide does not reach it.

Tree-tenders wash the roots with fresh water. Hall-keepers schedule around the work. Factors file damaged salt below the rise and decline to file this. No explanation is required for the salt to delay a stand or start an argument.

## GM Notes

- **High reach is not high faith.** Mature abundant Trees follow early sea-carried grafting, wealth, drainage, and care in player text. Commercial belief does not weaken the wood, and healthy wood does not prove doctrine.
- **Salt on roots is the region's single inscrutable.** Keep it practical, localized, and deniable. Do not let it forecast a colour, speak a name, or become a second Grey Summer symptom.
- **Connected disease, mundane mechanism.** Strandoren should feel vulnerable because people and goods move quickly, not because the sea carries a curse.
