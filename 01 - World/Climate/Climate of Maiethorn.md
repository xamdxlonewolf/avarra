# Climate of Maiethorn

Maiethorn holds four of the world's eight climate bands, the wet Crossing-shore, the four-season Mother-core, the snowy Rain-Wall or Thaw-Wall, and the dry Rain-Shadow beyond it, and all four sit inside the Motherland. A traveller can go from salt drizzle to old forest, cross a mountain pass that floods like a lake in thaw-water, and reach a well-town measuring dust, all without leaving it.

The deep difference is water. Towns in the core drain it, [[Lirorn]] on the Wall waits to levy the thaw the range lets go, [[Ornsael]] in the shadow divides what the wells and the cisterns hold, and every living graft wants a share. A graft is the living cutting of motherwood a town keeps so that a Hand, a Tree mature enough to Turn, can stand there at all.

## At the table

A core week is two wet days, one bright break, and then a river rise. A Wall week freezes at night and melts by noon. A shadow week is dry wind until one hard shower fails to fill the well. A Crossing week is mild rain with a squall in Hale-month, the month when the land is supposed to be sound and the roads fill. The table is bread and peas and orchard fruit in the core, barley and roots and hard cheese on the Wall, millet or barley with lentils, onions, and goat cheese in the shadow, and herring, mussels, and rye bread on the coast. The thing that walks into a scene is a cistern turn for the Tree, a Shelf-gate bench filling with stranded carts, a night-bucket taken out of a well-share, a Salt Walk family watching the harbour mouth, a Returned crew on the recovery after a flood, or a neighbour-slate at [[The Mill-hold]].

## Reading the year

People speak the month and the slope together. "Hale-month on the Wall" is not the same promise as "Hale-month at the hinge," the Crossing shore where a family is still trying to make the water. Through the Deep, Hearth-month, and the Thaw, which is also called Liren, the core and the Crossing take frost and snow inland and cold rain at the salt, and then swollen rivers and soft roads, while the Wall is in deep snow and a hard shelf-wind before a melt that may begin slowly or all at once, and the shadow spends cold clear nights guarding cisterns, with the Well-wash, the seasonal water that is not the drinking well, maybe waking briefly in Liren. Seed-month, Hale-month, and Turning-month, also called Eolthael, open the ground and bring blossom and then long light and crowded roads in the core. Lower fields on the Wall sow late. A kind Hale-month opens the passes while high snow remains. The shadow sows terraces on stored water, the dust rises, and Turning-Week crowds compete with the driest approach of the year.

Gift-month, Ledger-month, and Harvest are hay, fruit, grain, river traffic, and whatever drying weather can be borrowed between showers. The Wall gets a short season of grazing and repair, and cuts oats and barley quickly. The shadow brings grain and lentils in early, and grapes and onions later, where the well held. The Tally, the Wait, and Dark-month are apples, leaf-fall, rain, and first frost, with coastal gales strengthening. Animals come down off the Wall, roofs are weighted, and the first snow closes the high road. The shadow gets clear cooling days, cold nights, and little new water, so every store includes a calculation of what is left to drink.

## Crossing-shore

The Old Crossing face is mild and wet. Rain comes sideways often enough that the quays roof the rope, the salt, and the people who are waiting, before they roof the road. A week opens under low cloud and fine rain on a workable tide, so the fish comes in, wool and grain can load, and the decks stay slick. The wind then freshens off the water, [[Far-Voiced]] warnings carry, and small craft tie up before the main berths close. The Far-Voiced are people who have to put the voice somewhere, and on this shore that voice is how a warning crosses weather. The turn of the week is a hard squall, especially in Hale-month, and a Salt Walk family, crossing in order to reach Turning-Week, must leave now, wait, or miss the inland margin. Afterward the air clears and the swell is confused, every delayed hull wants the same berth, and wreckwood and weed mark the strand.

The coast grows rye, oats, cabbage, beans, and orchard fruit behind the salt edge. Herring, eels, mussels, and cockles make a bad field-week survivable. Salt spray can burn a young graft even where rain is abundant, so the town Trees sit above the quay with fresh-water cisterns and sheltering walls.

Quay cough, wet bedding, and hold fever are ordinary fears. The memory of [[The Grey Summer]] makes a tied hull where someone is coughing more persuasive than any sermon. Stilled surgery, the work of the [[The Stilled|Stilled]], whose upkeep is the hard use of the body, is reachable in the rich ports. Returned plague-and-dead work, done by the [[Returned]], sits beyond the clean water. A Returned person's Purpose can include that work. The port does not get to decide that it does.

## Mother-core

West of the Wall and inland from the salt is the four-season heart: rounded hills, river valleys, old forest, worked fields, and the densest mature grafts in the world. Winter is snow inland, thawing slush by market-day, and a hard frost after, and the pressure sits on firewood, fodder, the weight on a roof, and a green room that has to stay living. Spring is three days of rain, a bright wind, and then the river bank-full, which is when mills, seed already in the low ground, the Tree's drains, and the road to a neighbour all come due. Summer is warm mornings, one thunderstorm, and long clear evenings, and the strain is the hay, the Turning-Week crowds, the water for the orchards, and every outlet in the square. Autumn is mist, two dry harvest days, and steady rain at the week's end, with grain going under cover, apples being picked, pigs turned out to mast, and the ruts in the road getting deeper.

The core's plenty is made, not granted. Rye and wheat hold the bread, oats and peas and beans and cabbage and turnips carry the poorer weeks, apples and pears store, and flax supplies the linen. Pigs feed in the woods under rights older than some guilds, and oxen and horses work the deep soils and the river roads.

Forage near a settled road is rarely ownerless. Hazel nuts, berries, nettles, mushrooms, honey, and river fish are available in season, and the gleaning and the mast rights belong to somebody. A [[The Taken-In|Taken-In]] person, who has to keep near living plants, can find more and can coax a damaged patch, but cannot turn a harvested hedge into a granary.

### Mortal wood in old country

Mature Trees survive because generations have built around what the wood needs. Drains keep the spring water from standing at the roots. Cistern turns separate the Tree's drink from the washing and from what the market uses. Tools that have been in the orchard or the motherwood are cleaned before they touch a graft. Grazing animals are kept off the exposed roots. After a gale or a snow, Root-Answered people brace the soil and the limbs, and they do not make dead wood live. That bracing is the craft of the [[The Answered|Answered]], earnest work in root and soil, and it stops where the wood has already died.

Age hides the risk, so an old culvert can choke and a rich square can assume that someone else already watered. [[The Mill-hold]] is a town like that this year: the mill-race stayed full and the Hand stood in the overflow. During the Grey Summer, some coastal Hands sickened after their human watering-hands died. Stored grain did not solve that. A choked drain is not another Grey Summer.

### Sickness

Spring flood brings flux where waste reaches the wells, dense Turning-month crowds bring coughs and the fevers of childhood, autumn cuts bring wound-rot, and winter rooms bring smoke and lung sickness. The core has the best Stilled infirmaries and the most Returned available for plague and for the work of the body. That lowers the deaths, and it also makes the poorer districts, and the dry east, wait on specialists who are already busy where the institutions stand.

## Rain-Wall / Thaw-Wall

The high range stores the winter as snow and then lets it go on a schedule the valleys have to live by. "Last year's snow" means the depth and hardness already waiting above the road when Liren begins. A thaw-week starts on a cold morning when the shelf holds and the carts move before noon, so everyone tries the same hours and a toll-house can call that an opening. The second day warms, runnels cross the path, pack loads have to rise above the wet ground, and mules matter more than speed. Rain on snow fills the cut and moves the stones. The Shelf-gate, the pass people are trying to use, can become a lake, and the lower valley prepares for the flood. Night freezes a skin over the water and hardens the mud, so the road looks safer than it is, with the broken footing waiting under the ice. A clear day leaves the upper snow bright and heavy, and a courier can see the next release before the valley can.

[[The Thaw-Break]] was this whole arrangement failing at once. Melt that should have taken a month came in seven days, and the roads stayed closed for two summers. It is not the expected thaw. It is why every expected thaw still has a water-line in the conversation.

The lower shelves grow oats, barley, rye, peas, cabbage, and turnips. Hay is wealth, because the animals of winter eat the summer that was cut and stored. Sheep and goats use the steep ground, hardy ponies and mules make the passes, cattle stay in the broader valleys, and bilberries, sorrel, mushrooms, and stream fish supplement the stores.

Cold and wet produce damaged feet, lung sickness, falls, and wound-rot. A Stilled surgeon can hold the harm on a high shelf and still needs clean water, hands, and a road home. Returned recovery crews are respected after an avalanche or a flood. [[Two-Bodied]] couriers, people with another self in a real animal body, take shapes suited to snow, flight, scent, or climbing. They remain people choosing the route, not public pack stock.

### The civic thaw

Snowfall fixes how much water is stored. The thaw then fixes the pass-days, the flood risk, and the water the valley will get. Those pass-days fix the tolls, the arrival of seed, the witnesses, and whether a cohort can still cross inside the same week. The delays decide which towns host the extra people, and which children miss. Lirorn can levy a road because the weather has made that road scarce. The injustice is not that the water is unreal. It is that a household controls the notch everyone must use.

## Rain-Shadow

East of the Wall, dry hills and terraces sit around the wells. Rain may fall hard and still leave little in the cistern, if it runs off before the ground drinks. A week opens on a cold clear dawn and a dry warm afternoon, so the work begins early and animals and people rest in the shade. Wind then lifts the dust off the terraces, eyes and lungs suffer, and the Far-Voiced use a courtyard or an open hill when they need an outlet. The turn is a short hard shower, or a cloud that gives nothing, and the roof-catch fills first. The Well-wash may run brown and then vanish. Afterward the sky is bright and the ground is crusted, a cistern looks full until the silt settles, and the mark on the well matters more than the look of the water.

Barley and millet take the broad terraces, lentils, peas, onions, and hardy greens grow where the water can be directed, and grapes hold the warmer slopes. Goats turn scrub into milk, cheese, hide, and dung, and they strip a young planting if the wall breaks.

Useful forage is brief, and it comes after rain: herbs, greens, small fruit, snails. Most of the food in this dry country is stored or tended, not found. Anyone promising a lush hidden valley within a day's walk is selling a route, not describing normal ground.

### Wells, Trees, and Tithes

At a well-town, several claims sit on the same civic drink. People come first, in most shares, animals and the terrace crops have a claim because next month also needs food, and the Tree has a claim because mortal motherwood cannot be told to endure doctrine. Taken-In plantings and the potted green feed both the people and a Tithe, the upkeep a changed person has to keep meeting. A traveller's right varies by custom and by how scarce the week has become.

A town can have a sound Hand and a failing well, and [[Ornsael]] is a town like that. The Well-wash is not its drinking water.

Flux follows a dirty rope, a cracked cistern, and the silt a flood leaves behind. Dust makes the coughs worse. Summer wounds foul quickly when the water for washing has been rationed. Returned work stays downwind and downhill from the drink. A travelling Stilled is a pass-story, so ordinary midwives, bone-setters, and clean-handed healers do most of the care.

## Tithe-weather by site

A Condition is a change a person lives with, and bad weather removes the livelihood and the Tithe together when those two were the same day's work. On a core farm or in motherwood, Taken-In green, Two-Bodied range, Answered craft, and Stilled labour all fit easily, until a flood closes the fields, a quarantine closes the green, or the emergency ends and a Stilled person still has to keep moving. A counted city can furnish green, outlets, medicine, and archives from the crown's stores, and it can also withhold a channel, or crowd everyone into the same provision when the weather packs the streets. A Crossing quay gives the Far-Voiced and the Tide-Answered their work, and novelty arrives on every hull, until a tied harbour removes the work, the outlet, the green cargo, and the new witness all at once.

A Wall pass still offers range, exertion, rescue, and the reading of weather, and the isolation can starve the green, the Purpose, or the novelty even while the physical work remains. A Rain-Shadow well keeps a Taken-In person whole on the living terraces, and goats and road-work keep other people employed, until the same bucket is the food, the green, and the Tree's drink, and the allocation itself becomes the leverage. A [[Long-Lived]] person snowed into a pass-house may find one new traveller enough to keep learning. The fourth week of the same storm is no longer new. Novelty is not grown in a field.

## Beasts as livelihood

Oxen plough and haul in the core, and a flooded ford can stop a district's grain without harming a single field. Horses and ponies serve riders and the lighter carts, and mules hold the mountain roads. Sheep turn the shelves into wool, cheese, meat, and manure. Goats suit the Rain-Shadow and become part of the well-share, because the herd drinks too. Pigs use the mast in the core woodland. Chickens and geese turn scraps into eggs and noise. Bees make the honey and the wax, and they show whether the orchard blossom survived a cold week.

Gap-cats and the other dangers of the road belong on [[Dangers of the Turning]]. The animals above enter a scene because they are hungry, injured, valuable, or in the way.

## The one thing the weather does not explain

The [[Named Ground|Core-thaw]], the river at [[Maiethlir]], runs louder the week before Leaf-Fall, the giving at the Tree in Turning-Week. Clerks file that rise as snowmelt, and watchers do not. The river does not wait for either account, and no one on the river settles it.

At the table, make it practical first. The water is loud enough that a Speaker has to raise their voice, a tablet has to be moved above the step, and a child cannot hear their own name.

## GM Notes

Healthy grafts, old drains, good soil, and concentrated tending are the reasons a player can be shown for why the core Turns reliably. Dry soil, salt, young wood, and distance explain the weaker edges. Those causes are as far as the player-facing page goes. Do not let the loud week become proof of a speaker in the river.

The loud week already belongs to Maiethlir, and Maiethlir does not explain it. Do not add an answering frost, a second river omen, or a Tree that forecasts the colour.

The pressure in this note is not ownership. The First Seat remains in the Motherwood beside Maiethlir's country. Climate does not move the grove, the Inner Close, or a sixteenth power.
