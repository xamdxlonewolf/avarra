# Climate of Maiethorn

Maiethorn contains four of the world's eight climate bands: the wet Crossing-shore, the four-season Mother-core, the snowy Rain-Wall or Thaw-Wall, and the dry Rain-Shadow beyond it. A traveller can go from salt drizzle to old forest, lake a mountain pass in thaw-water, and reach a well-town measuring dust—all without leaving the Motherland.

The deep difference is water. In the core, towns drain it. On the Wall, Lirorn waits to levy it. In the shadow, [[Ornsael]] divides it. Every living graft wants a share.

## At the table

| Ask | Use this |
|---|---|
| **Weather this week** | Core: two wet days, one bright break, then a river rise. Wall: freeze at night and melt by noon. Shadow: dry wind until one hard shower fails to fill the well. Crossing: mild rain with a Hale-month squall. |
| **What you can eat** | Core bread, peas, cabbage, orchard fruit, pork or river fish; Wall barley, roots, hard cheese; Shadow millet or barley, lentils, onions, goat cheese; coast herring, mussels, and rye bread. |
| **What can enter a scene** | A cistern turn for the Tree; a Shelf-gate bench filling with stranded carts; a night-bucket taken from a well-share; a Salt Walk family watching the harbour mouth; a Returned recovery crew after flood. |

## Reading the year

| Months in common speech | Mother-core and Crossing | Rain-Wall / Thaw-Wall | Rain-Shadow |
|---|---|---|---|
| **the Deep · Hearth-month · the Thaw / Liren** | Frost and snow inland, cold rain at salt; then swollen rivers and soft roads | Deep snow, hard shelf-wind, then a melt that may begin slowly or all at once | Cold clear nights; cisterns guarded; the Well-wash may wake briefly in Liren |
| **Seed-month · Hale-month · Turning-month / Eolthael** | Ground opens, blossom comes, then long light and crowded roads | Lower fields sow late; Hale-month opens passes in a kind year; high snow remains | Terraces sow on stored water; dust rises; Turning-Week crowds compete with the driest approach |
| **Gift-month · Ledger-month · Harvest** | Hay, fruit, grain, river traffic, and drying weather borrowed between showers | Short grazing and repair season; oats and barley cut quickly | Grain and lentils come in early; grapes and onions later where the well held |
| **the Tally · the Wait · Dark-month** | Apples, leaf-fall, rain, first frost; coastal gales strengthen | Animals descend, roofs are weighted, first snow closes the high road | Clear cooling days, cold nights, little new water; every store includes a water calculation |

People speak the month and the slope together. "Hale-month on the Wall" is not the same promise as "Hale-month at the hinge."

## Crossing-shore

The Old Crossing face is mild and wet. Rain comes sideways often enough that quays roof rope, salt, and waiting people before they roof the road.

### A playable week

| Day-shape | Weather | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | Low cloud, fine rain, workable tide | Fish comes in. Wool and grain can load. Decks stay slick. |
| Middle | Wind freshens from the water | Far-Voiced warnings carry. Small craft tie up before the main berths close. |
| Turn | A hard squall, especially in Hale-month | A Salt Walk family must leave now, wait, or miss the inland margin before Turning-Week. |
| After | Clear air and confused swell | Every delayed hull asks for the same berth. Wreckwood and weed mark the strand. |

The coast grows rye, oats, cabbage, beans, and orchard fruit behind the salt edge. Herring, eels, mussels, and cockles make bad field weather survivable. Salt spray can burn a young graft even where rain is abundant, so town Trees sit above the quay with fresh-water cisterns and sheltering walls.

Quay cough, wet bedding, and hold fever are ordinary fears. The memory of [[The Grey Summer]] makes a tied coughing hull more persuasive than any sermon. Stilled surgery is reachable in rich ports; Returned plague-and-dead work sits beyond the clean water.

## Mother-core

West of the Wall and inland from the salt is the four-season heart: rounded hills, river valleys, old forest, worked fields, and the densest mature grafts in the world.

### A playable week

| Season | Week-weather | Put pressure on |
|---|---|---|
| Winter | Snow inland, thawing slush by market-day, hard frost after | Firewood, fodder, roof weight, and a green room that must stay living |
| Spring | Three days of rain, a bright wind, then the river bank-full | Mills, seed in low ground, Tree drains, and the road to a neighbour |
| Summer | Warm mornings, one thunderstorm, long clear evenings | Hay, Turning-Week crowds, orchard water, and every outlet in the square |
| Autumn | Mist, two dry harvest days, steady rain at week's end | Grain under cover, apples picked, pigs turned to mast, road ruts deepening |

The core's plenty is made, not granted. Rye and wheat hold the bread; oats, peas, beans, cabbage, and turnips carry poorer weeks. Apples and pears store. Flax supplies linen. Pigs feed in woods under rights older than some guilds; oxen and horses work the deep soils and river roads.

Forage near a settled road is rarely ownerless. Hazel nuts, berries, nettles, mushrooms, honey, and river fish are available in season, but gleaning and mast rights belong to somebody. A Taken-In can find more and can coax a damaged patch, but cannot turn a harvested hedge into a granary.

### Mortal wood in old country

Mature Trees survive because generations have built around their needs.

- Drains keep spring water from standing at the roots.
- Cistern turns separate the Tree's drink from washing and market use.
- Orchard and motherwood tools are cleaned before touching a graft.
- Grazing animals are kept off exposed roots.
- After gale or snow, Root-Answered brace soil and limbs; they do not make dead wood live.

Age hides risk. An old culvert can choke. A rich square can assume someone else watered. During the Grey Summer, some coastal Hands sickened after their human watering-hands died. Stored grain did not solve that.

### Sickness

Spring flood brings flux where waste reaches wells. Dense Turning-month crowds bring coughs and childhood fevers. Autumn cuts bring wound-rot. Winter rooms bring smoke and lung sickness.

The core has the best Stilled infirmaries and the most Returned available for plague and body work. That lowers deaths; it also makes poorer districts and the dry east wait for specialists who are busy where the institutions already stand.

## Rain-Wall / Thaw-Wall

The high range stores winter and releases it as law. "Last year's snow" means the depth and hardness already waiting above the road when Liren begins.

### A playable thaw-week

| Watch | What happens | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| First cold morning | The shelf holds; carts move before noon | Everyone tries the same hours. A toll-house can call this an opening. |
| Warm second day | Runnels cross the path | Pack loads rise above wet ground. Mules matter more than speed. |
| Rain on snow | The cut fills, stones move | The Shelf-gate can become a lake. The lower valley prepares for flood. |
| Night freeze | Water skins over, mud hardens | The road looks safer than it is. Broken footing waits under ice. |
| Clear day | The upper snow remains bright and heavy | A courier can see the next release before the valley can. |

[[The Thaw-Break]] was this system failing at once: melt that should have taken a month came in seven days, and roads stayed closed two summers. It is not the expected thaw. It is why every expected thaw still has a water-line in the conversation.

Lower shelves grow oats, barley, rye, peas, cabbage, and turnips. Hay is wealth because winter animals eat last summer. Sheep and goats use steep ground; hardy ponies and mules make the passes; cattle stay in the broader valleys. Bilberries, sorrel, mushrooms, and stream fish supplement stores.

Cold and wet produce damaged feet, lung sickness, falls, and wound-rot. A Stilled surgeon can hold harm on a high shelf but still needs clean water, hands, and a road home. Returned recovery crews are respected after avalanche and flood. Two-Bodied couriers take animal shapes suited to snow, flight, scent, or climbing; they remain people choosing the route, not public pack stock.

### The civic thaw

1. Snowfall fixes how much water is stored.
2. The thaw fixes pass-days, flood risk, and valley water.
3. Pass-days fix tolls, seed arrival, witnesses, and whether a same-week cohort can cross.
4. Those delays decide which towns host extra mouths and which children miss.

Lirorn can levy a road because weather has made the road scarce. The injustice is not that the water is unreal. It is that a household controls the notch everyone must use.

## Rain-Shadow

East of the Wall, dry hills and terraces sit around wells. Rain may fall hard and still leave little in the cistern if it runs off before the ground drinks.

### A playable week

| Day-shape | Weather | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | Cold clear dawn, dry warm afternoon | Work begins early. Animals and people rest in shade. |
| Middle | Wind lifts dust from the terraces | Eyes and lungs suffer. Far-Voiced use courtyards or open hills for an outlet. |
| Turn | A short hard shower, or a cloud that gives nothing | Roof-catch fills first. The Well-wash may run brown and vanish. |
| After | Bright sky, crusted ground | A cistern looks full until silt settles. The well mark matters more. |

Barley and millet take the broad terraces. Lentils, peas, onions, and hardy greens grow where water can be directed. Grapes hold warmer slopes. Goats turn scrub into milk, cheese, hide, and dung; they also strip a young planting if the wall breaks.

Useful forage is brief after rain: herbs, greens, small fruit, snails. Most dry-country food is stored or tended, not found. Anyone promising a lush hidden valley within a day's walk is selling a route, not describing normal ground.

### Wells, Trees, and Tithes

At a well-town, the civic drink has several mouths:

- people first, in most shares;
- animals and terrace crops, because next month also needs food;
- the Tree, because mortal motherwood cannot be told to endure doctrine;
- Taken-In plantings and potted green, which feed both people and a Tithe;
- travellers, whose right varies by custom and scarcity.

A town can have a sound Hand and a failing well. [[Ornsael]] is that playable square. The Well-wash is not its drinking water.

Flux follows dirty ropes, cracked cisterns, and flood silt. Dust worsens coughs. Summer wounds foul quickly when washing water is rationed. Returned work stays downwind and downhill from the drink. A travelling Stilled is a pass-story, so ordinary midwives, bone-setters, and clean-handed healers do most care.

## Tithe-weather by site

| Site | Easy fit | Week of exposure |
|---|---|---|
| Core farm or motherwood | Taken-In green, Two-Bodied range, Answered craft, Stilled labour | Flood closes fields; quarantine closes the green; emergency work ends and the Stilled must keep moving |
| Counted city | Crown-furnished green, outlets, medicine, archives | A channel can be withheld; weather crowds everyone into the same provision |
| Crossing quay | Far-Voiced and Tide-Answered work; novelty arrives by every hull | A tied harbour removes work, outlet, green cargo, and new witness together |
| Wall pass | Range, exertion, rescue, weather-reading | Isolation starves green, Purpose, or novelty even while physical work remains |
| Rain-Shadow well | Living terraces keep the Taken-In whole; goats and road-work keep others employed | The same bucket is food, green, and the Tree's drink; allocation becomes leverage |

Novelty is not grown in a field. A Long-Lived snowed into a pass-house may find one new traveller sustaining; the fourth week of the same storm is no longer new.

## Beasts as livelihood

- **Oxen** plough and haul in the core; a flooded ford can stop a district's grain without harming a field.
- **Horses and ponies** serve riders and lighter carts; **mules** hold the mountain roads.
- **Sheep** turn shelves into wool, cheese, meat, and manure.
- **Goats** suit the Rain-Shadow and become part of the well-share.
- **Pigs** use core woodland mast; **chickens and geese** turn scraps into eggs and noise.
- **Bees** make honey and wax and show whether orchard blossom survived a cold week.

Gap-cats and road dangers belong on [[Dangers of the Turning]]. The animals above enter scenes because they are hungry, injured, valuable, or in the way.

## The one thing the weather does not explain

The [[Named Ground|Core-thaw]] runs louder the week before Leaf-Fall at [[Maiethlir]]. Clerks file snowmelt. Watchers do not. The river does not wait for either account, and no one in this note settles it.

At the table, make it practical first: a Speaker raising their voice, a tablet moved above the step, a child unable to hear their name. Let the explanation remain where the water left it.

## GM Notes

- **Keystone discipline:** dense healthy grafts, old drains, good soil, and concentrated tending are the player-facing reasons the core Turns reliably. Dry soil, salt, young wood, and distance explain weaker edges. Do not let the loud week become proof of a speaker in the river.
- **The loud week is the region's single inscrutable.** It already belongs to Maiethlir. Do not add an answering frost, a second river omen, or a Tree that forecasts the colour.
- **Pressure, not ownership:** the First Seat remains in the Motherwood beside Maiethlir's country. Climate does not move the grove, the Inner Close, or a sixteenth power.
