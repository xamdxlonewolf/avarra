# Kin at the Door

Two of the faiths already describe a presence that a bestiary would try to file as a spirit, and both of those accounts are already written, so this note only says where you stand when you meet one, and then it stops.

## A door

[[The Old Ways]] say the dead stay in the ground. Hills, waters, old trees that are not grafts, and the people who went into them are kin. At a door, whether that is a drowning-stone, an oath-stone, or a gap where a first-meal of food is left, the living put that food down and ask to be known. [[The Door-Keepers]] know which doors still open, and they do not call up a body to fight.

Ancestors in that rite are kin who remained, and they are not a creature with a stat block. A [[Returned]] person is a different door: someone who died with something unfinished and got back up, which is what the Old Ways already say.

## A return

A return is a Returned person who carries a card and a neighbour's life, and whose Purpose is the unfinished thing they came back for. Towns send them where the living die of the work. You do not summon one; you ask, or you meet them at the ward, the collapse, or the door they kept.

[[When the Town Buries]] already puts a little of the meal at the threshold. If the dead kept the Old Ways, the rest of that food goes to the door when the street is finished. That is a funeral, and it is not a calling.

## What the other faiths do not add

[[The Watching]] keeps the Tree as the Tree, [[Maieth]] is a belief that sits beside [[The Leaf-Mother]], and [[The Open Table]] keeps a table. A door and a return are the only spirit-like things the faiths describe.

## GM Notes

Do not confirm the Other Hands from this note, do not name a hand in the water, and do not turn a first-meal into proof that someone else walks the door. The faith already has the door and the return. A later note must not add a spirit adversary, a summoning, or a quota of unique creatures.
