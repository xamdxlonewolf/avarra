# Kin at the Door

Two faiths already have a presence that a bestiary would try to file as a spirit. Both are already written. This note says where to stand, and stops there.

## A door

[[The Old Ways]] say the dead stay in the ground. Hills, waters, old trees that are not grafts, and the people who went into them are kin. At a door — a drowning-stone, an oath-stone, a gap where a first-meal is left — the living put food down and ask to be known. [[The Door-Keepers]] know which doors still open. They do not call up a body to fight.

Ancestors in that rite are kin who remained. They are not a creature with a stat block. [[Returned]] are a different door: a person who died with something unfinished and got back up. The Old Ways already say so.

## A return

A return is a Returned person. They have a card, a Purpose, and a neighbour's life. Towns send them where the living die of the work. You do not summon one. You ask, or you meet them at the ward, the collapse, or the door they kept.

[[When the Town Buries]] already puts a little of the meal at the threshold. If the dead were of a door, the rest of that food goes to the door when the street is finished. That is a funeral. It is not a calling.

## What the other faiths do not add

[[The Watching]] keeps the Tree as the Tree. [[Maieth]] is a belief beside [[The Leaf-Mother]]. [[The Open Table]] keeps a table. The list is the door and the return.

## GM Notes

Do not confirm the Other Hands from this note. Do not name a hand in the water, and do not turn a first-meal into proof that someone else walks the door. The faith already has the door and the return. A later note must not add a spirit adversary, a summoning, or a quota of unique creatures.
