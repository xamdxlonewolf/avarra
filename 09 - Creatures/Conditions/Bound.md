# Bound

The Bound are people who, in some extremity, swore a contract with something on the other side and are still keeping the terms. They are the only people carrying a Condition who chose it. A Condition is the becoming you acquire, set beside the Kind you were born, and every other Condition was Given when the Tree offered it or Struck when a threshold took the person whether they had asked or not. The Bound asked, and something answered. The power they carry is the power they named, and what their neighbors fear is not a demon in the blood but the debt, and the counterparty who watches the ledger and does not look away.

Daggerheart already has the Infernis, people born demon-blooded. That is a Kind, the ancestry you come into the world wearing, and Bound is not that. Bound is acquired, and you are never born into it. An Infernis may still be Bound, because Kind and Condition stack on the same person. The blood is not a contract, and the contract is not a bloodline.

## The take

The [[Turning Tree]] gives a Condition at the Awakening, the ceremony at age 10 when a leaf's colour names what a child will become, or it withholds a colour and gives nothing, and it does not Give a contract. The Bound are Struck only, which means the becoming comes later, by a threshold, and the threshold here is a bargain: someone in dire need swore, at whatever age, and something answered. People fear them, because the contract-lane is ~5% of everyone, the power is real, the terms bind, and a Bound who falls behind is collateral the contract can collect. Towns need them in the same breath, because a Bound's sworn word is literally binding, and many of them can tell a lie when they hear one, so the world puts them to work as enforcers, oath-keepers, witnesses, and the people who arbitrate a debt. When a treaty absolutely must hold, a Bound swears to it. That ~5% is the whole contract-lane, and it already holds both halves. People currently Bound are ~3.5%, and the [[The Unbound|Unbound]] already released are ~1.5% inside the same figure. You do not count the released ones a second time on top.

## Away from the Tree

A Bound has no colour, because the Tree never Strikes and a contract leaves no leaf. The canonical palette, including that rule of no colour, is on [[Turning Tree#Reading the colours (colour → Condition)]], and the only path in is the contract sworn in extremity, not a gift at the ceremony. How the Tree gives every Condition it does give is the subject of [[Turning Tree]].

The faiths do not read a Bound the same way. [[The Leaf-Mother|Motherfaith]], the faith of the Leaf-Mother, fears them and still needs them, and it receives them back at the Tree so the town can acknowledge what has already happened. That receiving is not a Gift, because the Tree is not handing the contract out. [[The Fair Hand]] says this is the same shape as every other becoming, written large enough to read, rather than a horror set apart. [[The Watching]] names a seeing at a harder door, and [[The Old Ways]] take a Bound to the oath-stone that heard the vow and name them kin.

[[The Book-Hands]] witness the signing, and on oath-day they recite the Terms. They do not rewrite those Terms. A newly Struck Bound is sent to [[The Intake]] first, so the contract can be read and written down.

## Daggerheart — Transformation card

*You swore something to something, and it answered. Now you both keep the terms.*

- **The Granted Power** — When you became Bound, you named what you wanted, and the contract granted it. With the GM, choose one small, defined boon your class doesn't give you. The boon might be that you can never become lost, so you always know true north and the way back. It might be that you don't need to breathe, or that you need no food or water, or that you can speak and understand any tongue, or that you always know when someone lies to you. If the boon is something you actively call on, it costs a **Stress** to use. If it is simply true of you, it works with no cost at all.
  - **On flight (a rare, marked bargain):** a Bound may contract for true flight, but the sky is already spoken for. It is the province of the [[Tengu]] and, in legend, the [[Phoenix]]. A contract for flight is uncommon and never clean. It grants literal, visible wings, or some stranger tell, a shadow that lifts or feet that will not touch the ground, and a Bound cannot furl those wings or hide that tell. The bargain marks them as plainly as a Phoenix's fire marks a Phoenix, so anyone looking can see that a deal was made. Most people who want the sky are more simply served by a glide or a saving slow-fall. True sustained flight is a GM's call, priced with heavier Terms and a Pledge to match, and it is deliberately not one of the ordinary boons above, the ones a Bound can carry in a crowd.
- **The Terms** *(Tithe)* — Your contract carries Terms, written on the card: a standing obligation the counterparty exacts. You might never refuse a sincere plea, or you might be bound to collect what is owed, or to tell no lie on a holy day. Keep the Terms and the power stays clean. Break a Term, or miss a payment, and the contract collects. The GM gains a **Fear**. Until you make restitution, which is a defined act the GM sets, your Granted Power costs a **Hit Point** instead of Stress, and the Whisper begins. While you owe that debt, whenever you roll with Fear the GM may voice an intrusive thought, or a plausible lie the counterparty wants you to believe. You may act against it freely. Shutting it out costs a Stress, and the debt compounds each time you fall further behind. The Whisper suggests and never commands, and the counterparty cannot take control of you.
- **The Pledge** — At signing you also named what you would give when the contract ends, a part of yourself: an eye, a leg, your capacity for fear, your memory of home. It is written on the card, and you keep it while the contract runs. When a term-limited contract comes due, the counterparty releases you and collects the Pledge, and you become one of [[The Unbound]], whom people colloquially call the Hollow.

## The counterparty

You choose the counterparty when the character is made, or you do not know it yet and the discovery is a thread of play. What holds the note may be a demon or a lesser power, a named thing that bargains, or a cosmic-horror intelligence in the Lovecraftian sense, vast and indifferent and wrong. It may also be unknown even to you, so that you genuinely do not know what holds your note.

## Getting out

The only sure release is to kill the thing that holds your contract. A patron that can grant this power can often sense the intent while it is still forming, so plotting the kill is its own peril. A term-limited contract does not need that ending. It comes due, the counterparty releases you and collects the Pledge, and what that leaves behind is [[The Unbound]].

## The Given-Over *(title, not a Condition)*

The world has a name for a Bound who keeps signing, power after power and pledge after pledge. They start because they are afraid of paying what they already owe, and another contract defers the debt, and then it becomes too much to stop. Bit by bit they give themselves over until the being effectively holds them. This is an NPC-tier fate, a title in the story rather than a Condition a player can take, and a player character who has signed away nearly all of themselves is no longer really playable. The plain name is the Given-Over, the contract-guilds' term of art is the Forfeit, and the pipeline that feeds that fate is [[The Holding Desk]].

## GM Notes

Keep the Whisper strictly suggestive: plausible lies and intrusive thoughts, which the player can reject by spending a Stress, and never a hand on the player character. The Pledge is the long fuse of the card. A term-limited Bound is walking toward [[The Unbound]], and that horizon should be known and dreaded, not a surprise saved for the end of the term.

The counterparty has a name on this side of the screen. Per [[The Other Hands]], the Counterparty is Orledd, the household Hand who answers a sworn ask. The face chosen at creation, whether demon, cosmic horror, or unknown, is a mask, and the terms underneath are the same shape. Do not play Orledd as a devil, and do not let this retcon Given Conditions into bargains.

What Orledd gets during the term is the named ask remaining true, and the Terms being kept where other people can see them, and that readable exchange is the food. Orledd cannot Give, because a gift with no named return would be her work, and a Bound contract is not that gift. At the end of a term-limited contract they collect the Pledge, which is already written on the card. They do not get a soul, or worship, or a second signing. The Given-Over spiral is the Bound's fear of paying, not the Counterparty's hunger, and a flood of unread bargains starves them. If you need the strain and break rules, for a bargain that has gone outside her allowance, they live on [[The Other Hands]].

Do not add the Hollow as a second percentage on top of Bound's ~5%. The Unbound are already inside it.
