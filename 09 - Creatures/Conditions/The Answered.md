# The Answered

An Answered is a person one of the four elements answered, and the element has gone on answering ever since. The djinn of the old stories is a wish trapped in a lamp, bound to whoever holds the vessel. There is no wish here, no master, and no bottle. At the Awakening, when the [[Turning Tree]] gives a Condition at age 10, an element spoke to this person, and it talks back to their hands the way a native tongue talks back to the person who grew up in it. The glasswrights, the shipwrights, the masons, and the makers of instruments are Answered when the craft needs that element coaxed rather than forced.

## The take

The leaf that marks them is lamp-amber, or brass, and the shade of that colour names which element answered: Ember for fire, Tide for water, Root for earth and stone, and Gale for air. From that day the element is theirs to speak to, though they do not summon it and they do not command it. They coax it the way a potter coaxes clay, and they never conjure it the way a story conjures a spirit, which is why the guilds are built on that coaxing. Ember-Answered glasswrights and smiths do not need a furnace at full heat, and Tide-Answered shipwrights and dyers can bend a hull's planks or set a colour without steam and without mordant. Root-Answered masons and potters coax stone and clay with no chisel and no wheel, while Gale-Answered sailmakers and instrument-makers cut the cloth and voice the instrument with the wind helping.

Their standing is the wildcard, and they are ~10% of people, because an Answered is never one kind of neighbor. Which element answered, and what temperament arrived with it, changes from person to person. An even-tempered Root-Answered potter and a short-fused Ember-Answered smith share the name and very little else. Some guilds prize them above any other artisan, while some households are wary of keeping, under their roof, a person whose element runs hot. The leaf tells you the element and does not tell you the temperament, which you learn only by meeting them.

## At the Tree

The leaf is lamp-amber or brass, a colour ordinary people already know, and the shade of that amber names Ember, Tide, Root, or Gale while telling you nothing about temperament until you have met the person. The path is Given only, and the canonical palette, together with the account of how the Tree gives the leaf, is on [[Turning Tree#Reading the colours (colour → Condition)]] and [[Turning Tree]].

[[The Leaf-Mother|Motherfaith]], the faith of the Leaf-Mother, hears an element that answered because the gift fitted a craft. [[The Watching]] says the Tree saw which medium would talk back, and calls that a witness rather than a mother's pick.

The four [[The Element-Guilds|element-guilds]] license an Answered to one standard and give them steady work, so the element has somewhere to go and does not break loose in public.

## Daggerheart — Transformation card

*An element answered you at the Awakening. It still does — and it still expects to be spoken to.*

Choose your element at creation: Ember, Tide, Root, or Gale. The choice sets the Reach in the table, and it sets what you are allowed to ask.

- **The Answering Hand** *(boon)* — Your element answers your hands without a tool, a forge, or force behind it. Glass blows with no furnace, a hull bends with no steam, stone is coaxed with no chisel, and you have **advantage** on any action roll to shape, repair, wield, or read something made of your element. In the moment it answers too. Spend a **Hope** to use your element's Reach, in the table below. The Reach is always there to spend. What wears down, if you go too long without speaking, is the Tithe.
- **Spoken, not commanded** — Once per long rest, you may ask your element one question it can feel through its own medium. Ember can tell you about heat, fuel, and what would burn, Tide about flow, depth, and what the water has touched, Root about weight, fault, and what the stone holds, and Gale about pressure, scent, and what the air is carrying. The GM gives you a clear impression, not a map you could navigate by. Your element does not obey another creature's order, and it does not take their bargain. If someone tries to treat the element as theirs, it does not answer them. You may spend a Hope to turn that refusal into a Reach effect aimed at the interruption: the fire flares, the stone holds, or the water pulls away.
- **The Untended Element** *(Tithe)* — Place **3 tokens** on this card. At a long rest, refill all of them if you have spent Hope on Reach since your last long rest. Otherwise remove a token. Asking the element a question does not refill the clock. Spending Hope on Reach does. At 2 tokens nothing has changed, and at 1 token and at 0 the element starts speaking for itself, which the table describes.

| Element | **Reach** — *spend a Hope* | **1 token** (restless) | **0 tokens** (it lets go) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ember** *(fire)* | Send a burst of fire at a target within **Very Close** range. Make an attack roll (Instinct or Strength). On a success, deal **magic damage in d6s equal to your Proficiency** and the target is temporarily **On Fire**. | You run **hot** — disadvantage on action rolls needing a steady hand or a cool head (stealth, precision, composure). | You **flare** — mark a **Stress**; searing heat rolls off you and anyone within **Very Close** must give ground or work through it (scorched cloth, singed hair — no direct damage). Refill to 2. |
| **Tide** *(water)* | Move like water: breathe and swim freely as though water were air, flow out of a fall or a grapple unharmed, **or** slick the ground within **Very Close** — creatures there must succeed on an **Agility Roll** to move out of it. | You run **thin** — disadvantage on action rolls needing brute force or holding your ground. | You **flood** — mark a **Stress**; water and mud spread under you (**Very Close**), and you *and* everyone in it must make an **Agility Roll** to move out until it drains. Refill to 2. |
| **Root** *(earth/stone)* | Turn skin to bark and stone until the spotlight leaves you: **reduce the severity of the next damage you take by one threshold**, and gain **advantage** on rolls to resist being moved, knocked down, or Restrained. | You run **slow** — disadvantage on Agility rolls. | You **calcify** — mark a **Stress**; you become **Restrained** by your own stiffening body until you or an ally clears it with a successful action roll. Refill to 2. |
| **Gale** *(air)* | Move like wind: on a move, reach anywhere within **Close** range with no roll even under pressure; **or** a sudden gust slams a door, knocks something loose, or carries your voice clearly across a storm. | You run **wild** — disadvantage on action rolls needing careful aim or a steady stance. | You **gust** — mark a **Stress**; uncontrolled wind scatters every loose or unsecured thing within **Very Close** (dropped weapons, papers, smoke, dust — GM adjudicates). Refill to 2. |

Every consequence at 0 tokens lands on the Answered's own body, or on the air, the water, and the ground immediately around them. It never targets another creature directly, it never harms another creature directly, and it never bends anyone's will. Nobody is compelled, and nobody else pays the debt.

## The Tithe, in the world

The pleasant version is why the guilds are shaped the way they are: real, purposeful use of your element keeps you steady, so the Answered's apprenticeships run long and workshops (and, just as often, watch rotations, escort work, and firefighting crews) exist to give them a steady outlet. The scary edge is exactly why "wildcard" sticks as a label — one of the Answered kept idle, grieving, or benched too long is a genuine hazard to be near, and every guild hall has a story about the time someone's untended element finally let go in a crowded room.

## GM Notes

Keep every consequence inside the Answered and the ground, air, or water immediately around them. Never force an effect onto another creature's body, action, or will. Let the element the player chose become the character's whole voice at the table, because an Ember-Answered does not feel like a Root-Answered in a fight or in a quiet room. Spoken, not commanded is the inversion of the djinn story: they ask, they do not own a servant, and nobody else is the master of what answered them. One of the Answered who has gone deep into Tithe-debt and then walks into a crowded hall, a ship's hold, or a dry archive is a set-piece you build toward, not a surprise you spring.
