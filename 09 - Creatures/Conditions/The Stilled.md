# The Stilled

One of the Stilled can hold a moment still. A wound that is bleeding stops bleeding, a poison that is spreading stops where it is, and a blade already swinging hangs in the air a beat longer than the arm meant. The gorgon of the old stories turns a person to stone and leaves them there. That curse, inflicted on someone else as the point of the power, is not how this works. The stillness has to be paid for out of the Stilled's own body, and if they do not keep moving, the stillness starts to settle into them instead. Towns put them to work as peacekeepers, as surgeons, and as the menders who have to hold a struggling patient steady under the knife.

## The take

Peacekeepers want that gaze on a riot line, because a brawl that has been stilled is a brawl that did not turn into a massacre, and surgeons and medics want it in the theatre, because a patient held perfectly still is a patient who survives the cut. They are feared, and they are regulated by a guild, rather than simply revered. ~7% of all people live this way. The same gaze that stops a wound can, if someone misuses it, hold a person helpless in a fight, so a licence and a strict code decide who may still a thing, and for what reason. The snake-hair of the old folklore survives as something you can see, not as a rule on the card. The hair stirs, faintly, when something nearby has gone dangerously still, a held breath or a nocked arrow or a predator about to spring, and the people who do this work have learned to read that tell.

The Condition comes by both paths, and they do not produce the same person. About six parts in seven are Given. They drew the pale-stone leaf, marble-white, at the Awakening, when the [[Turning Tree]] gives a Condition at age 10, and they trained young under the guild, so they know how much stillness they can spend and how to keep moving until the spent stillness sheds, and they come to the table composed and professional. About one part in seven is Struck by a later threshold rather than a leaf. They survived being turned to stone, held rigid and unbreathing for however long the reversal took, and they came back with the stillness living in them, unpracticed and closer to the surface than any guild training would have left it.

## At the Tree

The leaf is pale stone, marble-white, a colour people know and fear, so the warden names it carefully when it falls, and a Struck Stilled has no leaf at all. Most were Given that marble at the ceremony and a few were Struck by surviving the turn to stone, and the canonical palette, with the account of how the Tree gives a colour, is on [[Turning Tree#Reading the colours (colour → Condition)]] and [[Turning Tree]].

[[The Leaf-Mother|Motherfaith]], the faith of the Leaf-Mother, hears a hard gift, and the gift is licensed because the same gaze that saves a life can leave a person helpless in a fight. [[The Watching]] names a seeing of stillness that the town then made civic, a witness rather than a mother's pick, and a Struck Stilled is a threshold the Motherfaith still argues over. [[The Old Ways]] take them back to the door that made them and name them kin.

From there they go to [[The Stillers]], who keep the strictest ticket, the infirmary, and the drill-yard. A newly Struck Stilled goes to [[The Intake]].

## Daggerheart — Transformation card

*You can hold a moment still. The stillness has to come from somewhere — and if you don't move it along, it settles into you.*

- **The Stilling Gaze** — You may still a wound, a spreading harm, or a struggle you can see. Nothing you still in this way is harmed by the stilling itself. Each use adds a Grey token, as The Grey describes below. You can keep one stilling at a time, and a new use lets the last one go.
  - A harm you can see, whether bleeding, poison, or disease, needs no roll. The harm pauses until you look away, use the Gaze on something else, or the scene ends.
  - A willing creature, or a creature that is down, needs no roll. They gain the Stilled condition, described below.
  - An unwilling creature requires an attack roll using Instinct or Presence. On a success they gain Stilled. A miss still adds a Grey token, because the stillness has already left you.
- **Stilled** *(special condition, not SRD Restrained)* — A Stilled creature cannot take actions or reactions. They can still speak, they can still perceive, and they can still be targeted. They are not Restrained. Other people can move them, and this effect does not by itself stop them from talking or from seeing. They may mark a Stress to clear Stilled at the moment they would act. The effect also clears if you look away, if you turn the Gaze onto another target, or if the scene ends. The same creature cannot gain Stilled twice at the same time.
- **The Grey** *(Tithe)* — The stillness you spend collects in you, and if you do not work it back out, it settles. Place Grey tokens on this card, starting at 0. This clock fills, and it does not drain. Add a Grey token each time you use the Stilling Gaze, and add one when you take a long rest that did not include vigorous exertion: a downtime move spent on labor, a spar, a drill, or a fight. Clear one token at the end of a scene in which you marked Stress from physical effort, or in which you dealt damage or took it. The stillness rises as the tokens do.
  - **1 token:** nothing has stiffened yet.
  - **2 tokens:** the grey creeps in. Your joints stiffen, and you have disadvantage on Agility rolls and on rolls to evade.
  - **3 tokens (full):** the stillness seizes. The next time you use the Stilling Gaze, or the next time you take damage, one of your own limbs locks stone-still and stays useless until it is tended and rested off. You describe which limb: an arm, a leg, your jaw. The GM may instead gain a Fear, and something small and nearby, a doorknob or a cup or a patch of ground, turns permanently to stone, collateral of the seizing. Afterward, clear back to 1 token.

The seizing only ever locks the Stilled's own body, or harmless scenery around them. It never petrifies another creature, and it never takes a player character's turn away from the player. Nobody's will bends, and nobody else pays the debt.

## The Tithe, in the world

The pleasant version of the Tithe is why the training looks the way it does. The Stilled drill hard, they spar often, and they stay physically busy almost as a matter of who they understand themselves to be, because every use of the gaze banks a little grey. The guilds build constant motion into the job itself, patrols and practice bouts and dance used as therapy, so the grey gets worked back out before it can settle. The frightening edge is why the licence exists. An unlicensed Stilled, or a careless one, who lets the Grey fill is a documented hazard, to themselves first of all. A doorknob turned to stone is one kind of accident. A hand gone still on a weapon is another, which is why the guild's oversight is strict rather than a ceremony people attend and forget.

## Getting Struck

The door is surviving being turned to stone. A trap, a curse, or an accident petrified them, and then they returned to flesh, however the reversal was managed. There is no leaf and no ceremony. The people watching call it a miracle, or a mercy, or a little unnerving, depending on who did the reversing. A Struck member of the Stilled usually needs a guild's help quickly, before an unmanaged Grey teaches them, in public, what a seizing feels like.

## GM Notes

Keep the seizing pointed at the Stilled. It locks their own limb, or it locks harmless scenery, and it never takes another player character's autonomy or forces an action. Stilled is not Restrained: Restrained only stops movement, while Stilled stops the creature from acting. Do not apply both unless some other effect has earned Restrained on its own. The unwilling Gaze needs a hit. A miss still costs the Grey token, because the stillness has already left the Stilled. A Struck Stilled's first use of the gaze, unpracticed, probably on someone they love and probably by accident, is material for the story of how they became this. It is not a punishment. A Given Stilled deep in Grey-debt, standing on a riot line or in an operating theatre, is a set-piece you build. It is not a surprise you spring.
