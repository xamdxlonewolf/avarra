# Far-Voiced

A Far-Voiced carries a voice with total emotional fidelity. It travels exactly as far as they mean it to travel, and it arrives exactly as true as they mean it, with no extra feeling poured in and none shaved off. The siren of the old stories sings the unwilling onto the rocks. This is not that song, and a Far-Voiced does not override anyone's will. The danger runs the other direction, into the singer, when a feeling they have been holding finally comes out of them whether they chose the moment or not. Towns build their relay-singers, their sworn witnesses, and the performers who fill a hall out of people who can do this.

## The take

Distance does not drown the voice, and neither does a storm, the noise of a battle, or water over the singer's head. The voice also never says less than the Far-Voiced means. That is why the signal network runs on them. Relay-singers take posts along coasts, roads, and border-towns, and a warning, an alarm, or an order in that voice travels faster and farther than any horn or drum. Courts and treaties want them as witnesses for a reason that cuts against the glamour. The moment a Far-Voiced is under pressure, their own voice betrays the feeling they actually hold, so a sworn word from them is trusted precisely because they are bad at lying once the strain is on. Concert halls want the same fidelity, because emotion delivered so the room can hear it, and delivered exactly, is the whole of the art. They are revered rather than feared, ~12% of all people. A Far-Voiced who has not mastered the channel is a genuine hazard to everyone in earshot, and that hazard is why the guild exists and why its training is what it is.

The Condition comes by both paths, and the two paths do not make the same person at the table. About eleven parts in twelve are Given. They drew the sea-blue leaf at the Awakening, the ceremony at which the [[Turning Tree]] gives a Condition at age 10, and they spent childhood in the guild, learning breath, projection, and how to vent a feeling on purpose before it vents itself, so they come to the table controlled, professional, and poised. About one part in twelve is Struck by a later threshold rather than a leaf, and the door is surviving drowning: they were pulled under, breath and voice both stopped, and both came back changed, raw and untrained, with the undertow already pulling before anyone had taught them how to swim it. It is the same Condition the guild trains, and it is not the same person at the table.

## At the Tree

When the leaf falls it is sea-blue, and people who have stood at a ceremony know that colour, though a Far-Voiced who came up from a drowning has no leaf at all. Most were Given the sea-blue, a few were Struck by surviving the water, and the canonical palette, together with the account of how the Tree gives a colour, is on [[Turning Tree#Reading the colours (colour → Condition)]] and [[Turning Tree]].

[[The Leaf-Mother|Motherfaith]], the faith of the Leaf-Mother, hears a gift of voice. If the person came up out of the water instead of receiving a leaf, the faith holds a quiet receiving-rite after the fact, the Wide-Hand saying she met them there and the Narrow-Hand uneasy. [[The Watching]] says the Tree, or the threshold itself, saw a voice that would carry. [[The Old Ways]] do not treat a drowning as a late gift from the Tree, and they name that person kin of the drowning-door.

[[The Hall-Keepers]] stamp the Voice-ticket that lets a Far-Voiced work a hall. A newly Struck Far-Voiced is sent to [[The Intake]] for help, before anyone has to restrain them.

## Daggerheart — Transformation card

*Your voice carries exactly as far, and exactly as true, as you mean it to.*

- **The Carrying Voice** — Your voice is never lost to distance, weather, noise, or water, and it lands with the emotional weight you intend. Spend a **Hope** to be heard clearly and understood by everyone you intend, across any distance or chaos in the scene, or to land a rally, warning, plea, or calming word with exact emotional force, and gain **advantage** on that roll.
- **The Undertow** *(Tithe)* — Place **3 Breath tokens** on this card. The clock is triggered by the scene, not drained by rest. When you mark Stress from feeling, or when the GM names grief, terror, rage, or joy as what is in you this scene, you must vent it before the scene ends. Sing, cry out, laugh, weep, something the room can hear. If you do not, remove a token.
  - **2 tokens:** nothing about the voice has changed yet.
  - **1 token:** the undertow pulls. Your voice starts leaking whatever you are actually feeling underneath the words you chose, and you have **disadvantage** on rolls to persuade, deceive, or perform a feeling you do not hold.
  - **0 tokens:** the next strong emotion you feel escapes as an involuntary Cry. Everyone within earshot marks a **Stress** as your raw feeling crashes into them, or the GM may instead gain a **Fear** when the scene is a crowd or already hostile. The Cry empties you. Mark a Stress yourself, then refill to **2 tokens**. It is out now, one way or another.

The Cry costs the listeners a Stress and costs the Far-Voiced a Stress as well, and it never compels an action, a belief, or a roll. What hits the room is an emotional blow rather than a command, and nobody's will bends under it.

## The Tithe, in the world

The pleasant face of the Tithe is why the jobs exist. Deliberate, controlled venting, in a performance, a ritual song, or a relay-call, is how a working Far-Voiced manages the channel, so the careers that need a big voice are also the careers that keep one healthy. The guild drills the breath, schedules the performance, and holds people to the discipline of singing it out before a shift ends, because an un-vented Far-Voiced is not merely unpleasant to stand beside. A Cry in a crowded hall, or in a command tent, is a real and documented hazard, and that hazard is the risk sitting under the reverence.

## Getting Struck

The door is surviving drowning. They were pulled under, breath and voice both stopped, and both came back changed. There is no leaf and no ceremony. People watching call it a miracle, or call it faintly uncanny, depending on who is watching. A Struck Far-Voiced usually needs a guild's help quickly, before they learn in public what happens at 0 tokens.

## GM Notes

Keep the Cry strictly a feeling that lands on the listeners. It costs Stress, and it is never a compulsion, never a forced action, and never a forced belief. A Struck Far-Voiced arrives with the Undertow raw and untrained, and you can use that as the drama of coming into the Condition. Their first Cry, before any guild has had them, is a formative beat of the story, not a punishment you assign. A Given Far-Voiced who has fallen deep into Tithe-debt, at 0 tokens, on duty in a courtroom or a relay tower, is a set-piece you build. It is not a surprise you spring.
