# Returned

A Returned is a person who died with something unfinished, and whose purpose keeps them walking. They are calm, they are deliberate, and they are unafraid of death because they have already been through it once. The corpse in the old stories rots and does not think. The Returned persist, mind intact, and the towns hand them the work that kills the living: the plague wards, the mine collapses, the recovery of a battlefield, and the tending of the dead, and the speaking for them.

## The take

The Returned are Struck only. You cannot be Given this at age 10, because the [[Turning Tree]] gives a Condition at the Awakening by the colour of a leaf, and this Condition requires a death. Someone dies with a thing left undone that is strong enough to hold them, and they get back up. Their standing is ordinary and quietly respected, ~7% of all people: solemn, dependable, the ones you send where the living cannot safely go. Gore does not shake them and death does not shake them, and death itself has trouble keeping them down.

## Away from the Tree

A Returned has no colour, because the Tree never Strikes and a death leaves no leaf. The only path is that death, with something left unfinished, and the canonical palette, including the no-colour rule, sits with the account of how the Tree gives every other Condition on [[Turning Tree#Reading the colours (colour → Condition)]] and [[Turning Tree]].

[[The Leaf-Mother|Motherfaith]] brings them to the Tree after the fact, for acknowledgment rather than for a Gift the Tree is handing out. The Wide-Hand says she met them at the death-door, and the Narrow-Hand is uneasy. [[The Watching]] says the threshold was always a place of seeing, and [[The Old Ways]] name them kin at the door that made them rather than calling the return a late gift. None of those faiths can close the question between them.

What comes next is the Purpose rather than a guild. Plague wards, mortuaries, and the other work that kills the living are part of [[Daily Life]], and [[Tithe-Infrastructure]] cannot furnish a Purpose, because there is no desk that issues one.

## Daggerheart — Transformation card

*You died with something unfinished. It keeps you walking.*

- **Unfinished Business** *(boon)* — You have already died once, and death does not hold you the way it holds other people. When you make a death move, you cannot choose **Avoid Death**. Your options are **Blaze of Glory**, **Risk It All**, and a fourth option this card gives you, **Refuse to Fall**. Avoid Death is the safe road in the SRD: you fall unconscious, you may take a scar, and you come back when an ally clears a Hit Point or the party finishes a long rest. Offering it here would make Refuse to Fall a worse version of a move every character already has, so it is off the list. When you Refuse to Fall, you go down and leave the current scene, and you pull yourself back together by the next long rest, which you describe. Coming back leaves a mark. You cross out a Hope slot as a scar. If that crossing would take your last Hope slot, you cannot Refuse to Fall. The unfinished thing finally lets go, and that death is true and final, which is the same scar rule the SRD already uses: crossing out your last Hope slot ends the character's journey. You may still choose Blaze of Glory, or gamble on Risk It All and die if the gamble fails. Refuse to Fall is the steady road back, and it is finite, because the scars count it. You are also unshaken by the dead and the dying. You automatically pass rolls to resist fear, horror, or despair that come from corpses, gore, and deathly places. That exemption belongs to this card alone. It is not the mind-lane on [[The Unbound|the Unbound]], and it is not Quiet.
- **The Tether** *(Tithe)* — Name your Purpose, the unfinished thing that holds you here, and write it on the card. While you can still act toward it, you are steady. If a long rest passes in which you did nothing in service of that Purpose, you begin to settle. You mark a Stress that cannot be cleared until you next act toward the Purpose, and your touch runs cold. A living creature you touch, or that touches you, feels a bone-deep wrongness and marks a Stress, and you have disadvantage on rolls to comfort, persuade, or charm the living until you act toward your Purpose again.

## Fulfilling your Purpose

When the unfinished thing is finally done, death's refusal gives way and you have the choice it denied you the first time. You may settle, lie down, and take the rest you skipped. Or you may lay the Condition aside and live out the rest of your days as a mortal, with no Condition at all. Taking up a new Purpose, instead of either of those, keeps you Returned and keeps you walking. That choice is a major beat for the character, and you make it with the GM.

## The Tithe, in the world

The Tithe is emotional as much as it is a rule on the card. A Returned is steady only while they have a reason, and if they neglect the reason they cool, in the temperature of the skin and in the way the living step back from them. That is why a touch can tell you a Returned is settling, and why the Returned build a life around the Purpose with a care that looks almost religious. The returns themselves are counted. Each Refuse to Fall burns a Hope slot, so a Returned who has walked a long time is a person you can watch running out of comebacks. Blaze of Glory stays available as the death they can choose. After a life of refusing rest, they can go out on their own terms, spectacularly, rather than on the last scar.

## GM Notes

Refuse to Fall means hard to keep down, and it does not mean immortal. Every use costs a scar, a Hope slot, exactly as any other character's brush with death costs one, so the road is finite and the table can watch the count fall. Going down still costs the scene. They are out of the current fight, and they only pull themselves together by the next long rest. When the last Hope slot would go, they cannot come back. That is the true death, and the tension of a death move is intact for a Returned who is near the end of their scars. Do not offer Avoid Death as a courtesy, and do not house-rule it back in as one more option on top of the card. The Returned already died, and the safe unconsciousness is what they spent when they did. A Purpose allowed to lapse is pressure for the story, not a punishment you assign. A Returned drifting toward settling is a character in a quiet crisis.

On this side of the screen, the hand has a name. Per [[The Other Hands]], a Returned is Struck by the Quiet Tally, Threnvaeth, the household clerk of leftover purpose, and she acts under the Leaf-Mother's allowance. She does not Give this Condition at the Tree, and she authorizes the catch when the death comes with work unfinished. Do not put that name, or that clerk, into anything a player reads. The unfinished business is still the Returned's own, and Threnvaeth does not puppet the Purpose.
