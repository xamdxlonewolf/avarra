# The Taken-In

A Taken-In is a person the wild took partway into itself and then gave back changed. Bark shows at the knuckles, moss grows in the hair, and somewhere on the body there is a spread of antler or a knot of root, and green things lean toward them when they stand still. The leshy of the old stories is a trickster that leads travelers in circles until they die. There is no haunting here, and no luring. The wild lives in them now, and it answers them, which is why the hard land gets worked at all. Farms, forests, herds, and the wardens of a frontier run on people the wild has already taken in.

## The take

One of the Taken-In can coax a thing into growing, read the wild, and move through it without being seen and without losing the way. That is why the frontier runs on them. A Taken-In steward makes thin soil yield, keeps a herd alive through a bad winter, walks a forest without ever losing north, and knows a day ahead which way the weather will turn. They are respected, and they are looked down on, and they are ~13% of all people. Cities and their guilds need the grain, the timber, and the wardens the Taken-In provide, and the cities eat well because of that work, but a Taken-In comes to town dirt-handed and backcountry, and no longer quite human to look at. The bark and the antler unsettle people who have never left the paved districts. Farm country that is prospering reveres them. Polite society in the city finds them a little coarse, a little uncanny, and keeps them at arm's length.

Both paths lead here, and they do not make the same person. About twelve parts in thirteen are Given. They drew the copper-green leaf, the colour of verdigris, at the Awakening, when the [[Turning Tree]] gives a Condition at age 10, and they grew up inside the change. A farm child or a forest child is comfortable in that half-wild skin, because the change came on gently and everyone around them already knew what it was. About one part in thirteen is Struck. They were lost in the deep wild, and they were dying there, days without a path and ready to lie down, and the wild took them in rather than let them die, remaking them partway so they would live. They come back more uncanny than a Given cousin, more overgrown, and quietly unsure whether they walked out as a person or as something the forest decided to send.

## At the Tree

The leaf is copper-green, the metallic green called verdigris, and people know it only if they have learned not to confuse it with the hug. The Tree's own summer-green is the colour of a child it holds and does not name, and this green is not that colour. A Struck Taken-In has no leaf, most of them were Given the verdigris, and a few were Struck by being lost and dying in the deep wild. The canonical palette, with the account of how the Tree gives a colour, is on [[Turning Tree#Reading the colours (colour → Condition)]] and [[Turning Tree]].

[[The Leaf-Mother|Motherfaith]] hears a gift of green, which people in the city call a little coarse. [[The Old Ways]] call them the grove's own children, the land making its own people whether or not the Tree had a hand in it, and a Struck Taken-In is, to them, the oldest door rather than a late embarrassment. [[The Watching]] names a seeing, and does not call it a taking-in performed by a mother.

What happens next is civic work rather than a college. [[Tithe-Infrastructure]] treats the green as a utility the town uses, not as a school that trains it, and [[The Greens-Keepers]] will hire the skill for the lot while refusing a Taken-In a seat at the high table.

## Daggerheart — Transformation card

*The wild took you in and gave you back green. It still lives in you — and it needs living things around it.*

- **The Green Word** *(boon)* — Growing things heed you. You have **advantage** on action rolls to forage, track, navigate wild land, calm or read an animal, or find your way, and you never become lost in a natural place. In the moment, the green answers, and each of these costs a Hope.
  - Spend a Hope and roots, vines, and brush erupt from any natural ground within Close range. Make an attack roll with Instinct or Strength against a target there. On a success, deal physical damage in d6s equal to your Proficiency, and the target is temporarily Restrained.
  - Spend a Hope while you are in any natural cover, brush or trees or tall grass or roots or soil, to draw that cover around you and become Hidden.
  - Spend a Hope to force a sudden useful growth: a branch that bridges a gap, a screen of thicket, a ladder of root, a burst of forage that is already ripe. The GM sets how large the growth can be.
- **The Rooting** *(Tithe)* — Place **3 Green tokens** on this card. At a long rest, refill all of them if you can touch a living plant where you rest, whether that plant is soil, a tree, a field, or a green in a pot. Otherwise remove a token. The test is yes or no. Open sky with no plant in reach does not count. A window-box does.
  - **2 tokens:** nothing has wilted.
  - **1 token:** you wilt. Your green browns and sheds, and you have disadvantage on action rolls to resist fear, despair, or exhaustion, because the wild in you is starving and it drags at you.
  - **0 tokens:** you go to seed. Mark a Stress. You are Vulnerable until you next touch living land, and the wild reaches for any living thing it can find. Weeds crack the flagstones, a beam sprouts leaves, roots buckle the floor, and the room around you goes riotously overgrown. The GM may instead gain a Fear if the scene is already tense. Reach living land and refill to 2 tokens.

The growth that comes when you go to seed erupts from the ground and from the structures around the Taken-In. It never entangles another creature, it never targets one, and it never harms one directly, and it never bends anyone's will. Nobody is compelled, and nobody else pays the debt.

## The Tithe, in the world

The pleasant version of the Tithe is why the Taken-In live where they live, because the work is the upkeep. A life spent on soil, among things that are growing, under open sky, keeps the green in them fed without anyone having to think about it. The Tithe bites when a Taken-In is pulled off the land: a long voyage by sea, a stretch in a gaol of stone, a season stuck inside a walled city on a lord's business. Old custom on the frontier is to send them home before they wilt. A city host who must keep one indoors learns to fill the room with potted green and to open the windows, a courtesy that is also how you keep your guest from going to seed on the parlour floor.

They also live in the city, and that life is not a contradiction of the frontier one. A copper-green leaf falls at a city Tree, where family and a hearth-witness keep the child, and grain-hands winter on the quay. [[The Greens-Keepers]] hire the green-skill for the lot and still will not sit them at the high table, and a rare Struck door opens in a walled grove. City pay covers what it costs to keep the Tithe and does not cover what it costs to leave, which is the gap that makes them easy marks for [[The Slide]], while polite districts keep them at arm's length for the bark, the antler, and the dirt. The fuller account of the money is on [[Economy and the Tithe#Why Taken-In live in stone cities]]. Their share of the world is ~13%, a stone city has fewer of them, and most of those work on the greens.

## GM Notes

Keep the going-to-seed strictly environmental. Growth bursts from the ground and from the structure of the room. Roots do not seize another creature, and they do not force an action. A Struck Taken-In's uncertainty, the question of whether they walked out or were sent, is material for a long game. Play it as a quiet dread about who they are, not as a string someone else is pulling, because the wild in them is part of them and not a master. One of the Taken-In dragged deep into a dungeon, or down into a ship's hold, or left inside a stone keep to wilt, is a set-piece you build, not a surprise you spring.
