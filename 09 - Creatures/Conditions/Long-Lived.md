# Long-Lived

A Long-Lived is an ageless keeper of memory, and they live on what they learn. The vampire of folklore feeds on the living, and a Long-Lived who is starved does not hunt anyone. They fade, they forget, and if the hunger runs all the way out they empty, the memories gone and the life with them. What is left of the old blood-hunger is an echo, and only an echo: a Long-Lived can read a memory in a single drop of blood, and by an ancient courtesy they will not cross a threshold uninvited. You can still recognize the old story in those two habits, and the predation has been taken out of both. Courts, guilds, and houses of record build themselves around these people, and use them as archivists, judges, notaries, and libraries that happen to be alive.

## The take

Aging simply stops, and nothing a Long-Lived has witnessed is ever forgotten, which is why a court will trust them as a record that does not corrupt. They are scarce on purpose: ~2.5% of people are Long-Lived, and every one of them is Given, never Struck. The [[Turning Tree]] gives a Condition at the Awakening, at age 10, when a leaf's colour names it, and the leaf that names a Long-Lived is a rare deep red, the colour of wine, with no later door. The shares for every Condition are on [[Conditions]], and a world with too many deathless people would ossify. They are revered, they are consulted, and they stand a little apart from the neighbors who will not live as long.

**The old courtesy.** A Long-Lived will not cross the threshold of another's home uninvited — they wait to be asked in, always. It is held as the deepest good manners, a mark of their trustworthiness (a keeper of records does not intrude), and no one quite remembers it as anything else — but it is old, and it is universal among them, and a Long-Lived who is *refused* the door feels the refusal more than they will say. It reads, faintly, as the last shadow of the thing the folklore vampire was: the one who could not enter unbidden. Nobody calls it that. It's simply how the Long-Lived have always been.

## At the Tree

The leaf is a deep red, wine-dark, and a neighbor who has seen the ceremony can already read it. The path is Given only, with no later door that Strikes a person into this, and the canonical palette, together with the account of how the Tree gives a colour, is on [[Turning Tree#Reading the colours (colour → Condition)]] and [[Turning Tree]].

[[The Leaf-Mother|Motherfaith]], the faith of the Leaf-Mother, hears a chosen keeper, a memory fitted to a town that needs a record. [[The Watching]] says the Tree saw a mind that would not fade, and calls that person a witness rather than a mother's pick. Those two readings do not get to close the argument between them.

From the ceremony they go to courts, guilds, and archives. Every Long-Lived is asked once to join [[The Pourers]], who give memory into a cup they say holds a god they made ([[The Unspent]]). A member gives one year of memory at a time, each year on its own night with its own minutes, and may stop before the next. Five years is what the circle asks of a member in all. For each year given, that year's papers go onto the long-house shelf and the circle counts the member one year younger. When a member leaves, the years already given stay in the cup, and the papers are what come back. Most Long-Lived say no to the ask, and a person who says no is received everywhere as before.

## Daggerheart — Transformation card

*Ageless keepers of memory, law, and record — they live on what they learn.*

- **Unfading Mind** — Your aging has stopped, and you forget nothing you take in. You have **advantage** on rolls to recognize, appraise, or recall what you have personally witnessed. Spend a **Hope** to recall a witnessed detail complete: the vow as it was spoken, the face, the exact count. The Hope pays for that perfect record. It does not pay for the ordinary work of a librarian.
- **Blood-Read** — Memory rides in blood. Taste a single drop of a creature's blood, freely given or taken from a wound that is already there, and you read a true memory carried in it, a fragment of what that person has lived. The GM chooses the fragment so that it fits what the blood remembers. It is never predation, and it never harms the donor. One drop gives one glimpse, and the older or more guarded the memory you reach for, the less of it you may get. This is the Long-Lived's quiet inheritance from the old vampire story. They feed on knowledge, and blood is the oldest record of all.
- **The Hunger to Know** *(Tithe)* — Place **3 Memory tokens** on this card. At a long rest, refill all of them if, since your last long rest, you spent Hope on Unfading Mind, used Blood-Read, or spent a downtime move in study or in first-hand witness. Otherwise remove a token. The advantage you always have does not refill the clock. Using the keeper's tools does.
  - **2 tokens:** nothing has begun to slip.
  - **1 token:** the fade begins. You have **disadvantage** on rolls to recall, recognize, appraise, or use Presence, and you start losing older memories, which you describe as they go. Other action rolls and reaction rolls are untouched.
  - **0 tokens:** you are starving. You are **Vulnerable**, and at the start of each long rest that you are still at 0 tokens you mark a **Stress**. If you would mark your last Stress this way, mark a **Hit Point** instead. If you would mark your last Hit Point this way, you do not make a death move. You go still. The memories are gone, the mind is emptied, and that stillness is the true death of the Long-Lived. You may clear Hit Points and Stress as usual while you still have them. Getting a token back, by a Hope spent on recall, a Blood-Read, or a downtime spent in study, stops the starve before the next long rest.

## The Tithe, in the world

The pleasant version of the Tithe is why they hold the jobs they do. A Long-Lived wants to keep learning, so scholarship, judging, and the keeping of records are careers that medicine the hunger while the work gets done. Deprivation is the danger: a prison, a siege where nothing new ever happens, exile in a place with nothing to witness. Isolating a Long-Lived is a recognized cruelty for that reason.

## The sink (why they don't overrun the world)

Long-Lived can have children, and they can have families, but no child of theirs is guaranteed the Condition, and most children do not inherit it. They watch a spouse grow old and die, and they watch the children do the same, and then they either stop, living on alone with the memory of that house, or they begin again and outlive the next family too. That repeating funeral is the deepest Tithe, underneath the tokens on the card. They are deathless, and they are not unkillable. Violence ends them, accident ends them, and a failed Tithe ends them, which is what keeps the numbers stable across the centuries.

## GM Notes

Going still is intentionally not a death move. The player can see it coming across long rests: Stress, then a Hit Point, then the stillness, when the memories are gone and the person dies. It is not a sudden kill, and it is not a spiral of Hit Points that cannot be cleared. Use it as pressure in the story, a Long-Lived found statue-still in a sealed archive, and not as a trick. The fade is a mind thinning. It is not a curse lying on every roll the character makes.

Blood-Read and the threshold are the two quiet callbacks to the vampire, recognizable and never the hunting cliché. Keep Blood-Read from becoming predation. One drop, freely given or from a wound that already exists, and a glimpse of memory that you narrate. It is never a feeding, never damage, and never a way to drain a person. It is a tool for investigation and for feeling: the dead man's last sight, the reason a witness is lying. It is not a power for a fight. The threshold custom is roleplay and the texture of a household. It is not a compulsion, and it is not a weakness an enemy can exploit on the sheet. A Long-Lived can cross a threshold uninvited if they truly must. They simply never do, and the manners run that deep. Do not let a table turn either habit into "the vampire rules."
