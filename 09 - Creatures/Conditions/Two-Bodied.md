# Two-Bodied

A Two-Bodied carries a second, animal self, and that self is always theirs to command. The werewolf of the old stories is a beast that overtakes a person when the moon is up. There is no moon-curse here, no rage, and no moment in which the person stops being the one who decides. What used to be told as a monster is, in this world, a useful animal partner you carry inside you. It is the common Condition, the one towns put to work.

## The take

A Two-Bodied has two bodies, the shape they were born in and one specific real animal, and shifting between them is voluntary and clean. That is what makes them the most common labor in the setting, and the most versatile, ~18% of people, Given only, when the [[Turning Tree]] drops a storm-grey leaf at the Awakening and a child of age 10 comes away with a second self. Rescue, herding, work in the wild, war, and the runs of couriers and scouts all use that second body. The point of the Condition is the utility, a shape suited to the job, rather than the spectacle of the change.

**The other self has a voice.** The animal shape is not an empty suit you climb into — it carries its **own personality, temperament and inner voice**, distinct from your born self, and you *converse with it*. Not aloud as a person (the animal mouth can't form human speech) but **in your own head**: a running second opinion — the wolf that wants to run the scent *now*, the hawk that trusts height over talk, the otter that finds the whole meeting tedious. It is named, it is known ("that's Bramble, her otter"), and townsfolk and players alike come to recognize its moods. **It never takes the wheel** — the change and every action stay yours to command (see the Tithe and GM Notes). What it does is *nag, want, and color* — a live companion you happen to be, giving the second body a real character and voice at the table.

The Condition runs in families, so the children of a Two-Bodied parent are far more likely to be Given it at their own Awakening, and usually as the same animal, which is why whole bloodlines are known as wolf-folk, hawk-folk, or otter-folk. A player character still picks their own animal, and someone who is first in the line, or who breaks the family type, is notable to the people around them.

Your other self is a real, natural animal, roughly between a housecat and a large wolf, a bear, a boar, or a horse in size, and nothing larger than that band, and nothing out of myth. You pick the shape, and with the GM you pick its one signature capability, which the card below describes. The rule is simple enough to say across the table: a real animal does exactly what that animal really does, so a hawk flies, an otter swims and holds its breath, and a bear is strong. The hard limits are the size, housecat-to-bear, never a giant hawk or a dire wolf, and the ban on myth, so there is no roc and no kelpie. The lines between this Condition and the other Kinds hold because of what the animal body lacks, not because anyone clips the hawk's wings. In your other self you have no hands, no person's mind at work, no speech as a person, and no tools. A flying Two-Bodied is a superb scout and a superb courier, and is still not a [[Tengu]], because a Tengu is a person aloft, with hands to carry and to fight and a human mind and a human voice in the air. An aquatic Two-Bodied swims brilliantly and works the water as a beast, not as a tool-using [[The Answered|Answered]] and not as a true amphibious person. The body is a magnificent animal, and it is not a person wearing fur.

## At the Tree

Storm-grey is already a proverb by the time a child watches it fall, because this is the common leaf and people know what it means before the warden speaks. The path is Given only, with no later door, and the canonical palette, together with the account of how the Tree gives the leaf, is on [[Turning Tree#Reading the colours (colour → Condition)]] and [[Turning Tree]].

[[The Leaf-Mother|Motherfaith]], the faith of the Leaf-Mother, hears the common gift, a second body the town can put to work. [[The Watching]] says the Tree saw two selves and made that seeing visible, a witness rather than a mother's pick.

After the ceremony there is no guild waiting and no college to enroll them. They range at herding, rescue, courier work, and scouting, with no ticket required, across civic land and ranging-commons that are described on [[Economy and the Tithe]] and [[Daily Life]]. What they are ranging against is on [[Dangers of the Turning]].

## Daggerheart — Transformation card

*A person who carries a second, animal self — not a beast that overtakes them.*

- **The Other Self** *(boon)* — You have a second body, a specific real animal, chosen when you become Two-Bodied, inside the band described above. You may shift between your two selves at will, as an action. The change is always yours to command. It costs no Stress and needs no roll, your gear melds with you, and the gear returns when you change back. In animal form you gain what follows.
  - **Natural build** — your animal's real gifts are yours: a natural weapon, in the table below, plus the beast's own movement, a wolf's run or an otter's swim or a hawk's flight. This is why the shift is worth making in a fight, and not only in order to leave one.
  - **A signature capability** — chosen with the GM to fit the animal, inside the band above. A hawk might see a mouse from the sky, a wolf might run a scent for miles, a bear might shrug off a blow. That is advantage on the fitting rolls, or a small effect once per scene that the GM sets. You start with one signature.
  - **An animal Experience** — choose one Experience that reflects the animal's nature, "Wolf's Nose" or "Cat's Balance" or "Bear's Strength", and use it only while you are in animal form.
  - **A voice, not a driver** — your other self has its own name, its own temperament, and an inner voice you can converse with, as the section above describes. The voice is pure roleplay. It flavors the scene and it nags, it grants no bonus of its own, and it can never take an action you did not choose. The spine of the Condition holds, and the body is always yours to command.

**Natural weapon** (animal form only):

| Category | Tier | Trait | Range | Damage | Burden | Feature |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary | 1 (does not advance) | **Instinct**, or **Strength** if the animal's signature is brute force (bear, boar) | Melee | **d8 phy** (housecat-sized: **d6 phy**; bear or boar: **d10 phy**) | One-Handed | **Natural:** cannot be disarmed; usable only in animal form; you cannot equip another Primary while in this form |

Roll damage dice equal to your **Proficiency**, as with any other weapon. You do not add a second weapon's dice on top.

- **Both Must Be Lived** *(Tithe)* — If a full day passes without time spent as your other self, it grows restless. The next time you roll with Fear, you shift into it whether you meant to or not, and you mark a Stress. You stay fully in control, and only the timing was not yours. You shift back in the ordinary way. This Tithe is not a clock that ticks at a rest. It rides the duality dice on purpose.

**Growth:** you start with one animal-form Experience and one signature capability. As you live longer as Two-Bodied, growth deepens the character of the animal, not the raw power of the body. Whenever you would gain an Experience, you may attach a new animal-form Experience to your other self, usable only in animal form. Your signature capability stays at one, with a single exception. A second signature is a once-ever milestone, never a routine pick. The GM grants it as the payoff of a genuine story, a season lived almost entirely in the other self or a defining trial passed in that shape, and that grant is the only way the count of capabilities ever grows. The limit is what keeps a long-lived Two-Bodied from quietly stacking scout, tracker, and bruiser into one body.

## The Tithe, in the world

Because the upkeep is simply being the animal sometimes, most Two-Bodied satisfy it without having to think about the rule. Their jobs already demand the animal form. The risk is the restless shift that comes when the day was skipped. It arrives at the worst moment, in the middle of a negotiation or in the middle of a stealth, it costs a Stress, and it never takes the wheel.

## GM Notes

Keep the forced shift about timing and inconvenience rather than about control. It is a nudge so that players keep living in both bodies, not a punishment that takes the character's choices away.

The other self is a partner in the roleplay, not a second player sitting at the table. It can want, argue, sulk, and celebrate. The wolf pushes to give chase, the cat refuses the cold water, and any of that is allowed. It never rolls, it never acts, and it never overrides a choice the player made. If a table ever reaches "the animal made me do it," the game has drifted off the spine of this Condition. Pull it back to nagging, and let the player decide. The forced shift, the Tithe, is the only moment in which the other self affects timing, and even then it does not choose the action. Encourage the player to give the animal a name and a voice that is not their own. That name is the cheapest richness this Condition has, and the richest.

The free shift, taken at will, and the natural weapon are deliberate. This is the commonest Condition, ~18% of people, and it used to be the thinnest one to play, so the boon is meant to be worth using in a scene and not a novelty you show off once. The guardrail is not a clipping of what the animal naturally does, since a hawk really flies and an otter really swims. The guardrail is the size, and the ban on myth, together with the fact that the animal form has no hands, no person's mind, no speech, and no tools. That is what keeps a Two-Bodied from eclipsing a Tengu, who is a person in the air, or the Answered, who use tools in the water. The Two-Bodied can out-animal either of them, and cannot out-person them. Hold that line when a player pitches an animal at the edge of the band. The count of capabilities is the other guardrail. One signature, and a second only ever by a milestone in the story, as Growth describes, so a veteran does not stack scout, tracker, and bruiser into one body. The natural weapon is a real Primary, with its own trait, its own die, its own burden, and its own feature. It does not stack with a weapon you are wielding, because you are one body or the other. The die does not rise when the character levels, while Proficiency does.
