# Two-Bodied

> **Reinvents the werewolf.** Not a beast that overtakes a person under the moon — a person who carries a **second, animal self** that is *always theirs to command*. No rage, no loss of control; the "monster" is reframed as a useful animal partner you carry inside you. The common workhorse Condition.

## The take

A Two-Bodied has two bodies: their born shape and a specific real **animal**. Shifting is voluntary and clean. This makes them the setting's most common and versatile labor force (~18%) — rescue, herding, wild-work, war, courier and scout work. Utility over spectacle: a second body suited to a job.

**Animal & inheritance:** the Condition runs in **families** — children of a Two-Bodied parent are far more likely to be Given it at their Awakening, and usually as the **same animal**. Whole bloodlines are "wolf-folk," "hawk-folk," "otter-folk." A PC picks their own animal (a first-in-the-line, or one who breaks type, is notable in-world).

**Choosing your animal (the band).** Your other self is a **real, natural animal** roughly between a housecat and a large wolf, bear, boar, or horse in size — no bigger, nothing mythic. Pick the shape and, with the GM, its **one signature capability** (see the card). Note the deliberate lane lines with other Kinds and Conditions, so Two-Bodied doesn't quietly out-do a whole ancestry: a **flying** animal (hawk, owl) gets *gliding and short flights*, not the sustained free flight of a [[Tengu]] or a Phoenix; an **aquatic** animal (otter, seal) can hold its breath long and swim superbly but isn't a true water-breather like a Ribbet or a Tide-[[The Answered|Answered]]; a **big** animal (bear, boar) grants strength and presence but not a Giant's reach. The animal can do what that animal naturally does, and can't do what it can't (no speech, no hands, no tools).

## Daggerheart — Transformation card

*A person who carries a second, animal self — not a beast that overtakes them.*

- **The Other Self** *(boon)* — You have a second body: a specific real animal (see *Choosing your animal* above), chosen when you become Two-Bodied. You may **shift between your two selves at will**, as an action — the change is **always yours to command**, no Stress and no roll (your gear melds with you and returns when you change back). In animal form you gain all three:
  - **Natural build** — your animal's real gifts are yours: a **natural weapon** (teeth, claws, antler, hoof) you can attack with, plus the beast's movement — a wolf's run, an otter's swim, a hawk's glide. This is why a Two-Bodied is worth the shift in a fight, not just out of one.
  - **A signature capability** — chosen with the GM to fit the animal, within the bands above: e.g. a hawk's *see a mouse from the sky*, a wolf's *run a scent for miles*, a bear's *shrug off a blow* (**advantage** on the fitting rolls, or a small once-per-scene effect the GM sets). One signature to start.
  - **An animal Experience** — choose one **Experience** reflecting your animal's nature (e.g. "Wolf's Nose," "Cat's Balance," "Bear's Strength"), usable only in animal form.
- **Both Must Be Lived** *(Tithe)* — If a full day passes without time spent as your other self, it grows restless. The next time you roll with **Fear**, you **shift into it whether you meant to or not, and mark a Stress**. You stay fully in control — only the *timing* isn't yours. Shift back normally.

**Growth:** you start with **one** animal-form Experience and **one** signature capability. Later, whenever you would gain an Experience, you may attach a new animal-form Experience *or* (with the GM) a second signature capability to your other self instead — the longer you live as Two-Bodied, the more your animal becomes second nature.

## The Tithe, in the world

Because the upkeep is simply *being your animal sometimes*, most Two-Bodied satisfy it without thinking — their jobs demand the animal form anyway. The restless forced-shift is the ~5%-scary edge: it strikes at the worst narrative moment (mid-negotiation, mid-stealth), costs a Stress, but never takes the wheel.

## GM Notes

Keep the forced shift about *timing and inconvenience*, never control. It's a nudge to keep players engaging with the dual-body fantasy, not a punishment that removes agency.

**On the free, at-will shift and the natural weapon:** these are deliberate — this is the *commonest* Condition (~18%) and it was the thinnest to play, so the boon is meant to be genuinely worth using in a scene, not a novelty. The **size/capability band** (housecat-to-bear, no true flight/water-breathing/giant-reach) is the guardrail that keeps it from quietly eclipsing a Tengu, a Ribbet, or the Answered — hold that line when a player pitches an edge-case animal. The animal's natural weapon uses the PC's normal attack; it doesn't stack extra damage dice on top of a wielded weapon (you're one or the other).
