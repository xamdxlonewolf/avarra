# A Hidden Phoenix

> **What this is.** These are the player-agency rules for a Phoenix who is not the public legend. On the open card, everyone knows who the Phoenix is, and towns already have an opinion before the party arrives. This page is the other way to sit in that same Condition. You carry scars you cannot name, the world thinks the one living Phoenix is in care, and fire still answers a name older than the one you use. The Transformation card is still [[Phoenix]], and nothing on this page changes that card.

> **Who reads this.** This page is a handout for the hidden-Phoenix player and the GM. It describes deaths that were prepared for you, and fire a house stored from a Rise, so the other players read it only when the table has agreed they should.

> **Whole table.** The consent rule is the same one the public card already uses, because a Phoenix campaign keeps arriving at fire. If anyone at the table does not want that, pick another Condition, or run the Phoenix as an NPC and let the player characters be someone else.

## Two legal shapes (pick one)

There is still **one Gift**, the single living Phoenix, and these are two ways to sit in that one slot. They are not two Phoenixes, and they are not a way to have two of the Gift alive at once.

| Shape | What you are | Typical start |
|---|---|---|
| **The walker** | You are the Gift that left a room where deaths had been prepared. You can still Rise, which means you can still come back from a death the way the Phoenix card allows, and you are the one. | You begin with Hope scars you cannot account for, deaths missing from the life you can tell, and a world that says the one living Phoenix is safe indoors. |
| **The unseen leaf** | You are a new Gift that fell where no Speaker, the warden who names what a leaf did, ever wrote it down. You can still Rise, and you are the one. | You begin with few scars, or none, while the stories people tell are still about an older name, and something in a far house still burns the way you do. |

Session zero is where the table picks the shape, and you do not pair this sheet to a named NPC as if that person's road were already the one you had walked.

If your next Rise would cross out your last Hope slot, the mark on the sheet that counts how many times you can still come back, you cannot Rise, and you are not a Phoenix player character on this card, because that last slot is the end of the Gift.

## Starting Hope scars

Before play, you and the GM agree a **count** of scars, not a biography, and you do not fill in the lives those scars came from.

- **Walker:** The working band is 1–3 scars, and each scar is a life you cannot name. You do not start on your last Hope slot, because that slot would be the end of the Gift, and then there would be no Rising left to play.
- **Unseen leaf:** The count is 0, or 1 if a later Rise already happened in a quiet year, and that shortness is on purpose.
- You write the scars on the sheet the way the card already writes them, as crossed Hope slots. The Dwindling, the countdown that spends a Hope slot each time you Rise, still runs from those marks.

You and the GM name **that the deaths are missing**, not what they were. "Three deaths I cannot see" is a start you can play. "I murdered my sister in a white room" is a plot someone else wrote, and you do not start with the murders filled in.

## Missing murder memories

The deaths that made the scars were **prepared**, which means someone arranged them, and the scenes did not come back with you when you rose. An ordinary Rise already costs a named piece of the old life, something you and the GM can point at, and these deaths took that piece and the dying itself, so you do not remember the room.

What you know at the start is optional. You pick any that fit, and none are required. You might know that you have more scars on the sheet than lives you can tell, or that someone kept you safe, or said that they had. You might know that fire answers a name that is not the one you use, or that a door opened that was not on the slate, the list of what was supposed to happen.

What you do not have to decide at session zero is who died, whether you chose those deaths, whether the keepers were kind, and whether you are the "real" one. Those stay later player calls, made when a fragment of one of the deaths actually lands in play.

## Fragment recovery

Play will sometimes offer a fragment. It might be a room, a smell, a name, or a Hope scar that suddenly has a face. When that happens, **the player chooses** which of these three to do.

1. **Take it.** You name what came back, and it is now yours. It can be ugly, it can be kind, and it can feel wrong and still be yours to keep.
2. **Refuse it.** The fragment stays in the world, and you do not have to take it in to be a good sport about the scene. A refused fragment can return later, and it does not get louder because you said no.
3. **Rewrite the meaning, not the fact.** If the table has established that a prepared room existed, you do not get to unmake the room. You do get to decide what it means that you walked out of it.

The GM offers the fragments, and does not assign their moral, so a fragment is not a cutscene that plays you while you watch. Recovery is not a clock you have to tick, and it is not a domain card, one of the class abilities the book hands you as you level. One fragment in a session is plenty, a session with none is allowed, and you do not turn the missing deaths into a mystery the campaign has to finish.

## What stored fire can show

Some houses keep fire from a Rise, the burning a Phoenix leaves when they come back. If you stand in that fire, or it stands in you, it can show **facts**. It cannot assign you a self, and it cannot tell you who you are supposed to be.

What it can show is a count of how many times a body burned in that room, and the layout of the prepared room, the yard, and the door that was not on the schedule. It can show a name you were called, or a name the fire still answers, and it can show that a death was arranged, which is the catch itself and not the motive of the people who arranged it. It can also show that the same fire knows a child and an old name at once.

What it cannot show is whether you should forgive anyone, whether a leftover body is "you," or what the Gift is *for*. It cannot show who the Leaf-Mother is, or whether she is, and it cannot show a second Gift, because there isn't one.

If the fire tries to tell you who you are, a keeper is the one talking, someone from the house that held you, and whether you believe them is a player choice.

## Truths that stay yours

These remain **player choices** for the whole campaign. The GM may pressure them in the fiction, and the GM may not decide them.

- **Whether you Rise** when a death move comes, the choice the book offers when a character would die. The card already says a Rise is never forced, and being hidden does not change that.
- **Which piece of the old life does not come back** on a Rise you chose to take. You name the piece, and the table does not name it for you.
- **Whether you want the missing deaths back** at all. Wanting them and refusing them are both ways to play the sheet, and the note does not pick one for you.
- **What you believe you are**, whether that is the one, a leftover, a fraud, or a story other people need. The belief can be false, and the Gift is still one, whichever story you are telling yourself.
- **Whether you use the public name.** A walker who furls the wings is still a Phoenix when they fly, and hiding is a tactic, not a second card.
- **Whether you walk back** to the house that kept you. The sheet does not send you there, and the house can wait until you decide.
- **What you do with anyone who opened a door**, whether that is gratitude, rage, or nothing. None of those is a reunion the note has already scripted.

The other players committed to a campaign that will arrive at fire, and they did not commit to your answers.

## What this page is not

- It is not a second Transformation card, so when you need the card, you use [[Phoenix]].
- It is not permission for two living Gifts, because there is one Gift, and this page does not open a second slot.
- It is not the campaign's opening, and the page still does not staple you to a named NPC, or to a road someone else already walked.
- It is not a licence to confirm the Leaf-Mother in play so that a scar has an explanation.

## GM Notes

- **Apparent two.** When the table thinks it is looking at two Gifts, what it has is leftover fire standing next to the one Gift, and that leftover is not a player character on this card.
- **Engine is [[When the Fire Is Caught]].** There is one Gift, and the original self ends at the death they cannot Rise from. Fire a house has harvested can force a Rise on a captive and take the memory of that death, and it can keep a remnant walking after the Gift has ended. A player character is never forced to Rise. If this player character can still Rise, they are the one Gift, and a remnant in the same campaign is leftover fire rather than a second card, and two Gifts at once is not legal.
- **Stored fire = facts, not meaning.** Use the list in the body of this note, and do not let banked fire lecture the player or confirm the Leaf-Mother. Do not let it name [[The Protectors]]' harvest in player-facing narration until the table is ready for that fight, and until then the public sentence is still that the one is in care.
- **Walker vs unseen leaf.** Both shapes are legal, and this opening uses the unseen leaf together with a remnant walker, with the kit on [[The Opening]] at [[Harrow's Green]]. [[Taeren]] holds the new-Gift seat, or a player character does, and not both. The walker on screen is leftover fire, not a player character on this card, so you do not staple the player character to Taeren's name, and you do not run a walker player character who can still Rise on top of Taeren. That would be a second Gift, and it remains a different opening.
- **Starting scars.** A walker begins with 1–3 scars, enough that the Dwindling is visible and the last Hope slot is still unspent, and zero for an unseen leaf is the right count. If you want a remnant and a player character in the same campaign, the remnant is the NPC and the player character is the new leaf, or the player character is the Gift that walked and the remnant is what the house still shows its guests, and you never put that the other way around on a player-character sheet. This opening, specifically, is a remnant NPC together with a new leaf.
- **Fragments are positional, not destined.** Offer them through rooms, through scars, and through other people's needs (`positional-revelation`): a guest's missing hour, a hush-letter, or fire that answers two names. You do not write the player character's Lie for them, and `character-arc` stays theirs.
- **Tone.** Keep the scare at ~5%, where the wrongness is a prepared death and a house that calls that death care. It is not a harvest-god, it is not a second cosmology, and it is not a Tithe that seizes the player character.
- **Public Phoenix vs this page.** If the table wanted the public legend, where everyone knows the Phoenix and every town already has an opinion, use the card's table note and skip this sheet. If they want the Phoenix hidden, the recognition tax is partial: furled wings pass, and flight and Kindle do not, so you do not run the public tax and the hidden tax both at full and call that fair.
