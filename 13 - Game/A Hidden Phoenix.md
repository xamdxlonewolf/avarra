# A Hidden Phoenix

> **What this is.** Player-agency rules for a Phoenix who is **not** the public legend. The open-card Phoenix is a fishbowl: everyone knows, towns already have an opinion. This page is the other seat — scars you cannot name, a world that thinks the one is in care, a fire that answers more than one mouth. The Transformation card is still [[Phoenix]]. This page does not change the card.

> **Whole table.** Same consent rule as the public card. If anyone does not want a campaign that will keep arriving at fire, pick another Condition or run the Phoenix as an NPC.

## Two legal shapes (pick one)

There is still **one Gift**. These are two ways to sit in that slot. They are not two Phoenixes.

| Shape | What you are | Typical start |
|---|---|---|
| **The walker** | The Gift that left a prepared room. You can still Rise. You are the one. | Hope scars you cannot account for. Missing deaths. A world that says the one is safe indoors. |
| **The unseen leaf** | A new Gift that fell where no Speaker wrote it. You can still Rise. You are the one. | Few or no scars yet. The stories are still about an older name. Something in a far house still burns like you. |

Session zero picks the shape. The campaign's opening — who walks, who stays, which town notices — is a later conversation. Do not pair this sheet to a named NPC as if the road were already walked.

If you cannot Rise (last Hope slot already gone), you are not a Phoenix PC. That leftover is not this card.

## Starting Hope scars

Agree a **count** before play, not a biography.

- **Walker:** 1–3 scars is the working band. Each scar is a life you cannot name. Do not start on your last slot — that would be the end of the Gift.
- **Unseen leaf:** 0, or 1 if a later Rise already happened in a quiet year. The count is short on purpose.
- Write the scars on the sheet the way the card already writes them: crossed Hope slots. The Dwindling still runs from here.

You and the GM name **that they are missing**, not what they were. "Three deaths I cannot see" is a start. "I murdered my sister in a white room" is a plot someone else wrote. Do not start with the murders filled in.

## Missing murder memories

The deaths that made the scars were **prepared**. Those scenes did not come back with you. Rise already costs a named piece of the old life; these deaths took the piece *and* the dying.

What you know at the start (pick any that fit; none are required):

- You have more scars than lives you can tell.
- Someone kept you safe. Or said they did.
- Fire answers a name that is not the one you use.
- A door opened that was not on the slate.

What you do **not** have to decide at session zero: who died, whether you chose those deaths, whether the keepers were kind, whether you are the "real" one. Those are later player calls, when a fragment lands.

## Fragment recovery

When play offers a fragment — a room, a smell, a name, a Hope scar that suddenly has a face — **the player chooses**:

1. **Take it.** Name what came back. It is now yours. It can be ugly. It can be kind. It can be wrong-feeling and still yours.
2. **Leave it.** The fragment stays in the world. You do not have to swallow it to be a good sport. A refused fragment can return later; it does not get louder because you said no.
3. **Rewrite the meaning, not the fact.** If the table has established *a prepared room existed*, you do not get to unmake the room. You *do* get to decide what it means that you walked out of it.

The GM offers fragments. The GM does not assign their moral. A fragment is not a cutscene that plays you.

Recovery is not a clock and not a domain card. One fragment a session is plenty. Zero is legal. Do not turn this into a mystery that must complete.

## What stored fire can show

Some houses keep fire from a Rise. If you stand in that fire — or it stands in you — it can show **facts**. It cannot assign a self.

It can show:

- A count (how many times a body burned in that room).
- A layout (the prepared room, the yard, the door that was not scheduled).
- A name you were called, or a name the fire still answers.
- That a death was arranged (the catch, not the motive).
- That the same fire knows a child and an old name at once.

It cannot show:

- Whether you should forgive anyone.
- Whether a leftover body is "you."
- What the Gift is *for*.
- Who the Leaf-Mother is, or whether she is.
- A second Gift. There isn't one.

If the fire tries to tell you who you are, that is a keeper talking. You may believe them. You may not. That is a player choice.

## Truths that stay yours

These remain **player choices** for the whole campaign. The GM may pressure them. The GM may not decide them.

- **Whether you Rise** when a death move comes. The card already says never force a Rise. Hidden does not change that.
- **Which piece of the old life does not come back** on a Rise you chose.
- **Whether you want the missing deaths back.**
- **What you believe you are** — the one, a leftover, a fraud, a story other people need. Belief can be false. The Gift is still one.
- **Whether you use the public name.** A walker who furls the wings is still a Phoenix when they fly. Hiding is a tactic, not a second card.
- **Whether you walk back to the house that kept you.**
- **What you do with anyone who opened a door.** Gratitude, rage, nothing. Not a reunion script.

The other players committed to a campaign that will arrive at fire. They did not commit to your answers.

## What this page is not

- A second Transformation card. Use [[Phoenix]].
- Permission for two living Gifts. Apparent two is leftover fire next to a Gift. The leftover is not a PC on this card.
- The campaign opening. Who notices, which town, which helper — later.
- A licence to confirm the Leaf-Mother in play to explain a scar.

## GM Notes

- **Engine is [[When the Fire Is Caught]].** One Gift. Original self ends at the death they cannot Rise from. Harvested fire can force a Rise on a *captive* and take that death's memory; it can keep a remnant walking after the Gift ends. **A PC is never forced to Rise.** If this PC can still Rise, they *are* the one Gift. A remnant in the same campaign is leftover fire, not a second card. Two Gifts at once is not legal.
- **Stored fire = facts, not meaning.** Use the list in the body. Do not let banked fire lecture. Do not let it confirm her. Do not let it name [[The Protectors]]' harvest in player-facing narration until the table is ready for that fight; the public sentence is still "the one is in care."
- **Walker vs unseen leaf.** Both legal. Epic 10 picks the opening. [[Taeren]] and [[Rosire]] are mouths, not this sheet. Do not staple the PC to those notes. Do not decide off-page whether the walker from the sanctuary was remnant or Gift so you can tidy a reunion.
- **Starting scars.** 1–3 for a walker keeps the Dwindling visible without spending the last slot. Zero for an unseen leaf is correct. If you want a remnant *and* a PC, the remnant is the NPC and the PC is the new leaf — or the PC is the Gift that walked and the remnant is what the house still shows guests. Never the other way around on a PC sheet.
- **Fragments are positional, not destined.** Offer them through rooms, scars, and other people's needs ([[positional-revelation]]): a guest's missing hour, a hush-letter, fire that answers two names. Do not write the PC's Lie for them. `character-arc` stays theirs.
- **Tone.** ~5% scary. The wrongness is a prepared death and a house that calls it care. It is not a harvest-god, not a second cosmology, and not a Tithe that seizes the PC.
- **Public Phoenix vs this page.** If the table wanted the fishbowl, use the card's table note and skip this sheet. If they want hidden, the recognition tax is *partial*: furled wings pass; flight and Kindle do not. Do not run both taxes at full and call it fair.
