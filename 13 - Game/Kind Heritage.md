# Kind Heritage

> **What this is.** These are the table rulings for Kind, the ancestry a person is born with, in The Turning. They say how the four custom Kinds sit beside Daggerheart's stock ancestries, which grant two features, and how Mixed Ancestry works when a custom package has three. They also say which Kind keeps the phrase "cannot be caught off guard." The fiction of hearths and of mixed children lives on [[Kinds of the Turning]]. Condition cards, the deal the Kept make, and the moment someone can be Struck during play live on [[At the Table]].

> The four custom packages keep all three of their features, and a stock character, whose features both come from the book's ancestries, takes a Hearth-Mark to make up the difference. Mixed Ancestry is allowed in the way the System Reference Document already allows it. A [[Yumboe]] player character needs the GM's leave and is always the full Kind, never a mix. The phrase "cannot be caught off guard" is issued once, on [[Tengu]], and the Mark is once per session, at +2.

## The power band

A stock Daggerheart ancestry grants **two** features, and each custom Kind written for this world grants **three**, so a stock character set beside one of those four looks weak, because the sheet is carrying less.

The decision is to keep the four three-feature packages as they were locked, and to make up the difference at the place that raised the character. You do not even the scales by cutting a Kitsune's fox, a Selkie's skin, a Tengu's wing, or the unseen hands of a Yumboe.

A character whose two ancestry features are **both from stock ancestries**, whether that is a single stock Kind or a mix of two stock Kinds, takes a **Hearth-Mark** at character creation.

A character who takes **any custom-Kind feature** does not take the Mark. For that character, the third feature is the hearth already written into the body.

Everyone still takes a **Community**, as the SRD requires. A Hearth-Mark is not a second Community, and it is not a third ancestry feature slipped in under another name.

## Hearth-Mark

Name the **place that raised you**. It might be a town, a quay, a ridge-road, a walk-hold, or a roll-street. You write it as a place-phrase, the way *Hallowquay salvage-child* is a Hearth-Mark, and not as a Kind-phrase, the way *Seal-blood* is not one.

**Once per session**, when you make an action roll or a reaction roll that leans on how that place taught you to live, you add **+2**. You do not spend Hope, the currency a player uses to push a roll, in order to add it. You may still spend Hope to add a relevant Experience on the same roll.

The Mark is not an immunity, it is not a power that spends Stress, and it is not a shopping list of tricks. If a phrase starts granting swim-breath, flight, or "cannot be caught off guard," it has become a feature; throw it back and write the place again.

You still take the two Experiences the SRD gives every character, and the Mark does not replace them.

## Feature order (for Mixed Ancestry)

The SRD's Mixed Ancestry rule takes the **top** feature, the first one listed, from one ancestry, and the **bottom** feature, the second one listed, from another. The custom Kinds have a third feature. That third feature is the **hearth feature**, the one that belongs to the full Kind, and it is not available when you are building a mix.

| Kind | Top | Bottom | Hearth (not mixed) |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[Kitsune]] | Slip the Frame | Fox's Glamour | Your Fox (Wilds / Snows / Sands) |
| [[Selkie]] | Second Skin | Slip the Hold | Seal-Kin |
| [[Tengu]] | Wind-Winged | Wind-Caller | The Mountain's Mood |

[[Yumboe]] is **not on this table**, for the reason in the section below.

A mixed Kitsune is fox-touched, which means they have Slip the Frame or Fox's Glamour, and they are not a Fox of the Wilds, the Snows, or the Sands unless they take the full Kind and the hearth feature that names which fox they are.

## Mixed Ancestry

The table allows what Daggerheart allows. Mixed Ancestry is a legal heritage on this world, an ordinary way to build a character, and you do not have to ask permission to use it. Two mainland Kinds, stock, custom, or one of each, mix by the SRD rule. The households those children grow up in are on [[Kinds of the Turning#Mixed-Kind children]].

You build one like this.

1. Take **one Top** from one parent Kind and **one Bottom** from the other. Use the table above for the three mainland customs, and use the printed card order for a stock ancestry.
2. Take **no** hearth feature from either custom Kind. The third feature stays with the people who took the whole package.
3. Take **no** Hearth-Mark if either of the features you chose is custom. Take a Hearth-Mark if both features are stock.
4. If both chosen features grant the same verb, fly and fly, or swim and swim, you keep one of them and take the unused listed Top or Bottom from either parent instead. You do not stack the same verb twice and call it a mix.
5. Write both sides on the body. That might be ears and webbing, or wings and a fox-brush, or whatever the pairing actually is. The visible mix is the mix, and it is not a hidden tell waiting to become a third feature.

You do not take three features from one custom Kind and then add a stock feature on top, and you do not take two hearth features. Community is still the SRD card it always was, and this ruling does not rewrite it.

Kind still stacks with a Condition. A character of mixed heritage Turns at the Tree, or is Kept, or is Struck, the same as anyone else.

## Yumboe (not a mix partner)

A [[Yumboe]] character needs the GM's **leave**. They are mythic-rare off [[The Sundering Isle|Kumbaan]], the isle on the far side of the storm-wall, so a mainland campaign that seats one has already decided that the wall let someone through.

If the table says yes, that character is the **full Kind**: Hollow-Hill, Moon-Waked, and The Unseen Hands, all three, and they take no Hearth-Mark. You do not split them into a Top and a Bottom, and you do not pair a Yumboe feature with a stock feature or with a mainland custom feature.

A child of a Yumboe and anyone else is either a full Yumboe, with the same GM leave, or the other parent's Kind, with at most a story-tell, a detail in the biography and not a feature on the sheet. They are never a mechanical mix, and the good people do not come in halves.

## One keyword, three lanes

**Cannot be caught off guard** is issued **once**, on [[Tengu|The Mountain's Mood]], and it applies only to environmental hazards and to ambushes that come out of the terrain.

The other three customs keep the fiction they already had, and they do it without that phrase.

- [[Kitsune]] never had the keyword: the Sands are one of the three foxes on the table above, and what they do is hear, which does not cancel a surprise.
- [[Selkie|Seal-Kin]] is a warning the sea names, together with aid from kin and a move the character can make. What you receive is information and help, and you do not receive an immunity.
- [[Yumboe|Hollow-Hill]] feels a gait, and the number of people making it, through the ground, which is a tremor-sense and not an immunity to being caught off guard.

## What this note is not

This note does not rewrite Community. Ridgeborne, Seaborne, Wildborne, and the rest of those cards stay what they are, the environment a character comes from. The Hearth-Mark is a place, one town or quay or road, and it is not a Kind-nation. How a Kind's culture actually passes from one kitchen to the next is the other subject, and it lives on [[Kinds of the Turning]]. The Kept deal, the timing of a Struck becoming during play, and the way a Condition advances are not on this page either. They live on [[At the Table]].

## GM Notes
*(Stripped on player-safe export.)*

- **The Mark's number.** The once-per-session +2 is the working number, and it is safe to retune if play finds that stock characters still feel thin beside the custom packages. Retuning the number does not reopen the two-layer model, Kind and Condition, and it does not reopen the decision that hearths are places rather than nations.
- **Why the packages were not trimmed.** The four three-feature packages are locked ancestry design, and Story R.3 offered a choice between trimming a feature and giving stock characters a hearth benefit. Trimming would have changed a locked package, so the Mark is the other door, deliberately weaker than a third feature: once per session, +2, and only when the fiction of that place applies. If play still finds stock characters thin, raise how often the Mark can be used, or write a second worked example of a place-phrase, and do not start cutting Foxes.
- **Do not let the Mark clone a Community.** If a player writes *Seaborne but free*, ask them for the name of the quay. The Mark has to be a place you could walk to, not a second copy of a Community card with the cost taken off.
- **Mixed Ancestry is the SRD rule, on purpose.** Do not walk it back to a world where everyone is one Kind unless someone asks for an exception, because mainland mixes are ordinary at this table. The two-layer model still holds while you do that: Kind is what you are born, and born-mixed counts as born, while Condition is what you become.
- **Yumboe stay whole.** The lock from 2026-08-30 is that there is no Yumboe player character without GM leave, and that a seated Yumboe is the complete three-feature Kind, never a mix. A child of a Yumboe and a mainland parent is a full Yumboe or the other parent's Kind, not a Top and a Bottom split between them. Do not invent an in-world blood-purity doctrine to explain that. The good people are overt and unmistakable, halves would make them a costume, and distance together with the storm-wall are enough.
- **Do not write "pure blood" into player-facing notes.** The lock is the full Kind, and it is not a racial law.
