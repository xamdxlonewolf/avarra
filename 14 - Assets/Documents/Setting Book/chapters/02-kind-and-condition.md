# Kind and Condition

Every person in the Turning carries two truths at once. You are born a **Kind**, the ancestry and the body that come with it: a Human, an Elf, a Drakona, or another ancestry the game already has, or one of the four this world adds. Kitsune are the fox-born. Selkie are born to a sealskin. Tengu are the feathered people of the high country, and the wings are theirs from birth. Yumboe are the hill-folk of Kumbaan, about waist-high to a mainlander, ordinary on that isle and a sailor's tale everywhere else. A Yumboe character needs the GM's leave, and is always the full Kind. Nobody blinks at a fox-tail in a Maiethorn market or at wings on a Heskoren ridge. No Kind keeps a country of its own. The place that raises you is the rest of who you are.

**Condition** is what you become after that birth. Something happens, and the person changes into what the old stories would have called a vampire, a werewolf, or their kin, so the change is a becoming, a thing you cannot be born into. Roughly three people in four acquire a Condition over a lifetime. Towns meet it with law, with a guild, with medicine, and with the work attached to it, because the society is built around what those people can do and around helping them live with the cost.

## How a becoming arrives

Which path a person took is the main division in society. A Condition arrives by one of two paths, or the Tree sees the child and leaves them as they are.

In a child's tenth year the family brings them to a **Turning Tree** for the **Leaf-Fall**, held in Turning-Week, the midsummer week of the solstice. There is one origin Tree, the Awakening Tree, named **Thaeloren** in the old liturgical tongue and worn down in everyday speech to the Thallow. It stands on Maiethorn. Most towns have a **scion** in the square, a living cutting of that same wood. In ordinary practice the cut is taken from a healthy town Tree cutting on the origin's behalf, and sometimes from Thaeloren itself. Either way it is cut tissue, kept alive on the road and planted, not a seedling from a seed the Tree dropped. A cutting that has just gone into the ground needs a generation, about twenty years, before it can hold a Leaf-Fall, and until then the children go to a neighbour, as they do from a town that is too new, too remote, or has lost its Tree. The leaves fall and wrap each ten-year-old. A tree-warden, the clergy who tend that wood, says aloud what the Tree did. In a village the town itself is the witness. In a city the children stand by hearth, among people who already know them, across the days of the week, and the square is still full.

When a colour falls, a Condition is **Given**, and the colour is the name of which one. Ordinary people can already read the common colours. A rare fall, or a colour the square has never seen, belongs to the warden and to the texts. The day is ceremonial, expected, and what people call proper, so a Given child grows up knowing the life ahead and is trained and guilded toward it. When no colour falls and the leaves hug the child, that child is one of **the Kept**. The Tree keeps them as they are, wanted and kept whole. The faith calls the hug a blessing, and that reading is still argued. The Tree only Gives or Keeps, and a Struck Condition happens somewhere else, with no leaf.

A child who never stood, because the week was missed, or the family refused, or the only wood on offer could not Turn anyone, is **unTurned**. From the street that life can look like a Kept life, since neither one carries a Tithe and neither one carries a guild. Kept means the town watched the hug, and unTurned means there was no hug to watch, so the law does not let a neighbour's kindness rename one as the other. The doors by which a person can still be Struck remain open later.

Later, some people are **Struck**. Some Conditions cannot be Given to a child, because a ten-year-old cannot die and walk back, and a child cannot swear a contract. Those arrive only when a person crosses a threshold in a later year, with no leaf and no ceremony, and the threshold they cross decides what they become. Onlookers call it tragic, miraculous, or a little suspect. Some of the people the Tree hugged are Struck in the years after, so a Kept life is not a door that has shut forever.

The Tree and the thresholds sort the roster the same way every time. What needs no act from the child is Given only. The **Long-Lived**, whom the old tales call vampires, are rare and revered, aging stopped, a witnessed life never forgotten, while the **Two-Bodied**, the werewolves, are the common neighbours, a second animal self that the person still commands. The **Answered**, the djinn, also called a jinni, were answered by an element, with no lamp and no master, and they have no single standing, because which element answered and what temperament came with it changes the neighbour you actually meet. The **Phoenix** is Given the same way, and only one is alive in the world at a time. They burn down to ash and rise again on wings of living fire, each death taking a piece of who they were, which is a different thing from the Long-Lived, who do not die of age. When the last of those lives is spent, the Tree gives a white-fire leaf, opal in the texts, to a single child, somewhere, and the next Phoenix begins. Almost nobody now living has seen that colour fall. Whether a player carries it is a decision the whole table makes, and the person running the game may keep the Phoenix as a person in the world instead. That conversation sits in Playing in the Turning.

What a child physically cannot have done is Struck only. The **Returned** are what the old tales called zombies: a person who died with something unfinished and was brought back by that purpose, once, which is a different door from the Phoenix, who dies outright and rises through the lives still left. The **Bound** are what the old tales called demons: a person who swore a contract. An **Infernis** is a person born demon-blooded, which is a Kind, and if that person swears a contract, the contract is the Condition. The two sit on the same person without becoming the same thing.

A near-death a person can survive may be foreseen by the Tree or walked into later by accident, and three Conditions come by either door. The **Far-Voiced**, whom the old stories call sirens, meet theirs in the water. Most drew a sea-blue leaf at ten and spent childhood in the guild, learning to let a feeling out on purpose, while a few were Struck, pulled under and brought up untrained, so that someone who survived a drowning at thirty carries the same becoming as the child with the blue leaf and is a very different person. The **Stilled**, the gorgons of those stories, can hold a moment still, a bleeding wound or a blade already swinging. Most were Given a pale, marble-white leaf at ten and trained under the guild, and a few survived being turned to stone and came back with the stillness already in them. Towns fear that gaze and license the work. The **Taken-In**, the leshy, are people the wild has taken partway into itself. Most drew a copper-green leaf at ten, a metallic green, not the ordinary summer-green of the hug, and grew up in the change. A few were Struck, lost and dying in the deep wild until the wild took them in. Either way they come back with bark at the knuckles and moss in the hair, and green things lean toward them when they stand still. Farm country that is prospering reveres them. The cities eat well because of that grain and timber, and polite company keeps them at arm's length.

Of the whole population, over a lifetime, about **60 percent** are Given at the Tree, about **15 percent** are Struck later, and about **25 percent** stay Kept. The Given and the Struck together are the three-quarters who carry a Condition. At the ceremony itself about two children in five are hugged and receive no colour, roughly **40 percent**. A little over a third of those hugged children are Struck someday. That later slice is the fifteen percent of everyone, and the hugged children who are never Struck are the twenty-five percent who stay Kept for life. Places with few Trees, or only young or sickening ones, run higher on the Kept, on the Struck, and on children who never stand. The Tree-dense heart runs higher on the Given. The lifetime mix of the whole world stays sixty, fifteen, and twenty-five.

## The Tithe

Every Condition carries a **Tithe**, the upkeep you meet in order to live well with the becoming. You pay it in the work and the rest that life already asks, and meeting it is what leaves you a valued citizen, the neighbour and the colleague and the kin people already take you for. Fall behind, and a small, real edge shows in the shape of that becoming, while the person is still the one who decides.

Starve a Long-Lived of anything new and they fade and forget, and keep a Two-Bodied from the other self and the restlessness builds until the change comes at a moment they did not pick, though they still choose what that body does. Hold a feeling too long and a Far-Voiced lets out a Cry, emotion filling the room without commanding an action, a belief, or a roll. Let the stillness settle and one of the Stilled can lock a limb of their own stone-still. Leave an Answered's element unspoken and it may let go in a crowded room, as heat, or water, or wind, or their own body going stiff, while the people nearby are not compelled and do not pay the debt. Guilds, licences, and the jobs themselves exist in large part so the upkeep can be paid in the day's work, where other people live, rather than in that edge.

The frightening part is something a person walks toward, or that circumstances press them toward. It does not seize the will, and it does not take a player character over. The cost is paid in the body, in memory, in Stress marked on the sheet, in the standing a town gives, and in the years. It is never paid by taking the bearer's will, or anyone else's. Children are already taught the sentence that covers it: the will is one's own.

Standing is not shared evenly across the roster. Some Conditions are common and ordinary, some are rare and looked up to, some are rare and looked down on, some are feared, and one is legendary. The table at the end of this chapter is the count.

## Nine to choose, ten in the world

Nine Conditions can be Given or Struck. The tenth is **the Unbound**. Common speech calls it the Hollow. It comes later, when a Bound person's term-limited contract ends and nobody signs a new one. You become Unbound only by way of Bound, in play or because the table agrees at the start that the term has already ended. About 1.5 percent of people are Unbound, and that figure sits inside Bound's share of about 5 percent. It is not an extra number laid on top. An open-ended contract does not come due in that way. People pity the Unbound, and feel uneasy around them, because they were released and the price they pledged is missing.

The world uses **the Given-Over** for a Bound who signs contract after contract. They start because they are afraid of paying what they already owe, and another signing puts the debt off, until it is too much to stop and the being on the other side effectively holds them. The contract-guilds' term for that person is **the Forfeit**. It is a title, not a Condition and not a card. A player character does not take the fate. Someone who has signed away nearly all of themselves is no longer a character the table is playing.

### Rules

A Condition is a Daggerheart **Transformation card**. The card opens with a line of flavor and then its features. At minimum there is a boon, what the Condition lets you do, and a Tithe with its name written on it, the drawback that keeps the boon honest. Both of them hook to resources the game already uses, Hope, Stress, Hit Points, Fear, weapon tiers, and Experiences, the phrases on the sheet for what a life has already taught, rather than to a private meter that only this card understands. A player character holds one transformation. It sits in the loadout the way a heritage card does, and it does not count against the limit on domain cards. How many features a card carries is allowed to vary. A Condition that takes the table's focus, or a complicated one, carries more than a common card. What stays constant is a clean boon, and a Tithe with its name on it.

The card does not level with the character. The card you hold at level 1 is the card you hold at level 10, while class features, domain cards, Experiences, weapon tier, and Hope scars, the marks that cross a Hope slot off the sheet, change around it. A few things look like the card is growing, and they are not a new rank of the becoming. A Two-Bodied may attach an animal-form Experience when they would gain an Experience, and a second signature capability only as a once-ever story milestone. Returned and Phoenix spend Hope slots, and what remains is a countdown rather than a power that climbs as they level. The standing obligations in a Bound contract, and the piece of the self named as the price, move when the story moves them. Attacks the card already grants roll more damage dice as Proficiency rises, because Proficiency is how many damage dice the game tells you to roll.

## Roster

The shares below are of all people, not of the three-quarters who carry a Condition. They are the one count this book uses. The Unbound row is already inside Bound's five percent, and adding it a second time would count those people twice.

| Condition | Folk still know | Path | Share of the people | Given / Struck | Standing |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Long-Lived | vampire | Given | ~2.5% | all Given | rare / revered |
| Two-Bodied | werewolf | Given | ~18% | all Given | common |
| Returned | zombie | Struck | ~7% | all Struck | normal / quietly respected |
| Bound | demon (by contract) | Struck | ~5%, counting the Unbound (about 3.5% currently Bound, 1.5% Unbound) | all Struck | feared |
| The Unbound *(the Hollow)* | the price of a contract | Struck, chains off Bound only | ~1.5%, *inside* Bound's ~5% | — | pitied / uneasy |
| Far-Voiced | siren | Both | ~12% | ~11% Given / ~1% Struck | revered |
| The Stilled | gorgon | Both | ~7% | ~6% Given / ~1% Struck | feared / guild-regulated |
| The Answered | djinn | Given | ~10% | all Given | wildcard (no single standing) |
| The Taken-In | leshy | Both | ~13% | ~12% Given / ~1% Struck | respected-but-looked-down / frontier |
| Phoenix | phoenix | Given | <0.1% (one at a time) | all Given | legendary |

The Kept are not on this table. They are the ~25 percent who carry no Condition for life. The Tree hugged them at the ceremony, the town was there to see it, and they are wanted in their own right.
