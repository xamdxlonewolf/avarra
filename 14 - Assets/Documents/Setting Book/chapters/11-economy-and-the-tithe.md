# Economy and the Tithe

In a world organized only around what people want, the work follows the appetite. In the Turning the work also follows what people have become. A **Kind** is what a person is by birth, already true before any leaf falls. A **Condition** is the change laid over that Kind, and three of every four people come to carry one. Every Condition has a **Tithe**, a permanent upkeep, and if the upkeep lapses the Condition's dangerous side shows. Once that many people carry one, the need stops being a private matter between a person and their gift.

The ordinary technology of the age is late medieval: guilds, letters of credit, merchant houses, and no exchange where a stranger buys a share of a company. The world moves past that age only where Condition-labor carries it, which is work that exists because someone carries a Condition. Those are its only advances. The roster of who carries what lives in *The Conditions*. The law under the markets is the chapter before this one.

**Tithe-provision** is a permanent civic utility, in the same way wells and roads are. You can see it in every town, people fight over it, and the people who depend on it cannot do without it. It is not the size of grain. Grain feeds everyone, every day. The greens and the halls keep an exposed minority from sliding into the danger their Tithe was holding back, and they keep the neighbours from having to live beside that slide. That is enough to organize a sector of a town's work. It is not the food supply.

Society organizes around the Conditioned, the people who carry a Condition, for that reason, rather than from reverence or from fear. The work fits the Tithe, and there is a net under the people whose Tithe does not pay itself. Most of the Conditioned pay by working, because for them the need and the job are the same act. A smaller share has to be furnished by someone else. The ones who furnish, and the ones who need furnishing, are often the same people. Whoever holds the gate holds power.

## Who actually buys provision

The share of people with each Condition is in *The Conditions*. This table sorts people by how they pay, and it gives no numbers of its own.

| Who | Conditions | What "provision" is |
|---|---|---|
| **Self-paying by vocation** | the Answered · Two-Bodied · the Stilled | The work is the Tithe. They do not buy a sector of the economy. They are a labor market. |
| **If the work fits** | Far-Voiced · Returned | They pay their own Tithe when the job fits. When it does not, they buy a hall-hour, an hour booked so a Far-Voiced voice can be let out, or they carry the need unpaid. |
| **Provided-for** | the Taken-In · Long-Lived · the Unbound · Bound Terms | Someone else supplies the green, the novelty, the warmth, or whatever clause the contract set. |
| **The Kept** | no Condition | No Tithe. They buy bread. They do not buy a lot-hour, which is an hour on the city's living green. |

Green is free on a farm, and most Taken-In live where it is free. The purchased or civic customer base is a minority of the people, and it concentrates in the stone towns: urban Taken-In, hall-users who have no stage of their own, people seeking novelty, and warmth that money can only approximate. The queues at those doors are real. The doors are not granaries. A city fights over lot-hours and over hall-bells, the hours a voice is allowed to vent, the way it fights over wells. A wilt for lack of green, or a Cry breaking in the square, is a public problem. Tithe-goods do not move in the grain carts, and they do not rival bread by the cartload.

## Self-paying vs provided-for Tithes

Not all Tithes cost the same to pay, and some do not cost coin at all, because paying them is doing useful work. That sorts the Conditioned along a range from self-paying to provided-for, and almost everything else follows from where a Condition sits. The line is a range, not a wall. Which side a person lands on can depend on the work they actually do, not only on the Condition. Far-Voiced and Returned are where that shows.

**Self-paying Tithes are the ones where the work is the payment.** For several Conditions, the upkeep is discharged by doing exactly the labor other people need done. The cost of staying whole and the work they produce are the same act.

| Condition | The Tithe | ...paid by doing |
|---|---|---|
| the Answered | use the element in earnest | **The craft itself.** A Tide-Answered shipwright, one whose element is the water, slakes the Tithe by building the ship. |
| Two-Bodied | spend time as the animal | **The ranging work:** herding, rescue, courier, scout. Being the beast is the job. |
| the Stilled | burn off the Grey by exertion | **Active labor:** surgery, riot-lines, demolition. The Grey is the stillness each Gaze leaves in the body. The vigorous work clears it. Left full, that stillness locks one of their own limbs. |

**On the seam, a person self-pays if the vocation fits, and falls to being provided-for if it does not.** Two Conditions do not sit cleanly in either column. Where they land turns on the job.

| Condition | The Tithe | Self-pays when… | Falls to provided-for when… |
|---|---|---|---|
| Far-Voiced | vent strong emotion each day | The vocation is the vent. Performers, criers, negotiators, and sworn witnesses feel in earnest on the job, and that feeling discharges the Tithe. | The voice has no outlet: an isolated posting, dull labor, or simple silence. They need a venting-hall furnished so the voice can be let out. |
| Returned | act toward the Purpose | The Purpose, the unfinished task they came back carrying, lines up with the work. Plague wards, recovery, and tending the dead pay the Tithe by being the deadly job. | The Purpose is personal or unmet by any trade: a vengeance, a vow, a person still to find. No employer can furnish it, and it pulls against ordinary work. |

**Provided-for Tithes are upkeep someone else has to supply.** For the rest, the Tithe is a cost with no productive by-product. Someone has to furnish the green, the novelty, or the warmth, and that furnishing is the town's Tithe-provision.

| Condition | The Tithe | ...must be provided |
|---|---|---|
| the Taken-In | rest on living land | **Green:** trivial on the farm, and a real expense in a stone city, where it means potted groves and leave to use a garden. |
| Long-Lived | learn something genuinely new | **Novelty:** books, travel, correspondence, and the ordinary traffic of a university town. |
| the Unbound | keep connecting to others | **Warmth:** company, and being known. It is the one Tithe money buys least well. |
| Bound | keep the Terms | **Whatever the counterparty set down,** which is idiosyncratic and sometimes ruinous. |

The self-payers are prosperous almost automatically, because their Condition is a trade. Prosperity is not the same thing as ease. The Stilled self-pay and are still the most tightly licensed people in the world. The Answered self-pay under heavy guild discipline. Self-paying buys a living. It does not buy social comfort along with the living. The provided-for are economically exposed. They depend on someone furnishing the upkeep, and whoever furnishes it holds power over them. The people on the seam are the most precarious of all. A Far-Voiced who loses the stage, or a Returned whose Purpose turns personal, can slide from self-paying to exposed overnight. That slide is where the injustice runs.

## Tithe-poverty and the safety net

Because the provided-for Tithes cost money, you can be too poor to stay whole. A Taken-In who cannot afford green, a Long-Lived with no access to anything new, a Far-Voiced with nowhere to vent: these people do not only suffer want. They slide toward the danger, into the wilting, the fade, or the involuntary Cry, a voice breaking out because it was not let out on purpose. Destitution here has a visible, dangerous face.

Who pays for that provision is the political question, and polities do not answer it the same way.

Where provision is treated as a public good, which is the Warm answer, the devout end of the world's theology, the town keeps it the way it keeps roads and wells. There are town greenhouses, free venting-halls, and endowed libraries, because a town whose Conditioned stay whole is a town that stays safe and keeps working. The pious way of saying the same choice is that you help every person tend their gift.

Where provision is left as a private burden, harsher polities hand it to the individual and to the guild. The Tithe-poor fall. They become a real hazard to the people around them, and the same polities then police the fall instead of preventing it.

The cynical middle is leverage. The town, the guild, or the employer does provide the Tithe, and holds what it provides, so the furnisher can withhold it. How that lands on the stances is at the end of this chapter.

## Labor: who does what

The Tithe and the vocation are usually the same shape, which is why a town's jobs sound like its Conditions. How large each workforce is lives in *The Conditions*. What follows is the work, not a census.

The people who move things are the Two-Bodied, and they are the plurality, the largest share of any Condition. Herding, haulage, rescue, courier, and scouting sit on them, and so does the fighting when a war actually comes. The Condition runs in blood, so a family can be wolf-folk or hawk-folk straight through, and the trade comes down with the animal.

The food those herds and that haulage depend on is Taken-In work: farmers, foresters, herders, and wardens. The frontier runs on them because they thrive on living land. A city still needs their grain, so it keeps a working minority inside the walls, at the social arm's length the next section describes. The distance is manners. It is not an empty workplace.

Once the goods are moving, someone has to be heard across the distance. Far-Voiced criers stand on the coasts, the roads, and the borders, and the same people perform, negotiate, and swear as witnesses when a court needs a feeling that cannot be faked. The relay is a live signal from coast to border. A medieval world would not otherwise have it, and it exists because the people posting it carry the voice.

The crafts that shape the materials are the Answered, guilded by the element they use in earnest. Ember is the smiths and the glasswrights, Tide the shipwrights and the dyers, Root the masons and the potters, Gale the sailmakers and the millers. When a fire breaks out, or a rescue needs those same hands, the guilds rotate into it.

Some of the work kills the living, so the Returned do it. Death cannot take them a second time until the Purpose is met, and the jobs are the plague ward, the mine after a collapse, the deep salvage, and the tending of the dead. Beside that, the Stilled do the work that needs a body held still: surgery, a riot-line, a demolition, a course of stone. They are licensed, and the guild disciplines them, because the Gaze is the hazard as well as the tool.

What has to be remembered, the archives and the notarial acts and the judgments and the banks, sits with the Long-Lived. They are few. If many more people never died, those chairs would stiffen and stop changing hands. Where a word has to hold, the Bound keep the oaths, the debts, and the treaties, and they stand as witnesses whose word is literally binding. The Unbound are in that same work. A released Bound is not a second workforce. They go back to whatever they did before, and they need other people if they are to keep at it.

A Phoenix is only a trace, and the economy does not employ them as a class of worker. They are a political symbol, not a trade. The Kept are the inversion of that whole ledger. They carry no Tithe, so there is no upkeep to fund, and they carry no Condition, so no Condition-guild claims them. They take ordinary work and move where the work is. Where the faithful pity them for missing a gift, the ledger calls them the least encumbered people in the world, a real freedom sitting under the slight.

## Why Taken-In live in stone cities

Green costs money in a stone city, and the cities keep the Taken-In at arm's length because the Taken-In thrive on the frontier. Both are true. Arm's length is how the polite districts stand toward the bark and antler a Taken-In carries on the body. The lots are still staffed.

They are in the city for five ordinary reasons.

1. **They were Given here.** A copper-green leaf, the Taken-In's colour, fell at a city Tree. Family, work, and a hearth-witness are what keep them. Leaving the city would mean leaving the circle that saw them Turn.
2. **Grain and timber come in, and people come with them.** Seasonal Taken-In work the quays and winter over, and some of them stay. They are the harvest-hands who do not go home in Hale-month, the spring month, also called Haelren.
3. **The lot is a job.** The greens-keepers need Taken-In labor. The city imports that green-skill for the commons, and then will not sit the workers at the high table.
4. **The door can open in a city's old green.** Being lost and dying in a deep wild is the Struck path that makes a Taken-In. A walled grove, a lot at night, or a river-garden after a flood can open the same door. It is rare, and it is often enough to count.
5. **They cannot afford to leave.** Green-poverty is a trap. You need the lot if you are to stay whole, the lot is gated, you work for the hours on it, and you never save the cart-fare home. That trap is the city's book of the Slide, the illicit market in Tithe-goods. Week-keepers sell it, and the green is what they sell when the gate will not.

The Taken-In share of the whole world is in *The Conditions*. A stone city runs lower than that share. The Taken-In who are there concentrate on the lots, the orchards, the river-gardens, and the week-keeper's pot, the private green that same trade rents by the week. There are enough of them to make a queue, enough for a wilt two streets off the lot, and enough for the Slide to have a customer. The frontier keeps the density and the honour. The city keeps the gate.

## Banking and the deathless houses

The banks of the Turning do not die. These are the **deathless houses**. A Long-Lived founds the house and is still personally running it two centuries later, the same memory, the same word, and the same unbroken ledger. In a world of letters of credit and merchant houses, that continuity is the great advance a Condition makes in finance. A letter of credit is a written promise to pay, carried in place of a chest of coin. Three things follow.

**The trusted note.** A note from a deathless house is trusted almost as coin, because everyone knows the banker cannot forget a debt, cannot be quietly succeeded by a defaulting heir, and will personally answer for the paper in fifty years. Their paper circulates further than any crown's, and it holds its value better. Other paper is trusted by how close it comes to that word. The nearest thing this world has to a standard that does not wear out is the word of a house that will not die.

**The long instrument.** Deathless houses lend and endow on horizons no mortal lender would risk: century loans, multi-generation trusts, and the patient financing of cathedrals, canals, and universities. The instruments are letters of credit and endowments. There is no stock exchange, and no company split into shares a crowd of strangers can buy.

**The concentration risk.** Wealth that never passes to an heir never disperses through inheritance. The great scattering that a death forces on every other fortune does not happen here. There are few of the deathless, and even so they can slowly gather an outsized share of the capital that stands still. Their small number is what keeps that share from becoming the whole of standing capital, and the economy has never fully resolved the gathering. An endowment does not undo it. The coin moves out, and the steering stays, so the influence concentrates even while the cash flows into institutions the same hands still direct.

Many of the houses began as road-houses in the Walking Years, the age when families still had to walk to Thaeloren. They were inns, beds, and century-credit for that road. The everyday name is still road-house. The cant, the working nickname, is *brenhael* *(BREN-hael)*, "the hearth kept whole." The lived version is a house still in the act of being an inn: the Held bed, a bed kept for a name that has not come, and a note a necessity-family pays on the home-walk, which is to say they eat now and pay when they walk home. When the walk stopped being required in the core, the lands nearest the Awakening Tree, the same Long-Lived still remembered every debtor, and the paper outlived the pilgrimage season. The inns became banks. Two fates of that memory are still standing, on opposite errands. The White Note House took the path of collecting on the old paper. The Third Hearth kept making the bed. The trusted note is an instrument from the Walking Years that outlived the road it was written for.

## Inheritance and the problem of not dying

Inheritance law is bent by the deathless, and the bend is a grief as well as a rule. A Long-Lived's children are not guaranteed to inherit the Condition. Most of them age and die while the parent does not. A deathless person therefore outlives most of the heirs, and the estate never enters the inheritance stream at all. A fortune that is never passed down still has to go somewhere.

The usual answer is that the deathless endow. They have no line of heirs waiting on the death, and their own Tithe needs a steady supply of novelty, new books, new scholarship, new things to learn. A Long-Lived characteristically funds the institutions that generate that knowledge: the libraries, the universities, the archives. The deathless banker becomes the deathless patron, and pays their own Tithe by building the places that feed it. It is also how they grieve, an endowment in the name of a child who died a century ago. It is not the same thing as letting the fortune go. An endowment converts coin into patronage that is permanent and directed. Because the founder does not die, the founder keeps the chair, names the scholars, and steers the institution for centuries. Endowment eases the concentration of coin, because liquid wealth flows out into public works, and it deepens the concentration of influence, because the same few deathless hands still guide the universities, the courts, and the archives their money built. Keeping the Long-Lived few does not end that lock-up. The lock-up changes shape, from hoarded coin to patronage that does not step down.

For everyone else, inheritance is ordinary, except in the Two-Bodied bloodlines, where the Condition, the family's animal, and often the family's trade descend together, so that a place in the guild and a Condition can be a single inheritance. The sharpest question the law still has open is economic at the root. The Given-Over is a Bound who contracted away so much of themselves that the counterparty effectively owns the person. That is the ultimate inheritance failure: an estate, and a self, absorbed by a creditor.

## Center and periphery

The economy is not spread evenly, and the unevenness follows the same geography that shapes the law. Near the mature Trees, in the core, Conditions lie thick, the guilds are strong, and specialized Condition-work and the goods that come from it are what the core sells to everyone else. Out at the margins, on the frontier, the giving thins. More of the people there are generalist Kept, or Struck and untrained, and fewer of them are Given specialists.

The core exports the specialized work and the finished goods, Answered crafts, Stilled medicine, deathless-house credit, and it imports the raw staples. The frontier leans hardest on the Taken-In, who thrive there and are neighbours, and on Kept labor that can turn its hand to whatever the week needs. It exports food, timber, and raw materials, and it imports the specialists it cannot grow. Distance, spoilage, and the thin reach together make the friction real. They leave the frontier specialist-poor and self-reliant. Cities still keep a working minority of Taken-In behind a gate. They do not ship the Condition out of town.

## The shadow economy

Every gate grows a market on the far side of it. The gates in this economy are Tithe-provision and vouching.

**Illicit Tithe-supply.** Where the legitimate provision is rationed, gated, or unaffordable, a shadow market fills the gap. The green it smuggles is for a Taken-In cut off from living land, and the customers of that urban book are the city-Given, the winter-stayers, and the people caught in the lot-hour trap. The same market keeps unlicensed venting-rooms, and it sells black-market novelty, rare texts and forbidden knowledge, to a Long-Lived who is starving for anything new. The customer is someone already sliding toward the wilting, the fade, or the involuntary Cry, which makes the trade both lucrative and cruel. In the street this trade is called **the Slide**. The same people are called week-keepers, because the sale is rented by the week.

**Off-book contracts.** The whole domain of the Bound has an underside: contracts sworn outside the honest tables, unregistered terms, and the predatory brokers who arrange them. That pipeline feeds the Given-Over. In the street this desk is called **the Holding Desk**. It sells clauses. It is not the Slide.

**Sold vouching.** Citizenship here is a place counting you as its own because a person will stand for you, rather than because a clerk has written your name down. The unvouched will buy a voucher, and a market exists in dubious character-witnesses. The one thing that cannot be faked is a Long-Lived's memory, which is why an honest vouch from them is priceless and a corrupt one is catastrophic. In the street this market is called **the Standing Trade**. It sells a person who will stand. It is not the Slide.

The Slide sells the upkeep. The Holding Desk sells clauses. The Standing Trade sells a witness.

## How polities vary (economic faces)

The rules of the Tithe are the same everywhere. Who actually receives what is politics. It follows the same three choices as the law: how gift-devout the polity is, how many healthy Trees it has, and how far the state reaches onto the person. Three stances keep recurring, and each has an economic face.

**The Warm** *(high theology).* A Warm polity treats Tithe-provision as a public good. It keeps town greenhouses, venting-halls and libraries that someone has endowed, and ranging-commons held in common, ground at the edge where the Two-Bodied can be their other selves. The Conditioned are kept whole at the town's expense, because a whole populace is a safe and productive one, and because the faith says to tend every gift. The economic injustice is soft. The same help arrives with an expectation attached. You are helped to tend your gift, and you are quietly obligated to spend it as the community sees fit, until the subsidy shades into conscription, a requirement to spend the gift as the town decides.

**The Watchful** *(governance at its extreme).* A crown that writes its people down, as the Tallied Crown and the Worn Count do, turns the need of a Tithe into leverage. It provides, and it provides efficiently, even generously, and it holds what it provides. The green, the outlet, and the novelty all come through channels the crown controls and can withhold. Its deathless-house finance is superb and centuries-deep. The injustice is cold and precise. A population whose upkeep runs through the state is a population that cannot afford to refuse the state. It is the most complete control in the world, and it is dressed as care.

**The Frontier** *(low reach).* There is little formal infrastructure for the Tithe, and there are few specialists. People improvise a way to meet the need, or they go without. The freedom is real. There is no guild monopoly and no crown holding the Tithe, and the Taken-In are neighbours rather than curiosities. The harshness is also real. The provided-for who cannot pay their Tithe slide with no net, and untrained Struck people manage dangerous Conditions alone. At the edge the economy runs on barter and favors, cash-poor and land-rich.

A polity's economy is set by where it stands on all three choices at once. A devout frontier runs commons-minded charity with almost no infrastructure to fund it. A secular Tree-rich republic has deep markets and thin welfare. A Watchful-and-pious core turns generous provision into the tightest hold in the world.
