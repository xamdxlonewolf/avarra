# The Kinds

## Kinds

You are raised by a place, not by your ears.

A Kind is the body you were born in, the shape you bring to the Turning Tree, and nobody blinks at a fox-tail in a Maiethorn market or at wings on a Heskoren ridge. What the world organizes itself around is the becoming, a Condition, the change that comes after birth, and most people carry one. Carrying it also tells you the Tithe, the upkeep that Condition asks, and often the guild or the licence that goes with the work. There are no Kitsune kingdoms, no Selkie empires of the sea, and no Tengu mountain-empires, because any of those would turn a whole people into one country, and that is not how a Kind works here.

What the four custom ancestries do have are hearths. A hearth is the ground they were born to, a forest, a coast, a ridge, a dry country, or an isle, and it is where they are densest, not where they are the only ones allowed to live. Other people live on that ground too, so a hearth is not a flag and it is not a government.

The Yumboes are the case that shows the rule. They are rare off Kumbaan, the far isle, because of the distance and because of the storm-wall that keeps the isle hard to reach, not because a Kind is a nationality. On the isle they are ordinary, and on the mainland they are a sailor's tale, the same people in both places, with the map as the thing that separated them.

### Kind, Condition, place

| Layer | What it tells you | What it does not |
|---|---|---|
| **Kind** | The birth-shape, and sometimes a hearth, the coast, ridge, wood, or isle where that shape is densest | Loyalty, faith, legal standing, or which country they belong to |
| **Condition** | What they became. Most people carry one. It also tells you the Tithe, the upkeep that Condition asks, and often a guild or a licence | Ancestry. The birth-body stays the Kind |
| **Place** | The drift they speak, which is the local wear on the old tongue, the names they use for institutions, the country that stood witness for them, and whether the second name is a byname or a house-name | What ears they have |

A byname is the second name a witness-town uses, *of a town* or *of a deed*, and a house-name is the inherited name a list uses when it has to find you after the witness is gone. Lestrand is the Ledger Coast, the secular docks. Threnmaieth is the Tallied Crown. Vaethorn is the Waiting Lands. A Kitsune clerk on the docks of Lestrand, a Tengu pilgrim in Threnmaieth, and a Selkie standing witness for someone in Vaethorn are all ordinary lives, and none of those lives is a contradiction. What matters is the Kind, the Condition, and the place that raised them, taken together. A Tithe belongs to a Condition.

### The four custom hearths

**Kitsune: three hearths, one Kind.** The fox-born already come in three Foxes, Wilds, Snows, and Sands. That split is the distribution, and it is written into the body. There is not one fox-country. There are three grounds, shared with whoever else lives there. They marry and mingle, and a child takes after one parent's line. You meet Kitsune in every port and every court, and you meet more of a given Fox where that Fox's ground is.

| Fox | Hearth | Where on the map |
|---|---|---|
| **Wilds** (red / forest) | tangled wood, hedgerow, Motherwood | densest in Maiethorn's Motherwood and Heskoren's deep forests |
| **Snows** (white / high cold) | high snow, pale open country | densest on Maiethorn's Rain-Wall and Heskoren's highland spines |
| **Sands** (pale / dry) | dry hills, rain-shadow, sand-country | densest in Maiethorn's Rain-Shadow, the leeward east of the Rain-Wall, the one true dry country in the known world |

The Rain-Wall is the high country on Maiethorn that catches the weather, and the Rain-Shadow is the dry country in its lee. A Fox of the Sands in a wet Strandoren harbour has simply moved, the way people do. Strandoren is the shore-continent, almost all coastline. A town in the Rain-Shadow with no fox-born at all would be the thing worth asking about.

Old Kitsune hearths still have a word for a fourth fox, and nobody is born as that fox anymore. A grandmother may still say the word, and she will not say what it means.

**Selkie: the coasts.** Selkies belong to two homes at once, shore and sea, so their hearth is the line between those homes: the strand, the estuary, the cold harbour, and the towns that face the water. They are densest on Strandoren's indented coasts. They are common in Maiethorn's western ports on the Old Crossing, the sea those ports face, present on Heskoren's wild coast, and thinner inland. They are not native to Kumbaan. A Selkie who makes that crossing is a story, not a population.

Inland Selkies do exist, because the skin Anchors, which is how they can always find their way back to water. They are simply fewer, and they miss the place they are not in, which they consider normal.

**Tengu: the ridges.** Tengu hearths are the peaks, the cliff-shelves, and the wind that lives there. They are densest on Maiethorn's Rain-Wall and on Heskoren's highland spines, the two continents that actually have killing height. They are rare as a settled people on low Strandoren. The ones you meet there are usually sky-couriers, a family, a perch on a clock-tower, and a run over the Old Crossing, and not a ridge-town picked up and set down on a quay.

Tengu settlements cling to crags and are reached by wing or by rope. A lowlander who arrives on foot is a guest who climbed, and that climb is the courtesy.

They keep a running, half-teasing rivalry with the Selkies. Sky-folk and sea-folk, each remains convinced their element is the one worth belonging to. The rivalry is colour between neighbours. It is not a border.

**Yumboe: the isle.** Kumbaan, and almost nowhere else. They are mythic-rare off the isle and ordinary on it. On the isle there is no Leaf-Fall, the tenth-year rite in which a Turning Tree Gives a Condition, and a Condition that arrives because someone was Struck is possible but rare. Struck means the change came later, at a threshold, rather than at that rite. Their Kind is their own, and so is their place at the table. A Yumboe on the mainland is usually the only one anyone in that town has ever seen. They have no mainland hearth, because the storm-wall keeps them on the isle.

A hearth is terrain, and not a government. A Kitsune on a Lestrand quay still sounds like a Kitsune, and that is a habit of kitchens, not a fox-principality at work.

You are named by the place that raised you, and you are taught by the other kitchen.

The civic year happens at home. That is the drift of the old tongue, the second name, and the town that will watch the child Turn, the tenth-year gathering where a child may be Given a Condition or Kept without one. The Kept are the people the Tree hugged and left as they were. The Kind-year is shorter, and it is borrowed from kin. In the summer before a custom-Kind child's tenth year, or the first summer the family can spare, the child is sent to another kitchen of the same Kind. It is not a pilgrimage, and it is not the walk to a Tree. It is an aunt's coast, a cousin's crag, or a fox-market three valleys over, and people just say it that way.

| Kind | What they call the season | What moves |
|---|---|---|
| Kitsune | a **fox-summer** | A child of one Fox-ground sleeps in another, so the Wilds, the Snows, and the Sands stay one Kind. The register is taught in person, and foxfire is shown, not described. |
| Selkie | a summer on **another strand** | Kin from a second coast attend the skin-days, or the child is sent. The stitch-pattern of a skin is a family thing, and it does not travel well as a letter. |
| Tengu | a season at the **other perch** | A visiting ridge, or a sky-courier family carrying a child over the Old Crossing. A first solo flight may be off a crag that is not the home one. |
| Yumboe | the **hill-feast** | They do not run a diaspora season. The isle is the hearth. A Yumboe on the mainland keeps the one register by being the only one in the room. |

Families who cannot send a child still teach at home. Poverty, a sick Tree-year, a war, or a child needed on the boat, and the register survives those. What the other kitchen does is keep a dispersed Kind from thinning into twenty private dialects. A fox-born child who has never left Hallowquay, a quay people name in the ordinary way, still answers to a Kusawe name, the fox-born hearth-register, because someone who had left came through to trade and to talk.

What travels, instead of a flag, is people, and not an office. Sky-couriers, night-brokers, salvage kin, and a grandmother who does the summers carry it from kitchen to kitchen. Faiths and guilds still recruit by Condition, by faith, and by office, and there is no Kitsune guild. The crafts that travel have to be taught with the hands in the room, foxfire and a skin-stitch and a wind-call and a service at the hill-feast. A recipe that worked from a letter would have made a nation, and these do not travel that way. A few objects go along that are slightly wrong in the next town: a fox-lamp whose pale fire only a shown hand can start, a perch-feather worn at a wedding on the strand, and a current that Selkies can name and will not draw.

A few things stay small, and people will not explain them: the word for the fourth fox, a mark on a ridge that lowlanders are not taught, and a current that Selkies can name and will not draw on a chart.

A hearth-neighbourhood is the other kitchen made local: a Selkie quay, a Tengu perch, or a Rain-Shadow fox-market. The town around it is still mixed. Ornsael is the worked example, a fox-market by the well, mixed Kinds, and no gate.

### Stock ancestries

Daggerheart's stock ancestries live everywhere the Trees do: Clank, Drakona, Dwarf, Elf, Faerie, Faun, Firbolg, Fungril, Galapa, Giant, Goblin, Halfling, Human, Infernis, Katari, Orc, Ribbet, and Simiah. They are not given continents. A few of them are more common in some kinds of country, and that is a tendency, not a law. The kitchens below use the ordinary larder of the place. An ancestry does not grow its own staple crop.

You are raised by a place, not by your ears. The tendencies are not rules. A Ribbet judge in inland Threnmaieth is ordinary. A Giant on a Lestrand dock is ordinary, and a Giant who works as a Lestrand banker is ordinary too. If a town has more of one ancestry, that is a fact about the town. Raillath, Denlad, and Tunral do not belong to any ancestry, and neither do Maiethlir and Orentel.

Humans are common, and they are not the normal body that everyone else is a variation on. There is no unmixed household that counts as the default. A marriage between Kinds is a marriage. Nobody files it, and nobody calls it marrying out.

A character whose two ancestry features both come from this stock list takes a Hearth-Mark from the place that raised them. That is how those characters stay playable next to the four custom Kinds, who each have three features. The Mark names a place. It does not turn a tendency into a country.

**The water-born.** Ribbet and Galapa are most common on the same coasts as Selkies, and they are not Selkies. Those coasts are Strandoren's indented shore, Maiethorn's western ports on the Old Crossing, and, less often, Heskoren's wild coast. A Ribbet can breathe underwater. A Selkie holds their breath, and you cannot keep a Selkie under. Galapa live on the same quays as both. A Ribbet who grew up inland grew up in that town. They cook on the quay, close enough to hear the tide, and the meal is bread softened in fish broth, with seaweed or a cockle when the tide was good. They do not own the coast, the Old Crossing, the deep water, or the Selkie strand. Some Seaborne communities live in the same weather. Seaborne is a community, a life people share, and not a Kind.

**Beside the ridges.** Giants are more common on Heskoren's uplands, next to Tengu ridges, and they do not take those ridges over from the Tengu. Some people raised on ridges, or in wild country, live in the same weather. Ridgeborne and Wildborne are communities, lives people share, and not a Giant law. A Giant who grew up on a Lestrand dock grew up on that dock. They eat the usual upland meal, oat mash with dried peas, and cheese or smoked fish when the house has them, and they do not eat it on a Tengu perch. They do not own the ridge, the wind, the high road, or the sky-courier's run. On Maiethorn's Rain-Wall, Tengu and Snows foxes are already common, and a Giant on that road is a neighbour.

**The old wood.** Elves, Faeries, Fungril, and Firbolg are more common in Maiethorn's Motherwood, beside the hearths of the Wilds foxes, and the clearing is still mixed. Heskoren's deep forests are Wilds-fox country too, so an Elf there is a neighbour in that forest. The place where this group is common is the Motherwood. Faeries are small and they can fly, which does not make them Yumboe. Halflings are small and people call them lucky, and that is a different thing from a Faerie's wings, and it still does not make them the good people of the isle. A winged neighbour in the Motherwood is someone the town already knows. They cook oat pottage with dried peas, and mushrooms when the week was wet, and they start the pot for whoever is already in the clearing, fox-born neighbours included. A Fungril household sets one spoonful aside, and they will not say who it is for. They do not own the Motherwood, the graft, which is a cutting of a Turning Tree, the Wilds hearth, the air, or Kumbaan.

**Hedgerow and herd.** Fauns and Halflings are more common on farmland, in the pastures of the Mother-core, on the Chart-run plain, which is Strandoren's interior river-plain, and in the valleys under Heskoren's spines, wherever a hedge and a herd already feed a town. If a village that already has a name has more of them, that is a fact about the village, and it does not make the village theirs. Halflings are small, and people call them lucky, and they are not Yumboe. They cook cabbage in the morning's milk and eat it in the yard, before they let the herd through the hedge. That is how that house eats, and it is not the guest-meal, the stranger's bowl. They do not own the field, the herd, luck, the small, or the isle.

**Loft, mast, and canopy.** Katari and Simiah are more common where the work is up high, in the Motherwood canopy, in the rigging on Strandoren, and in the lofts over a market or a drying floor. They live next to the old wood and next to the quays, and the town below is still mixed. They eat a handful of hazelnuts, or cold fish, in the place where they were working, a loft, a mast, or a branch, and they still come down to the table for the town's supper. They do not own the canopy, the mast, the night, or the market.

**The warm bench.** Clank, Dwarves, Goblins, and Drakona are more common where a town already mends things, a flood-wall, a hull, a mill, a drying loft, or the bottom of a road that climbs to a perch. Maiethlir and Orentel have that work, and the work does not become a neighbourhood that belongs to them. You will meet more Drakona in a house that keeps a work-fire going from morning until dark. Some of them are Ember-Answered. Ember is the fire-work of the element-guilds, and that fire-work is common. Ember is not a Drakona chapter. They eat bread and beans at the bench while the bench is still warm from the work. They are using the job's heat, and they did not build a separate kitchen for the Kind. They do not own the wall, the quay, the mill, the forge, Ember, or the stone under a Tengu ridge. Faiths and guilds still recruit by Condition, by faith, and by office. Nobody is recruited because of their ears.

**The carrying yard.** Orcs are more common where the day's work is lifting, on the thaw-roads, at the landings of the Salt Walk, the sea-then-land road through Strandoren, in the dock-gangs, and hauling on a live front, which is a young graft or a neighbour's wood that cannot Turn yet. Other Kinds do that same lifting. A crew that is mostly Orcs is a fact about that crew, and the town is still mixed. They eat a pot of peas in the yard while the cart is still hitched, with smoked fish if the road met a quay. They do not own the road, the ford, the watch, or a war.

**Human.** Humans are common on every continent that has Trees, and they are no more common on one of those continents than on another. A street with many humans is just that street. They are not the body everyone else is measured against. They cook what the town cooks, the same meal as the street they live on. They do not own being the ordinary person, the standard for any other Kind, or a government that belongs to humans.

**Born to the blood.** Infernis are born with demon blood, so the blood is a Kind. They are not Bound. Bound is a contract a person swears, and therefore something they become. The blood and the contract are different things. They have no home landscape and no country of their own. They are born wherever the Trees are, and a street with more of them is a family that stayed there. An Infernis who swears a contract is Bound like anyone else. The blood is what they were born, and the contract is what they became. They eat from the same pot as the rest of the house, and they do not set a separate bowl because of the blood. They do not own the contract, Bound, or a homeland.

The quay, the ridge, the wood, the field, the canopy, the mill, the road, and the pot belong to the town. A kitchen cooks there. It does not hold the ground.

### Hearth-Mark

A stock Daggerheart ancestry grants two features. Each custom Kind written for this world grants three, so a stock character set beside one of those four looks weak, because the sheet is carrying less. The four three-feature packages stay as they are. You do not even the scales by cutting a Kitsune's fox, a Selkie's skin, a Tengu's wing, or the unseen hands of a Yumboe. The difference is made up at the place that raised the character.

A character whose two ancestry features are **both from stock ancestries**, whether that is a single stock Kind or a mix of two stock Kinds, takes a **Hearth-Mark** at character creation. A character who takes **any custom-Kind feature** does not take the Mark. For that character, the third feature is the hearth already written into the body.

Name the **place that raised you**. It might be a town, a quay, a ridge-road, a walk-hold, or a roll-street. You write it as a place-phrase, the way *Hallowquay salvage-child* is a Hearth-Mark, and not as a Kind-phrase, the way *Seal-blood* is not one.

**Once per session**, when you make an action roll or a reaction roll that leans on how that place taught you to live, you add **+2**. You do not spend Hope, the currency a player uses to push a roll, in order to add it. You may still spend Hope to add a relevant Experience on the same roll.

The Mark is not an immunity, it is not a power that spends Stress, and it is not a shopping list of tricks. If a phrase starts granting swim-breath, flight, or "cannot be caught off guard," it has become a feature. Throw it back and write the place again. The +2 is the whole of the Mark.

Everyone still takes a **Community**, as the published rules require, the card for the sort of place that raised the character. A Hearth-Mark is not a second Community, and it is not a third ancestry feature slipped in under another name. Ridgeborne, Seaborne, Wildborne, and the rest of those cards stay what they are. The Mark is one town, or one quay, or one road, and it is not a Kind-nation. You still take the two Experiences the rules give every character, and the Mark does not replace them.

### Mixed Ancestry

The table allows what Daggerheart allows. Mixed Ancestry is a legal heritage here, an ordinary way to build a character, and you do not have to ask permission to use it. The published rules, the System Reference Document, take the **top** feature, the first one listed, from one ancestry, and the **bottom** feature, the second one listed, from another. Two mainland Kinds, stock, custom, or one of each, mix by that rule.

Custom Kinds have a third feature. That third feature is the **hearth feature**, the one that belongs to the full Kind, and a mix leaves it out.

| Kind | Top | Bottom | Hearth feature, only on the full Kind |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kitsune | Slip the Frame | Fox's Glamour | Your Fox (Wilds / Snows / Sands) |
| Selkie | Second Skin | Slip the Hold | Seal-Kin |
| Tengu | Wind-Winged | Wind-Caller | The Mountain's Mood |

Yumboe is not on this table. That Kind is written below.

Two parents means two hearths and a body that can wear both, and nobody files the marriage. There is no mixed-Kind licence. The live question is still whose kitchen, not whether the marriage was allowed. A mixed mainland child carries two features, one from each side, which is the pairing the published rules describe, and not two full custom packages stacked together. If either side is a custom Kind, the child answers to one register, often the kitchen they spent the summer in, or simply the name that stuck, and the second name still comes from the place. Both sides show on the body, ears and webbing, or a fox-brush under a wing, and that is ordinary, not a secret third feature. If a Kind they wear keeps another kitchen, they may visit it, and they may visit both, without growing a second hearth feature just for having gone.

A mixed Kitsune is fox-touched, which means they have Slip the Frame or Fox's Glamour, and they are not a Fox of the Wilds, the Snows, or the Sands unless they take the full Kind and the hearth feature that names which fox they are. Kitsune Fox-lines are still one Fox when the child is a full Kitsune.

How to build one:

1. Take **one Top** from one parent Kind and **one Bottom** from the other. Use the table above for the three mainland customs, and use the printed card order for a stock ancestry.
2. Take **no** hearth feature from either custom Kind. The third feature stays with the people who took the whole package.
3. Take **no** Hearth-Mark if either of the features you chose is custom. Take a Hearth-Mark if both features are stock.
4. If both chosen features grant the same verb, fly and fly, or swim and swim, keep one of them and take the unused listed Top or Bottom from either parent instead. You do not stack the same verb twice and call it a mix.
5. Write both sides on the body. That might be ears and webbing, or wings and a fox-brush, or whatever the pairing actually is. The visible mix is the mix, and it is not a hidden tell waiting to become a third feature.

You do not take three features from one custom Kind and then add a stock feature on top, and you do not take two hearth features. Community is still the card it always was. Kind still stacks with a Condition, so a character of mixed heritage Turns at the Tree, or is Kept, or is Struck, the same as anyone else, and Kind does not vote.

### Yumboe at the table

A Yumboe character needs the GM's **leave**. They are mythic-rare off Kumbaan, the isle on the far side of the storm-wall, so a mainland campaign that seats one has already decided that the wall let someone through.

If the table says yes, that character is the **full Kind**: Hollow-Hill, Moon-Waked, and The Unseen Hands, all three and nothing mixed in. They take no Hearth-Mark, because the three features already include the hearth. You do not split them into a Top and a Bottom, and you do not pair a Yumboe feature with a stock feature or with a mainland custom feature, because the good people do not come in halves.

A child of a Yumboe and anyone else is either a full Yumboe, with the same GM leave, or the other parent's Kind, with at most a story-tell, a detail people say about the child and not a feature on the sheet. There is never a top-and-bottom split that includes a Yumboe feature. Distance and the storm-wall are why a mainland table asks first.

A Condition arrives on its own clock, so a mixed-Kind child, and a Yumboe child, Turns, or is Kept, or is Struck, the same as the neighbour's child.

## Kitsune

Kitsune are slight and quick-eyed, fox-born, with a fox's ears and a brush of tail, and the cold pale light of **foxfire** always a thought away at their fingertips. They say *kitsunebi* for that fox-flame. They are born with the ears, the tail, and the fire already theirs, so the neighbours treat that shape as ordinary: a Kind is the body a person arrives with, and a Condition is a change that comes later, which this shape is not.

In folklore the fox is a shapeshifter and a liar, and the truth here is gentler, and it is real. A Kitsune can throw a **glamour**, foxfire drawn into the shape of another face, a borrowed voice, or a trick of the eye. The glamour is a seeming, not flesh. It changes what a person perceives, and it never touches what they decide. A Kitsune is as honest as their neighbours. They simply cannot quite be pinned down. They redirect, step out of the frame of a situation, and leave you looking at where they were, or at a face that isn't theirs. The tail is real and worn openly, and it is often the surest way to catch a glamoured fox, the one thing the fire will not hide.

Foxfire is the thread that runs through all of them, the ghost-light that leads travelers off the mountain path, the pale fire that hides the sacred white fox, the flame the old nine-tails were said to carry. Each kind of fox bends the same fire a different way.

Just as the world holds arctic, desert, and forest foxes, the fox-born come in kinds shaped by the country they were born to. The **Fox of the Wilds** is red-and-russet, a forest and hedgerow fox. The **Fox of the Snows** is white-coated, born to the cold north and the high snows. The **Fox of the Sands** is pale and huge-eared, born to the dry country. All of them are Kitsune. They share the same slipperiness and the same foxfire, and they marry and mingle freely, a child taking after one parent's line or the other. Each carries a gift bred into it by its home ground, and turns its foxfire toward a different old fox-story: the will-o'-wisp that leads a hunter astray, the pale flame that swallows the white fox from sight, and the ward-fire of the fox that always knows what is coming.

A Kitsune is not changed into anything. They are known for never quite being where you left them, a reputation for misdirection without deceit. The fox slips the trap, and it does not lie about the trap being there.

### Values

Kitsune prize the graceful exit and the unfought fight, the deal that dissolves before it comes to blows, and the door found before the room fills with smoke. They tend to distrust a cornered situation, because their instinct in a corner is to make a new corner, and they admire cleverness over force. They say, "Never the last one holding it." They keep their word carefully, precisely because they are so slippery that an untrustworthy Kitsune would be trusted by no one. A Kitsune's given word tends to be very solid, and they know it is their most valuable possession.

### Customs

Because they defuse rather than escalate, and because they can never quite be cornered, Kitsune work as mediators, brokers, and night-couriers. They carry delicate news between rival houses, hold the neutral ground in a negotiation, walk the dark roads by their own cold foxfire, and are trusted to be somewhere unnoticed and then gone again without a scene. Foxfire is a small everyday grace as well, so children are taught to make it before they are taught to read, and a home with a fox-born in it never wants for a soft, safe light. The summer before they Turn, the season when the town will gather to watch them, many spend a fox-summer at another Fox-ground, so the Wilds, the Snows, and the Sands stay one Kind. Older Kitsune, by tradition, carry themselves with more tails in the telling of it. The folklore of nine tails survives as reputation and as an honorific, not as literal biology and not as a power. The body keeps one brush, and the extra tails live in the telling.

### Daggerheart ancestry features

Every Kitsune has **Slip the Frame** and **Fox's Glamour**, and chooses one **Fox** at character creation. The Fox is a full feature. Each grants a lasting knack, the real animal's gift bred in by its home ground, and a foxfire signature move, the fox-spirit myth, each kind turning the same ghost-flame a different way. In a mix, the top feature is Slip the Frame and the bottom is Fox's Glamour. Your Fox stays with a full Kitsune. The full package takes no Hearth-Mark.

- **Slip the Frame:** When you would take damage from an attack, you can mark a **Stress** to slip aside: halve the damage, then immediately move up to **Very Close** range to a position out of the attacker's reach. If you end this movement out of line of sight, you are **Hidden**.
- **Fox's Glamour:** You can wrap yourself in foxfire-glamour to seem other than you are: veil your fox features to pass unremarked, cast your voice or a small sound a short way off, or throw a minor sensory illusion (a flicker of movement, a scent, a light) into a space within **Close** range. Once per scene, you can mark a **Stress** to **wear another face**: take on the convincing seeming of another person you've seen, holding it as long as you concentrate. The glamour is illusion, not flesh. It doesn't change your body or fool touch, and a creature that has strong reason to doubt what it's seeing can make an appropriate roll to catch the tell (a fox-shadow, a reflection that's a beat late, an animal's unease). This is disguise and misdirection. It changes what others perceive, and what they decide stays theirs.
- **Your Fox:** Choose the fox you were born to. Each has a lasting knack and a foxfire move:
  - **Fox of the Wilds** *(red / forest fox: the will-o'-wisp that leads travelers off the path).* *Knack:* you are at home in tangled, broken, or overgrown country: **advantage** on Agility Rolls to climb, leap, tumble, or move through difficult terrain, and you ignore its movement penalty. *Foxfire (Wisp-Lure):* mark a **Stress** to send dancing ghost-lights and false trails around a creature within **Close** range; until the end of its next turn it is drawn after the lights: its attacks against anyone but you are made with **disadvantage**, and you may immediately move up to **Very Close** range without drawing an attack. The lights are a lure, not a leash. The target is baited to look the wrong way, and still chooses where to move.
  - **Fox of the Snows** *(arctic fox: the sacred white fox that vanishes in its own pale fire).* *Knack:* you leave no trail worth following. Adversaries have **disadvantage** on rolls to track, trail, or find you, and you have **advantage** on rolls to **Hide** or move unseen in open, snowy, pale, or exposed terrain. *Foxfire (Cold Vanishing):* once per scene, mark a **Stress** to wrap yourself in cold white foxfire and let the eye slide off you, and become **Hidden** even while observed and in the open, so long as you don't take an action that would obviously reveal you.
  - **Fox of the Sands** *(fennec: the huge-eared fox that hears what's coming).* *Knack:* you see clearly in darkness and dim light as far as you would in daylight, and your great ears miss nothing: **advantage** on rolls to hear, notice, or locate hidden creatures, sounds, or movement. *Foxfire (Ward-Flame):* when you or an ally within **Close** range would be targeted by an attack, you can mark a **Stress** to throw up a sudden curtain of foxfire. The target gains a **+2 bonus to their Evasion** against that attack. What the Sands do is hear, and hearing does not cancel a surprise. Nine tails stay reputation and honorific, and they are not this Fox's body.

### Naming

The hearth-register is **Kusawe**, said koo-SAH-way, a light, slipping, sibilant tongue, full of *s* and *sh*. Daily speech is the local Maiethren drift, the local wear on the old tongue, and the fox-name is what they answer to when someone is trying to pin them down. They consider this funny. Given names include Raki, Nenyu, Wishi, Kiraye, Suti, Hatu, Ranyu, Sinro, Foryo, Heka, Sofan.

A Kitsune raised devout in Threnmaieth, where a clerk says the house first, may still be Raki at home and Maethaem, Raki on the roll. Where they live is three hearths, not a fox-country.

## Selkie

Selkies are sleek, cool-skinned people who carry a second self, a **sealskin** they can wear or set aside. They are sea-born, skin-kin, born to that skin, so it is a Kind, the ancestry they arrived with, and not a Condition, a change that came later. The skin is not a costume and not a disguise. It is genuinely them, a spare body kept close, and slipping it on or off takes only a moment. Distinct from the frog-folk, who live underwater and breathe there, a Selkie's gift is not breathing the deep. It is that you cannot keep hold of one. A Selkie in the water, in a net, in a grip, in a locked room, has a way out. The sea taught them that being held is temporary.

Out of the skin, a Selkie reads as an ordinary person with a few quiet tells: cool, faintly sleek skin, large dark eyes, and sometimes a little webbing at the fingers. Nobody stares, because the seal-blood shows the way any other Kind's does, and the real change lives in the skin, not the face.

A Selkie belongs fully to **two homes at once**, shore and sea, and so they move easily between those homes when the people on either side do not understand the other. The removable skin is a private, tender thing, and it is not a leash. Loaning it is a real intimacy, and having it taken is a real violation. Both are matters of story and trust, and never a penalty on the sheet.

### Values

Selkies value belonging without being bound, a deep loyalty freely given and resented the instant it is demanded. They are hard to detain and hard to hold onto. A Selkie loves fiercely and will not be caged, and a relationship or a job that starts to feel like a net is one they will quietly, gracefully leave. They carry a comfortable double-homesickness their whole lives and consider it normal. To always miss the place you aren't is, to them, simply the price of getting to have two homes.

### Customs

Because they can't be kept down and can't be kept out, Selkies work as rescue swimmers, cold-water divers and salvors, and go-betweens, the ones sent into the surf after a drowning, down to a wreck, or across the water to a community the shore-folk can't talk to. That same talent for getting free makes them trusted couriers for anything that must not be seized in transit. The sealskin is treated with real ceremony. Where a Selkie keeps their skin, who they would ever let hold it, and the day a child is first given the run of their own skin, are all significant and private. Kin from another strand often come for those skin-days, or the child spends a summer on a second coast, which is how a quay-born Selkie still sounds like the sea.

Surviving a drowning is the threshold that Strikes the Far-Voiced, a Condition that can arrive by that drowning rather than by birth. Selkies, being so at home in the water, have a complicated, storied relationship with that threshold.

### Daggerheart ancestry features

Every Selkie has all three. In a mix, the top feature is Second Skin and the bottom is Slip the Hold. Seal-Kin, the hearth feature, stays with a full Selkie. The full package takes no Hearth-Mark.

- **Second Skin:** You carry a **sealskin**, a second self you can don or shed in a moment. While you wear it, you ignore the effects of cold and deep water, you can hold your breath far longer than anyone should, and you always know which way is up and where the nearest surface or shore lies. You have **advantage** on Agility Rolls to swim, dive, or move through water.
  - *Anchored.* Your skin is your fixed point in the world. Wherever you leave it stashed, you always know the direction and distance to it and can find your way back to it unerringly, however far you've travelled or however turned-around you are. Loaning your skin, or having it taken, is a matter of story and trust, never a mechanical penalty.
- **Slip the Hold:** When a creature grabs or restrains you, or succeeds on an attack against you, you can mark a **Stress** to slip free like a seal from a net: you end any grab or **Restrained** effect on you, ignore any forced movement from that attack, and move up to **Very Close** range out of the creature's reach.
- **Seal-Kin:** Seals and the beasts of the sea treat you as kin, not as prey or stranger. You have **advantage** on rolls to calm, read, or call an animal of the sea. Once per scene, while you are in or near water, you can mark a **Stress** to let the sea itself warn you: the GM **names** what is approaching through the water, and until the end of your next turn the nearest seal-kin surfaces to aid you — a distraction that draws a foe's attention, a tow through the current, or a shove that carries you toward shore or safety. You may move up to **Close** range before the named approach arrives. What you receive is a warning and a help, and you do not receive an immunity.

Selkies are at home in the water: a long breath-hold, the cold and the deep set aside, and advantage when they swim. Ribbets are the frog-folk who breathe underwater. Seal-Kin calls seal-kin to aid. The phrase "cannot be caught off guard" is not written here.

### Naming

The hearth-register is **Sakoa**, said sah-KOH-ah, a liquid, rounded, coastal tongue, the sort of name you can say with cold air in your mouth. Daily speech is the local Maiethren drift. Given names include Lavo, Fesa, Moka, Dovo, Neson, Foaro, Veakea, Didel, Roadoa, Woanun, Laknea.

The hearth-name is spoken to the water the day a child is first given the run of their skin. The second name, a byname or a house-name, comes later, from the town that watched them Turn. Two homes, two names, and they do not consider this a conflict. Where they live is the coasts, not a sea-nation.

## Tengu

Tengu are lean, sharp-featured people of the high country, crow-born, with dark feathers through their hair and down their arms, black bright eyes, and broad wings that carry them off any cliff or updraft. They are born with those wings, so the feathers are a Kind, the ancestry they arrived with, and not a Condition, a change that came later. They are the folk of the peaks and the wind, born where the air is thin and the weather turns in a heartbeat, and utterly unbothered by either. Where lowlanders see a killing drop and a coming storm, a Tengu reads a launch point and a rising thermal. They fly, and the deeper truth of a Tengu is that the mountain never surprises them. They feel the rockfall before it slides and the squall before it breaks.

The crow-born of the Turning belong to the high wild the way Selkies belong to the sea: at home in the exact place that kills everyone else. The fox-born move unseen through society, the sea-born through water, the crow-born through the sky and the heights, and the Yumboe, the good people, keep the hill and the night.

The wings are always present, so a Tengu reads as Tengu at a glance, as ordinary in the towns as any other Kind.

### Values

Tengu prize the view from height, and the habit of mind that comes with it. Their instinct in any tangle is to get above it and look down: gain height, gain distance, and see the whole shape of a thing before acting. They tend toward blunt directness, the habit of people who are used to seeing exactly where a path leads and saying so, and they run out of patience with anyone who will not climb and look for themselves. They say, "The valley argues about the storm; the ridge just watches it come." They respect earned skill and hard-won height, and they have little patience for status that was not climbed to.

### Customs

Because they can go where a footpath cannot, and because they read the mountain's mood before it turns, Tengu work as high-pass guides, weather-callers, sky-couriers, and mountain rescue. They carry word over the peaks faster than any road, they call an avalanche warning down to the valley towns, and they go over the edge after a fallen climber. Tengu settlements cling to cliff faces and ridge-shelves, reached by wing or by rope. A Tengu's first solo flight is as significant a coming-of-age as the day a Selkie child is first given the run of their own skin, and that flight is often off the other perch, a cousin's crag or a courier family's clock-tower, so ridge-speech does not thin into twenty private winds. They keep a running, half-teasing rivalry with the Selkies, sky-folk and sea-folk each convinced their own element is the one worth belonging to.

### Daggerheart ancestry features

Every Tengu has all three. In a mix, the top feature is Wind-Winged and the bottom is Wind-Caller. The Mountain's Mood, the hearth feature, stays with a full Tengu. The full package takes no Hearth-Mark.

- **Wind-Winged:** You can fly. While flying, you can mark a **Stress** to fold your wings and **dive** on a target within Close range: treat it as a **Finesse** attack dealing **d8** physical damage using your Proficiency, and end the dive anywhere within **Very Close** range of that target.
- **The Mountain's Mood:** You read the high wild before it moves. You have **advantage** on rolls to sense danger in the natural world (a coming storm, rockfall, avalanche, unstable footing, treacherous terrain) and to navigate mountainous or aerial ground. You **cannot be caught off guard by an environmental hazard or a terrain-based ambush**. You always feel it coming in time to act or call a warning before it lands. It does not cover a person stepping from a doorway.
- **Wind-Caller:** You command the mountain wind the way the old fan-bearers did. Mark a **Stress** to call a sudden gust and choose one: **shove** a target within **Far** range a step closer or farther and knock it **Vulnerable**; **carry** yourself or a willing ally an extra move on the wind (a great leap, a saving glide down from a height, a boost across a gap); or **sweep** an area within Close range clear of smoke, gas, fog, or loose airborne projectiles until the start of your next turn.

**Cannot be caught off guard** is written once, on The Mountain's Mood, and it applies only to environmental hazards and to ambushes that come out of the terrain. The other three customs keep the fiction they already had, and they do it without that phrase. The Sands fox hears, and hearing does not cancel a surprise. Seal-Kin is a warning the sea names, together with aid from kin and a move the character can make. Hollow-Hill feels a gait through the ground, which is a sense of who is moving and not an immunity to being caught off guard.

### Naming

The hearth-register is **Gonan**, said GO-nan. It is clipped, windy, and high-country, and it does not sound like Kusawe, because this is ridge-speech rather than the foxes' slipping names. Daily speech is the local Maiethren drift. Given names include Teku, Kukra, Gobak, Hosen, Kotak, Buka, Rekren, Kuhos, Hrarat, Gonan. Gonan is also the people-name. Giving it to a child honors the hearth, the way naming a child after the ridge does.

A Tengu raised where people are witnessed often takes a perch or a pass as the byname, Teku of Highgate, Hosen of the White Stair, and not a harbour. Raised on a roll, they wear the house-name like anyone else, and the perch stays the name the ridge uses. Where they live is the ridges, not a mountain-empire.

## Yumboe

Yumboes are the good people, *Bakhna Rakhna* in their own tongue, the small, moon-waked hill-folk of the far isle of Kumbaan. They are born into that shape as a Kind, and not changed into it later by a Condition. They stand about waist-high to a mainlander, with pearl-pale skin that catches the light, hair like spun silver, and wide dark eyes made for the night. They live within the green hills of their home, a warren of hearths and long feast-halls hollowed under the turf, and they come up by moonlight, because they keep the night the way other folk keep the day. They are famously and defiantly hospitable. A Yumboe's table is open to native and stranger alike, laid with more than a small people should be able to carry, and waited on by hands and feet you can plainly see and bodies you cannot, the unseen servants that are the good people's oldest wonder.

Unlike Kitsune, Selkie, and Tengu, who are native to the mainland and unremarkable there, a Yumboe is mythic-rare off that far isle. Most people of Maiethorn, Strandoren, and Heskoren have only heard stories of the pearly, silver-haired good people from across the drowning sea.

They are an ordinary, warm, whole people who live somewhere almost no one can reach, so their strangeness is distance, not nature. They are not strange to themselves. They are a legend to a world that has never had them to supper. The mainland files them, and the hands with no bodies, under fairy-tale.

A Yumboe reads as a Yumboe at a glance, which is why they are remarked on the moment they leave their isle.

### Values

Yumboes prize hospitality above nearly all else. To feed a guest, and to be fed as one, is their deepest courtesy and their firmest bond, and a shared table binds them the way a sworn word binds the Bound, people who carry a contract they swore. They are a people of the threshold and the hearth, comfortable underground and after dark, unhurried, and quietly certain that the bustle of the surface at noon misses most of what matters. Because they are small, and because their home is hidden, they have never learned to meet force with force, so they meet it with welcome, with misdirection, and with a great many helping hands. They say, "No one is a stranger twice." They find the mainland's daylight faiths loud, and they find its treatment of anyone left unwelcomed baffling.

### Customs

On Kumbaan, life runs by the moon. The good people wake at dusk, they work and feast and dance through the bright nights, and they sleep out the glare of day in the cool of the hills. Their halls are famous, in their own telling, for feasts that outshine their means, because the unseen hands fetch and pour and serve, so that even a poor household sets a table like a lord's. They keep terrace-gardens and standing-stone marks up on the surface, and they keep the real life below. They fish the storm-walled shallows and, the old accounts say, were not above lifting a little corn from anyone careless enough to leave it out, a small people's small mischief, told fondly.

Off the isle, among the very few who make the crossing, a Yumboe is a walking wonder and knows it. They tend to become hosts, keepers, cooks, and go-betweens wherever they land, turning the one thing they always have, a welcome and hands to lay a table, into a place in a world that first meets them as a myth. A Yumboe abroad is usually the only one anyone in that town has ever seen. They do not run a season away from the isle. The hill-feast stays where the hill is, and a Yumboe on the mainland keeps the one register by being the only one in the room.

On the isle there is no Leaf-Fall, so no child there is Given a Condition, and a Condition that arrives because someone was Struck is possible but rare. Kind does not vote on that clock, and when a Condition does come it sits on the birth-shape the way it sits on anyone else's.

### Daggerheart ancestry features

Every Yumboe has all three: **Hollow-Hill**, **Moon-Waked**, and **The Unseen Hands**. This Kind is not a Mixed Ancestry partner. There is no split into a top feature and a bottom feature, and no pairing of a Yumboe feature with a stock feature or with a mainland custom feature. The package is the whole Kind, and it takes no Hearth-Mark. Playing one needs the GM's leave.

- **Hollow-Hill:** You are one of the good people: **small** (hill-folk stature), and born to the deep earth. You can move through gaps and spaces too tight for a larger creature, and you have **advantage** on Agility Rolls to squeeze, climb, or move through cramped, broken, or underground terrain and to **Hide** where there is cover to slip behind. Underground, or within stone and hill, you always know your depth and heading and **cannot become lost**. You feel movement through the ground: the **direction, number, and gait** (walk, crawl, or slide) of creatures within **Close** range who are on or under the earth. That is a tremor-sense, a sense of who is moving. It is not an immunity, and it is not the phrase "cannot be caught off guard," which belongs to the Tengu.
- **Moon-Waked:** You keep the night. You see in darkness and dim light as clearly as others see by day, and you have **advantage** on rolls to notice, search, or act **at night or underground**, anywhere the sun doesn't reach. When you take rest **under the open night sky or moonlight**, you clear **1 additional Stress** (or your party gains a little of the good people's ease: the GM's call on the fiction). Daylight does not harm you. Glare is when you are least at your best.
- **The Unseen Hands:** You can call the good people's unseen servants, **hands and feet plainly visible, but no body between them**, to attend you. At will, they perform small tasks within **Close** range: fetch and carry a light object, pour and serve, hold a door or a rope, steady a load, lay a table. Once per scene, you can mark a **Stress** to set them to **harry** instead: a flurry of unseen hands swarms a target within **Close** range, and until the end of its next turn choose one: the target has **disadvantage** on its next attack, **or** the next ally to act against it has **advantage**. The hands are a conjured seeming of many small helpers. They can distract, fumble, and fetch. Damage, a weapon, and holding a determined creature are outside their work.

### Naming

The Yumboes speak their own tongue, unrelated to Maiethren. It is the one tongue that owes nothing to the Leaf-Mother's liturgy, the prayers of the faith that grew up around the mainland's Turning Trees, because the Trees never crossed the storm-wall. The sound is open-voweled and warm, with soft clusters at the front of a beat, *mb, nd, nk, nj*, and a lilting, drum-like rhythm, so a Yumboe name sounds foreign the instant it is spoken.

- They call themselves *Bakhna Rakhna*, the good people.
- They call the isle *Kumbaan*, said koom-BAHN, the hidden home. Mainlanders call it the Sundering Isle.
- Given names include Ambaa, Njeela, Kumbo, Sambanka, Ndeya, Mbaraan, Njunda, Rakhilo, Sonkaa, Mbindaa, Yumbaka, Ndoolu, Sambiyo, Lunji, Yaaba, Saalo, Mbela, Soonke, Njili, Yendo, Waandi, Koriwaa.

Yumboes are too rare on the mainland to have grown a local byname-layer. They keep the one register, and that is why the introduction sounds foreign the moment it is spoken.
