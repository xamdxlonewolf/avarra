# The Four Continents

The map is a gradient of how far the Trees have reached. Read it outward and the old sacred country nearest the origin is dense with Trees, the merchant coasts beyond it still have many, the frontier runs thin, and past the last capes a storm-walled isle has none. That distance is the reach.

There is one Awakening Tree, **Thaeloren**. Every town Tree is a living graft of it, a piece of that first Tree kept alive on the road and planted in a new square so a child can Turn under it. People call that planted Tree a Turning Tree. The farther a land sits from the deep, old, densely grafted core, the thinner its reach tends to run. The Trees are fewer, younger, and more fragile. More children are hugged at the Tree and stay Kept, which means the leaves left them with no colour and no Condition, the change a person carries. More children miss a Tree entirely and stay UnTurned. More people are Struck later, alone, with no one there to see the change. How far a land sits from Thaeloren is also how long ago the wood arrived. The Ages chapter tells that history.

The old speech drifts along the same line. Names keep the old *th* nearest the Tree, where the liturgy is still almost unchanged. A frontier town wears the sound down and puts it back when someone prays. A dock town sands it off. You can hear how far a land sits from the grove in how people say the same word.

## What the names mean

The names on the map are old words glued together. Here is what they are saying the first time they appear as places.

| **Maiethorn** | MAI-eth-orn | the Mother's Land |

Each of the three large continents has one famous corner, named by gluing two old words together and wearing them down. The farther the corner sits from the Awakening Tree, the more the old sound has worn off.

The other named powers on each continent are built from the same old words. You will meet them as you go. **Language and Naming** will let you hear one as it appears, or make another.

## How the continents sit

Reading the world roughly **east to west**, which is also the direction the wood thins:

Maiethorn anchors the **east**. It is the land that has been settled longest, and the Awakening Tree still stands there, which is why everything the world calls "the center" is on this continent.

Strandoren lies **west across the Old Crossing**, close enough for constant, ancient trade. That is why Maiethorn and Strandoren are spoken of as the Old World pair. Strandoren's long indented coasts face the water, and that water is the busiest in the world. People call the two continents the Old World pair because the water is old and close, and because it made them into one argument. They do not call the water "inner."

Heskoren sprawls **further west and south**, beyond Strandoren's far coast. It was reached late and grafted late, and it is the edge of the settled world. Between Strandoren and Heskoren the sea widens into the West Water, the reach thins, and "the frontier" is the name people give to what begins on the far shore.

The Sundering Isle sits **alone in the far west ocean, or in the deep south**, past Heskoren's last capes, and the compass is not the part that is fixed. A band of killing weather, currents, and reef has wrecked most of the people who tried to cross, so the isle is off the edge of the reach entirely. It has no Tree, the grafts never crossed to it, and its people are mythic-rare on all three of the other continents, known mostly as a sailor's story.

What has to hold, if a later map turns the page, are the relative positions and the distances. Maiethorn and Strandoren stay close and old. Heskoren stays far and thin. The Isle stays sundered beyond the last capes.

Each of the three large continents carries several of the fifteen great powers. Threnmaieth, Lestrand, and Vaethorn sit one to a continent, so a traveller cannot meet all three by walking one road. The Motherfaith is one faith among several. The Watching, the Fair Hand, and the Old Ways live on the large continents, where they rub against the Motherfaith. The Open Table is the Isle's own.

## Maiethorn: the Motherland

*(mah-YETH-orn. The name is "the Mother's Land," spoken almost unchanged from the liturgy.)*

Maiethorn is the world's old heart, the continent people mean when they say "the center." It is old the way a cathedral town is old: long-settled, deeply grafted, every hill with its Tree and every Tree with its centuries. This is where the Motherfaith began, where the Awakening Tree grows, and where a child's Turning is least remarkable because it is most certain. The Trees are healthy enough that more children are Given here, a coloured leaf falling on them at the solstice, than in the world as a whole, so a hug at the Tree is less common here than elsewhere. The hug is not rare, and every Maiethorn school has Kept children. The Conditions chapter gives the shares.

The work and trade that runs on what people became goes to its full depth here. Deathless houses, the households of the Long-Lived, stand centuries old, Stilled infirmaries are part of ordinary care, and the Far-Voiced, whose feeling carries in the voice, are on every court and every quay. If the rest of the world is that fullness thinning as you travel outward, Maiethorn is the thick end the thinning is measured from.

Devotion here is the heaviest in the world, because the Awakening Tree stands on this continent. It runs through institutions and through politics, and living close to the holy brings pressure, not peace. The faith is old and contested. Threnmaieth counts its people and calls the count sacred. A stubborn heartland heresy claims the same center.

### Geography and climate

At the heart of the continent stands **Thaeloren, the Awakening Tree**, called the First Hand, in a deep old grove that is the destination of the world's great pilgrimage. The land around that grove is the most densely grafted on earth: an old, gentle country of rounded hills, river valleys, and towns crowned with Trees, worn smooth by long settlement.

A vast old-growth forest of motherwood and its wild kin rings that core. Here are the healthiest Trees, and the graft-nurseries from which the cuttings travel to the whole world. The First Seat and the faith's institutions cluster in the clearings. **Rothallo** sits a day's walk out from the wood. People still call it the Inner Close, and it stands inside Orenbren, not as a sixteenth power.

**The Rain-Wall**, which Lirorn calls the Thaw-Wall, divides the continent's watersheds. The climate on the settled side is temperate and seasonal, with four true seasons and winters real enough that a Leaf-Fall timed to the solstice means something. Guild towns keep mechanical clocks, and there is no printing press. Tengu hearths, the crow-born people of the high country, and Fox-of-the-Snows country sit on the high snows. Those are families on that ground, not a nation of one born people. The range is also why the east is dry.

Two passes cross the range. The **Noon Pass** is the old high road, and the water-line of the Thaw-Break still sits on its ribbon-notch. The **Shelf-gate** is the road Narol of the Pass took after that Break, when the old stone sat under the water. Narol is dead, so the household that inherited the toll still levies it, and the person does not. The river that runs west off the range is the **Core-thaw**, and Maiethlir sits where that water slows.

East of the Rain-Wall, on the leeward side away from the Old Crossing, the land falls into **dry hills and sand-country**, the one true dry reach in the known world. The Well-wash is a river in a kind Liren, the thaw when the water is gentle, and a line of silt in a cruel one. This is the hearth of the Fox of the Sands, and it is still Maiethorn: the same Trees, the same old *th*, and the same pilgrimage west toward Thaeloren. The weather here is the dry one. Towns cluster at wells and at whatever graft will take in thin soil. **Larbril** is where the west road meets the Well-wash, short of the pass. **Ornsael** is the smaller well-town farther into the dry, where people sow at a well. Open sand-country lies between it and Larbril, and the town sits inland. Children in the sacred core could already Turn at a Turning Tree while families here were still walking west to Thaeloren.

The western coast faces the busy **Old Crossing** toward Strandoren, the oldest trade route in the world. Ports here are ancient and rich, if less frantic than Strandoren's. The Hinge Shore is the face that says what a hull is carrying before anyone across the water prices it. Selkie families, the sea-born, are common on these quays, as they are on any old water.

### Reach

The reach is **full**, the densest and healthiest grafting anywhere. The core runs higher on the Given than the world as a whole. Long-Lived memory, the sworn word of the Bound, and Far-Voiced feeling are thick on the ground, and those three gifts are the pillars a witnessed life travels on. The Kept are fewer than elsewhere, but there are Kept children in every town, and in the more fervent districts they are quietly pitied as passed-over. The Walled Book, behind Rothallo's walls, teaches that a hug is rare. That is the Walled Book's lesson, not the count. Nowhere else are so many children Given, or so many of those three gifts at work. What the rest of the world treats as ordinary was written as if this fullness were normal everywhere.

The wood arrived with the First Cut. The years since that first successful planting are Cut-years. Maiethorn filled in Cut-years 0–80, origin-towns first. A cutting could travel about a day while the wood was still alive, and then it had to meet new ground. The **Rain-Shadow was slower**: the same continent, thin soil, and wells. Towns in the sacred core had Turning Trees, and their children stopped walking to Thaeloren, while sand-country families were still walking west. That difference is dirt, not a different gospel. The Ages chapter has the years.

### Powers

**Threnmaieth, the Tallied Crown** (theology high · reach high · governance high) is the wealthy, pious, surveilled kingdom near the sacred center. Its census, the **Threnhael**, treats the writing-down of people as reverent stewardship of the Mother's gifts. Maiethorn's full reach is why Threnmaieth has so much to count. Its seat is **Maiethlir**, a town on the thaw-river with its own Turning Tree. The grove of Thaeloren stands outside the crown's rule, and Maiethlir does not own it.

**Maiethvael**, the Gift-Realm, is devout and rich, and it keeps no census. Its capital is **Seinbrun**, a large city in the warm core, off the thaw-river where Maiethlir sits. People furnish a hall, a green, and medicine. The Down-Bank is the road below Maiethlir's Down Gate, and gift-folk live along that road. Seinbrun is farther on, down the Core-thaw and north of Maiethlir on the atlas, more than a day from Maiethlir. The rest of that walk is not recorded.

**Orenbren**, the Witness-Hearths, is the lodging on the roads to the grove. Pilgrims sleep in its inns, and that lodging is the power. The Awakening Tree stands outside its rule. Its capital is **Rothallo**, the walled city one day's walk from the wood. People still say the Inner Close, and they mean this city. The First Seat is in the wood. The Third Hearth is a road-house on the same pilgrim road, and the Mill-hold and Nelath are squares of the same lodging-country.

**Saelthael**, the Sown Tree, is the Rain-Shadow, and families here still walk west to a healthier canopy to Turn. Its city is **Larbril**, east of the Rain-Wall on the dry side, where the west road meets the Well-wash, short of the pass. **Ornsael** is the well-town farther into the dry, where people sow at the well.

**The Hinge Shore** is Maiethorn's coast on the Old Crossing. Before a hull is priced across the water, this shore says whether it carries pilgrims, a cutting in a pot, a person, or cargo. Its port is **Votaer**. Orentel is the city on the far shore. Votaer is the port on this one.

**Lirorn**, the Thaw-Land, holds the Rain-Wall and the snowmelt that feeds the lowlands. Its walled town is **Tasain**, in the valley below the Shelf-gate, on the west road south of Thaeloren on the atlas. Larbril is on the far side of that pass. The days between them are not recorded. The toll said at the gate is still Narol's, and the collector of that household says so.

Tengu, Fox, and Selkie live in hearths inside these six powers.

### Faith

This continent is the birthplace and the stronghold of the Motherfaith. Its institutions, its pilgrimage, and its authoritative tree-wardens all seat here. The sacred center is contested, not serene.

**The heartland heresy is the Watching**, whose own name is *Haelthael*. An older Tree-faith the Motherfaith grew over persists in Maiethorn's oldest districts, so the argument lives at the center rather than out on a frontier. They keep the Tree as witness and refuse the Mother-name. The Tree sees, in their telling, and it does not owe you a mother's name. Because the faith is old, it claims to remember what the world believed before the Trees were understood as the Mother's hands. Watchers keep a night at the **Seeing-Ring**, a ring of old stones on a hill in the old Watching country, north-east of Maiethlir. They will not call it a temple.

In the Walking Years, a Maiethorn child Turned by walking to Thaeloren. That inland trip is the Near Mile: days to a couple of weeks. The pilgrimage to Thaeloren still brings people from everywhere. The Ages chapter tells those years.

## Strandoren: the Shore-lands

*(stran-DOR-en. Everyday **Strandor**. The name is "the Witnessed Shore," and the docks have worn it smooth.)*

Strandoren is the crossroads of the known world, a continent of harbours. The coastline is so deeply indented with bays, sounds, and peninsulas that no point of it sits far from saltwater. If Maiethorn is the old cathedral heart, Strandoren is the market that grew up facing it: charter-towns, guild halls, banking houses, and quays. The Trees here are mature and many, the reach is high, and the Given are dense, but the faith has been worn light and folded into commerce, until a child's Turning is watched less as a sacrament than as the question of which guild the colour opens. This is where deathless banking, the finance of houses that outlive an ordinary span of years, reaches its full scale, and where Condition-labor buys the world's most advanced public works.

It is free, prosperous, and cool, and nothing here is policed, so your faith, your gift, and your marriage are your own, while everything is priced. The injustice is the market's. A person who cannot pay fades while a rich continent steps politely around them, and liberty is its virtue.

### Geography and climate

The indented coast is the fact the whole continent is built on. The shoreline is a run of natural harbours, tidal estuaries, and sheltered sounds. Every notable town is a port, and the sea is the continent's highway. This is the densest Selkie hearth in the world, a lot of shoreline rather than a nation of the sea-born.

**The Old Crossing** lies to the east, the narrow, crowded, ancient water between Strandoren and Maiethorn, and it is the busiest crossing in the world. Orentel faces it. The Hush-rate, the charge left from the peace that ended the fight over who priced this water, is still taken here.

**The West Water** lies to the west and south, the wider sea toward Heskoren and, far beyond the last capes, the Sundering Isle. Netstrand faces that water. Strandoren's boldest houses run the frontier trade, and almost none of them have crossed the storm-wall. The people who claim they have crossed are the ones who tell the Yumboe stories.

Inland, fertile lowlands follow **the Chart-run**, the river that feeds the port-cities. The climate is mild and maritime, wet and temperate. Mature grafted Trees stand thick along the wealthy coasts and thin only in the deep interior. Trenledd sits on the stretch of river rich enough to be written into the count. The inland stretches that waited while the coasts were already served are this run.

### Reach

The reach is **high**. The grafts are mature and abundant, especially on the coast, the Given are dense, and the three pillars are the machinery a huge commercial society runs on rather than a reverence. Long-Lived notaries remember what a contract said, the Bound close a bargain with a sworn word, and the Far-Voiced are on every dock and in every court. The Struck, people who changed later and alone, are vouched in briskly. An unvouched person is a transaction that cannot clear.

The wood arrived by sea in Cut-years 40–160, wealthy coasts first. A ship outran the work of planting every inland hamlet, and a rich coast could pay the First Seat for a Cutting-leave, the permission to carry a living cutting, while a village on the Near Mile was still waiting its turn. The interior, along the river, was planted later. Clerks of the Fair Hand call that arrival "the carrying," and they file it as cargo.

### Powers

**Lestrand, the Ledger Coast** (theology low · reach high · governance low) is the premier merchant power. Guild self-rule is carried as far as it will go, deathless-house finance runs at scale, and the Tithe, the upkeep a Condition needs if it is not to fade, is a private market. You buy your green, your novelty, your outlet, and you fade if you cannot pay. This is the richest and coolest corner of the world. Its seat is **Orentel**, on the Salt Quay. The White Note House keeps its office on that quay and keeps the old notes, and it is not crowned there. The guilds govern the city.

**Brenledd**, the Hearth-League, is many charter-towns under one compact. Each town keeps its own Tree and its own vouching, and the league stays a league of hearths rather than becoming one state. The throne-city is **Raitin**, a river-city inland, south-west of Orentel on the atlas, where the council sits and the shared notes clear. Six charter-towns sit around it, each with its own hearth. Raitin is where they meet.

**Leddvael**, the Reckoned Gift, keeps the Fair Hand as the city's own rite. Leaf-Fall here is a festival and a signing-watch. Its port is **Naenor**, down the coast south-west of Orentel and south of Raitin on the atlas. The Book-Hands stamp a signing and recite. They work on this water and keep no hall of their own.

**Trenledd**, the Worn Count, writes its people on a roll of tax and hazard. The count carries no hymn. Its city is **Lunbra**, on the Chart-run, where the roll is kept and the money is shown.

**Netstrand**, the Night Shore, faces the open ocean and quotes the trade to Heskoren. Its harbour is **Braetu**. One berth on that quay stays unlit, and no house is attached to it.

### Faith

The faith is worn light, and it is not absent. Here it has a rival with real doctrine.

**The Fair Hand** (*Leddoren*) is a mercantile theology. It does not read a Condition as a gift given, which is the Motherfaith's line. It reads a Condition as a bargain struck. The world, in this telling, is a ledger of deals, and what you became is what you, or your line, bargained for. That is a direct rival to the doctrine of the gift, and it is at home among the Bound. It reframes the Tithe as a term to be serviced, rather than an upkeep to be tended.

The Salt Walk was Strandoren's way to a Turning: a ship across the Old Crossing, then the Near Mile inland. The berth had to be caught in fair weather. A family that missed the last crossing missed the year. That hinge-month is Eolthael.

## Heskoren: the Sundered Reach

*(hesk-OR-en. Everyday **Heskorn**. The name is "the Outer Land," a frontier coinage, and the root does not appear in the old liturgy.)*

Heskoren is the edge of the world that the Trees reached last. Maiethorn is old and full of healthy Trees, and Strandoren is rich and tied together by its harbours, but Heskoren is young in wood, thin, and still waiting. It is a broad, rugged, half-settled country where the grafts came recently, take poorly, and sicken often, so that whole districts wait on a Tree that has not taken and on a gift that may never fall.

This is the continent that runs high on the Kept, the children the leaves hugged and left without a colour, and on the Struck, the people who changed later, alone, with no one there to witness it. It also has many UnTurned children, who reached no healthy Tree in the year they turned ten. It is where the Motherfaith burns hottest, because scarcity of the gift answers with more devotion rather than less: pilgrimage, longing, and a theology of waiting. In the Walking Years the same pattern was the rule of distance. The Long Mile from this continent to Thaeloren took weeks or months, and it cost more than most families could spend. Some districts still send children that far, or to a neighbour's Tree. Distance and history did the waiting.

It is warm, poor, and self-reliant: the kindest place in the world to a whole neighbour and the loneliest to an unvouched one, a person no one will stand for. Raiders are seldom what threatens a household. The danger is a lapsing Condition with no infirmary near enough to reach, and a faith that consoles what it cannot cure, a quiet cruelty in which scarcity of the gift shades into a doctrine that the people who went without were unworthy of it.

### Geography and climate

The country is rugged and various, with highland spines, upland moors, deep forests, and long river-runs down to a wild coast. The weather is bigger here than on the Old World pair, the winters are harder, and more of the ground is still untamed. These spines are not the Rain-Wall. That range is Maiethorn's, and it does a different job. Tengu ridge-towns and Fox-of-the-Snows hearths sit on the heights, the Fox-of-the-Wilds live in the deeper woods, and Selkie families hold the wild coast. A dense hearth is a family's livelihood on that ground. It is not a state of that Kind.

Settlement clusters wherever a graft has taken. A healthy Tree is the seed of a town, and the map of Heskoren's civilization is a map of its surviving Trees. Between those towns lies wild that no power claims, thinly held marches, and long roads from one Tree to the next. **Harrow's Green** sits above the Rise-water. Downstream is the ford of Brenod, Vaelun, and Ornath. The Seat will not learn those three names, so it keeps calling the place by the water. A day's walk from Vaelun, sometimes two, takes you into the Yield, the country of Vaelhesk, and not back along that stream. There you find the First Bowl, a guest-grove in an old green, not a second Harrow's.

**The frontier coast** is the east coast. It faces the West Water back toward Strandoren, and that crossing is the lifeline for trade and for new grafts. Eolvaeth is a vale behind this coast. It is a place people walk to in order to wait, not one of the towns where a new graft has just taken.

**The slate-shore** is the west coast, in Ornled. Sanbreo's beach faces the last capes and, far beyond them, the storm-wall. A hull that wants a beach here comes round the capes. Heskoren's westernmost capes are the closest settled land to the Isle, and the source of many people who have seen the storm-wall even if they never crossed it.

The climate is cooler, wetter, and wilder than Maiethorn or Strandoren. The Leaf-Fall is timed to the solstice everywhere, and here it is a hard-won event of a decade rather than a certainty that comes round every year.

### Reach

The reach is **thin**. The grafts are young, scattered, and prone to sickening, and the three pillars are stretched thin, with few Long-Lived to remember a neighbour and few Bound to vouch for one, so citizenship-by-witness is fragile. The unvouched Struck are a constant, aching problem, and the frontier meets that problem with faith where there is little machinery. Kept and Struck are both common, many children stay UnTurned, and the specialist care other continents take for granted is scarce. People here put the thinness down to distance and history. The Ages chapter calls the unfinished carrying the Grafting, and it is this decade's work, not a closed age.

The wood reached Heskoren in Cut-years 200–387, and the work is still incomplete. After Strandoren's coasts, cuttings died on the Long Mile. Each new Turning Tree had to grow up before it could supply the next town, and the queue planted the town that could pay. That gap is a history of distance and of a queue. Vaethorn will preach that the wait means the people were unworthy. The years do not agree. Where the Old Ways hold a planting, the first meal is the host-right. A planting without that meal is a claiming. **Harrow's Green** is the canopy the neighbours can see and use. The Three Hamlets Past the Ford — Brenod, Vaelun, and Ornath — are still walking their children to a Tree this year.

### Powers

**Vaethorn, the Waiting Lands** (theology high · reach low · governance low) is the frontier's touchstone. The faith is ardent, the Trees are thin, and there is almost no state. It is a warm, poor, half-lawless pilgrim edge, and its ruling mood is waiting: waiting for a graft to take, and waiting for a gift that may not come. Its injustice is a guilt, scarcity read as unworthiness. Its seat is **Eolvaeth**, a vale on the pilgrim edge, inland of the coast that faces Strandoren. People walk here to wait. Saelvaeth's march lies on another road.

**Ornled**, the Outer Ledger, is the secular frontier: the same thin Trees as the waiting country, and no congregation to tend them. Its town is **Sanbreo**, on the slate-shore of Heskoren's west coast, its beach facing the last capes and the storm-wall. It is on the brink of a small city, and the slate is kept there. A hull that wants the beach pays a beach-fee. A hymn is not what the town sells.

**Vaelhesk**, the Far Yield, keeps the Old Ways' host-rights. The first meal at a planting is the law they keep. The land itself is the seat, and there is no city. The First Bowl is the guest-grove, where a stranger is fed.

**Saelvaeth**, the Sown Waiting, is the march on the live edge, where new grafts are taking. The march-voice stands at **Natai**, west of Harrow's Green, in the country between Sanbreo and that grove. The days to either are not recorded. **Harrow's Green** is a grove-town in this march's country, and it lies in the power's orbit without being the seat. People Turn under its canopy. The hamlets' ford is the crossing Brenod, Vaelun, and Ornath use.

The four powers are fewer, poorer, and more scattered than the powers of the old continents. Wild country with no power of its own fills the space between them.

### Faith

Two faiths stand in tension here, and the friction belongs to the frontier itself.

The **Motherfaith** burns hottest here. It is a theology of longing and pilgrimage that grew up around the scarce gift, around the Trees that sicken and the children who wait.

**The Old Ways** (*Vaeloren*) are an animist and ancestor faith that predates the grafts. Their own sentence is, "The Mother's reach is new here. We had our own ways before her Trees came." They revere the Struck, and the Taken-In, people the wild took partway into itself and gave back changed, as the land's own children, and they resent the Motherfaith as a latecomer. The grievance has real theological standing.

## The Sundering Isle: Kumbaan

*(koom-BAHN, "the hidden home." Sailors say **the Sundering Isle** or **the Sunder**. Outsiders name the barrier. The Yumboes name the hearth.)*

The Sundering Isle sits off the edge of the reach entirely. It is the one land the grafts never reached, and dead wood is not a Tree. Where Heskoren is thin, the Isle is empty of the Trees the rest of the world is built around, and its people become what they are by their own path, outside the Leaf-Fall altogether. Nobody on the mainland knows how that becoming works. The storm-wall has wrecked most of the people who tried the crossing, so that in Maiethorn, Strandoren, and Heskoren alike a Yumboe is a figure from a sailor's tale: pearly-skinned, silver-haired, "the good people from across the drowning sea." Some of the stories are even true.

Kumbaan is an ordinary home, warm and hospitable, that happens to be nearly impossible to reach. The Yumboes are not strange to themselves, only to a world that has never met them. They are a hill-people who love a feast, served by hands you cannot see, and the rest of the world thinks they are a legend.

### Geography and climate

**The storm-wall** is a near-permanent girdle of violent weather, treacherous currents, and reef, and it is the reason the Isle stays sundered. Ships that try the crossing are wrecked, turned back, or simply never heard from again. There may be a season, or a way, in which the wall thins, and nobody knows which, or whether. The wall is not a ferry, and it is not shut for good.

**The Paps** are the hill country, the Isle's defining landform. The Isle is rolling green hills, honeycombed beneath with the dwellings the Yumboes actually live in. They live within the hills and come out by moonlight. What you see by day is pasture, terrace, and standing-stone, and the life of the place is below.

The climate is mild, wet, and moonlit, a temperate maritime island under the storm-wall's cloud. In the Isle's own tellings it is famous for its bright nights, because the people are nocturnal and moonlight is the light they do the day by.

The Isle is small, the one small continent of the four, an island you can walk and that its own people know in full, which is the opposite of Heskoren's trackless vastness.

You do not book a passage here. A hull out of Netstrand may try, because that coast is where the far crossing gets quoted, and a quote is not a date. Shore-sitters do not swim out and do not keep a rescue boat. If you live, you are on the sand or you are not, a shore-sitter decides that you are alive, the living are walked uphill, and the first night is a guest's night in a hall. At the second meal a table-keeper decides whether you are kin. That belonging holds on the isle, and a mainland watch will not take it as papers. The hall a sailor comes into is **Ndenjoo**, the Feeding Hill. Njunda keeps the table. The sand below that hall is Soonke's stretch, and Soonke is the person who sits it.

### Reach

The reach is **near-none**. There is no Tree on the Isle, and there never was a graft, because the grafts never crossed the storm-wall, so the count for this isle in the First Cut's years is never. Sailors tell of pots that went into the water, and those pots were dead wood that never took as Trees. The Yumboes do not Turn at a Tree, and whatever they become, they become otherwise.

### People: the Yumboes

The Isle is the homeland of the **Yumboes**, who call themselves *Bakhna Rakhna*, "the good people." They are pearly-white-skinned and silver-haired, they live in the hills, and they keep the moonlit hours. They are famously hospitable to a stranger, whether the stranger was born on the Isle or came off a wreck.

They live beneath the hills, come out by moonlight, and hold great feasts served by hands and feet you can see but bodies you cannot. That service at the table is the image the mainland repeats whenever it tells the isle. They are native to Kumbaan and rare beyond it, because the storm-wall keeps them home, so the few who do cross become the source of every legend the mainland tells. Their tongue and their faith are their own, and they owe the Leaf-Mother nothing.

### Faith

**The Open Table** (*Ndeyaan*) is the Yumboes' own cosmology. It grew in isolation, and it owes nothing to the Motherfaith. Their old accounts bind the good people up with the ancestors and the dead, and the tales told on the mainland call them "the spirits of the dead." They are a living people, with a faith of hill, moon, hospitality, and the staying-kin, which is what they call the ancestors and the dead kept with the living, and they are not ghosts. The Table-Keepers keep the hall, and the Shore-Sitters keep the sand.
