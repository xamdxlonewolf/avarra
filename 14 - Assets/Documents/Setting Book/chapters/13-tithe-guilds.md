# Tithe-Infrastructure and Guilds


Towns furnish green, novelty, and outlets in public, and three guilds stamp a licence when a Tithe can hurt a bystander. A ticket is a guild licence to practice a hazardous Tithe. The Slide is the paid week after that official door has closed.

Everyday people say **the furnishing**, or just **the greens and the halls.** This is civic work. Vaethorn, the Waiting Lands, says **the gift-works**. Lestrand, the Ledger Coast, says **the provision** the way it says *the wells*. Threnmaieth, the Tallied Crown, files **the counted channel**.

**In one sentence:** they keep people whole *enough*, in public, and they stop at the gate.

### Shape of the sector

- **Furnishing and ticketing are two jobs.** **Furnishing** keeps a provided-for Tithe from becoming a public hazard (green, an outlet, a new page). **Ticketing** marks competence for the three Conditions that can hurt a bystander (the Stilled, Far-Voiced, the Answered). A town may put both jobs in the same building. They are still two jobs. The Bound's hearing is at a table. It covers their Terms. The three body-licences remain the Stilled, the Far-Voiced, and the Answered.
- **Official work stops at enough.** A commons-share, a scheduled hour, a library that is open. The product is a person who can walk away until tomorrow. The Slide's product is a person who cannot. The town is glad of both and will not say so about the second.
- **They talk about the work in front of them.** Wells. Yards. A playhouse. A surgeon's college.
- **Each town keeps its own.** A town keeps its own lot and hall. A deathless house endows its own long-house. The licence-guilds charter *their* members.

### Who furnishes what

| Tithe | The official door | Lived house | What they will not do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Taken-In green | garden-commons / the greens / a leaf-lot | the Greens-Keepers | Will not follow you home. Will not rent you a private pot after the share fails. That is the Slide. |
| Far-Voiced outlet | the ael-hall (the venting-hall: a playhouse, and the scheduled vent) | the Hall-Keepers | Will not give you an unscheduled Cry. Will not keep you half-vented so you come back tonight. |
| Long-Lived novelty | the long-house of books | *no new order*: the deathless house that endowed it, still sitting the chair | Will not ration a page to own you. A junior who does that has started a week. |
| Unbound warmth | *almost none* | family; an attic of the Table-Keepers; the Slide's paid sitting | The sector cannot buy being known. Family, an attic, or a paid sitting is what there is. |
| Bound Terms | the honest table | the Book-Hands (civic year) + the Counterparty (the clause) | The hearing covers the Terms. The three body-licences remain the Stilled, the Far-Voiced, and the Answered. |
| Returned Purpose | *cannot be furnished* | a Book-hand may find a payable year; no one can supply a vengeance | No one can supply a vengeance. |
| Two-Bodied ranging | the ranging / a beast-yard | civic land, no guild | Self-paying on civic land. No ticket. |

**The three tickets** (danger-to-others only, held by the person, issued by the craft):

| Ticket | Condition | Lived house | Why a ticket at all |
|---|---|---|---|
| strictest | the Stilled | the Stillers | the Gaze (their stilling look) can hold a person forever |
| heavy, training-first | Far-Voiced | the Hall-Keepers (the same building as the outlet) | a Cry in a crowded hall: feeling that got out, and everyone in earshot took it |
| heavy, training-first | the Answered | the Element-Guilds | an untended element in a workshop |
| *not a body-ticket* | Bound | a table, not a guild-yard | the Whisper cannot command another person; the hazard is the contract, heard at a table |

**The Intake** is the shared desk of the three ticket-houses. It takes a raw Struck who is already live and has no childhood apprenticeship. The work is one catching-week in a shed, and then the apprenticeship.

### Fellowships

A mill, a hull, and a road have people who keep them. Those people are fellowships. The work does not licence a body. The Stillers, the element-guilds, the Intake, and the hall-keepers already stamp the hazardous uses. A mill-share, a hull-wright, and a road-mend do not stamp any of those. A Gale-Answered miller can hold a ticket for the wind and still owe a share of grain. The ticket licences the wind. The share is the grain he still owes the mill.

You are of a fellowship when you have kept the thing through a season and someone of it will say so. A fellowship belongs to its town. Any Kind may keep the work. Kumbaan does not keep these three.

The **mill-share** is the people who will say whose race this is. At the Mill-hold the share is still taken when the culvert is opened. When the race is drawn down, they leave one measure of last year's grain on the lip until the water comes back. They will not say why.

The **hull-wrights** know whether a hull will take water. They do not classify the cargo, set the Hush-rate, or sell a mouth that will say *I was there*. Before a new hull takes water, they walk it once empty, from stem to stern, and do not speak. They will not say why.

The **road-mend** fills a hole, clears a ford, and shores a culvert. They are not Road-hands. A Road-hand carries living wood. A mender carries a shovel. At Nelath the mend stops with the road. They do not cut the thorns back. At the first ford after a town, they leave the shovel standing in the bank overnight after a mend. They will not say why.

### The work people hire

Daggerheart's domains are jobs a town already pays someone to do. People do not enroll in a school of magic. Arcana is workshop heat, water, stone, air, or light. Blade is a weapon, a watch until morning, a pruning knife, a cohort walking children to a sound Hand, a town whose Tree can Turn them. Bone is where to stand. Codex is paper. Grace is a guest-meal or a factor's terms. The hazardous Cry stays the hall-keepers' ticket. Midnight is a shut door and a trade kept off the public square. Sage is ground, herd, weather, the bark of a young graft. Splendor is the room after harm. Valor is standing a gap.

A Spoken colour is the warden's sentence at Leaf-Fall. Hope does not buy a colour. A Condition is a becoming, with a Tithe. A craft does not Turn anyone. If the same person has both, each one does what its own card says.

### The long-house (novelty without a new order)

A Long-Lived house that has endowed a library is already Tithe-infrastructure. The founder is still alive. They still name the scholars. They still sit the chair. The money went out. The choice of scholars stayed.

Staff are clerks, often the Kept, unencumbered, able to fetch a book the founder has not read. A junior Long-Lived copies. There is no liturgical name and no college of librarians.

What they furnish is *something genuinely new*. What they will not furnish is a page at a time to a person who cannot walk in. A junior who extends a subscription one more week has started the Slide's measured week beside the long-house.

### The gate (the sector's shared courtesy)

Every official door stops at a line the town has agreed to call *enough*:

- The greens-keeper keeps the lot. They do not walk the wilting room.
- The hall-keeper keeps the hour. They do not open after the lamps for a voice that missed its slate.
- The long-house is open to a person who can walk in. It will not carry news to a Long-Lived shut in a room.
- The ticket-house trains the Given child and stamps the competent adult. The raw Struck is the Intake's problem, and the guild is glad to forget them if the catching-year is quiet.

The Slide begins where that line is. The guilds **pretend not to know**. That pretending is already part of the town.

The Slide's measured week is a different shape. The guilds keep the commons. They do not keep the person.

### How they live in the three corners

- **Vaethorn (the gift-works).** Tithe-provision as a public good with almost no guild to fund it. A commons that is a cousin's field. A blessing-to-practice said over a stiller who learned on a riot-line because there was no infirmary. You were helped, so the valley expects you to spend the gift as it sees fit. The Slide here is *the last pot* when the commons run out.
- **Lestrand (the provision).** Posted. A leaf-lot by the season. A release-house that is also the playhouse. Guild-tickets as master's papers. You can be priced out of staying whole. The Slide is week-rent after the season-fee failed.
- **Threnmaieth (the counted channel).** The crown furnishes, efficiently, even generously, and *holds* what it furnishes. A counted hall. A tended green. A crown-warrant. The crown can close that channel, and it calls the closing care. The Slide is the late week the channel will not admit failed.

**Kumbaan.** Almost no Given Tithes. There is no exported greenhouse or ael-hall. A wreck walked uphill is a guest.

A bed-list is plants. A hall-slate is hours. A guild-roll is practitioners of a dangerous art. The one kingdom that writes *persons and gifts* is still only Threnmaieth. Road-hands carry living wood. Greens-keepers keep soil. Hall-keepers keep an hour. The ticket-houses keep a licence.

## The Greens-Keepers

Greens-keepers keep the garden-commons, the greens, and a leaf-lot. They furnish *enough* living land that a Taken-In can rest and walk away. A private pot after the share fails is the Slide.

Everyday: **greens-keepers**, or **lot-keepers.** Liturgical: **the Saelhael** (SAYL-hayl). *sael* "sowing; the first green" + *hael* "whole": those who *keep you hale by putting you back on growing ground.* Vaethorn's second word is **Mother's-green hands**; Lestrand's is **lot-keepers** (ordinary, like saying *gardener*); Threnmaieth files them as **the Tended**.

**In one sentence:** they keep the lot, and they will not follow you home.

### Shape of the house

- **The house is the lot.** A town has a lot, a strip along the wall, pots in a yard the size of a tannery. Vaethorn's "lot" is often a cousin's field held in common. The house is whoever waters it and says who sleeps on which bed tonight.
- **Enough, then the gate.** A share is a night on living dirt, a season on a bed, a commons-hour. The person should leave able to work. A person who cannot leave is the Slide's work.
- **They talk about the garden.** Soil, blight, whose beans took.
- **A bed-slate is plants.** Rows, which bed is tired, which needs a year of beans. Names get onto it because a person is *on* a bed. The keepers will tell you it is horticulture. In Threnmaieth the crown already has another list.

### If you meet one

Dirt under the nails. A key to a gate in a wall. A slate. Ask where a Taken-In sleeps when the rooms are stone.

A stone town cannot keep its Taken-In without a lot. The faith, if any, is extra.

### Who comes, and when

| Who | Why they come | What the greens-keeper does | What they will not do |
|---|---|---|---|
| A Taken-In who works in town | The rooms are stone; the Tithe is due | Give a share: a bed, a night, a commons-hour. Write the bed, not the person, if they write at all. | Will not follow them home with a pot. Will not open the gate after the share-hour because the wilt has started. |
| A family, before the town sees | A parent is going to seed indoors | Open the lot if there is a bed. Send them home with the hour named. | Will not make the wilt a sermon. Will not tell the warden unless the person asks. |
| A Struck Taken-In, raw | They came through a green door later, alone | Find them dirt *now*. Point them at a door-keeper if the door still needs walking. | Will not name them Given. Will not licence a grove. |
| A Book-hand with a settling | The term *assumes* a greens-share | Honour the term if there is a bed. Mark the slate *full* if there is not: the book becomes *owing*, not this lot's lie. | Will not invent a bed to close someone else's book. |
| A crown-clerk (Threnmaieth) | The tended green must match the channel | Give the beds the roll already expects | Will not pretend a wilt off the lot is not happening. They also will not walk to it. |
| Almost no one who is not Taken-In | the ordinary week | Water. Turn tired beds. Argue beans. | Will not become a food-guild. Grain is a different door. |

### An ordinary week

They **open the gate.** That is the whole kindness. A Taken-In lies down on living ground. The tokens fill. They leave. The bed is turned. The next row is watered.

They bury last year's commons-leaf under a new bed. No one will explain this to a guest. A first-year who asks is told, by whoever is on the gate, "That is how the lot remembers."

When the queue is longer than the wilt, they still open the gate for the share they have. They close it when the share is gone. They do not walk the three streets to the room where someone is going to seed. They know a cousin still has a pot. They do not say the cousin's name.

A child of the house learns the work by carrying water and by being told not to take the key home.

They will not follow you home. They will not rent a private pot after the share fails. They will not steal a scion to make a grove. They will not call the share a gift on Lestrand, or a debt anywhere they can help it. They call it a share. They will not run a Kumbaan export.

### Who may keep a lot

**Not by Kind.** A human, a Kitsune, a Tengu who will water and keep the gate can be of the house.

**By practice first.** You are of the house when the lot is still alive in the dry month, and the town still sends its Taken-In to you.

**Condition leans, not Condition-gates.** Watering and blight often fall to a Taken-In who kept a commons, or a Kept with no Tithe of their own who can sit the gate year-round. The slate to whoever can read a tired row, sometimes a Long-Lived who remembers which bed failed twenty years ago. The gate at close to a Kept, or anyone who will lock it.

### How they live in the three corners

- **Vaethorn (Mother's-green hands).** The commons *is* a cousin's field. They will say they are tending a gift. The quiet obligation is the harvest: you were laid on the Mother's green, you will spend the green as the valley asks. When the field fails, the last pot is the Slide, and they will still call it tending.
- **Lestrand (lot-keepers).** Posted. A leaf-lot by the season. Coin on the gate. A Taken-In who cannot pay the season is not this lot's problem until they wilt in the street, and then they are the watch's. The Slide is the lot that still opens.
- **Threnmaieth (the Tended).** The crown furnishes the tended green and can withhold it. Beds match the channel. A wilt off the lot is a leak the roll does not want to file. The keepers look like gardeners. What the crown can shut off is the water.
- **Kumbaan.** No greens-keepers, and no lot is sent there.

### The gate

Whoever furnishes a provided-for Tithe holds power over its bearer. The greens-keepers hold that power *and refuse to carry it past the wall.*

That refusal is their kindness (they will not own you) and their cruelty (they will not walk three streets). The town is glad. A person who wilts *quietly* off the lot is not a public hazard. A devout town can say the gift was offered. A Lestrand quay can say the season was posted. The Threnmaieth crown can say the channel held.

The need for living land is the Tithe. People built the gate.

Tired beds are real. A lot can be botanically full and still have a queue. The keepers are also choosing who sleeps on stone.

### A bed-slate

Lestrand leaf-lot, nailed inside the gate. The verso is last season's slate, the tired rows still marked.

> *Beds 1-12: taken through the next moon. 13-16: beans, do not lie here. 17 is tired; it will take next year. Share is one night or one hour at open sky, then the gate. If you cannot come, send no one. A bed is not a letter. We do not walk the rooms. If you have already wilted, the lot is still the lot. Come when we open. If you cannot wait, we do not know a pot.*

On its face: competent gardening, a little cold, a commons keeping itself honest. The sentence "we do not know a pot" is the lie on the slate. The child who carries water can name the cousin. The slate cannot.

## The Hall-Keepers

Hall-keepers keep the official outlet and the Far-Voiced ticket in the same building. A Far-Voiced person needs both the scheduled hour and the licence. The ael-hall is a playhouse, and it is where a voice is emptied on that schedule. They furnish *enough* voice that a person can walk until tomorrow. A room that keeps you after the lamps, never quite emptied, is the Slide.

Everyday: **hall-keepers**, or **ael-hands,** in every register. A crossed-out roster may still show *Aeloren*. No current hall uses it as a title. Vaethorn's second word is **gift-hall folk**; Lestrand's is **release-hands** (ordinary, like saying *box-office*); Threnmaieth files them as **counted-hall clerks**.

**In one sentence:** they sell you an hour in which you may feel in earnest, and they will not open after the lamps.

### Shape of the house

- **One building, two jobs.** The hall furnishes the outlet and stamps the Voice-ticket. The hall-keeper who says "your hour is the third" is the one who says "you may stand in a crowded room."
- **The hour is enough.** A scheduled vent: song, weeping, a relay-call, a laugh that empties the chest. The person should leave emptied and able to work. The Slide's shut room is one outlet that is never quite enough. This house wants you gone until tomorrow.
- **They talk about the playhouse.** Bills, benches, who carries the high note.
- **Each hall keeps its own slate.** The oldest playhouse on a Lestrand quay is old. The next town's hall does not write to it for leave.

### If you meet one

Ink on a finger, or chalk. A slate of hours. A hall that smells like sweat and lamp-oil and last night's joy. Ask where a voice can be loud without becoming a Cry in the market.

A town with Far-Voiced and no hall has a Book-hand writing *owing* and a watch waiting for the market.

### Who comes, and when

| Who | Why they come | What the hall-keeper does | What they will not do |
|---|---|---|---|
| A Given Far-Voiced child, after the colour | The voice must be trained before it is a hazard | Apprentice them: breath, projection, venting on purpose. The ticket comes when the town can stand next to them. | Will not Speak the colour. The warden did that. Will not call the voice a gift on Lestrand. |
| A working Far-Voiced (performer, crier, witness) | The vocation *is* the vent: self-paying | Give them the stage, the relay-post, the stand. Stamp the year if the ticket is due. | Will not invent a crisis so they can vent off-slate. |
| A Far-Voiced who lost the honest stage | Isolated posting, dull labor; the Undertow is close | Sell or grant a **scheduled hour**. Empty them. Send them out. | Will not open after the lamps. Will not keep them half-full so they return tonight. |
| A Struck Far-Voiced, raw (the drowning-door) | The voice came back untrained | Hand them to the Intake. Sit the first hour *with* Intake if the Cry is already in the room. | Will not stamp a ticket on a raw throat. Will not leave them in the market. |
| A Book-hand with a settling | The term *assumes* a seventh-day hour | Honour it if the slate has a hole. Mark *full* if it does not. | Will not pretend Greyhull has a bench when Greyhull is full. |
| Anyone who came for a play | The hall is also the playhouse | Sell a bench. Take the coin. Let the working voices pay their Tithe *as* the show. | Will not stop a performance to process a ticket. The show *is* the Tithe for the people on the boards. |

### An ordinary week

**Morning: the slate.** Hours named. Relay-posts listed. A child's breath-lesson in the cold hall. The ticket-desk is a stool by the door.

**Evening: the house.** Feeling in earnest, on purpose, where a neighbour paid to hear it. This is the pleasant version of the Tithe, and it is also the town's entertainment. No one calls that a coincidence.

**The unswept bench.** One seat is never swept. A keeper says, "That's the first Cry's seat." No one will explain which Cry. A first-year who sits there is moved without a sermon.

When a voice misses its hour, the keeper does not open the side door. They know a room that still shuts. They do not say the room's name.

They will not stamp a raw Struck. That is the Intake. They will not rewrite a Bound's Terms because a voice was used to swear them. They will not run a mission-hall on Kumbaan.

### Who may keep a hall

**Not by Kind.** A Selkie crier and a human neighbour hold the same stool if they can keep a slate and hear when a voice is about to break.

**By practice first.** You are of the house when the town still comes to your hour, and when you have pulled a child off a Cry before it landed.

The boards, the relay, the stand often go to Far-Voiced whose vocation *is* the vent. The ticket-stool to a Given Far-Voiced who trained young, or a sharp Kept, or a Long-Lived who remembers last century's Cry. The first hour with a raw throat to whoever will sit it. Intake borrows this room.

### How they live in the three corners

- **Vaethorn (gift-hall folk).** A barn, a festival, a blessing said over the first note. Almost no ticket-stool. A Struck throat is a late gift and a hazard the valley will stand around, singing, until someone who has been to a real hall arrives. You were given the hour, so the valley expects you to spend the voice as it asks: crier, witness, hymn.
- **Lestrand (release-hands).** The playhouse *is* the hall. Posted hours. Guild-ticket as master's papers. A voice without coin for a bench still has a labour-hour at the back if they will sweep. A voice without even that goes to week-rent.
- **Threnmaieth (counted-hall clerks).** The crown furnishes the counted hall and can withhold the hour. Tickets are crown-warrants in all but the stamp. A Cry off-slate is a column. The hall looks like a festival. What the crown can withhold is the hour.
- **Kumbaan.** No ael-hall.

### The scheduled hour

The hall sells (or grants) the right to feel in public *on time*. Off-time is a ticket problem. A performer who lost the honest stage still has the hour, if the slate has a hole, if the season-fee was paid, if the crown still lists them.

The town is glad. An emptied voice is not a Cry in the market. A devout town can say the gift was aired. A Lestrand quay can say the bill was posted. The Threnmaieth crown can say the channel held.

The eighth week, the week the passage-money fails, is not kinder because someone called the hour a gift. A voice must vent. People built the slate.

Self-paying voices subsidize provided-for ones when the polity lets them. The show *is* some people's Tithe and other people's night out. Lestrand calls this the market. Vaethorn calls this tending. Both can be true in one barn.

The first Cry's bench stays unswept. That is how the hall remembers.

### An hour-slate

Lestrand release-house, chalked on the door beside tonight's bill. The verso is yesterday's slate, not washed.

> *Third hour: the boards (paid). Fifth: relay-drill, apprentices. Sixth: open hour, two benches, no coin if you sweep. After the lamps: the house is shut. A missed hour is a missed hour. We do not keep a throat half-full. If you have already cried in the street, this is still the house. Come at open, not now. If you cannot wait, we do not know a room.*

On its face: a playhouse being practical, almost kind (the sweep-hour). The sentence "we do not know a room" is the same lie the greens tell. The first Cry's bench is not on the slate. Everyone knows which one it is.

## The Stillers

The Stillers licence the Stilled and run the infirmaries. The guild issues the ticket. The state watches the guild. It does not keep a list of every Stilled person.

Everyday: **stillers**, or **the Stillers' Guild.** Liturgical: **the Stelhael** (stel-HAYL). *stel* "to still; to hold from moving" + *hael* "whole": those who *keep you hale by holding you still.* Vaethorn's second word is **blessing-surgeons**; Lestrand's is **ticket-surgeons**; Threnmaieth files them as **crown-stillers**.

**In one sentence:** they will save you on the table, and they will make you drill until the Grey is gone. The Grey is the stillness that collects when they use the Gaze. Left unpaid, it locks a limb toward stone. The ticket is competence. The infirmary's pace follows the surgeon's Grey.

### Shape of the house

- **The same kind of roster as the other two tickets.** The Hall-Keepers' Voice-ticket and the Element-Guilds' element-ticket work this way: a roster of people who practice a dangerous art.
- **The infirmary and the yard are one guild.** The infirmary is the table. The yard is spar, patrol, and the riot-line. Using the Gaze *fills* the Grey. The yard empties it. The guild's best work creates the guild's Tithe. That is the job.
- **They talk about a surgeon's college.** Apprentices, a code, a ticket like a master's papers.
- **The charter is local.** A rich core infirmary is rich. A frontier stiller is one tired person. The state watches the charter.

### If you meet one

Calm. Flat-eyed when they are working. Hair that stirs when something nearby has gone too still. You meet them in an infirmary, on a drill-floor, or on a line that has not yet become a massacre. You find the guild hour when you need a ticket.

They are useful the way a surgeon is useful. A town without one makes do with a rope-and-prayer.

### Who comes, and when

| Who | Why they come | What the stiller does | What they will not do |
|---|---|---|---|
| A Given Stilled child, after pale stone | The Gaze must be trained before it is a forever | Apprentice them. Drill. Theatre. The ticket when they can still a wound and not a person. | Will not Speak the colour. Will not put them on a riot-line in the first year. |
| A patient | Bleeding, poison, a knife that needs a still field | Hold the moment. Work fast. Walk to the yard after and burn the Grey. | Will not hold a will. Cannot. Will not still a person as punishment. |
| The watch, or a street that is turning | A brawl about to become a pile of bodies | Stand the line. Still the swing, not the person, if they can help it. | Will not become the town's executioner. A forever-hold is a crime the code names. |
| A Struck Stilled, raw (they were stone) | The Grey is already in them, unpracticed | Hand them to the Intake. Sit the first drill *with* Intake. Reverse what can still be reversed. | Will not stamp a ticket on a raw Gaze. Will not leave them in the square. |
| A charter-clerk | Someone unlicensed stills in public | Protect the charter: take them in, or swear they were not of the house | Will not write the town's people down. The guild-roll names stillers. The Threnhael, Threnmaieth's census of persons and their gifts, is a different list. |
| Anyone who wants the Gaze as a weapon | no honest errand | Refuse. The code is the ticket. | Will not sell a forever. Anyone who does is not of this house. |

### An ordinary week

**Theatre.** The pleasant horror outsiders remember: a gaze that stops blood, a surgeon who must then go and *move*. Between patients they drill. They spar. They dance, badly, because dance works the Grey out. No one calls this therapy in the core; they call it the job.

**The doorknob.** They touch a doorknob before they look at a patient. If the knob has gone still under an unwatched Gaze, the room is already wrong. A first-year who forgets is sent back to the yard.

**The yard.** Exertion on purpose. The Tithe paid as professional identity.

**The ticket-stool.** A master's mark. Held by the person. The guild *knows its members* the way any craft does. In Lestrand the ticket can come to include a place, a chair, and a name on a door. That fight stays in that house. Other towns do not have to keep it.

When a raw Struck arrives, the paying floor loses a master for a week. The guild is not grateful. That week is the Intake.

They will not still a will. They cannot. They will not pretend they can to frighten a street. They will not let the Grey run to a public seize if they can still drag the person to the yard. A seize on the floor is a ruined charter. The roll names stillers. It does not name every Stilled person, and it is not Threnmaieth's census. They will not export a college across the storm-wall.

### Who may hold a ticket

**Not by Kind.** The Gaze does not care what you were born.

**By practice first.** You are of the house when you can still a wound and then *move*, and when the town has watched you do both.

Theatre and the line go to the Stilled. The Tithe and the vocation are the same shape. The yard to anyone who will make them *move*: a Kept drill-master; a Two-Bodied who will run them. Intake week to the master who can be spared (often the one the floor can least spare), or a Struck who remembers stone and will sit the first drill.

Skeptics are welcome. The Gaze does not require a Mother. Believers in Vaethorn will still say a blessing-to-practice. Both can hold the same ticket.

### How they live in the three corners

- **Vaethorn (blessing-surgeons).** One tired stiller, a blessing-to-practice, a riot-line that is also the only theatre. Almost no college. You were trained, so the valley expects you to stand every line it has. The rope is still how a raw Struck is met if the stiller is three valleys away.
- **Lestrand (ticket-surgeons).** Superb infirmaries. Guild-ticket as property-adjacent. You can be priced out of the chair. The charter is a commercial good. Unlicensed practice threatens the charter as much as the street.
- **Threnmaieth (crown-stillers).** The crown wants the ticket as a crown-warrant and the roll as a column. Some masters cooperate, and call that stewardship. Some refuse: the guild-roll names stillers, and the Threnhael is the census. The Gaze that saves you runs through a channel that can be withheld from a household the crown dislikes. It is the darkest infirmary in the world, and it is still a real infirmary.
- **Kumbaan.** A travelling stiller is a guest. The guild has no chapter there.

### The Grey as labor

The guild's best work fills the Grey that will lock a limb if they do not then labor. So the house needs them on the table and in the yard. The charter needs them never to lapse in public. The infirmary's pace is set by the surgeon's Grey as much as by the patient.

It is a hospital that runs on its staff's Tithe.

The ticket-as-property argument (Lestrand, leaking elsewhere) is the second face: competence lives in a person; the chair can start to live in the guild.

Having the Gaze does not make the drill optional. A gaze costs. People wrote the roster.

Intake is badly paid. The raw Struck who most need a week in the shed are the week the floor can least spare. The paying floor treats that week as a loss.

### A Grey-drill roster

Core infirmary, chalked on the yard wall. The verso is yesterday's, washed badly.

> *After third theatre: spar, full glass. After the line: the long run, no Gaze. Apprentices: the knob first, always. A Grey that stays overnight is a Grey we file as lapse, not as rest. The shed is not on this wall. If you were stone last week, you are not on this wall. Come to the door that does not face the street. We do not write that door. We do not still a person to close a day. Touch the knob. Then look.*

On its face: a college keeping its people hale, the code in a work-order. The paying floor will not write Intake's door on this wall. "A Grey we file as lapse" makes a tired surgeon a charter problem. The person may need a longer run. They will not say why the knob comes first.

## The Element-Guilds

The third safety-ticket, and the craft that pays its own Tithe: the Answered, sorted by element. Four houses share one way of licensing. Each house works a different element.

Everyday: **the element-guilds**, or the **Ember-house**, **Tide-house**, **Root-house**, **Gale-house.** Umbrella: **the Crae** (kray). *crae* "craft; the working of an element." A single Answered is *of the Crae*; they are *of Ember* if you need the door. Vaethorn's second word is **gift-shops**; Lestrand's is **the craft-houses**; Threnmaieth files **the Reckoned Craes**.

**In one sentence:** the shop is how an Answered pays the Tithe. Leaving the shop leaves the element idle, and idle is the hazard.

### Shape of the house

- **Four houses, one way of licensing.** Ember keeps smiths, glass, and firefighting. Tide keeps hulls, dyers, and any work that needs water to behave. Root keeps masons, potters, and the carrying-earth a Road-hand may buy. Gale keeps sails, mills, instruments, and the wind a Tengu already knew. They share a ticket-logic and an Intake shed. An Ember-master does not set Tide's fees.
- **Self-paying until you leave.** Earnest work slakes the Tithe, so the guild is the upkeep. Bench an Answered and they are unemployed and idle. Idle is how an element leaves the shop.
- **They talk about the craft.** Apprenticeships, shop-rules, which coal may go dark.
- **The name is shared. The shops are not.** An even-tempered Root potter and a short-fused Ember smith are both of the Crae, and the work is not the same.

### If you meet one

Sweat, clay, salt, or chalk-dust, depending on the door. A shop-rule nailed up. A ticket-stool that looks like any master's. You would hire them to build a hull. Ask, "Who may let an element speak in a crowded yard?" That answer is the ticket.

A town already needed smiths and masons. The Condition made those guilds necessary again, so the work does not let the element go.

### Who comes, and when

| Who | Why they come | What the house does | What they will not do |
|---|---|---|---|
| A Given Answered child, after lamp-amber | The shade named the element; the shop must catch them young | Apprentice to *that* door. Earnest work. The ticket when the element answers the hands and not the room. | Will not Speak the colour. Will not switch a child's element because the town needed Tide. |
| A working Answered | The craft *is* the Tithe | Give them the shop, the watch-rotation, the fire-crew, the escort. | Will not bench them "for a rest" without a plan. Idle is the hazard. |
| An Answered who lost the shop | Grief, a wrecked yard, a lord who benched them | Find earnest use *now*: a crew, a demolition, a night at the bellows. | Will not rent them a week of "just enough spark." That rented spark is the Slide. |
| A Struck Answered, raw | A threshold let an element in later, untrained | The Intake. The right door, not the nearest fire. | Will not stamp a ticket on a raw Reach. Will not put them on a hull the same day. |
| Anyone who wants a thing made | A hull, a glass, a wall, a sail | Make it. Charge as a craft charges. | Will not let an unlicensed cousin "just coax the stone" in the street. The charter is the street's safety *and* the shop's monopoly. |
| A Road-hand needing carrying-earth | Root can coax living earth to travel | Sell the earth. Do not walk the scion. | Walking the wood stays with the Road-hands. Their liturgical name is the Thaelvaeth. |

### Four doors

| Door | The work | The shop-rule you notice | If left idle |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ember** | smith, glass, fire-crew | they never let the last coal go dark | heat finds a place to be |
| **Tide** | hull, dye, anything wet | a cup of yesterday's water on the lintel | water finds a place to be |
| **Root** | mason, potter, carrying-earth | clay under the nails they will not wash on a work-day | stone and soil find a place to be |
| **Gale** | sail, mill, instrument | a window that is never fully shut | wind finds a place to be |

The element goes somewhere. It does not seize a person's will. The guild's job is to make that place the shop, not the crowded hall.

### An ordinary week

The shop. The rotation (fire-crew, escort, the watch that is also Tithe). The ticket-stool. The shop-rule no one explains to a guest.

When a master is pulled to Intake, the paying floor loses a Reach for a week. Ember hates this during a dry month. Tide hates this when a hull is mid-bend. That hatred is why the paying floor resents the shed.

They will not switch an element to suit a labour shortage. The shade named it. They will not bench "for their own good" without earnest use waiting. That is how a room lets go. They will not stamp a raw Struck. They will not export a Crae-college to Kumbaan.

### Who may hold a ticket

**Not by Kind.** Any Kind may hold the ticket. A Tengu who is Gale-Answered is common. The element is not reserved to a Kind.

**By practice first.** You are of the house when the element answers your work and not the rafters.

The shop goes to the Answered of that element. Fire-crew and rescue rotations to Ember and Gale, also Two-Bodied on the line and Returned in the wreck. Carrying-earth to Root, also Taken-In who will not call it a grove-rite. Kept labor is the free generalist around the Reach: strikers, pullers, the person who holds the mundane tool. Lestrand's *free*. Vaethorn's *Waited*, still useful.

### How they live in the three corners

- **Vaethorn (gift-shops).** One mixed yard, four tools, a blessing-to-practice. You were answered, so the valley expects you to spend the element as it asks: the mill, the wall, the fire-line. Idle is rare because the valley will not let a gift sit.
- **Lestrand (the craft-houses).** Deep shops, posted tickets, monopoly-teeth. You can be priced out of the *chair* and still need the work, which is how a person slides from self-paying to exposed overnight. The charter is money and safety in one paper.
- **Threnmaieth (the Reckoned Craes).** The crown wants element and person on the same column. Workshops are channels. A benched Answered is a withheld Reach. The shop looks like a gift-works. What the crown can withhold is the fire, the slip, the stone, or the wind.
- **Kumbaan.** A wrecked Tide-Answered is a guest. There is no Tide-house on the sand.

### The shop you cannot leave

Self-paying holds until you want to stop. The Tithe does not care that you buried a child, that the yard burned, that you are tired of glass. Idle, the element acts without the person's hands.

So the guild's kindness, a shop, a crew, a rotation, is also how the guild holds you. Leaving is a Tithe-crisis. Vaethorn calls that a vocation. Lestrand calls that a contract. Threnmaieth calls that a channel.

A year away from the shop is not safe. An element answered. People built the shop-rule.

The last coal, the cup of yesterday, the unwashed clay, the window that will not shut: those are how each door remembers.

### A shop-rule

Ember-house, Lestrand glass-yard, nailed above the last coal. The verso is a Tide-house rule from the next quay, used as backing.

> *The last coal stays. A Reach used in play is not a Reach used in earnest: the ticket will not have it, and the room will. If you cannot work, you will still work: the fire-crew, the night bellows, the watch. We do not bench. We do not rent a spark. The shed is not this door; raw hands go to the door that does not face the street. If you have already let go, this is still the house. Come to the coal, not to the square. Gale will not take you. Tide will not take you. The coal will.*

On its face: a craft keeping its people steady, a little fierce, a monopoly wearing safety. "We do not bench" also means you may not leave. The Tide-rule on the verso says the same thing about a cup of water. The two houses did not copy each other. They did not have to.

## The Intake

The Stillers, the Hall-Keepers, and the Element-Guilds share one shed. It takes a dangerous Struck in hand for the catching-week. That person had no childhood apprenticeship. The Slide is the paid week that keeps a person. This shed tries to make them safe enough to leave.

Everyday: **the Intake**, or **the raw-desk**, or **the shed.** Guild-cant: **the catching-year.** There is no liturgical name. The paying floor will not write the office on the wall. Vaethorn says **the late-catching**; Lestrand says **the emergency ticket**; Threnmaieth files **the unwitnessed warrant**.

**In one sentence:** they have a week to make a live hazard into a person the town will stand next to, and if they succeed, no one writes it down.

### Shape of the office

- **The Struck is brought to the shed.** A runner arrives, or a master is pulled off the paying floor. When they travel, the nearest ticket-house is three days away and a rope is already in someone's hand. That is distance. Road-hands already walk the long road. Intake does not.
- **Three ticket-houses, one shed.** A raw Gaze goes to the Stillers. A raw throat goes to the Hall-Keepers. A raw Reach goes to the Element-Guilds, to the right element. The wrong house is how a Cry gets stilled quiet, or an Ember is put on a bench to sing. The shed is shared. Each hazard still has its own door.
- **They catch, they steady, and they hand the person to the apprenticeship.**
- **Success is silence.** Guild leadership is indifferent, and on a bad week hostile. A quiet catching-year is a week the floor lost. A loud one is a charter hearing. Nobody changes the guild's mind about the shed. A grudging extra pair of hands is the most the yard will grant, and they will take them back.

### What the catching-week requires

- **The task.** Take a raw Struck (Stilled, Far-Voiced, or Answered) in hand and begin the apprenticeship at once, before the town meets them with a rope. What they are racing is time: the hazard is already live, and the town wants them gone.
- **What they have.** The shed that does not face the street. It is not the theatre, the boards, or the paying shop. One master pulled off the floor, whoever will sit, maybe a Kept who has no Tithe of their own. They can take the person if the person will come. They cannot overrule a frontier rope. They cannot stamp a ticket this week. The charter is at risk if the person harms someone, or if they refuse the Struck and the town later says the guild left a hazard. Leadership forgot them on a good day and will scapegoat them on a bad one. The yard lends a hand only while the week stays quiet.
- **Who sits.** Any Kind may sit it. The floor does not send its favourites. The master who can be spared is often the one who cannot. A Kept will sit because they are *free* and the floor resents that. A Struck who remembers their own door will sit. A junior ticket on probation will sit. The floor assigns the week, or last year's sitter hands it on. People are rarely recruited for it.
- **What counts as success.** The person is still alive, still here, and beginning to be safe to stand next to. The town pretends this was always going to happen. The guild does not apologize.

### If you meet one

Tired. Chalk on a sleeve. A key to a door the slate does not name. You would miss them if you came in the front of the infirmary or the playhouse. Ask, "Where did the one who was stone yesterday go?" Or you are the runner, and you already know the door.

They are who a town has when a becoming has already happened, and the town may prefer it had not.

### Who comes, and when

| Who | Why they come | What Intake does | What they will not do |
|---|---|---|---|
| A raw Stilled (they were stone) | Grey already filling; a rope in the square | Get them off the square. First drill. Reverse what can still be reversed. Sit the Gaze until it is a wound-hold, not a forever. | Will not stamp a ticket. Will not still the crowd as a lesson. |
| A raw Far-Voiced (they drowned) | Undertow already pulling; a Cry in the market | Get them into the hall's back room. First hour *with* a hall-keeper if one will sit. Vent on purpose before it vents them. | Will not open after the lamps as policy. Will not keep them half-full. |
| A raw Answered | An element let go later, untrained | Right door. Earnest use *now* that is not the crowded shop. | Will not put them on a hull the same day. Will not bench them. |
| A family, or a door-keeper, or a Book-hand | Someone must stand; someone wrote *owing* | Take the person. Ask who will stand for them *as what they are now*. | Will not sell a vouch. Will not call them Given. |
| The watch | The town wants them off the square | Bargain for the shed instead of the rope. Sometimes lose. | They have a key. They cannot command the street. |
| The paying floor | They want their master back | Finish the week or fail it. Return the master. | Will not promise this will not happen again. |

### An ordinary catching-year (it is not ordinary)

There is no ordinary week. There is a week.

1. Get them off the square, out of the water, away from the shop that is already wrong.
2. Name what is already true: *you carry a Gaze; you carry a voice; you carry a Reach.* Do not name a Mother unless the person does.
3. Find the first honest payment of the Tithe that is not a public hazard: a drill, an hour, a coal that is not the public forge.
4. Find someone who will stand for them *anew*. Intake does not take the place of that vouch.
5. At week's end: still raw, beginning to be safe, handed to the real apprenticeship, or gone. Gone means the rope, the road, or a week-keeper who will not make them safe.

They write the catching-year on the **inside** of a ticket-blank, not on the face. The face stays empty until a real ticket is earned. A first-year who writes on the face is made to strike it.

They will not stamp a ticket this week. Competence is not a week old. If they must walk, it is toward this Struck. Road-hands already have the long road. They will not rewrite Bound Terms. A raw Bound goes to a table. The Whisper cannot command. They will not take a raw Taken-In, Returned, Two-Bodied, or Long-Lived. A raw Taken-In goes to a lot. A Returned's purpose cannot be furnished here. A Two-Bodied uses civic land. A Long-Lived uses a long-house. None of those is a bystander ticket, and none of them is this shed.

### Who may sit the shed

**Not by Kind.** By practice first: you sat a week and the person did not become a public story.

The first drill / hour / coal to a ticket-holder of *that* hazard. The sitting, the water, the food to a Kept (no Tithe of their own; can stay). A Struck who came through Intake once may say, "I remember the door." The runner to whoever can run: Two-Bodied; a Far-Voiced crier who will not use the Cry to clear a street.

### How they live with the other houses

The parent houses pretend the shed is not on the wall. Success is silence. A raw Taken-In goes to a lot, for dirt. If the week fails, a week-keeper may take the overflow. Intake wants them safe enough to leave. The Slide wants them not-fallen enough to stay. A quiet receiving-rite may happen the same week as a warden's. Civic settling may send them here. A door-keeper names the Struck kin at the door that made them. That naming is kinship. The ticket is still unstamped. The naming and the catching-week are two courtesies in the same week. A Watcher's second mouth may describe the threshold. The guild's stamp is a separate act.

### How they live in the three corners

- **Vaethorn (the late-catching).** The rope is real. The shed may be a barn. Once caught, the valley will spend them. The late-come are vouched graciously *and* put to work.
- **Lestrand (the emergency ticket).** Posted, almost. A fee if anyone can pay it. The charter-risk is commercial. A raw Struck who cannot stamp a fee is still taken, the street-hazard demands it, and the house will remember the debt.
- **Threnmaieth (the unwitnessed warrant).** The crown wants the Struck *named* as they are caught. Intake's inside-writing is already a defiance. A quiet week looks like a leak. A loud week looks like incompetence. The darkest pressure: catch them for the channel.
- **Kumbaan.** A wreck walked uphill is a guest. There is no shed on the sand.

### The week no one writes

The paying floor needs the master. The town needs the hazard gone. The Struck needs a year. Intake is given a week and a shed, and is blamed for all three.

If they succeed, the story becomes: *the guild trains its own.* If they fail, the story becomes: *the shed.* Devotion does not make the rope kinder, and it does not give them a better door. The becoming was unwitnessed. The town built a square that would rather use a rope.

Badly paid, out of what the parent house has left after the paying floor: the shed, the food, the master's lost week. They do not furnish a commons. They do not sell an hour. They catch.

### A catching-year slip

Written on the inside of a blank ticket, stillers' shed, core town. The face is empty.

> *Inside only. Gaze, raw. Came from stone on the third night. Stood for by the sister and by N. who will not give a family name. First drill held. No forever. No ticket. If the week ends quiet, burn this. If it does not, the charter already knows. We do not face the street. We do not write the wall. If they have already been a rope-story, this is still the shed. Come if they will come. If they will not, we cannot make a town take them. The Mother does not reach twice by chance. Neither do we. That line is the wardens'. Strike it if you must. I have not.*

On its face: a competent catching, a little desperate, a college's shame in a folded paper. The empty face is the point. The writer kept the wardens' line on the slip. "Burn this" is how a quiet week becomes the guild's ordinary story, and the Struck's becoming stays unwitnessed a second time. The sister stood. N. would not be named. Both are someone standing for the Struck.

## Three corners: Vaethorn, Lestrand, Threnmaieth

Every polity in the Turning chooses three things separately: how devout it is, how many healthy Trees it has, and how far the state reaches onto a person. Law, livelihood, and daily life stay the same from power to power, because they come from the Trees and the Conditions. What changes is the combination of those three choices.

These three lands are set so that each pair matches on one choice and differs on the other two.

| Name | Devotion | Healthy Trees | The state on the person | The one-line feel |
|---|:---:|:---:|:---:|---|
| **Vaethorn** *(the Waiting Lands)* | high | low | low | Ardent faith, thin Trees, almost no state: a warm, poor, half-lawless pilgrim edge |
| **Lestrand** *(the Ledger Coast)* | low | high | low | Faith worn lightly, dense Trees, guilds not crowns: a rich, cool, transactional merchant power |
| **Threnmaieth** *(the Tallied Crown)* | high | high | high | Sacred *and* surveilled: a wealthy, pious core where the census is a holy duty (the darkest corner) |

- Vaethorn and Lestrand share **low governance**: same light-touch state, opposite everything else. Warm-poor-faithful against rich-cool-secular.
- Vaethorn and Threnmaieth share **high theology**: same ardent faith, wildly different circumstances. Devotion at the starving edge against devotion in the sanctified, watched core.
- Lestrand and Threnmaieth share **high reach**: same Tree-wealth spent two opposite ways. Free markets against a crown that watches every person and calls the watching holy.

High devotion produces both the kindest corner to a whole neighbour (Vaethorn) and the most inescapable control in the world (Threnmaieth). Devotion makes whatever else the polity is doing stronger.

The name a polity gives a shared institution reveals its stance. A devout land's "gift-hall" is a surveillance-crown's "counted hall." Same building, three worldviews.

---

### Vaethorn: the Waiting Lands

*(vay-THORN · devotion high · few healthy Trees · the state barely reaches a person)*

A broad, thinly-peopled land at the world's Tree-poor margin, where the faith burns hottest *because* the Trees are scarce, and where the ruling mood is *waiting*: for a graft to take, for a distant Tree to be reached, for a gift that may never fall. This is a frontier where devotion runs at its hottest. The two facts feed each other. Where the Trees are few and young, more children come away Kept, more people are Struck later and alone, and the Motherfaith answers that scarcity with more devotion: pilgrimage, longing, and a theology built around waiting for a gift that may not come.

**Law here.** Governance is almost absent. No crown reaches the individual, and custom outruns written law the way it does on any frontier. Citizenship is witnessed in the oldest, most literal way: a settlement small enough that everyone *was* there when you Turned. The Lands' thin reach makes citizenship-by-witness fragile. With few Long-Lived to remember and few Bound to vouch, the three pillars that make proof-by-people work are stretched thin: a Long-Lived's memory, a Bound's vouch, and a Far-Voiced's voice. The **unvouched Struck** (a person who changed alone, with no guild to catch them and few witnesses to stand for them) is a constant, aching problem Vaethorn meets with faith rather than machinery. A feared Condition here is more likely to be met with a rope or a blessing than a licence, because there is no guild to issue one. Standing-for, someone vouching for you in a new place, is called *the speaking*, and it is done at worship. The ticket is called *the blessing-to-practice*. The Inviolate Will, the rule that no Condition can command another person's will, is called *the Mother's own line*.

**Economy here.** Little formal Tithe-infrastructure and few specialists, but the theology reframes the frontier's poverty as *charity*. Where a purely secular frontier improvises Tithe-provision as barter and favor, Vaethorn does it as **alms and congregation**: the venting a Far-Voiced needs happens at worship (the *gift-hall*), the green a Taken-In needs is furnished by neighbours who count it a sacred duty (*the Mother's green*), and the Taken-In whom rich cities keep at arm's length are here the honoured **backbone of the land**. Working the living earth *is* the holy economy. It is commons-minded generosity with almost nothing to fund it: real warmth, real want, and a persistent thin edge where the provided-for Tithe-poor slide with only prayer for a net. Open land where a Two-Bodied spends time as the animal is called *the wild-leave*. The long-house of books is called *the deathless gift*. The Slide, when the commons run out, is *the last pot*, also called *gift-credit*.

**Daily life here.** Thin ceremony made enormous by devotion. A child's Turning at a small, hard-won grafted Tree is the event of a decade, held as a joyous, anxious sacrament, and the **Kept child is felt most sharply here**, because in a land starved of the gift, to be passed over reads (wrongly, and cruelly) as being passed over *by the Mother herself.* People here call the Kept *the Waited*, or *Mother-kept*: passed over, and pitied for it. They call the Struck *the late-come*. Medicine is a travelling stiller if the settlement is lucky and a rope-and-prayer if it is not; the core-vs-edge inequity is at its widest here. Families are warm, mixed, and self-reliant; a lapsing Condition with no infirmary to reach is a frontier tragedy the faith consoles but cannot cure. The tree-warden is most of the state. Vaethorn calls wardens Hands-folk. Door-keepers, called the door-folk, keep the older custom of the doors. Guest-reading towns, which kept those doors when a Tree arrived, pilgrimage and still walk them. First-reading districts treat the graft as a guest in an older house: they tend a dying graft and still refuse the blessing.

**Its injustice.** The harm is a **guilt**. The scarcity of the gift becomes a theology of *unworthiness*: the Kept wonder what they lacked, the Struck wonder why the Mother came to them at the wrong door and alone, and a hard, quiet piety can shade into treating want as a spiritual failing. Vaethorn is the kindest place in the world to a whole neighbour and the loneliest to an unvouched one.

**The seat.** **Eolvaeth** *(ay-ohl-VAYTH)*, a pilgrim-edge vale behind the Strandoren-facing coast. People walk *here to wait*. The Tree is not a certainty.

---

### Lestrand: the Ledger Coast

*(leh-STRAND · devotion low · many healthy Trees · the state barely reaches a person)*

A dense, Tree-rich merchant power where the Trees are mature and many, the Given are dense, and the faith is worn *lightly.* Deep markets, thin welfare, and a cool, transactional read on the whole business of becoming. It shares Vaethorn's light-touch governance: no crown writes anyone down. Where Vaethorn's light state leaves a *gap the faith fills*, Lestrand's light state leaves a *space the guilds fill,* completely.

**Law here.** **Guild self-rule**, carried as far as it goes. The state is a charter-granting council of merchant houses and craft-guilds; it charters the Stilled's guild and the element-guilds and watches the guild, never the individual. Citizenship-by-witness runs *smoothly* here because reach is high: abundant Long-Lived notaries, Bound whose sworn word closes every contract, Far-Voiced on every dock and in every court. The three pillars are **infrastructure** here, the machinery that lets a huge commercial city run on trust without a register. The Struck are vouched-in briskly, because an unvouched person is a transaction that can't clear. Here the Struck are called *the turned*. The Inviolate Will is cited as sound commercial law: a Bound's contract binds property, not persons, and the Coast likes that line because it makes the Bound the witness a written contract wants. Standing-for is called *warranting*, a commercial guarantee. The ticket is called *the guild-ticket*. The Inviolate Will is called *the free-will clause*. The Motherfaith is called *the old rite*, and it is worn light. The Fair Hand, the faith of honest accounts, is called *the Settling*, and on this coast that is ordinary.

**Economy here.** The richest corner, and the coolest. This is where **deathless banking** is fullest: deathless houses whose trusted notes are what other houses measure their paper against, century instruments, and fortunes endowed rather than left as inheritances, at a scale the other lands do not match (the great endowed libraries and universities cluster here, funding the Long-Lived's own novelty-Tithe). Self-paying Conditions prosper, the Answered crafts, the Two-Bodied haulage and courier trades, the Stilled surgeons, because their labor *is* their upkeep and the market pays for the labor. **Tithe-provision is a private market, not a public good:** you buy your green (*the leaf-lot*), your novelty (*the endowment* / *the house*), your outlet (*the release-house*, also the playhouse), and if you can afford it you stay whole in comfort. **Its injustice is the market's.** The provided-for Tithe-poor get *thin welfare in a rich city*, the starkest have-and-have-not gap in the world, and the shadow economy (smuggled green, black-market novelty, sold vouching, off-book Bound contracts feeding the Given-Over) runs biggest here because the legitimate market is biggest here. The concentration risk the deathless pose is at its most acute: a handful of houses that never die, quietly steering the wealthiest polity through patronage that never disperses. The Slide is *week-rent*, the rented week after a fee fails. The Kept are called *the free*, meaning labor with no Tithe to furnish. The ranging is *the beast-yard*, a hired yard. Tree-wardens are called *tree-tenders*. Book-hands are called *the Settling-hands*.

**Daily life here.** Superb Condition-medicine (the great Stilled infirmaries and deathless-endowed hospitals are *here*, in the rich core), for those who can reach it; the queue outside it is longest here too. Family life shrugs at theology: a **marriage market that reads Conditions as assets**, a Two-Bodied bloodline is a trade dynasty, a Long-Lived match is a century of credit, and the aching Long-Lived marriage gets weighed coolly as a balance of grief against advantage. The Turning is a civic and commercial milestone more than a sacrament. A family's Leaf-Fall is watched for what guild the child's colour opens. It is free, and it is prosperous. An attic-host of the Open Table, the faith that feeds a guest before asking who they are, is a curiosity and a good supper. Sailors print "no one is a stranger twice" as a proverb on a card. The Settling will not enforce it.

**Its injustice.** The coolest of the three. Nothing is *policed*. Your marriage, your gift, your faith are your own. Everything is *priced*, and a person who cannot pay slides with a whole rich city stepping around them. Lestrand will never file you or conscript you. It will simply let you fade if you run out of coin, and feel that it owed you nothing.

**The seat.** **Orentel** *(or-EN-tel)*, a salt-quay city on the inner sea. The White Note House sits on the third quay, north side. It keeps terms. It does not govern the coast.

### Threnmaieth: the Tallied Crown

*(THREN-my-eth · devotion high · many healthy Trees · the state reaches the person; the darkest corner)*

A pious, Tree-rich core kingdom, and the one crown that keeps a census of persons and their Conditions. Set that census in the wealthiest, most devout, most Tree-dense land, and the roster is kept as a holy duty: the Mother's gifts are precious and must be accounted for. This is what makes it the darkest of the three. The other two have injustices you can name and resent. This one puts its injustice in the two things the world most trusts, faith and plenty.

**Law here.** Alone among polities, the Tallied Crown keeps **a record held on people**: every subject and their gift written down, not as guild rolls or tax ledgers (everyone has those) but as a **census of persons**, framed at home as reverent stewardship of the Mother's blessings and experienced as surveillance. The census is the **Threnhael**. Because reach is high, there is a great deal to count: dense Given, called *the marked*; dense guilds; abundant Long-Lived whose memory the crown distrusts because that memory is not the crown's. What gives Threnmaieth away is this: it will not rely on the three pillars the rest of the world runs on, because living memory answers to itself and a ledger answers to the crown. The unvouched are hunted and the Struck pursued to be catalogued. That pursuit is filed as *the unwitnessed*. The Bound are watched far past their real hazard. The roster's own clerks know the Inviolate Will means the Whisper cannot command. The watching is stigma, kept as if it were safety. Devotion supplies the justification a census without faith would lack. A steward will say, "You would not hide a gift from the Mother, so why hide it from her stewards?" Standing-for is called *the naming*: you are named to the crown. The ticket is called *the crown-warrant*. The Inviolate Will is called *the crown's assurance*. The Motherfaith is called *the Reckoned Faith*. Watchers are filed as *the Grown-Over*, a column on the roll. Book-hands are filed as *the Uncounted Book*. Door-keepers are filed as *the Unreached*. The Open Table is off the map.

**Economy here.** Tithe-need turned to leverage, and consecrated. The crown provides Tithe-upkeep **efficiently, even generously** (a pious, rich state can afford to), but *holds* what it provides: the green (*the tended green*), the novelty (*the crown archive*), the outlets (*the counted hall*), the Stilled medicine, all furnished through channels the crown controls and can withhold. A population whose staying-whole runs through the state is a population that **cannot afford to refuse it**, the most complete control in the world. To receive your Tithe-provision is to receive the Mother's bounty through her appointed hands, and to be cut off is framed as *your* lapse, not the crown's lever. Its deathless-house finance is superb and centuries-deep, but the Long-Lived here are courtiers and crown-archivists as much as bankers, their incorruptible memory bent to the census rather than free to check it. The ranging, land where a Two-Bodied spends time as the animal, is filed as *the ranging-roll*. The furnishing is *the counted channel*. The Slide is *the late week*: the leak the roll cannot file without admitting the channel failed. The roll pretends not to know.

**Daily life here.** The sacrament and the census fused. A child's Turning is a **joyous holy day that is also a state registration**. The colour that falls is celebrated with a hymn *and* recorded by a clerk. Marriages of heritable Conditions (Two-Bodied bloodlines especially) are *noticed*, encouraged or discouraged, filed. The medicine that furnishes a Tithe is excellent and runs through the crown's channels. A household whose **bloodline and upkeep are both on file** is a household that cannot easily refuse the crown anything: domestic surveillance with a blessing said over it. The Kept are pitied here the way a devout land pities them, and recorded the way this crown records them: *the unmarked*, or *the passed-over*, passed over by the Mother, and noted as such. Tree-wardens are filed as *the Reckoned Hands*. The First Seat stands in the same Sacred Core. The crown wants wardens named to the Threnhael as officers of the Reckoned Faith. Some Speakers cooperate. Some refuse: the Tree witnessed; a ledger is a second, lesser seeing. Nearness to the Seat is pressure on the college. The crown does not own the Seat. The college can refuse the roll, and some Speakers don't. A Yumboe in the Sacred Core is a diplomatic incident of manners. The crown cannot file a people who were never at a Tree, so a Yumboe is not a column on the roll. That is a relief and a slight at once.

**Its injustice.** The worst of high devotion and a state that reaches every person, each excusing the other. The devotion makes the surveillance feel like reverence: it is piety to be counted. The surveillance enforces the devotion: to refuse the count is impiety. A wealthy, faithful, orderly people genuinely believe that accounting for the Mother's gifts is the highest form of tending them, and that belief makes the control almost impossible to resist. It reads as chilling because **no other polity keeps such a list**. This census is this one crown's invention, and the faith is what lets it feel like grace.

**The seat.** **Maiethlir** *(my-eth-LEER)* is a Hand on a thaw river in the Sacred Core. A Hand is a town whose children can Turn at its own Tree. People who already have that Tree still walk the extra mile. The Threnhael is the census kept over that older walk. The First Seat of the wardens sits in the Motherwood beside the town. The crown does not own Thaeloren.
