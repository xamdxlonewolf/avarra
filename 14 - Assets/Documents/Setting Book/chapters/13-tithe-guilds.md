# Tithe-Infrastructure and Guilds


The lived official sector — who furnishes green / novelty / outlets, and who stamps the handful of hazardous-Condition tickets. Not clergy. Not the Slide.

Everyday: **the furnishing**, or just **the greens and the halls.** There is no liturgical umbrella — this is civic work, not a fifth faith. Vaethorn says **the gift-works**. Lestrand says **the provision** the way it says *the wells*. Threnmaieth files **the counted channel**.

**In one sentence:** they keep people whole *enough*, in public, and they stop at the gate.

### Shape of the sector

- **Two jobs that are not one house.** **Furnishing** keeps a provided-for Tithe from becoming a public hazard (green, an outlet, a new page). **Ticketing** marks competence for the three Conditions that can hurt a bystander (the Stilled, Far-Voiced, the Answered). A town may house both under one lintel. They are still two jobs. The Bound's "ticket" is a table, not a body-guild.
- **Official means *enough*, not *a week*.** A commons-share, a scheduled hour, a library that is open. The product is a person who can walk away until tomorrow. The Slide's product is a person who cannot. The town is glad of both and will not say so about the second.
- **They think they are in a public-works story.** Wells. Yards. A playhouse. A surgeon's college. They do not call themselves monster-wardens.
- **No Furnishing Seat.** A town keeps its own lot and hall. A deathless house endows its own long-house. The licence-guilds charter *their* members. Nothing in this sector micromanages a far commons.

### Who furnishes what

| Tithe | The official door | Lived house | What they will not do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Taken-In green | garden-commons / the greens / a leaf-lot | the Greens-Keepers | Will not follow you home. Will not rent you a private pot after the share fails. That is the Slide. |
| Far-Voiced outlet | the ael-hall (playhouse and safety-valve) | the Hall-Keepers | Will not give you an unscheduled Cry. Will not keep you half-vented so you come back tonight. |
| Long-Lived novelty | the long-house of books | *no new order* — the deathless house that endowed it, still sitting the chair | Will not ration a page to own you. A junior who does that has started a week. |
| Unbound warmth | *almost none* | family; an attic of the Table-Keepers; the Slide's paid sitting | The sector cannot buy being known. There is no warmth-guild. |
| Bound Terms | the honest table | the Book-Hands (civic year) + the Counterparty (the clause) | Not a body-licence. There is no fourth hazard-guild. |
| Returned Purpose | *cannot be furnished* | a Book-hand may find a payable year; no one can supply a vengeance | There is no Purpose-desk. |
| Two-Bodied ranging | the ranging / a beast-yard | civic land, no guild | Self-paying. No ticket. There are no wolf-police. |

**The three tickets** (danger-to-others only, held by the person, issued by the craft):

| Ticket | Condition | Lived house | Why a ticket at all |
|---|---|---|---|
| strictest | the Stilled | the Stillers | the Gaze can hold a person forever |
| heavy, training-first | Far-Voiced | the Hall-Keepers (the same building as the outlet) | a Cry in a crowded hall |
| heavy, training-first | the Answered | the Element-Guilds | an untended element in a workshop |
| *not a body-ticket* | Bound | a table, not a guild-yard | walking legal instrument; the Whisper cannot command |

**The Intake** is the shared underdog office of the three ticket-houses: the raw Struck, already live, no childhood apprenticeship. Not a travelling clergy. Not a week.

### The long-house (novelty without a new order)

A Long-Lived house that has endowed a library is already Tithe-infrastructure. The founder is still alive. They still name the scholars. They still sit the chair. Coin went out; steering stayed.

Staff are clerks — often the Kept, unencumbered, able to fetch a book the founder has not read. A junior Long-Lived copies. There is no liturgical name and no college of librarians.

What they furnish is *something genuinely new*. What they will not furnish is a page at a time to a person who cannot walk in. That junior — the one who will extend a subscription one more week — is how the Slide sits next to a long-house without being one.

### The gate (the sector's shared courtesy)

Every official door stops at a line the town has agreed to call *enough*:

- The greens-keeper keeps the lot. They do not walk the wilting room.
- The hall-keeper keeps the hour. They do not open after the lamps for a voice that missed its slate.
- The long-house is open. It is not a courier of rumours to a fade in a closed chamber.
- The ticket-house trains the Given child and stamps the competent adult. The raw Struck is the Intake's problem, and the guild is glad to forget them if the catching-year is quiet.

The Slide begins where that line is. The guilds **pretend not to know**. That pretending is already part of the town.

This is not kindness-as-cruelty in the Slide's shape (the measured week). This is **jurisdiction**. We keep the commons. We do not keep *you*.

### How they live in the three corners

- **Vaethorn (the gift-works).** Tithe-provision as a public good with almost no guild to fund it. A commons that is a cousin's field. A blessing-to-practice said over a stiller who learned on a riot-line because there was no infirmary. Soft injustice: you were helped, so you will spend the gift as the valley sees fit. The Slide here is *the last pot* when the commons run out.
- **Lestrand (the provision).** Posted. A leaf-lot by the season. A release-house that is also the playhouse. Guild-tickets as master's papers. You can be priced out of staying whole. The Slide is week-rent after the season-fee failed.
- **Threnmaieth (the counted channel).** The crown furnishes — efficiently, even generously — and *holds* what it furnishes. A counted hall. A tended green. A crown-warrant. The darkest leash in the world, dressed as care. The Slide is the late week the channel will not admit failed.

**Kumbaan.** Almost no Given Tithes. There is no exported greenhouse or ael-hall. A wreck walked uphill is a guest.

This sector is not a fifth faith. Not a census. A bed-list is plants. A hall-slate is hours. A guild-roll is practitioners of a dangerous art. The one kingdom that writes *persons and gifts* is still only Threnmaieth. Not the Road-hands. They carry wood. These houses carry dirt, an hour, a ticket.

## The Greens-Keepers

The lived face of official green — garden-commons, the greens, a leaf-lot. They furnish *enough* living land that a Taken-In can rest and walk away. They are not the Slide.

Everyday: **greens-keepers**, or **lot-keepers.** Liturgical: **the Saelhael** — SAYL-hayl — *sael* "sowing; the first green" + *hael* "whole": those who *keep you hale by putting you back on growing ground.* Vaethorn's second word is **Mother's-green hands**; Lestrand's is **lot-keepers** (ordinary, like saying *gardener*); Threnmaieth files them as **the Tended**.

**In one sentence:** they keep the lot — and they will not follow you home.

### Shape of the house

- **A commons, not a church.** There is no Green Seat. A town has a lot, a strip along the wall, pots in a yard the size of a tannery. Vaethorn's "lot" is often a cousin's field held in common. The house is whoever waters it and says who sleeps on which bed tonight.
- **Enough, then the gate.** A share is a night on living dirt, a season on a bed, a commons-hour. The person should leave able to work. A person who cannot leave is not this house's product. That person is how you find the Slide.
- **They think they are in a gardening story.** Soil, blight, whose beans took. They do not call themselves wardens of a people.
- **A bed-slate is plants.** Rows, which bed is tired, which needs a year of beans. Names get onto it because a person is *on* a bed. The keepers will tell you it is horticulture. In Threnmaieth the crown already has another list.

### If you meet one

Dirt under the nails. A key to a gate in a wall. A slate. You would miss them as clergy if you came looking for a lintel. You would find them if you asked *where a Taken-In sleeps when the rooms are stone.*

They are useful the way a well is useful. A stone town cannot keep its Taken-In without a lot. The faith, if any, is extra.

### Who comes, and when

| Who | Why they come | What the greens-keeper does | What they will not do |
|---|---|---|---|
| A Taken-In who works in town | The rooms are stone; the Tithe is due | Give a share: a bed, a night, a commons-hour. Write the bed, not the person, if they write at all. | Will not follow them home with a pot. Will not open the gate after the share-hour because the wilt has started. |
| A family, before the town sees | A parent is going to seed indoors | Open the lot if there is a bed. Send them home with the hour named. | Will not make the wilt a sermon. Will not tell the warden unless the person asks. |
| A Struck Taken-In, raw | They came through a green door later, alone | Find them dirt *now*. Point them at a door-keeper if the door still needs walking. | Will not name them Given. Will not licence a grove. |
| A Book-hand with a settling | The term *assumes* a greens-share | Honour the term if there is a bed. Mark the slate *full* if there is not — the book becomes *owing*, not this lot's lie. | Will not invent a bed to close someone else's book. |
| A crown-clerk (Threnmaieth) | The tended green must match the channel | Give the beds the roll already expects | Will not pretend a wilt off the lot is not happening. They also will not walk to it. |
| Almost no one who is not Taken-In | — | Water. Turn tired beds. Argue beans. | Will not become a food-guild. Grain is a different door. |

### An ordinary week

They **open the gate.** That is the whole kindness. A Taken-In lies down on living ground. The tokens fill. They leave. The bed is turned. The next name — no, the next *row* — is watered.

They bury last year's commons-leaf under a new bed. No one will explain this to a guest. A first-year who asks is told *that is how the lot remembers.*

When the queue is longer than the wilt, they still open the gate for the share they have. They close it when the share is gone. They do not walk the three streets to the room where someone is going to seed. They know a cousin still has a pot. They do not say the cousin's name.

A child of the house learns the work by carrying water and by being told not to take the key home.

They will not follow you home. They will not rent a private pot after the share fails. They will not steal a scion to make a grove. They will not call the share a gift on Lestrand, or a debt anywhere they can help it. They call it a share. They will not run a Kumbaan export.

### Who may keep a lot

**Not by Kind.** A human, a Kitsune, a Tengu who will water and keep the gate can be of the house.

**By practice first.** You are of the house when the lot is still alive in the dry month, and the town still sends its Taken-In to you.

**Condition leans, not Condition-gates.** Watering and blight often fall to a Taken-In who kept a commons, or a Kept with no Tithe of their own who can sit the gate year-round. The slate to whoever can read a tired row — sometimes a Long-Lived who remembers which bed failed twenty years ago. The gate at close to a Kept, or anyone who will lock it.

### How they live in the three corners

- **Vaethorn (Mother's-green hands).** The commons *is* a cousin's field. They will say they are tending a gift. The quiet obligation is the harvest: you were laid on the Mother's green, you will spend the green as the valley asks. When the field fails, the last pot is the Slide, and they will still call it tending.
- **Lestrand (lot-keepers).** Posted. A leaf-lot by the season. Coin on the gate. A Taken-In who cannot pay the season is not this lot's problem until they wilt in the street — and then they are the watch's. The Slide is the lot that still opens.
- **Threnmaieth (the Tended).** The crown furnishes the tended green and can withhold it. Beds match the channel. A wilt off the lot is a leak the roll does not want to file. The keepers look like gardeners. The leash is the water.
- **Kumbaan.** No.

### The gate

Whoever furnishes a provided-for Tithe holds power over its bearer. The greens-keepers hold that power *and refuse to carry it past the wall.*

That refusal is their kindness (they will not own you) and their cruelty (they will not walk three streets). The town is glad. A person who wilts *quietly* off the lot is not a public hazard. The Warm can say the gift was offered. The quay can say the season was posted. The crown can say the channel held.

Confirming the Leaf-Mother is real and kind does **not** open a free parlour-grove. She gave the need for living land. She did not build the gate.

Tired beds are real. A lot can be botanically full and still have a queue. That is not a lie they tell. It is also a choice about who sleeps on stone.

### A bed-slate

Lestrand leaf-lot, nailed inside the gate. The verso is last season's slate, the tired rows still marked.

> *Beds 1–12: taken through the next moon. 13–16: beans, do not lie here. 17 is tired; it will take next year. Share is one night or one hour at open sky, then the gate. If you cannot come, send no one — a bed is not a letter. We do not walk the rooms. If you have already wilted, the lot is still the lot. Come when we open. If you cannot wait, we do not know a pot.*

On its face: competent gardening, a little cold, a commons keeping itself honest. Underneath: the gate confessing itself, and a blindness the keeper cannot afford — that "we do not know a pot" is the only lie on the slate. The child who carries water can name the cousin. The slate cannot.

## The Hall-Keepers

The lived face of official outlets and the Far-Voiced ticket — one lintel, because the seam Condition *is* the building. The ael-hall is a concert house until it is a release-valve. They furnish *enough* voice that a person can walk until tomorrow. They are not the Slide.

Everyday: **hall-keepers**, or **ael-hands.** Liturgical: **the Aeloren** — ayl-OR-en — *ael* "to air; to let feeling out" + *oren* "to witness": those who *hear you vent and will say you did.* Vaethorn's second word is **gift-hall folk**; Lestrand's is **release-hands** (ordinary, like saying *box-office*); Threnmaieth files them as **counted-hall clerks**.

**In one sentence:** they sell you an hour in which you may feel in earnest — and they will not open after the lamps.

### Shape of the house

- **One building, two jobs.** The hall *furnishes* the outlet (Tithe-infra) and *stamps* the Voice-ticket (safety-guild). The same mouth that says *your hour is the third* says *you may stand in a crowded room.*
- **The hour is enough.** A scheduled vent — song, weeping, a relay-call, a laugh that empties the chest. The person should leave emptied and able to work. The Slide's shut room is one outlet that is never quite enough. This house wants you gone until tomorrow.
- **They think they are in a playhouse story.** Bills, benches, who carries the high note. They do not call themselves a police of feeling.
- **No high hall with authority.** The oldest playhouse on a Lestrand quay is old. The next town's hall does not write to it for leave.

### If you meet one

Ink on a finger, or chalk. A slate of hours. A hall that smells like sweat and lamp-oil and last night's joy. You would buy a bench and not know which hour was "church." You would find them if you asked *where a voice can be loud without becoming a Cry in the market.*

They are useful the way a well is useful, and the way a master's ticket is useful. A town with Far-Voiced and no hall has a Book-hand writing *owing* and a watch waiting for the market.

### Who comes, and when

| Who | Why they come | What the hall-keeper does | What they will not do |
|---|---|---|---|
| A Given Far-Voiced child, after the colour | The voice must be trained before it is a hazard | Apprentice them: breath, projection, venting on purpose. The ticket comes when the town can stand next to them. | Will not Speak the colour. The warden did that. Will not call the voice a gift on Lestrand. |
| A working Far-Voiced (performer, crier, witness) | The vocation *is* the vent — self-paying | Give them the stage, the relay-post, the stand. Stamp the year if the ticket is due. | Will not invent a crisis so they can vent off-slate. |
| A Far-Voiced who lost the honest stage | Isolated posting, dull labor; the Undertow is close | Sell or grant a **scheduled hour**. Empty them. Send them out. | Will not open after the lamps. Will not keep them half-full so they return tonight. |
| A Struck Far-Voiced, raw (the drowning-door) | The voice came back untrained | Hand them to the Intake. Sit the first hour *with* Intake if the Cry is already in the room. | Will not stamp a ticket on a raw throat. Will not leave them in the market. |
| A Book-hand with a settling | The term *assumes* a seventh-day hour | Honour it if the slate has a hole. Mark *full* if it does not. | Will not pretend Greyhull has a bench when Greyhull is full. |
| Anyone who came for a play | The hall is also the playhouse | Sell a bench. Take the coin. Let the working voices pay their Tithe *as* the show. | Will not stop a performance to process a ticket. The show *is* the Tithe for the people on the boards. |

### An ordinary week

**Morning — the slate.** Hours named. Relay-posts listed. A child's breath-lesson in the cold hall. The ticket-desk is a stool by the door.

**Evening — the house.** Feeling in earnest, on purpose, where a neighbour paid to hear it. This is the pleasant version of the Tithe, and it is also the town's entertainment. No one calls that a coincidence.

**The unswept bench.** One seat is never swept. *That's the first Cry's seat.* No one will explain which Cry. A first-year who sits there is moved without a sermon.

When a voice misses its hour, the keeper does not open the side door. They know a room that still shuts. They do not say the room's name.

They will not stamp a raw Struck. That is the Intake. They will not rewrite a Bound's Terms because a voice was used to swear them. They will not run a mission-hall on Kumbaan.

### Who may keep a hall

**Not by Kind.** A Selkie crier and a human neighbour hold the same stool if they can keep a slate and hear when a voice is about to break.

**By practice first.** You are of the house when the town still comes to your hour, and when you have pulled a child off a Cry before it landed.

The boards, the relay, the stand often go to Far-Voiced whose vocation *is* the vent. The ticket-stool to a Given Far-Voiced who trained young, or a sharp Kept, or a Long-Lived who remembers last century's Cry. The first hour with a raw throat to whoever will sit it — Intake borrows this room.

### How they live in the three corners

- **Vaethorn (gift-hall folk).** A barn, a festival, a blessing said over the first note. Almost no ticket-stool. A Struck throat is a late gift and a hazard the valley will stand around, singing, until someone who has been to a real hall arrives. Soft injustice: you were given the hour, you will spend the voice as the valley asks — crier, witness, hymn.
- **Lestrand (release-hands).** The playhouse *is* the hall. Posted hours. Guild-ticket as master's papers. A voice without coin for a bench still has a labour-hour at the back if they will sweep. A voice without even that goes to week-rent.
- **Threnmaieth (counted-hall clerks).** The crown furnishes the counted hall and can withhold the hour. Tickets are crown-warrants in all but the stamp. A Cry off-slate is a column. The hall looks like a festival. The leash is the hour.
- **Kumbaan.** No. A bright night is not an ael-hall.

### The scheduled hour

The hall sells (or grants) the right to feel in public *on time*. Off-time is a ticket problem. A performer who lost the honest stage still has the hour — if the slate has a hole, if the season-fee was paid, if the crown still lists them.

The town is glad. An emptied voice is not a Cry in the market. The Warm can say the gift was aired. The quay can say the bill was posted. The crown can say the channel held.

Confirming she is real does **not** make the eighth week kind. She gave a voice that must vent. She did not build the slate.

Self-paying voices subsidize provided-for ones when the polity lets them — the show *is* some people's Tithe and other people's night out. Lestrand calls this the market. Vaethorn calls this tending. Both can be true in one barn.

The first Cry's bench stays unswept. That is not a theology. It is how the hall remembers.

### An hour-slate

Lestrand release-house, chalked on the door beside tonight's bill. The verso is yesterday's slate, not washed.

> *Third hour: the boards (paid). Fifth: relay-drill, apprentices. Sixth: open hour, two benches, no coin if you sweep. After the lamps: the house is shut. A missed hour is a missed hour. We do not keep a throat half-full. If you have already cried in the street, this is still the house — come at open, not now. If you cannot wait, we do not know a room.*

On its face: a playhouse being practical, almost kind (the sweep-hour). Underneath: the slate confessing the gate, and the same lie the greens tell — "we do not know a room." The first Cry's bench is not on the slate. Everyone knows which one it is.

## The Stillers

The model safety-guild. They licence the Stilled. They run the infirmaries. They are not a census and not monster-police. Guild-issued tickets; the state watches the guild, not the person.

Everyday: **stillers**, or **the Stillers' Guild.** Liturgical: **the Stelhael** — stel-HAYL — *stel* "to still; to hold from moving" + *hael* "whole": those who *keep you hale by holding you still.* Vaethorn's second word is **blessing-surgeons**; Lestrand's is **ticket-surgeons**; Threnmaieth files them as **crown-stillers**.

**In one sentence:** they will save you on the table, and they will make you drill until the Grey is gone — the ticket is competence, and the clock in the surgeon is the hospital's clock.

### Shape of the house

- **The model the others imitate.** If you understand this guild, you understand a Voice-ticket and an element-ticket. A roster of practitioners of a dangerous art. Not a list of a people.
- **Two rooms, one clock.** The infirmary (wonder) and the yard (spar, patrol, the riot-line). Using the Gaze *fills* the Grey. The yard empties it. The guild's best work creates the guild's Tithe. That is not a paradox they have solved. It is the job.
- **They think they are in a surgeon's-college story.** Apprentices, a code, a ticket like a master's papers. They do not call themselves keepers of monsters.
- **No Stillers' pope.** A rich core infirmary is rich. A frontier stiller is one tired person. The charter is local. The state watches the charter.

### If you meet one

Calm. Flat-eyed when they are working. Hair that stirs when something nearby has gone too still. You meet them in an infirmary, on a drill-floor, or on a line that has not yet become a massacre. You would not know which hour was "guild" unless you already needed a ticket.

They are useful the way a surgeon is useful. A town without one makes do with a rope-and-prayer.

### Who comes, and when

| Who | Why they come | What the stiller does | What they will not do |
|---|---|---|---|
| A Given Stilled child, after pale stone | The Gaze must be trained before it is a forever | Apprentice them. Drill. Theatre. The ticket when they can still a wound and not a person. | Will not Speak the colour. Will not put them on a riot-line in the first year. |
| A patient | Bleeding, poison, a knife that needs a still field | Hold the moment. Work fast. Walk to the yard after and burn the Grey. | Will not hold a will. Cannot. Will not still a person as punishment. |
| The watch, or a street that is turning | A brawl about to become a pile of bodies | Stand the line. Still the swing, not the person, if they can help it. | Will not become the town's executioner. A forever-hold is a crime the code names. |
| A Struck Stilled, raw (they were stone) | The Grey is already in them, unpracticed | Hand them to the Intake. Sit the first drill *with* Intake. Reverse what can still be reversed. | Will not stamp a ticket on a raw Gaze. Will not leave them in the square. |
| A charter-clerk | Someone unlicensed stills in public | Protect the charter: take them in, or swear they were not of the house | Will not write the town's people down. A guild-roll is not a Threnhael. |
| Anyone who wants the Gaze as a weapon | — | Refuse. The code is the ticket. | Will not sell a forever. Anyone who does is not of this house. |

### An ordinary week

**Theatre.** The pleasant horror outsiders remember: a gaze that stops blood, a surgeon who must then go and *move*. Between patients they drill. They spar. They dance, badly, because dance works the Grey out. No one calls this therapy in the core; they call it the job.

**The doorknob.** They touch a doorknob before they look at a patient. If the knob has gone still under an unwatched Gaze, the room is already wrong. A first-year who forgets is sent back to the yard.

**The yard.** Exertion on purpose. The Tithe paid as professional identity.

**The ticket-stool.** A master's mark. Held by the person. The guild *knows its members* the way any craft does. In Lestrand the ticket can start to behave like property — a place, a chair, a name on a door. That is the house's live argument, not a world-law.

When a raw Struck arrives, the paying floor loses a master for a week. The guild is not grateful. That week is the Intake.

They will not still a will. They cannot. They will not pretend they can to frighten a street. They will not let the Grey run to a public seize if they can still drag the person to the yard. A seize on the floor is a ruined charter. They will not become Threnmaieth's register. A roll of *stillers* is not a roll of *the Stilled*. They will not export a college across the storm-wall.

### Who may hold a ticket

**Not by Kind.** The Gaze does not care what you were born.

**By practice first.** You are of the house when you can still a wound and then *move*, and when the town has watched you do both.

Theatre and the line go to the Stilled — the Tithe and the vocation are the same shape. The yard to anyone who will make them *move* — a Kept drill-master; a Two-Bodied who will run them. Intake week to the master who can be spared (often the one the floor can least spare), or a Struck who remembers stone and will sit the first drill.

Skeptics are welcome. The Gaze does not require a Mother. Believers in Vaethorn will still say a blessing-to-practice. Both can hold the same ticket.

### How they live in the three corners

- **Vaethorn (blessing-surgeons).** One tired stiller, a blessing-to-practice, a riot-line that is also the only theatre. Almost no college. Soft injustice: you were trained, you will stand every line the valley has. The rope is still how a raw Struck is met if the stiller is three valleys away.
- **Lestrand (ticket-surgeons).** Superb infirmaries. Guild-ticket as property-adjacent. You can be priced out of the chair. The charter is a commercial good. Unlicensed practice threatens the charter as much as the street.
- **Threnmaieth (crown-stillers).** The crown wants the ticket as a crown-warrant and the roll as a column. Some masters cooperate (stewardship). Some refuse: a guild-roll is not a Threnhael. The Gaze that saves you runs through a channel that can be withheld from a household the crown dislikes. The darkest infirmary in the world, still a real infirmary.
- **Kumbaan.** A travelling stiller is a guest, not a chapter.

### The Grey as labor

The guild's best work *fills* the clock that will lock a limb if they do not then labor. So the house needs them on the table and in the yard. The charter needs them never to lapse in public. The infirmary's pace is set by the surgeon's Grey, not only by the patient.

That is not a scion-queue. That is not a week. That is a hospital that runs on its staff's Tithe.

The ticket-as-property argument (Lestrand, leaking elsewhere) is the second face: competence lives in a person; the chair can start to live in the guild.

Confirming she is real does **not** make the drill optional. She gave a gaze that costs. She did not write the roster.

Intake is badly paid. The raw Struck who most need a week in the shed are the week the floor can least spare. That is why Intake is the underdog, not a prestige rotation.

### A Grey-drill roster

Core infirmary, chalked on the yard wall. The verso is yesterday's, washed badly.

> *After third theatre: spar, full glass. After the line: the long run, no Gaze. Apprentices: the knob first, always. A Grey that stays overnight is a Grey we file as lapse, not as rest. The shed is not on this wall. If you were stone last week, you are not on this wall. Come to the door that does not face the street. We do not write that door. We do not still a person to close a day. Touch the knob. Then look.*

On its face: a college keeping its people hale, the code in a work-order. Underneath: the paying floor confessing that Intake is a door they will not write, and a blindness the master cannot afford — that "a Grey we file as lapse" is how a tired surgeon becomes a charter problem instead of a person who needs a longer run. The knob is not explained. It does not need to be.

## The Element-Guilds

The third safety-ticket and the self-paying craft — the Answered channelled by element. **One sector, four doors.** Not four cloned orders.

Everyday: **the element-guilds**, or the **Ember-house**, **Tide-house**, **Root-house**, **Gale-house.** Umbrella: **the Crae** — kray — *crae* "craft; the working of an element." A single Answered is *of the Crae*; they are *of Ember* if you need the door. Vaethorn's second word is **gift-shops**; Lestrand's is **the craft-houses**; Threnmaieth files **the Reckoned Craes**.

**In one sentence:** the shop *is* the Tithe — leaving it is not a career change; it is a hazard with your name on it.

### Shape of the house

- **Four doors, one grammar.** Ember (smiths, glass, firefighting). Tide (hulls, dyers, anything that wants water to behave). Root (masons, potters, the carrying-earth a Road-hand may buy). Gale (sails, mills, instruments, the wind a Tengu already knew). They share a ticket-logic and an Intake shed. They do not share a pope. An Ember-master does not set Tide's fees.
- **Self-paying until you leave.** Earnest work slakes the Tithe. The guild is therefore the upkeep. That is why the monopoly has metaphysical teeth: a benched Answered is not merely unemployed.
- **They think they are in a craft-mystery story.** Apprenticeships, shop-rules, which coal may go dark. They do not call themselves elemental police.
- **Wildcard standing stays.** An even-tempered Root potter and a short-fused Ember smith share a name and almost nothing else.

### If you meet one

Sweat, clay, salt, or chalk-dust, depending on the door. A shop-rule nailed up. A ticket-stool that looks like any master's. You would hire them to build a hull. You would find the *ticket* if you asked *who may let an element speak in a crowded yard.*

They are useful the way a craft guild is useful. A late-medieval town already needed smiths and masons. The Condition made the guilds *necessary twice* — once for the work, once so the work does not let go.

### Who comes, and when

| Who | Why they come | What the house does | What they will not do |
|---|---|---|---|
| A Given Answered child, after lamp-amber | The shade named the element; the shop must catch them young | Apprentice to *that* door. Earnest work. The ticket when the element answers the hands and not the room. | Will not Speak the colour. Will not switch a child's element because the town needed Tide. |
| A working Answered | The craft *is* the Tithe | Give them the shop, the watch-rotation, the fire-crew, the escort. | Will not bench them "for a rest" without a plan. Idle is the hazard. |
| An Answered who lost the shop | Grief, a wrecked yard, a lord who benched them | Find earnest use *now* — a crew, a demolition, a night at the bellows. | Will not rent them a week of "just enough spark." That is a Slide-shaped lie. |
| A Struck Answered, raw | A threshold let an element in later, untrained | The Intake. The right door, not the nearest fire. | Will not stamp a ticket on a raw Reach. Will not put them on a hull the same day. |
| Anyone who wants a thing made | A hull, a glass, a wall, a sail | Make it. Charge as a craft charges. | Will not let an unlicensed cousin "just coax the stone" in the street. The charter is the street's safety *and* the shop's monopoly. |
| A Road-hand needing carrying-earth | Root can coax living earth to travel | Sell the earth. Do not walk the scion. | Will not become Thaelvaeth. Different cart. |

### Four doors (faces, not chapters)

| Door | The work | The shop-rule you notice | If left idle |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ember** | smith, glass, fire-crew | they never let the last coal go dark | heat finds a place to be |
| **Tide** | hull, dye, anything wet | a cup of yesterday's water on the lintel | water finds a place to be |
| **Root** | mason, potter, carrying-earth | clay under the nails they will not wash on a work-day | stone and soil find a place to be |
| **Gale** | sail, mill, instrument | a window that is never fully shut | wind finds a place to be |

The "place to be" is self-contained, never a seized will. The guild's job is to make sure that place is the shop, not the crowded hall.

### An ordinary week

The shop. The rotation (fire-crew, escort, the watch that is also Tithe). The ticket-stool. The shop-rule no one explains to a guest.

When a master is pulled to Intake, the paying floor loses a Reach for a week. Ember hates this during a dry month. Tide hates this when a hull is mid-bend. That hatred is why Intake is the underdog.

They will not switch an element to suit a labour shortage. The shade named it. They will not bench "for their own good" without earnest use waiting. That is how a room lets go. They will not stamp a raw Struck. They will not become a fourth clergy. They will not export a Crae-college to Kumbaan.

### Who may hold a ticket

**Not by Kind.** Ember is not a Drakona chapter. Gale is not a Tengu chapter. A Tengu Gale-Answered is a common *lean*, not a gate.

**By practice first.** You are of the house when the element answers your work and not the rafters.

The shop goes to the Answered of that element. Fire-crew and rescue rotations to Ember and Gale, also Two-Bodied on the line and Returned in the wreck. Carrying-earth to Root, also Taken-In who will not call it a grove-rite. Kept labor is the free generalist around the Reach — strikers, pullers, the person who holds the mundane tool. Lestrand's *free*. Vaethorn's *Waited*, still useful.

### How they live in the three corners

- **Vaethorn (gift-shops).** One mixed yard, four tools, a blessing-to-practice. Soft injustice: you were answered, you will spend the element as the valley asks — the mill, the wall, the fire-line. Idle is rare because the valley will not let a gift sit.
- **Lestrand (the craft-houses).** Deep shops, posted tickets, monopoly-teeth. You can be priced out of the *chair* and still need the work, which is how a person slides from self-paying to exposed overnight. The charter is money and safety in one paper.
- **Threnmaieth (the Reckoned Craes).** The crown wants element and person on the same column. Workshops are channels. A benched Answered is a withheld Reach. The shop looks like a gift-works. The leash is the fire, the slip, the stone, the wind.
- **Kumbaan.** A wrecked Tide-Answered is a guest. There is no Tide-house on the sand.

### The shop you cannot leave

Self-paying is an economist's miracle until you want to stop. The Tithe does not care that you buried a child, that the yard burned, that you are tired of glass. Idle, the element speaks for itself.

So the guild's kindness (a shop, a crew, a rotation) is also the leash. Leaving is a Tithe-crisis. The Warm calls that vocation. The quay calls that a contract. The crown calls that a channel.

Confirming she is real does **not** make a rest-year safe. She answered. She did not build the shop-rule.

The last coal, the cup of yesterday, the unwashed clay, the window that will not shut — those are how each door remembers.

### A shop-rule

Ember-house, Lestrand glass-yard, nailed above the last coal. The verso is a Tide-house rule from the next quay, used as backing.

> *The last coal stays. A Reach used in play is not a Reach used in earnest — the ticket will not have it, and the room will. If you cannot work, you will still work: the fire-crew, the night bellows, the watch. We do not bench. We do not rent a spark. The shed is not this door; raw hands go to the door that does not face the street. If you have already let go, this is still the house — come to the coal, not to the square. Gale will not take you. Tide will not take you. The coal will.*

On its face: a craft keeping its people steady, a little fierce, a monopoly wearing safety. Underneath: exit refused as care, and a blindness the master cannot afford — that "we do not bench" is also *you may not leave.* The Tide-rule on the verso says the same thing about a cup of water. The two houses did not copy each other. They did not have to.

## The Intake

The shared underdog office of the Stillers, the Hall-Keepers, and the Element-Guilds — mandatory guild-intake for dangerous Struck. Not clergy. Not a travelling circuit. Not the Slide.

Everyday: **the Intake**, or **the raw-desk**, or **the shed.** Guild-cant: **the catching-year.** There is no liturgical name. An office that the paying floor will not write on the wall does not get a holy one. Vaethorn says **the late-catching**; Lestrand says **the emergency ticket**; Threnmaieth files **the unwitnessed warrant**.

**In one sentence:** they have a week to make a live hazard into a person the town will stand next to — and if they succeed, no one writes it down.

### Shape of the office

- **A door, not a road.** The Struck is brought *here*, or a runner arrives and a master is pulled off the paying floor. They are not a circuit. When they travel it is because the nearest ticket-house is three days and the rope is already in someone's hand — an accident of distance, not a route. The Road-hands already have the route.
- **Three ticket-houses, one shed-logic.** A raw Gaze, a raw throat, a raw Reach. Right house. Wrong house is how a Cry gets stilled quiet or an Ember is put on a bench to sing. The office is shared; the door is not.
- **They think they are in a first-aid story.** Catch. Steady. Hand to the year. They do not think they are in a redemption-play.
- **Success is silence.** The guild leadership is **indifferent-to-hostile**. A quiet catching-year is a week the floor lost. A loud one is a charter hearing. No one converts. A grudging extra pair of hands is the most the yard will grant, and they will take them back.

### The underdog shape

- **Mandate.** Take a raw Struck (Stilled / Far-Voiced / Answered) in hand *and* begin the apprenticeship at once, before the town meets them with a rope. The enemy is **time**, an already-live hazard, and a town that wants them *gone*.
- **Constraints.** The shed that does not face the street — not the theatre, not the boards, not the paying shop. One master pulled off the floor, whoever will sit, maybe a Kept who has no Tithe of their own. They can take the person *if the person will come*. They cannot overrule a frontier rope. They cannot stamp a ticket this week. The charter is at risk if the person harms someone *or* if they refuse the Struck and the town later says the guild left a hazard. Leadership forgot them on a good day and will scapegoat them on a bad one. Support is **conditional** on silence.
- **Who ends up on it.** Not a Kind-unit. Not the floor's favourites. The master who can be spared (often the one who cannot). A Kept who will sit because they are *free* and the floor resents that. A Struck who remembers their own door. A junior ticket on probation. Formation is **assigned** or **inherited** — last year's leftovers — more often than recruited.
- **Victory.** The person is still alive, still here, and beginning to be *safe to stand next to*. The town pretends this was always going to happen. The guild does not apologize.

### If you meet one

Tired. Chalk on a sleeve. A key to a door the slate does not name. You would miss them if you came in the front of the infirmary or the playhouse. You would find them if you asked *where the one who was stone yesterday went,* or if you were the runner.

They are useful the way a midwife is useful, except the birth is a later becoming and the town may prefer it not happen.

### Who comes, and when

| Who | Why they come | What Intake does | What they will not do |
|---|---|---|---|
| A raw Stilled (they were stone) | Grey already filling; a rope in the square | Get them off the square. First drill. Reverse what can still be reversed. Sit the Gaze until it is a wound-hold, not a forever. | Will not stamp a ticket. Will not still the crowd as a lesson. |
| A raw Far-Voiced (they drowned) | Undertow already pulling; a Cry in the market | Get them into the hall's back room. First hour *with* a hall-keeper if one will sit. Vent on purpose before it vents them. | Will not open after the lamps as policy. Will not keep them half-full. |
| A raw Answered | An element let go later, untrained | Right door. Earnest use *now* that is not the crowded shop. | Will not put them on a hull the same day. Will not bench them. |
| A family, or a door-keeper, or a Book-hand | Someone must stand; someone wrote *owing* | Take the person. Ask who will stand for them *as what they are now*. | Will not sell a vouch. Will not call them Given. |
| The watch | The square wants them gone | Bargain for the shed instead of the rope. Sometimes lose. | Will not fight the town as a knightly order. They have a key, not a charter to command a street. |
| The paying floor | They want their master back | Finish the week or fail it. Return the master. | Will not promise this will not happen again. |

### An ordinary catching-year (it is not ordinary)

There is no ordinary week. There is a week.

1. Get them off the square, out of the water, away from the shop that is already wrong.
2. Name what is already true: *you carry a Gaze; you carry a voice; you carry a Reach.* Do not name a Mother unless the person does.
3. Find the first honest payment of the Tithe that is not a public hazard — a drill, an hour, a coal that is not the public forge.
4. Find someone who will stand for them *anew*. Intake is not a hiding-place from the vouching seam.
5. At week's end: still raw, beginning to be safe, handed to the real apprenticeship — or gone. Gone means the rope, the road, or a week-keeper who will not make them safe.

They write the catching-year on the **inside** of a ticket-blank, not on the face. The face stays empty until a real ticket is earned. A first-year who writes on the face is made to strike it.

They will not stamp a ticket this week. Competence is not a week old. They will not become a circuit. If they must walk, it is toward *this* Struck, not along a route. They will not rewrite Bound Terms. A raw Bound is a table's problem, not a shed's. The Whisper cannot command; they are not this office. They will not take a raw Taken-In, Returned, Two-Bodied, Long-Lived. No bystander ticket. Those doors have other houses.

### Who may sit the shed

**Not by Kind.** By practice first — you sat a week and the person did not become a public story.

The first drill / hour / coal to a ticket-holder of *that* hazard. The sitting, the water, the food to a Kept (no Tithe of their own; can stay). "I remember the door" to a Struck who came through Intake once. The runner to whoever can run — Two-Bodied; a Far-Voiced crier who will not use the Cry to clear a street.

### How they live with the other houses

The parent houses pretend the shed is not on the wall. Success is silence. A raw Taken-In is *not* this shed — dirt is the lot. If the week fails, a week-keeper may take the overflow. Intake wants them *safe enough to leave.* The Slide wants them *not-fallen enough to stay.* A quiet receiving-rite may happen the same week as a warden's. Civic settling may *send* them here. A door-keeper names the Struck *kin* at the door that made them; kinship is not a ticket. Two courtesies in one week. A Watcher's second mouth may describe the threshold; description is not a stamp.

### How they live in the three corners

- **Vaethorn (the late-catching).** The rope is real. The shed may be a barn. Soft injustice: once caught, the valley will spend them. The late-come are vouched graciously *and* put to work.
- **Lestrand (the emergency ticket).** Posted, almost. A fee if anyone can pay it. The charter-risk is commercial. A raw Struck who cannot stamp a fee is still taken — the street-hazard demands it — and the house will remember the debt.
- **Threnmaieth (the unwitnessed warrant).** The crown wants the Struck *named* as they are caught. Intake's inside-writing is already a defiance. A quiet week looks like a leak. A loud week looks like incompetence. The darkest pressure: catch them for the channel.
- **Kumbaan.** A wreck walked uphill is a guest. There is no shed on the sand.

### The week no one writes

The paying floor needs the master. The town needs the hazard gone. The Struck needs a year. Intake is given a week and a shed, and is blamed for all three.

If they succeed, the story becomes: *the guild trains its own.* If they fail, the story becomes: *the shed.* Confirming she is real does **not** make the rope kind, and does not give them a better door. She did not unwitness the becoming. People built the square that prefers a rope.

Badly paid. The parent house's leftover stipend. In kind: the shed, the food, the master's lost week. They do not furnish a commons. They do not sell an hour. They catch.

### A catching-year slip

Written on the inside of a blank ticket, stillers' shed, core town. The face is empty.

> *Inside only. Gaze, raw. Came from stone on the third night. Stood for by the sister and by N. who will not give a family name. First drill held. No forever. No ticket. If the week ends quiet, burn this. If it does not, the charter already knows. We do not face the street. We do not write the wall. If they have already been a rope-story, this is still the shed — come if they will come. If they will not, we cannot make a town take them. The Mother does not reach twice by chance. Neither do we. That line is the wardens'. Strike it if you must. I have not.*

On its face: a competent catching, a little desperate, a college's shame in a folded paper. Underneath: the office stealing the Seat's sentence because it is the only heavy line the writer knows, and a blindness they cannot afford — that "burn this" is how success becomes the guild's ordinary story and the Struck's becoming stays unwitnessed *twice*. The empty face is the point. The sister stood. N. would not be named. Both are the vouching seam in one slip.

## Three corners: Vaethorn, Lestrand, Threnmaieth

Every polity in the Turning sets three knobs, independently: theology, reach, governance. The three grammars — law, economy, daily life — are portable: they hold across the great powers because they flow from the Trees and the Conditions, which are universal. A polity's character is the **combination** of the three axes it sets separately.

Three corners, chosen so that each pair shares exactly one axis and differs on the other two.

| Name | Theology | Reach | Governance | The one-line feel |
|---|:---:|:---:|:---:|---|
| **Vaethorn** *(the Waiting Lands)* | high | low | low | Ardent faith, thin Trees, almost no state — a warm, poor, half-lawless pilgrim edge |
| **Lestrand** *(the Ledger Coast)* | low | high | low | Faith worn lightly, dense Trees, guilds not crowns — a rich, cool, transactional merchant power |
| **Threnmaieth** *(the Tallied Crown)* | high | high | high | Sacred *and* surveilled — a wealthy, pious core where the census is a holy duty (the darkest corner) |

- Vaethorn and Lestrand share **low governance** — same light-touch state, opposite everything else: warm-poor-faithful vs. rich-cool-secular.
- Vaethorn and Threnmaieth share **high theology** — same ardent faith, wildly different circumstances: devotion at the starving edge vs. devotion in the sanctified, watched core.
- Lestrand and Threnmaieth share **high reach** — same Tree-wealth spent two opposite ways: free markets vs. a sanctified panopticon.

Theology is not a moral axis. High-theology produces both the *kindest* corner to a whole neighbour (Vaethorn) and the *most inescapable* control in the world (Threnmaieth). Devotion is a multiplier on whatever else the polity is, not a virtue or a vice by itself.

The name a polity gives a shared institution reveals its stance. A devout land's "gift-hall" is a surveillance-crown's "counted hall." Same building, three worldviews.

### Vaethorn — the Waiting Lands

*(vay-THORN · theology high · reach low · governance low)*

A broad, thinly-peopled land at the world's Tree-poor margin, where the faith burns hottest precisely *because* the Trees are scarce — and where the ruling mood is *waiting*: for a graft to take, for a distant Tree to be reached, for a gift that may never fall. This is the Frontier with the theology knob turned all the way up — and the combination is not a contradiction, it's a feedback loop: where the Trees are few and young, more children come away Kept, more people are Struck later and alone, and the Motherfaith answers that scarcity not with less devotion but with *more* — pilgrimage, longing, and a theology built around waiting for a gift that may not come.

**Law here.** Governance is almost absent — no crown reaches the individual, and custom outruns written law the way it does on any frontier. Citizenship is witnessed in the oldest, most literal way: a settlement small enough that everyone *was* there when you Turned. But the Lands' thin reach makes citizenship-by-witness fragile: with few Long-Lived to remember and few Bound to vouch, the three pillars that make proof-by-people work are stretched thin, and the **unvouched Struck** — a person who changed alone, with no guild to catch them and few witnesses to stand for them — is a constant, aching problem Vaethorn meets with faith rather than machinery. A feared Condition here is more likely to be met with a rope or a blessing than a licence, because there is no guild to issue one. Standing-for is *the speaking*, vouched at worship. The ticket is *the blessing-to-practice*. The will is *the Mother's own line*.

**Economy here.** Little formal Tithe-infrastructure and few specialists — but the theology reframes the frontier's poverty as *charity*. Where a purely secular frontier improvises Tithe-provision as barter and favor, Vaethorn does it as **alms and congregation**: the venting a Far-Voiced needs happens at worship (the *gift-hall*), the green a Taken-In needs is furnished by neighbours who count it a sacred duty (*the Mother's green*), and the Taken-In whom rich cities keep at arm's length are here the honoured **backbone of the land** — working the living earth *is* the holy economy. It is commons-minded generosity with almost nothing to fund it: real warmth, real want, and a persistent thin edge where the provided-for Tithe-poor slide with only prayer for a net. The ranging is *the wild-leave*. The long-house of books is *the deathless gift*. The Slide here is *the last pot* / *gift-credit*.

**Daily life here.** Thin ceremony made enormous by devotion. A child's Turning at a small, hard-won grafted Tree is the event of a decade, held as a joyous, anxious sacrament — and the **Kept child is felt most sharply here**, because in a land starved of the gift, to be passed over reads (wrongly, and cruelly) as being passed over *by the Mother herself.* The Kept are *the Waited* / *Mother-kept*. The Struck are *the late-come*. Medicine is a travelling stiller if the settlement is lucky and a rope-and-prayer if it is not; the core-vs-edge inequity is at its widest here. Families are warm, mixed, and self-reliant; a lapsing Condition with no infirmary to reach is a frontier tragedy the faith consoles but cannot cure. The warden *is* most of the state: Hands-folk. The door-folk keep the older state the land still keeps. Guest-reading towns pilgrimage *and* walk. First-reading districts tend a dying guest and refuse the blessing.

**Its injustice** *(the soft-frontier blend).* Not a boot — a **guilt**. The scarcity of the gift becomes a theology of *unworthiness*: the Kept wonder what they lacked, the Struck wonder why the Mother came to them at the wrong door and alone, and a hard, quiet piety can shade into treating want as a spiritual failing. Vaethorn is the kindest place in the world to a whole neighbour and the loneliest to an unvouched one.

**The seat.** **Eolvaeth** *(ay-ohl-VAYTH)* — a pilgrim-edge vale behind the Strandoren-facing coast. People walk *here to wait*. The Tree is not a certainty.

### Lestrand — the Ledger Coast

*(leh-STRAND · theology low · reach high · governance low)*

A dense, Tree-rich merchant power where the Trees are mature and many, the Given are dense, and the faith is worn *lightly.* Deep markets, thin welfare, and a cool, transactional read on the whole business of becoming. It shares Vaethorn's light-touch governance — no crown writes anyone down — but where Vaethorn's light state leaves a *gap the faith fills*, Lestrand's light state leaves a *space the guilds fill,* completely.

**Law here.** The world's warm default at its most developed: **guild self-rule** carried as far as it goes. The state is a charter-granting council of merchant houses and craft-guilds; it charters the Stilled's guild and the element-guilds and watches the guild, never the individual. Citizenship-by-witness runs *smoothly* here precisely because reach is high — abundant Long-Lived notaries, Bound whose sworn word closes every contract, Far-Voiced on every dock and in every court. The three pillars aren't reverence here; they're **infrastructure**, the machinery that lets a huge commercial city run on trust without a register. The Struck are vouched-in briskly and without much fuss — not graciously, just *efficiently*, because an unvouched person is a transaction that can't clear. They are *the turned*. The Inviolate Will is cited less as sacred bedrock than as sound commercial law: a Bound's contract binds property, not persons, and the Coast likes that line because it makes the Bound the perfect notary. Standing-for is *warranting*. The ticket is *the guild-ticket*. The will is *the free-will clause*. The Motherfaith is *the old rite*, worn light. The Fair Hand is *the Settling*, ordinary.

**Economy here.** The richest corner, and the coolest. This is where **deathless banking** reaches full flower — deathless houses whose trusted notes are the nearest thing the world has to a gold standard, century instruments, the whole endow-not-inherit loop running at scale (the great endowed libraries and universities cluster here, funding the Long-Lived's own novelty-Tithe). Self-paying Conditions prosper — the Answered crafts, the Two-Bodied haulage and courier trades, the Stilled surgeons — because their labor *is* their upkeep and the market pays for the labor. **Tithe-provision is a private market, not a public good:** you buy your green (*the leaf-lot*), your novelty (*the endowment* / *the house*), your outlet (*the release-house*, also the playhouse), and if you can afford it you stay whole in comfort. **Its injustice is the market's** — the provided-for Tithe-poor get *thin welfare in a rich city*, the starkest have-and-have-not gap in the world, and the shadow economy (smuggled green, black-market novelty, sold vouching, off-book Bound contracts feeding the Given-Over) runs biggest here because the legitimate market is biggest here. The concentration risk the deathless pose is at its most acute: a handful of houses that never die, quietly steering the wealthiest polity through patronage that never disperses. The Slide is *week-rent*. The Kept are *the free*. The ranging is *the beast-yard*. Tree-wardens are *tree-tenders*. Book-hands are *the Settling-hands*.

**Daily life here.** Superb Condition-medicine (the great Stilled infirmaries and deathless-endowed hospitals are *here*, in the rich core) — for those who can reach it; the queue outside it is longest here too. Family life shrugs at theology: a **marriage market that reads Conditions as assets** — a Two-Bodied bloodline is a trade dynasty, a Long-Lived match is a century of credit, and the aching Long-Lived marriage gets weighed coolly as a balance of grief against advantage. The Turning is a civic and commercial milestone more than a sacrament — a family's Leaf-Fall is watched for what guild the child's colour opens. Warm it is not; free and prosperous it very much is. An attic-host of the Open Table is a curiosity and a good supper; "no one is a stranger twice" is printed, by sailors, as a proverb on a card. The Settling will not enforce it.

**Its injustice** *(the market blend).* The coolest of the three. Nothing is *policed* — your marriage, your gift, your faith are your own — but everything is *priced*, and a person who cannot pay slides with a whole rich city stepping around them. Lestrand will never file you or conscript you. It will simply let you fade if you run out of coin, and feel that it owed you nothing.

**The seat.** **Orentel** *(or-EN-tel)* — a salt-quay city on the inner sea. The White Note House sits on the third quay, north side, and is not the crown.

### Threnmaieth — the Tallied Crown

*(THREN-my-eth · theology high · reach high · governance high — the darkest corner)*

A pious, Tree-rich core kingdom that is also Watchful. Take the Watchful touchstone — the one aberrant crown that breaks the world's norm and keeps *a census of persons and their Conditions* — and set it in the wealthiest, most devout, most Tree-dense land, and you get surveillance not as a nervous king's paranoia but as **sanctified duty**: the roster is kept because the Mother's gifts are precious and must be *accounted for.* This is what makes it the darkest of the three — the other two have injustices you can name and resent; this one dresses its injustice in the two things the world most trusts, faith and plenty.

**Law here.** The aberration in full sacred flower. Alone among polities, the Tallied Crown keeps **a record held on people** — every subject and their gift written down, not as guild rolls or tax ledgers (everyone has those) but as a **census of persons**, framed at home as reverent stewardship of the Mother's blessings and experienced as exactly what it is: surveillance. The census is the **Threnhael**. Because reach is high, there is a great deal to count — dense Given (*the marked*), dense guilds, abundant Long-Lived whose memory the crown *distrusts precisely because it isn't the crown's* (Threnmaieth's deepest tell: it will not rely on the three pillars the rest of the world runs on, because living memory answers to itself and a ledger answers to the crown). The **unvouched are hunted and the Struck pursued to be catalogued** (*the unwitnessed*); the Bound are watched far past their real hazard — pure stigma dressed as safety, since the Inviolate Will means the Whisper can't command and the roster's own clerks know it. Devotion supplies the justification the bare Watchful crown lacks: *you would not hide a gift from the Mother, so why hide it from her stewards?* Standing-for is *the naming*. The ticket is *the crown-warrant*. The will is *the crown's assurance*. The Motherfaith is *the Reckoned Faith*. Watchers are *the Grown-Over*, a column on the roll. Book-hands are *the Uncounted Book*. Door-keepers are *the Unreached*. The Open Table is off the map.

**Economy here.** Tithe-need turned to leverage — and consecrated. The crown provides Tithe-upkeep **efficiently, even generously** (a pious, rich state can afford to), but *holds* what it provides: the green (*the tended green*), the novelty (*the crown archive*), the outlets (*the counted hall*), the Stilled medicine, all furnished through channels the crown controls and can withhold. A population whose staying-whole runs through the state is a population that **cannot afford to refuse it** — the most complete control in the world, and here it wears a halo: to receive your Tithe-provision is to receive the Mother's bounty through her appointed hands, and to be cut off is framed as *your* lapse, not the crown's lever. Its deathless-house finance is superb and centuries-deep — but the Long-Lived here are courtiers and crown-archivists as much as bankers, their incorruptible memory bent to the census rather than free to check it. The ranging is *the ranging-roll*. The furnishing is *the counted channel*. The Slide is *the late week* — the leak the roll cannot file without admitting the channel failed. The roll pretends not to know.

**Daily life here.** The sacrament and the census fused. A child's Turning is a **joyous holy day that is also a state registration** — the colour that falls is celebrated with a hymn *and* recorded by a clerk. Marriages of heritable Conditions (Two-Bodied bloodlines especially) are *noticed*, encouraged or discouraged, filed. The medicine that furnishes a Tithe is excellent and runs through the crown's channels. A household whose **bloodline and upkeep are both on file** is a household that cannot easily refuse the crown anything — domestic surveillance with a blessing said over it. The Kept are *pitied* here in the Warm's way *and* recorded in the Watchful's — *the unmarked* / *the passed-over*, passed over by the Mother, and noted as such. Tree-wardens are *the Reckoned Hands*. The First Seat stands in the same Sacred Core. The crown wants wardens named to the Threnhael as officers of the Reckoned Faith. Some Speakers cooperate. Some refuse: the Tree witnessed; a ledger is a second, lesser seeing. Proximity is pressure, not ownership. The college can refuse the roll — and some Speakers don't. A Yumboe in the Sacred Core is a diplomatic incident of manners, not a column. The crown cannot file a people who were never at a Tree. That is a relief and a slight at once.

**Its injustice** *(the sanctified-surveillance blend).* The worst of both the high knobs, each excusing the other. The devotion sanctifies the surveillance (*it is reverence to be counted*), and the surveillance enforces the devotion (*to refuse the count is impiety*). There is no clean villain and no cackling tyrant — only a wealthy, faithful, orderly people who genuinely believe that accounting for the Mother's gifts is the highest form of tending them, and a machinery of control that this belief makes almost impossible to resist. It reads as chilling **precisely because no other polity keeps such a list** — the creepy record is this one crown's invention, and the faith is what lets it feel like grace.

**The seat.** **Maiethlir** *(my-eth-LEER)* — a thaw-river Hand in the Sacred Core. The extra mile is the leftover; the Threnhael is the layer. Does not own Thaeloren. The First Seat sits in the Motherwood beside.
