# GM Canon

Read the player-facing chapters first if you want to meet the world the way a player would. The table does not start the campaign already knowing this chapter.

What follows is what is actually true, whether or not anyone alive can prove it. The central fact is the **keystone**: the Leaf-Mother is real and kind, her reach has a limit, and every gift costs her something to give. Skeptics who say there is no mind behind the Trees are sincere, and they are wrong about that mind. If a campaign ever confirms her, the moment should land as warmth, with an ache under it, because a mother who saves whom she can reach, and grieves the rest, is the one this world has.

A **Tithe** is the upkeep a person has to meet to live well with a Condition, the becoming that sits on top of the ancestry they were born with. The frightening edge of a Tithe is pressure in the story. It is never a trick you spring, and it never takes the player's character out of the player's hands. Consequences land on that person's own body, memory, Stress, or the ground immediately around them, unless a feature on the card says otherwise. Nobody's will is bent. Keep the frightening edge at about five percent, small enough that the day stays neighbours and work and the obstacle underneath stays real.

## GM only: Conditions

The cards do not level. If a Condition is ever going to advance, that has to be a new decision, and you do not quietly write levels onto a card. There is one Phoenix Gift in the world. How a house can catch the fire, and what a hidden Phoenix player still gets to choose, is under **Secrets and reveals** later in this chapter. The population shares, including the warning not to count the Unbound twice, already sit in Kind and Condition. Do not keep a second census here.

**Long-Lived.** Going still is how a Long-Lived dies, which is what happens when the memories are gone, and that death is not a death move you drop into a fight. Across several long rests the player can see it coming: Stress, then a Hit Point, then the stillness. Use that as story pressure, a deathless archivist found statue-still in a sealed room, and do not use it as a sudden kill or as a spiral of Hit Points that will not clear. The fade before the stillness is a mind thinning. It is not a curse lying on every roll the character makes.

Blood-Read is how you investigate and how you let a scene feel something, because one drop, freely given or taken from a wound that is already open, shows a true memory you narrate, such as a dead man's last sight or the reason a witness is lying. That drop is not feeding, not damage, and not a drain, and you do not spend it as a weapon in a fight. The threshold courtesy is the texture of a household. A Long-Lived can cross a threshold uninvited if they truly must, and they simply never do, because the manners run that deep. A foe who tries to put either habit on the sheet and call it a vampire rule has left the Condition.

**Two-Bodied.** The forced shift, which is the Tithe, is about timing and inconvenience. It nudges the player to live in both bodies, and it does not take the choice of action away. The other self is a partner in the roleplay, not a second player at the table. It can want, argue, sulk, and celebrate. The wolf pushes to give chase, and the cat refuses the cold water. It never rolls, never acts, and never overrides a choice the player made. If a table reaches "the animal made me do it," pull the game back to nagging and let the player decide. Encourage the player to give the animal a name and a voice that is not their own. That name is the cheapest richness this Condition has.

This is the commonest Condition, about eighteen percent of people, so the free shift and the natural weapon are meant to be worth using in a scene. A hawk really flies, and an otter really swims, and you do not clip what the animal naturally does. What you do hold is the size, the ban on mythic beasts, and the fact that the animal body has no hands, no person's mind, no speech, and no tools. A Two-Bodied can be a better animal than a Tengu, who is a person in the air, or one of the Answered, who use tools in the water, and cannot be a better person than either of them. One signature capability is the start, and a second comes only by a milestone in the story, so a veteran does not stack scout, tracker, and bruiser into one body. The natural weapon belongs to the animal body. It does not stack with a weapon the person-body is holding, because they are one body or the other, and its die does not rise when the character levels. Proficiency does.

**Returned.** Refuse to Fall means hard to keep down, which is a finite road and not immortality, because every use costs a scar, a Hope slot, the same mark any character spends when they brush death, and the table can watch the count fall. They leave the current scene and pull themselves together by the next long rest. When the last Hope slot would go, they cannot come back, and that is the true death, so a Returned near the end of their scars still feels a death move. Do not offer Avoid Death as an extra courtesy, because they already died, and the safe unconsciousness is what they spent when they did. A Purpose left to lapse, the unfinished work they came back to finish, is a quiet crisis, not a punishment you assign.

The hand that Strikes them has a name on this side of the screen. It is the Quiet Tally, *Threnvaeth*, the clerk of the Leaf-Mother's household who keeps the unfinished work of the dead, and she acts under the Leaf-Mother's allowance. The Leaf-Mother does not Give this Condition at the Tree. Threnvaeth authorizes the catch when the death comes with work still unfinished, and she does not steer that work. The Purpose stays the Returned person's own. Player text does not name Threnvaeth.

**Bound.** Keep the Whisper strictly suggestive, plausible lies and intrusive thoughts which the player can reject by spending a Stress, and never puppet the character. The Pledge, the part of themselves they named to give when the contract ends, is a known and dreaded horizon toward the Unbound, whom people call the Hollow. It is not a surprise you save for the last session of the term.

The Counterparty who answers a sworn ask is **Orledd**, a Hand of the Leaf-Mother's household. The face chosen at creation, whether demon, cosmic horror, or unknown, is a mask Orledd wears, and the terms underneath have the same shape whatever the face. Do not play Orledd as a devil, and do not let this turn a Given Condition into a bargain. During the term, what Orledd receives is the named ask remaining true, and the Terms being kept where other people can see them. At the end of a term-limited contract they collect the Pledge, which is already on the card. They do not take a soul, worship, or a second signing. The Given-Over, a Bound who signs again and again until the other side effectively holds them, is that person's fear of paying, not Orledd's hunger. A flood of unread bargains starves a Counterparty. How allowance strains and breaks is under **The Other Hands**.

Do not add the Hollow as a second percentage on top of Bound's about five percent. The Unbound, about one and a half percent of people, are already inside that contract-lane. About three and a half percent are currently Bound. Listing both as parallel shares double-counts them.

**The Unbound.** The Quiet tempts. It does not compel. Every time the emptiness reaches for a bargain, they can refuse, and the refusal costs a Stress. The tragedy to play toward is an Unbound who stops connecting. The quiet inside gets loud, they sign again to mute it, they pay another Pledge, and they slide toward the Given-Over. Warmth, another person who knows their name and stays, is the only thing that actually fills the hole. A new contract only mutes it.

Keep three lanes apart when a table starts to blur them. Absence stops Stress, or Fear, that comes through the pledged hole, and it is not an immunity to every roll in the same broad lane. Quiet spends a token so the character can act anyway. The unshaken clause on the Returned is corpses, gore, and deathly places, and only the Returned have it. If a table is automatically passing every fear roll on both cards, pull the ruling back to those lines.

**Far-Voiced.** The Cry is a feeling that lands on listeners and costs them Stress, and it never forces an action or a belief. A Struck Far-Voiced arrives with the voice raw, so their first Cry, before any guild has had them, is a formative beat of how they became this, and you do not assign it as a punishment. A Given Far-Voiced deep in Tithe-debt, at no tokens, on duty in a courtroom or a relay tower, is a set-piece you build toward. It is not a surprise you spring.

**The Stilled.** The Gaze against an unwilling target is an attack, Instinct or Presence. A willing creature, or a creature that is down, needs no roll. A miss still adds a Grey token, the stillness already spent off the Stilled and onto the card, because the stillness has left them whether or not it landed. **Stilled** is a special condition, not the ordinary Restrained condition. Restrained only stops movement. Stilled stops the creature from acting. They can still speak, be moved, and be targeted, and they may mark a Stress to clear it when they would act. Do not apply both conditions unless some other effect has earned Restrained on its own.

The seizing, which comes when the Grey is full at three tokens, locks the Stilled's own limb, or it locks harmless scenery. It never takes another player character's turn, and it never forces an action. A Struck Stilled's first, unpracticed Gaze, probably on someone they love and probably by accident, is the story of how they became this. A Given Stilled deep in Grey-debt on a riot line or in an operating theatre is a set-piece you build. The apprentices touch the knob first. The yard does not explain that drill, and a knob that has gone still is a practical tell of a seizing, not a relic.

**The Answered.** Every consequence at no tokens stays on the Answered and on the ground, air, or water immediately around them. Never force an effect onto another creature's body, action, or will. Let the element the player chose become the character's whole voice, because an Ember-Answered does not feel like a Root-Answered in a fight or in a quiet room. They ask. They do not own a servant, and nobody else is the master of what answered them. One of the Answered deep in Tithe-debt, walking into a crowded hall, a ship's hold, or a dry archive, is a set-piece you build toward.

**The Taken-In.** Going to seed stays in the room around them, because the growth comes out of the ground and out of the structure, and the roots do not seize another creature or force an action. A Struck Taken-In's uncertainty, whether they walked out or were sent, is a quiet dread about who they are. The wild in them is part of them, not a master and not a string someone else is pulling. One of the Taken-In dragged deep into a dungeon, down into a ship's hold, or left inside a stone keep to wilt, is a set-piece you build.

**Phoenix.** You run two things at once. The count: let everyone watch the Hope slots go, and make each rising cost something the player feels, a face or the reason they started, not merely a mark on the sheet. The attention: a Phoenix on the open card is never anonymous. Seed every town with people who revere them, worship them, hunt them, and hate them, and let those often be the same crowd. Never force a Rise, because Rise From Ash is always the player's choice among the death moves, beside the standard three, and a Phoenix may choose Blaze of Glory and end if the story has earned that ending. Fire immunity stays full, because a Phoenix is fire. The bonus to Evasion comes once per rest, and a Rise is a new body rather than a full resetting of the person. Played as a legendary non-player character, the rebirths make markers you can hang an era on: the same soul, met three times, remembering the party a little less each time.

The rule's name is One Gift. There is never a second living Phoenix Condition while this one can still Rise. If the game seems to need two, the leftover is not a Phoenix. The house that catches the fire is the Protectors, and the rules they use, which they do not get to rewrite, are later in this chapter.

## GM only: Peoples

Where a Kind lives, Kind meaning the ancestry a person is born into, tells you something about her reach only for the Yumboes. Mainland hearths are ordinary geography: foxes in the woods, seals on the coasts, crows on the ridges. A Kind-hearth is a people's home ecology, not a nation. Kumbaan's isolation is the one Kind-fact that also maps the edge of the keystone. There is no Tree there, no Gift, and the people are themselves without her. "Yumboes have no Conditions" is a fact about the place. It is not a racial immunity.

No power is founded for a Kind. A place where many Foxes live is a Rain-Shadow polity that happens to have many Foxes, and it runs on the same faith, the same reach, and the same government as everyone else. The moment a government exists for a Kind, Kind has become a country, and the two-layer model has broken, because Kind is what you are born and a Condition is what you become, and they stack. Factions recruit by Condition, by guild, and by faith. A tree-warden order that took only Kitsune would be that break. A town can have a fox-market, a Selkie quay, or a Tengu perch without the town being that Kind. Ornsael is the worked example: a market by the well, mixed Kinds, no gate.

A grandmother still says the word for the lost fourth Fox. If you use it, use the folklore that stayed when a hearth died, a dry lake that vanished or a wood that was cut. Nobody explains the word, and it is not a secret ancestry feature. A Fungril household sets one spoonful aside, and they will not say who it is for. That spoonful is the same kind of habit. Do not turn it into a feature, and do not decide who the spoon is for.

An ordinary introduction carries two names, a Kind and a place, and the players should hear both. It is not a secret identity. A Yumboe who takes a mainland second name has decided to stay. A Threnmaieth clerk who refuses to record hearth-names is following the roll, which wants the house, not the kitchen. Witness-lands use a byname. Threnmaieth, Trenledd, the Inner Close, and filing houses use an inherited house-name, said the way a surname is said, where a list has to find the person again.

Each polity's vocabulary is how it excuses itself. It is not the truth of what it does. Threnmaieth's "keeping-whole," **Threnhael**, is a census that calls itself care. Lestrand's "the free," for the **Kept**, the people the Tree hugged and left without a colour, is a market reframing a social slight as a virtue. The Tree hugged them, with no colour and no Condition Given. Vaethorn's "the Waited" wraps real pity in reverence. Confirming the Mother does not make any of those three names honest. They are how each society excuses its own injustice, and a kind Mother did not author the excuse.

The word-box and the how-to for naming a person live in Language and Naming. What the linguistics words mean, and the sound-change that makes a name wear the same way every time, is the next section.

## GM only: Language

Language and Naming is where you name a person, mint a new one, glue a hall, and look up what a land calls a building. Keep that chapter as the how-to. This section is what the linguistics words mean, which sounds each way of speaking uses, and the regular wearing-down that lets you push any old word through a land and still have it sound like that land. Use it when you need a name that still belongs and the lists are not enough. It is not taught at the table.

### What the linguistics words mean

Linguists have names for things everyone already does with speech. Here they are in this world's terms, so a reader who has not opened the language chapter can still follow a name.

| Word | What it means here |
|---|---|
| **Root** | The smallest chunk of meaning. *oren* means "to witness." You glue roots to make names. |
| **Lexicon** | The word-box. The list of roots you are allowed to build from. The old-word table in Language and Naming is the lexicon. |
| **Compound** | Two roots glued. **Thaeloren** is *thael* (Tree) + *oren* (witness), said THAEL-or-en, with the stress on the first root. |
| **Etymology** | Where a name comes from. Players hear a pretty word. You know Thaeloren means "the Tree that witnesses." |
| **Phonology** | Which sounds a language uses, and which it will not. English has *th*; many languages do not. Kusawe has *sh* and no *th*. |
| **Syllable** | One beat of sound. *Ra-ki* is two syllables. |
| **CV / CVC** | A recipe for a beat. **C** is a consonant, **V** is a vowel. CV is *ra*. CVC is *tek*. CVF in Kusawe means the beat may end on n, m, or r. |
| **Stress** | The beat you hit harder. Capitals mark it. This is not the Stress a character spends to stay in a scene. A transparent compound, one you can still hear as two roots, stresses the first root: THAEL-or-en, VAETH-orn. |
| **Digraph** | Two letters, one sound. *ae* is "ay" as in *day*. *ai* and *ei* are "eye." *eo* is "ay-oh." |
| **Onset** | The consonant or cluster at the start of a beat. *Kr* in Kukra is an onset. Tengu likes those as ordinary speech. The Old Tongue only inherited a few. |
| **Prenasal** | A hum before a stop: *mb*, *nd*, *nk*, *nj*. Yumboe uses them. The Old Tongue does not. |
| **Register** | A way of speaking used for a job, not a whole language you write sentences in. Kusawe is the fox hearth-register: given names and in-group talk. |
| **Liturgical / Old Tongue** | The church language, **Maiethren**. Like Latin: nobody's first language at the market, still used for the rite and for old names. |
| **Vernacular / common tongue** | What people actually say day to day. Worn-down Old Tongue. *The Kept*, *the ranging*, *tree-wardens*. |
| **Daughter language / drift** | When one language splits into regional versions. Threnmaieth, Vaethorn, and Lestrand all come from Maiethren. They did not invent new tongues. They wore the same one differently. |
| **Conservative** | A drift that kept the old sounds. Threnmaieth still says *th*. |
| **Worn** | A drift that wore them down. In ordinary speech *th* after a vowel becomes *t*, and endings simplify. Written Vaethorn is spoken **Wetorn**. Careful prayer keeps the *th*. |
| **Eroded** | A drift that sanded sounds off. Lestrand dropped *th* to t or d, or dropped it. |
| **Sound-change** | A regular swap, not a one-off. If *th* becomes *t* on the docks, it does that in every word, not just one. That is why you can push any old word through the same rule and it will still sound like Lestrand. |

You do not need the rest of a linguistics toolkit. There is no grammar to learn, and you do not dress English sentences in swapped vocabulary and call the result Maiethren. Nobody writes sentences in Kusawe, Sakoa, or Gonan. If a scene needs an in-group word, coin one word from that Kind's sounds and say what it means in the same breath.

### The Old Tongue's sounds

The Old Tongue feels warm and weighty, a hall-and-hearth tongue. Liquids and nasals, *l*, *r*, *m*, and *n*, carry the warmth. The *th* of *thin*, and the long vowels, carry the weight. Clusters exist, and they are a small inherited set, not an invitation to pile consonants together.

**Sounds it uses.** Consonants: m n, l r w j, p t k, b d g, f v s h, and *th* as in *thin*. Vowels: a e i o u, plus the digraphs *ae*, *ai*, *ei*, and *eo*. An ordinary beat is an optional opening consonant, an optional *r* or *l* after it, a vowel that may be written as a digraph, and an optional closing consonant. The clusters the old words already use are *br*, *cr* or *kr*, *st*, *str*, and *th* plus *r*. Closings already in the language include *sk* and *nd*. Those patterns are what license *bren*, *crae*, *stel*, *strand*, *thren*, and *hesk*. A new root should prefer the simpler shape. Do not invent a three-consonant opening except *str-*, and do not invent a four-consonant knot.

**Sounds it does not use.** Prenasals, *mb*, *nd*, and *nj*, belong to Yumboe. *Sh* is a fox sound, not a church sound. Free front clusters such as *gr* and *hr*, and *kr* used as the ordinary feel of a name, belong to Tengu. The Old Tongue's *kr*, as in *crae*, is one inherited word-shape, not that ridge habit.

**How to say it.** Capitals are the stressed beat. *Th* is as in *thin*, never as in *the*. *Ae* is "ay" as in *day*, *ai* and *ei* are "eye," and *eo* is "ay-oh." *A* is as in *father*, *e* as in *bed*, *i* as in *machine*, *o* as in *go*, and *u* as in *moon*. A simple root keeps the stress listed for it. A compound you can still hear as two roots takes its main stress on the first root and a lighter beat on the second, so the Awakening Tree is THAEL-or-en and the Waiting Land is VAETH-orn. Stress is not a rule that blindly hits the second syllable. Old ceremonial forms, and personal names, may keep a stress they already had, and the pronunciation given the first time you use the name is the one to trust.

The word-box is the old-word table in Language and Naming. Glue one or two roots. Then wear the compound the way the land wears it.

### How an old word wears in each land

One Tree, with grafts carried out from it. One old tongue, with names wearing down as they travel. Linguists would call Threnmaieth, Vaethorn, and Lestrand daughter languages, three children of Maiethren. In this book they are the three drifts. There is no fourth human drift.

The sound-changes are regular. Push any old word through the same rule.

| Drift | Land | What happens to the sounds | How it feels |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Conservative** | Threnmaieth, pious Maiethorn | Almost nothing. *Th* and long vowels kept whole. | Archaic, formal, holy. Sounds like the rite because it barely moved from it. |
| **Worn** | Vaethorn, Heskoren folk | *Th* after a vowel, or sitting between vowels, becomes *t* in speech (Vaethorn becomes Wetorn). Endings simplify. Unstressed vowels flatten. Careful prayer keeps the *th*. | Rough-warm folk-speech that still reaches for the old holy words. |
| **Eroded** | Lestrand, Strandoren docks | *Th* becomes t or d, or drops. Long vowels clip. Word-ends wear off. Trade-words sneak in. | Smooth, clipped, mercantile. The most worn name belongs to the least-devout place. |

The same rule, worked through:

- *thael* plus *oren*, kept whole, is **Thaeloren** (THAEL-or-en). That is the Awakening Tree, "the Tree that witnesses." The worn everyday name is **the Thallow**.
- *vaeth* plus *orn*, kept with *th* in writing, is **Vaethorn**. Spoken, it is **Wetorn**.
- *ledd* plus *strand*, with the *th* already gone, is **Lestrand**.
- *thren* plus *maieth*, kept whole, is **Threnmaieth**. The same *thren* worn to *Tren* is **Trenledd**: the sacred count with the sacred sanded off.
- *oren* plus *thael*, with the *th* dropped, is **Orentel**. That is the salt-quay seat.
- A devout river-town on a thaw, *maieth* plus *lir*, is **Maiethlir**, *th* kept.

A named power uses these roots and the drift that matches how it stands. Vaelhesk keeps the older *vael*, what the land yields, and the frontier *hesk-*. That is still this family, worn further. Kumbaan is not in this family.

Coinage is frozen. Do not make another liturgical compound merely because two familiar roots fit. The families closed to new compounds are *eol-*, *sael-*, *vael-* and *vaeth-*, *bren-*, *maieth-* and *thren-*, *-oren*, and *-hael*. The words that already exist remain. What is still available, and should be used sparingly, is *orn*, *lir*, *neth*, *ledd*, *hesk*, *strand*, *stel*, and *crae*, and a new use still has to be said out loud next to the names it might be mistaken for. Two cants stay unused and should not be fleshed out: *Orenvaeth* and *Thaelneth*. *Aeloren* is retired. *Eolstrand* is retired in favour of the Hinge Shore.

| Family | Existing words that remain | Ruling |
|---|---|---|
| **eol-** | Eolthael, Eoloren, Eolvaeth | Closed. *Eolstrand* retired; the name is the Hinge Shore. |
| **sael-** | Saelthael, Saelvaeth, Saelorn, Saelhael, Ornsael | Closed. Use common handles in speech. |
| **vael- / vaeth-** | Vaethorn, Vaelhesk, Vaeloren, Vaelbren, Vaelun, Vaethledd, Maiethvael, Leddvael, Thaelvaeth, Brenvaeth | Closed. The final *th* is too slight a spoken distinction to carry another category. |
| **-oren** | Thaeloren, Vaeloren, Leddoren, Eoloren, Nethoren, Orenhael, Orenbren, Orentel, Heskoren, Strandoren | Closed. *Aeloren* retired. |
| **bren-** | Brenvaeth, Brenorn, brenhael, Brenthael, Brenledd, Brenod | Closed. |
| **maieth- / thren-** | Maiethren, Maiethorn, Maiethvael, Maiethlir, Threnmaieth, Trenledd, Threnhael | Closed. |

Place-names and house coinages stay off the person-list. Do not name a person Vaethorn, Lestrand, Threnmaieth, Thaeloren, Maieth, Kumbaan, Ndeyaan, Haelthael, Leddoren, or Vaeloren, and do not give them Orenhael, Thaelvaeth, Nethoren, Leddhael, Vaelbren, Njaalo, Njawaal, or Vaethledd. The squares Ornsael, Brenthael, Brenod, Vaelun, Ornath, and Harrow's are reserved the same way, and so are the seats Eolvaeth, Orentel, and Maiethlir, and the hall Ndenjoo.

### Why the drifts sit where they sit

People in the world will tell you that names wear down with distance and trade. That reading is true as far as it goes. The daughters drift furthest where the Trees are thinnest, and the deeper pull that keeps the old sound near the Trees is the Mother's nearness. Same fact, two readings. Player text does not state the deeper one.

History adds a mechanism you can play. The pilgrimage itself was a conserving pull, because people from far away walked to the same grove each year and the old sound got refreshed. Local Trees then let the daughter drifts grow in the place where people lived, so the change of the Years of Hands sits on a root the Walking Years had held closer.

**Thaeloren** is not decoration. The name means "the Tree that witnesses." She is real, and the Leaf-Fall, the tenth-year ceremony when a leaf chooses a child, is her seeing each child. The language keeps a truth the skeptics have explained away. Player text does not call that mere poetry. Let a scholar notice it, or save it for a reveal late in a campaign.

### The four Kind registers

Each custom Kind has a register, a closed set of sounds for given names and in-group talk. It is not a language you write sentences in. The lists in Language and Naming are what you pick from. These inventories are how you mint a new name that still belongs.

**Kitsune: Kusawe** (koo-SAH-way). Light, slipping, a little hissy.

**At the table**

- Consonants k s sh n r m t w y f h
- Vowels a i u e o, and *ai* as in *eye*
- Beats CV, or CV ending on n, m, or r. Two or three beats.
- Never uses *th* (church), *mb/nd/nj* (Yumboe), *kr/gr* (Tengu)

**Selkie: Sakoa** (sah-KOH-ah). Liquid, rounded, coastal.

**At the table**

- Consonants l r n m s v f d b k w
- Vowels o u a i e, and *ea*, *oa*
- Beats CV or CVC. Two or three beats.
- Never uses *th*, *sh*, *kr/gr*, *mb/nd/nj*

**Tengu: Gonan** (GO-nan). Clipped, windy, high-country. The names start hard, on a single consonant or on *kr*, *gr*, or *hr*. This is ridge-speech, not the fox register, and Tengu and Kitsune do not share one.

**At the table**

- Consonants k t g n r h s b d. Front clusters *kr*, *gr*, *hr* are at home.
- Vowels a o u e. Few *i* (that vowel lives with the foxes).
- Beats CVC or CV. One to three beats, prefer two.
- Never uses *th*, *sh*, *mb/nd/nj*, long *ai/ea/oa*

**Yumboe.** Not Maiethren. Open vowels, a drum-like rhythm, prenasals *mb*, *nd*, *nk*, and *nj*. Their own name is *Bakhna Rakhna*, "the good people." Home is *Kumbaan*, "the hidden home." It has no *th*, and it is not clipped down to a dock-name.

A name that bleeds across registers is a tell. A Selkie named Wishi, or a Tengu named Maethaem, was raised away, adopted, or is passing. Use it on purpose, or do not use it at all. If the first fox-name that comes to mind is Yuki, throw it back. Same for Morag on a Selkie and Kenji on a Tengu. Pick from the middle of the existing list, or build from the inventory above until the name could sit next to Raki, Lavo, or Teku without anyone blinking.

The player recipe stays in Language and Naming: pick from a list, or use the make-another steps, or glue two old words and wear them for the land. The inventories above are the same registers written as sound-lists, so a new name can be checked against the rule. The sound-change table is how you push any old word through a land without inventing a fourth human drift.

## GM only: Society and Faiths

The Leaf-Mother exists. The Turning Trees are genuinely her hands, so when a Tree chooses, she is choosing. A **scion** is a grafted town Tree, continuous living tissue of the one Awakening Tree. The Conditions are real gifts. The Leaf-Fall is an act of love: she looks at a child and gives them a becoming the world will need and that they can carry. The Tithe is the honest weight of that gift, not a hidden price and not a trap. She is kind and finite. She cannot lay a hand on every child, cannot reach every threshold in time, and cannot give without spending. The limit is real. She is doing her best against it. A mother who could save everyone and did not would be sinister. That is not the mother this world has.

The Kept are spared, and some of them are simply past her edge. A hug and no colour is, for most, a deliberate and kind choice, the gift of being left whole. The constraint means the Kept are not all one thing. Some were chosen for wholeness, and some are children she could not reach far enough to give to this turning, held safe rather than gifted. She does not tell them apart, and neither can they. Both are held. Not both are chosen. No later week is offered to sort them. The Given-door is one week, a completed standing is once, and a miss is not a retry.

The Struck are still hers, at the farthest reach of her arm. She Gives at the Tree and does not Strike, so the catch at a later door, a death, an oath, a drowning, stone, or the deep wild, is enacted by lesser gods of her household, each restricted to one door, working under her allowance. The Struck are still hers because those hands are hers to send. **Narrow-Hand**, the reading that she only Gives at the Tree, is right about that limit. **Wide-Hand**, the reading that the Struck are still hers, is right about belonging. Neither holds the whole.

Her kindness is cosmological, not social. She authored the gifts. She did not author the injustice built on top of them. Confirming that she is real and kind must not make the society just. The keystone answers whether anyone is there and whether they mean well, and the answer is yes. It does not answer whether what people built around her is fair, and often it is not. Protect that gap. That is where the play is. The queue for a scion, the suspicion that follows an unvouched Struck person, the over-watching of the Bound, the guild that conscripts the Given, Tithe-need used as leverage, the Kept pitied as spare, the Given-Over's lost personhood, the lintel recut over an older faith, the book that will not close, the host of a dying guest, the sentence that will not travel, the week that will not end, the gate, the scheduled hour, the Grey used as labour, the shop you cannot leave, and the week no one writes: these are things people built. A kind Mother does not dissolve them.

The three corners do not share a mechanism. Vaethorn's injustice is a guilt-theology of unworthiness. Lestrand's is a market that lets the poor fade. Threnmaieth's is a sanctified census. Confirming she is kind does not make any of the three fair. Rival faiths can be used as leverage. Dissent is not automatically the moral good. The same confirmation does not dissolve Threnmaieth's counting of heretics, Lestrand's priced fading, or Vaethorn's guilt.

The household is still GM-only. She is first of a household. She Gives, and lesser Hands Strike at restricted doors under her allowance. Player text does not confirm this. The Open Table's staying-kin are not of that household, and Kumbaan is not part of it.

Each rival holds a true kernel and misses the whole.

The Watching correctly remembers a Before, and a cost, and a household from before that household was named Mother. They miss that the seeing is hers. The Masked reading, that she is a later face, and the Bare Tree reading, that there was never a she, are both playable. Neither is the answer, and the lintel is a clue, not a verdict. The cost they remember is real. What was spent is still the open nature of her limit. They get no seat in a late reveal, because that would turn the heretics into a second Motherfaith. The warmth lands through them, not as them.

The Fair Hand is right that a Counterparty exists for the Bound, and wrong that every becoming is a bargain. The Mother's Gifts are still gifts. They have misread a cost as a price, and they have correctly felt that giving is not free. They are not devil-worshippers. A Book-hand cannot turn a Given child into a debtor, and cannot rewrite the Counterparty's clause. A petition-watch is keeping the door. It is not the authority of an acolyte.

The Old Ways are right that land-doors are real. They miss that she authorized the guests. Keep the grievance real: the grafts are late, and the Motherfaith did talk as if becoming were new. Keep the miss intact. Ancestor-meals are not the Returned. The dead in the ground are kin who remained. The Returned are a different door.

The Open Table is the cleanest experiment: no Gifts, rare Struck, a faith that was never hers. Their claim, that they were never lacking, is still a late beat. They are not ghosts. The unseen hands do ordinary household work. "No one is a stranger twice" is courtesy with legal weight on the isle, and a proverb on the quay. It is not a geas, which would be a binding spell, and it is not a trap-cult, and it is not a Fair Hand contract. A wreck is not a Struck-door she authorized.

If a campaign works toward the keystone, the three old corners are the Watching, the Old Ways, and the Open Table: pre-Mother, pre-graft, and never-grafted. The Fair Hand is the modern misreading, commerce wearing a theology. Use all four. No one of them speaks the reveal for the others.

The Tree is something players can see, and it sits next to the keystone. "Every Tree is literally the one Tree's hand" is the physical fact that, followed to its end, confirms she is real and present. Present that as doctrine the faithful hold, never as demonstrated cosmology. Skeptics grant the grafting and deny the mind. The Tree is not eerie, and the ceremony is not sinister. Dread lives in individual Tithes, not in the rite.

Use the schism again. The three questions, what a hug means, what a Struck becoming means, and whether a mind is there, keep supplying people, factions, and family arguments. No player-facing scene awards the win to either branch. Believers see the hand and miss the edge. Skeptics see the edge and miss the hand. The world is built so that being a skeptic costs nothing day to day, which keeps the argument poignant. It does not turn the skeptics into a joke.

**The Child at Four Doors.** The paradox is the point. The child cannot pick a door without already having been claimed, and cannot be claimed without picking. Each faith makes the middle bearable by deciding that its door was the one that had been open the whole time. None of the last lines is the true cosmology. The true cosmology is that a mother reached, at cost, and did not reach everyone the same way. The fable will not say that. Which ending a person offers is a faster read of their stance than a speech about doctrine. There is no official fifth door. The Open Table already refuses the frame of four. A children's story does not settle the keystone. Save the warmth for the confirming beat, if it ever comes.

**The Orenhael** are the tree-wardens, and the everyday name is tree-wardens. The liturgical name in use is Orenhael, and that form is still polishable the way the household names are. They are right about the wood and incomplete about the hand. Every scion is continuous tissue of the one Tree, and when any warden Speaks a colour they are naming a choice she made. Present them as competent neighbours, not as people who secretly know. A skeptic town-warden is good at the job and wrong about the mind. The Seat runs believer-heavy. A skeptic Speaker at the grove is possible, and uncomfortable. The split is the queue, not the Tree. The Seat sees soil and carrying-earth. The hamlet past the ford sees another year of borrowed Turnings. Which town gets the next graft is a human rationing of her reach. Threnmaieth does not own the Seat. Being near it is pressure, not ownership. A First Seat that was the Threnhael would be a darker setting. Keep the college able to refuse the roll.

**The Slide.** They do not know what story they are in. They think they are in a rent-and-credit story. They want the week to continue, not a victim they relish, and a corpse is a ruined asset. Play them as patient, useful, and sure they helped. The town is complicit, because the town is glad not to see the Cry, and the week-keeper is not the only villain. Official Tithe-halls and the licence-guilds are the public, posted, counted version of furnishing. This house is the overflow those guilds pretend not to know, and a gate that closed on purpose can now meet them at the table. They do not rewrite Terms, steal scions, or sell a false Turning-witness. Those trades have other faces. The everyday name, the Slide, and the name week-keepers, are settled. The cant Vaethledd may still be polished. It is not Orledd, and it is not the retired Nethledd.

**Official halls and greens.** The people who keep them think they are in a wells-and-yards story. They want a town that does not wilt, Cry, or seize in the square. They do not want a client. Every closed gate feeds a week-book. The words they are comfortable inside are share, hour, ticket, and enough. She gave the Tithe. She did not build the lot.

**Greens-Keepers.** They want a living lot, not a client. A wilt on the lot is a ruined commons. A wilt off the lot is weather they have decided not to see. The buried leaf is how the lot remembers. A player character who digs it up has committed a local rudeness, and has not found how the world works. The bed-slate is plants. If it is already a quiet census, that is Threnmaieth leaking, not the world's baseline. They will not tell a guest what the leaf is for.

**Hall-Keepers.** Put both jobs in the one hall, the furnished hour and the Voice-ticket, and keep them two jobs. Far-Voiced people are where those jobs meet. Write the unswept bench as what the hall does. Nobody says which Cry it remembers, and you do not explain it. The old liturgical form Aeloren is retired. No living hall claims it.

**Stillers.** They want a street that does not seize and a patient who lives. They do not want a register of the feared. Holding a citizen still is ordinary violent crime, the one harm the code names. The Gaze can leave a person helpless in a fight. It never seizes a will, it does not hold them past the scene, and it does not turn them to stone. Whether a ticket can be held as guild property is Lestrand's live argument, not world-law. A ticket that becomes a week's rent has left the yard and met the Slide. Do not merge the two doors.

**Element-Guilds.** The four elements are four doors under one set of rules, the Crae, from *crae*, "craft." They want work that slakes the Tithe, and a room that holds the element. They do not want a client. Write the last coal, yesterday's water, the unwashed clay, and the unshut window as what the guild does. Nobody in the guild explains them, so do not turn them into theology or into four relics. "We do not bench" is care in the voice of the shop, and it is also the exit refused. Gale-Tengu is a lean. Ember-Drakona is a lean. Leans are not chapters. The Root guild does not walk a scion for a Road-hand, and there is no Tide-house on Kumbaan's sand.

**The Intake.** They want a quiet week. They do not want a client, a relic, or a route. Success is burned, so the town never has to look at the week, and Given children got a childhood of training the Struck will never get. Travel is accidental, a runner toward this person. If you find them on the road every session, you have cloned the Road-hands, who already walk. The shed does not take a Bound person, because that contract is a table and not a body-ticket, or a Taken-In person, because that upkeep is a lot. It does not take a Returned person, a Two-Bodied person, or a Long-Lived person. The rope, which is the sentence a hearing can pass, alongside the wall, for murder or for holding a citizen still, is also what people say when a raw Struck person has nowhere left but the street. The shed exists so the week can end somewhere short of that rope. The cant, the catching-year, is not settled.

**The reach-edge stays GM-side as cause.** Vaethorn's high rates of Kept and Struck, the dense Given of Lestrand and Threnmaieth, and the medicine gap between core and frontier all read, inside the world, as plain distance and biology. That mundane reading is the skeptics' whole case. The real reason, that her reach is bounded and thins where the Trees are thin, stays on this side of the page. No player-facing beat states the cause. Vaethorn is the sharpest test, because a devout population there most wants an explanation for the thin gift, and the true answer would confirm the guilt-theology's worst fear.

**The Long-Lived marriage** is the quietest grief you can bring back more than once. A Long-Lived stops aging, and the spouse does not, so the Long-Lived outlives and buries a spouse, and sometimes a child. Save it for a scene that needs the weight. It also gives you people you can put in a chair: the deathless widower who has stopped, and the one who keeps beginning again.

**The cold embrace** is a diagnostic and a hook. A Returned's family can feel a lapsing Purpose at a touch. A loved one who has run cold is a quiet family crisis, and the question in the room is what unfinished thing is slipping, and whether anyone can meet it before the Returned settles or turns cold to everyone.

Whoever furnishes a provided-for Tithe holds power over its bearer. That is the sharpest economic lever in the setting, coercion with a friendly face.

Player-facing notes keep her unproven. Skeptics have their full dignity. No player-facing beat confirms the Mother. The confirmation is yours to place, and it should land as warmth and relief with an ache under it. There is no harvest waiting under the kindness, and no betrayal waiting to be uncovered. The floor is sound: bounded, costly, and grieving, and still sound. The order in which a table might come to believe that, and what a square does if the belief lands, is the appendix.

### The Pourers, the Unspent, and the Walled Book

Three groups stay apart, because they do not share a job. The Pourers give away their own memory. The Walled Book ranks people. The Protectors catch Phoenix fire and hide it behind worship. No page in the library connects them, and you do not merge any two into one description.

Inside the Pourers there is an inner circle. Most members do not know it exists, and its work does not go through Molevad's minute-book. It chooses a Long-Lived person for the knowledge that defines that person's life, a judge with three hundred years of rulings, or the last person who can read a dead hand. The person does not have to be a member. Someone who refused the invitation can still be chosen.

The inner circle takes the person and holds them until they give that knowledge into a cup. The means are confinement, isolation, and threats. Shutting a Long-Lived away from anything new is already a known cruelty, and the inner circle uses it on purpose. Some victims are deceived instead. A member is told that this is an ordinary one-year gift, and the inner circle chooses what goes in. No new power is involved. The gift works the way every gift works. The difference is that the person did not choose it.

What is taken is personal memory and the understanding the person spent years earning: the cases a judge learned from, the reasons a scholar trusts one source over another, the people they loved and why. The person keeps their speech and their ordinary skills. They can still recognize the world around them. They know what is gone, and they can say so. They are not starving under the Long-Lived Tithe, and the Long-Lived card applies to them as written. Each victim loses something different, so no two are the same afterward.

The taken memory does not go into the shared store. It goes into a second cup, the one on the shelf nobody will talk about. The founder reads that cup. The founder is the Long-Lived person who started the Pourers, and is still alive. This book does not name the founder. Because a Long-Lived forgets nothing they take in, the founder keeps all of it. The founder believes that enough of this knowledge will make them a god. The founder also believes the attempt might ruin what they hope to save, and has accepted that risk. That is the founder's belief, and nothing more. This book does not say the founder can become a god, and it does not say the world could end. Player text never calls the founder a god.

Ordinary members still give one year at a time of their own accord, and that is most of what the Pourers do. "Every gift is freely given" is the circle's claim, and it is true of them. The circle meets on a shelf in a long-house in Lestrand, and the other circle meets in an upper room in Orenbren, which is not the upper room at Harrow's Green. Do not give a date or a month for the nights of giving. Do not add a library, a city, or a medical house. Medicine stays with the Stilled.

The Unspent is the presence in the cup. It stores what is put in, and it does not know or care whether the person chose to give it. It is older than the name the circle gave it. It has always taken in the memories that deathless people could not keep carrying, and it had no public face. The Long-Lived named it, gave memory to the name, and decided that naming it had made it. It lets them say so, and it does not correct them. It does not make plans, does not feed on people, and does not need worship. It cannot Give a Condition, Strike someone into one, make anyone Long-Lived, or save anyone at a threshold. If a circle asks the cup for more years, a second Condition, or a rescue from death, the cup only keeps what it already holds. The founder is a person, not this presence. The Unspent will not take over a will by itself, sort the Kept, confirm the Leaf-Mother, name the Other Hands, take Phoenix fire, or give a memory back in the form of a Condition.

Each year a member gives has three parts. The memory goes into the cup, that year's papers go onto the long-house shelf, and the long-houses that keep the circle count the member one year younger. The papers are ordinary paper on an ordinary shelf, and they are not part of the Unspent. They are how a party learns about a gift without reading the cup. A member's letters, account books, and court notes from the years they gave are filed under their name, and Molevad can let a visitor read them. A person named in one of those letters may want it back. The circle answers that the letter was the member's to give, and a party can be hired into that argument. The papers of a person the inner circle took from do not go onto the open shelf. Where they go is not written. The count is a long-house custom. It changes where a member sits and who speaks first. It changes nothing on the Long-Lived card.

The harm a party meets is one person, a Long-Lived who has lost the knowledge that defined their life and can tell you so. They may not know who took it. Daily life with the Pourers is courtesy and real scholarship. The inner circle is the one hidden thing underneath, at about five percent frightening. Members who want to stop giving want it for different reasons. Some found out about the taking. Some helped with a taking without knowing what the inner circle was doing. Some believed in the practice until they saw what it did to someone. Player text gives them no single shared reason. A member who stands in a passage in the minutes is one of those withdrawers. The page does not say why they stand there, and you do not explain it.

The Walled Book ranks people by Condition. Its injustice is the Book, not a hymn. It ranks the tithed and disinherits a Given heir, and its heir-roll lists only its own heirs. Threnmaieth's census writes everyone down and calls it care. A party that steals both lists finds they do not say the same thing about the same child. The Book teaches that a hug is rare. The Close knows the real count, which lives with the population shares and is not repeated here, and teaches the lesson anyway. Volnai is the one teacher who says that lesson aloud. Volnai is a given name only, not a plot. The named people of the Book stay Delamem, Talnin of the Inner Close, and Hithaen.

The list of turns is a duty of living inside the walls, not ownership. A person with a Condition can own a stall, keeps their wages, and can leave by the gate. The Book does not sell, lend, or hand over a person. Outside the walls, the Book's manners reach three places only: Orenbren's inns near the gate, Vaethorn's gift-hall at Eolvaeth, and the supper benches at Brenthael. Brenthael keeps no copy of the Book, and nobody says who started its seating. Do not explain that seating. Hithaen, a disinherited Given heir of the Closed houses, eats in Eolvaeth's gift-hall.

The Closed Seat once sat the grove and decided who could walk. The First Cut broke that hold. The Closing was the war that followed, and Hildal's Retreat is what closed the walls a day's walk from the Motherwood. Do not name the cutter in order to explain the war, and do not make Hildal a villain-king. Rothallo, still called the Inner Close, is Orenbren's capital and stays inside Orenbren. It is not a sixteenth power, not Threnmaieth's inner court, and not the college in the wood. The Book keeps no Phoenix fire. Do not move the walls to the Hinge Shore, and do not let them capture the First Seat.

## The Leaf-Mother

She is real, and she is benevolent, and her reach is not infinite, and giving costs her.

She exists as a genuine, present power, not a metaphor and not a dead god whose name survived her. The Turning Trees are genuinely her hands. The town scions are not a natural phenomenon that people only read as divine. They are how she reaches into the world, and when a Tree chooses, she is choosing. The Conditions are real gifts. At the Leaf-Fall she looks at a child and gives a becoming the world will need and that the child can carry. The Tithe is the honest weight of that gift, not a hidden price and not a trap.

She is kind and finite. Her reach has an edge, and every gift costs her something to give. She cannot lay a hand on every child, cannot reach every threshold in time, and cannot give without spending. That limit is not a flaw hidden under the warmth. It is why the warmth aches. The limit is real. She is doing her best against it.

A hug and no colour is, for most Kept, a deliberate and kind choice, the gift of being left whole, wanted in its own right. The constraint means some Kept were chosen for that wholeness, and some are children she could not reach far enough to give to this turning, held safe rather than gifted. She does not tell them apart for us, and neither can they. Both are held. Not both are chosen. A later week is not offered to sort them. The Given-door is one week, a completed standing is once, and a miss is not a retry.

A becoming that arrives through a later threshold, a drowning, an oath, a death survived, is not a Condition that escaped her. She meets people at those doors too, and the threshold is the edge of her reach, not her ordinary hand. There she catches rather than gives. It is chancier, costlier, and not always in time. That is why the Struck feel received and still arrive without ceremony, and why they can feel a half-step outside the fold. They were reached for at the limit, not gifted at the centre. She Gives at the Tree. She does not Strike. The catch is enacted by lesser gods of her household, each restricted to one door, under her allowance. The Struck are still hers because those hands are hers to send. Narrow-Hand and Wide-Hand are both half-right.

The believers are right about the big things. She is real, she is kind, and the gifts are gifts. The skeptics are wrong that no one is there, and they are not entirely wrong, because they have correctly felt the edge: the Kept who seem passed over, the Struck who arrive unmet by ceremony, the sense that the giving is not seamless. There is a seam. They have mislabelled a limit as an absence. No one in the world holds the whole shape. Believers see the hand and miss the edge. Skeptics see the edge and miss the hand. The faith is true, and incomplete in exactly the way its own doubters sense.

The world keeps her as belief, not as confirmed cosmology. No one can prove her. Revelation is quiet. Honest skeptics remain honest.

What she is stays open. A goddess, the Trees' shared soul, or something older wearing a mother's face are all still possible, and this book does not pick among them. The maternal, benevolent intent is settled. Her metaphysical species is not. What she wants, past the giving of good gifts, is unwritten. Where she is, and whether anyone can reach her, is unwritten. There is no throne to visit. That her reach has an edge, and that giving costs her, is settled. Why she is finite, whether by nature, from some old wound, by rationing a power that could run out, or by holding something back in order to hold something else up, is deliberately unwritten. A wound in the deep past is one available cause among several. Before the Walk does not pick it. The history shows the edge moving when wood was carried. It does not explain the wound, the finitude, or the rationing.

### How it surfaces

Because she is quiet and genuinely kind, revelation gathers as things fitting together, and then as one object that can close the question. The first five steps stay deniable on purpose, so a skeptic can still walk away. The sixth is a pin, not a speech you are told to reserve forever. The setting still works if a table never uses it, and it must remain possible to use.

1. **The gifts fit too well.** Over a lifetime, a town notices that the Conditions Given tend to be the ones that town will need, as if something is looking ahead. The pattern is easy to dismiss as hindsight, counting only the gifts that worked, which is what the skeptics do.
2. **The Kept are cared for, not merely skipped.** Many Kept report that the ceremony felt like a choice made for them, not like an absence, and not all of them report that, so the difference nags. The testimony is hard to falsify and easy to call sentiment. Both readings are true of different Kept, some chosen and some past her edge, which is why the testimony never resolves cleanly.
3. **The reach has an edge.** Remote hamlets far from any mature Tree run higher on the Kept and higher on the Struck. A Tree strains, and a child goes ungifted where a central town's Tree would not have missed. The giving thins where the Trees are thin. It reads as distance and biology, which is the skeptics' whole case, and it is in fact the shape of a bounded hand. Before the grafts spread, only those who could make the pilgrimage were reached, and the far-flung stayed unTurned for want of the trip. That the reach has grown with the grafts is itself the quiet argument that a hand, and not a climate, is behind it.
4. **A Tree does something a phenomenon should not.** Now and then, rarely and quietly, a scion acts with apparent intent. It withholds a colour that would have doomed a child, or it reaches, and sometimes visibly strains, for a specific person. The moment is never flashy, and it is always deniable.
5. **The Struck are met, barely.** Testimony from the Struck says the threshold felt received, as if someone reached from the far side of the door, late, or thin, or at the very limit of arriving at all. It is the strongest experiential evidence, and the clearest felt sense of the edge, and it is still only testimony. This step does not name the Other Hands.
6. **The confirming beat is the Spent Leaf.** The Spent Leaf is a colourless remainder of a Giving, kept by the Remainder in the First Seat's side-yard. Early checks stay as deniable as the five steps above. It answers motherwood, and the yard-log says it is lighter after a Giving week. The confirming act is to hold it under a healthy Tree during a real colour-fall. The holder feels warmth, and a grief that is not theirs. The canopy thins for a breath and then recovers. The next child is not shorted. That gesture is a mind, kindness, and cost together. Land it as warmth and relief with an ache under it. The answer to whether anyone is there is that she meant it kindly, and she is doing what she can against a limit.

The leaf will not sort the Kept into chosen and past-her-edge. It will not name a Hand or a door. It will not explain the nature of her limit. It will not rerun a week or change a fall.

### If confirmation lands

Use this when the square, or the people who matter to the party, believe the leaf. One clerk's private feeling is not that moment.

Right practice, the habit of doing the rite correctly, was what kept the peace. A proved mind makes the skeptics choose whether to convert, leave the festival, or become a named dissent. Threnmaieth already files the Watchers as the Grown-Over, a counted column on the roll, and it will want a Denied column beside that one.

If a bounded hand is real, then waiting under a Tree is the rationing of a person, not a run of weather. The scion-queue becomes a moral sentence. Road-hands get more leave and more blame. The hamlets past the ford do not get their twenty years back.

In Vaethorn the guilt-theology's worst fear is confirmed, because a thin reach is real. Some hearths stop bringing children rather than risk a hug they now read as a miss, and more children grow up unTurned. That spike is not her will.

Lestrand sells viewings, copies, and what it will call cost-insurance. If the Seat keeps the box, the leaf is not a product. The story of the leaf is.

Threnmaieth tries to write her into the roll as a counted fact. Speakers who refuse become a problem for the crown. The college can still refuse the roll. Keep that refusal available.

The Watching is not promoted. They will say they told you there was a cost, and they still will not say Mother. The warmth lands through them, and not as them.

Some of the Kept will want the leaf to tell them which hug they were. It will not. She still does not tell them apart.

Confirmation changes what people believe. It does not change the justice of the queue, the Grown-Over column, the rope, or the week that will not end. The rope here is the sentence already named, the hanging or the wall, and the week that will not end is the Slide's book, which keeps a person by extending the same week.

Confirming her does not reveal the Other Hands. That comes later, or never, and never first. They know that she spends. They do not know why.

If the Seat buries the leaf instead, the square goes on as before. The five earlier steps still work. A clerk from the Remainder walks off with what they know, and the yard-log still exists. That clerk is how a party gets involved without a prophecy.

### What players will think first

"It's just what the Trees do" is the skeptic's reading, biology with no mind behind it. It is sincere, it is common, and it is wrong about the mind. The people who hold it have genuinely seen the edge, the thinning at the margins and the unmet Struck, and mislabelled a limit as an absence. It is safe to let them hold it. Being half-right is what makes it durable.

"If she's real and kind, why the Tithes, why the Struck, why anyone Kept?" stays live. The answers ache, and they are true: a gift has honest weight, she reaches the thresholds only at the edge of her arm, and some Kept are chosen for wholeness while some are simply past her reach. The answers are not provable, so the questions stay in the room.

"A kind god would save everyone" is the misreading the constraint invites, the claim that a bounded mother is not really benevolent. In the world it is a real theological wound. She is kind and finite, and the gap between those two facts is where the grief lives. Do not let the table solve the gap into omnipotence, and do not let them solve it into indifference.

The dark reading, that a benevolent front hides a harvest or a betrayal, is one this setting rejects. The floor stays sound. Bounded, costly, and grieving, and still sound.

"The Spent Leaf is harvested fire, a second Phoenix, or proof she is dying" is the wrong object. The leaf is a remainder of a Giving. She spends, and nobody is farming her. The leaf has nothing to do with the Protectors.

"The Closed Lamp is a leaf, an uncounted fall, or the same remainder" is the other wrong object. The lamp is metal and a banked coal. It belongs to the fire plot, not to the keystone.

### Her kindness does not launder society

The floor being sound at the divine layer does not make the social layer kind. People build unjust things on top of true gifts she never authored and her kindness does not excuse. Struck stigma and legal suspicion, Tithe-halls that become leverage over the people who need them, guild conscription of the Given, communities that treat their Kept as spare labour, and communities that manage the feared Conditions, the Stilled and the Bound, as problems to be filed: confirmation does not dissolve any of that.

The keystone answers whether anyone is there and whether they mean well. It does not answer whether the society people built around her is just. The moral tension of the setting lives in that gap.

Fifteen injustices, not one injustice wearing different hats. Maiethvael is not the kind foil to Threnmaieth. Piety is a social tax there, and the Kept are pitied in plenty. Trenledd is not Threnmaieth with the hymns taken out and the counting made kinder. It is colder, because there is no duty to keep you hale. Ornled is not freedom. There is no congregation-net, and light government plus thin Trees is exposure. Confirming the Mother is kind launders none of them.

Vaethorn's guilt, Lestrand's market, and Threnmaieth's census are three different injustices, all built by people on top of the same true gifts. They do not share a mechanism. They share only that people built them. The Tithes are honest costs, not divine leverage. A Tithe never seizes the player character. The schism stays a good-faith disagreement, and the constraint makes it two-sided. Believers hold the hand. Skeptics hold the edge. Neither holds the whole.

## The Other Hands

The Leaf-Mother Gives. She does not Strike. The Struck are still hers, because the hands that catch at those doors are of her household, working under her allowance.

She is not exclusive. She is the first and greatest of a household. The Struck are the work of the Other Hands, lesser gods of that household, each with one restricted door and her permission to reach through it. The world still holds her as belief. The other faiths already describe these powers as they themselves understand them. Confirmation is a late beat. When it lands, it should feel as if the household had always been in the next room, not as a list of gods dumped on the table. A campaign never opens with a pantheon.

A Condition is the becoming itself. Given means she gave it at the Leaf-Fall. Struck means an Other Hand caught the person at that Hand's door. She cannot, or will not, Give a death or a contract to a ten-year-old, because those doors do not open at the Leaf-Fall, and that is why Returned and Bound are Struck-only.

### The division of labour

| Path | Who | Where | What |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Given** | the Leaf-Mother alone | a healthy Turning Tree at Leaf-Fall | she looks, she chooses, she Gives. Long-Lived, Two-Bodied, Answered, and Phoenix are hers only. No Other Hand may grant them. |
| **Struck** | one Other Hand, by her allowance | that Hand's own door (death, oath, drowning, stone, wild) | they may grant only their Condition, only at that door, only within her allowance |
| **Both** | Mother or the relevant Hand | Tree or door | Far-Voiced, the Stilled, and the Taken-In can be Given at ten or Struck later. Same Condition. Different hand. Different person at the table. |

### The household

There are five Hands, five doors, and five Conditions that can arrive without a leaf. Each Hand is restricted to one door, one grant, and her allowance. They are not a dark court, and they are not a rival pantheon. They are family labour, the work she cannot do at the Tree without breaking what a gift is. The liturgical names are Maiethren, the Old Tongue. The division of labour is settled. The names may still be polished.

| Door | Condition | Everyday name | Liturgical | What they want | Play them as |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| died with work unfinished | Returned | **the Quiet Tally** | *Threnvaeth* (THREN-vayth), "the counted waiting" | The last task done well, the book closed honestly | Not a reaper. A clerk of unfinished work who will not close the book while work remains. Warm and solemn, the way the Returned themselves are. |
| a contract sworn in extremity | Bound, and the Unbound when the term ends | **the Counterparty** | *Orledd* (or-LED), "the witnessed reckoning" | An extremity that can still be answered, and the answer stay readable. Fewer kept bargains, not a flood of unread ones. | Not a devil. The one who answers a sworn ask and keeps the term. The face the Bound chose is a mask that person gave, and Orledd wears it. |
| survived drowning | Far-Voiced, on the Struck path | **the Second Breath** | *Eloren* (ay-LOR-en), "the deep-witness" | Voices that would have stopped, used: song, warning, comfort. Breath spent, not stored. | Not a sea-god. The deep that returns a voice. The Far-Voiced who was Given is hers. The drowned one is Eloren's. |
| survived being turned to stone | the Stilled, on the Struck path | **the Holding** | *Ornhael* (orn-HAYL), "the whole-ground" | A pause someone walks out of. Stillness that saves, then lets go. | Not a curse-god. The ground that holds a person still and then lets them walk out. |
| lost and dying in the deep wild | the Taken-In, on the Struck path | **the Taking** | *Vaelorn* (VAYL-orn), "the yield-land" | That no one die unmade in the wild. The lost taken in. | Not a hungry forest. The land that will not let you die unmade. *Vael* in the Old Ways' older sense, what the land yields. |

The collective everyday name is the Other Hands. She is the First Hand. They are the rest. They do not have temples of their own on the mainland as a set, because the Motherfaith grew over that memory, and the rival faiths kept pieces of it.

*Eloren* (ay-LOR-en) sits one letter from *Eoloren*, the liturgical name of the First Cut. *Ornhael* (orn-HAYL) sits close to *Ornthael*, the liturgical name of the Years of Hands. Both stay as they are. Say them with the stress given here, so a table does not hear one name and answer with the other.

The Unspent, whose everyday name is the Poured God, is in the household, and it is not a sixth Hand and not a door. It existed before the Pourers named it. It cannot Give, Strike, or catch a person at a threshold. No circle can pour anyone into being Long-Lived.

### Allowance and purchase

Allowance is her permission for a Hand to Strike. An Other Hand cannot Strike a Condition outside their door, cannot Give at a Tree, and cannot exceed what she has permitted. That is also why the Struck feel received and still unmet by ceremony. A lesser hand caught them, at the edge, by permission.

Purchase is not clerical magic, and it is not a matter of praying for a domain spell. It is how easily a Hand reaches where its door is kept: funerals and unfinished work for Threnvaeth, oaths and books for Orledd, drowning-water for Eloren, stone and stillness for Ornhael, wild and grove for Vaelorn. On the mainland those doors exist even for skeptics, because the world is full of them. Belief helps. Geography and practice matter more. Her Giving does not need worship. Skeptics still Turn. The Trees are her hands, not her collection plate. The Leaf-Fall is not something a person has to believe in before it works.

During a term, Orledd receives two things, and if the table never learns this they will play the Counterparty as a devil. The first is the ask remaining true. The Granted Power works because the want was real and was named, and a vague "save me" that was never turned into a clause is not a bargain they can hold. The second is the Terms being kept in public. Each kept Term is a witnessed reckoning, which is how a Counterparty exists in a world whose ordinary hand Gives. Orledd cannot Give. A gift with no named return would be her work, so the exchange has to stay readable or the labour at this door collapses. At the end of a term-limited contract, Orledd receives the Pledge, which they have been holding through the term. The collection at the end is the release of what they held, not a surprise theft. Orledd does not receive a soul, worship, a second signing, or the Bound's personhood. Play a Counterparty who would rather have one kept Term than ten whispered extras. The mask does not change what is received.

Allowance is not a mood. It is a permission with a failure mode. Use strain often and quietly, and use a break almost never. Strain means the door still works, and the catch feels thin, late, or off, so that Narrow-Hand testimony gets louder.

| Hand | A strain looks like |
|---|---|
| Threnvaeth | A Returned is kept from their Purpose and used as cheap deathless labour, so the book cannot close. |
| Orledd | Terms are written to eat toward the Given-Over, or the ask was never extremity and was only appetite. |
| Eloren | A Struck voice is silenced, or used only as a weapon, or a drowning is arranged so that a voice will come back. |
| Ornhael | Someone is held as a tool and not allowed to walk out, or the Grey is treated as a prison. |
| Vaelorn | The lost are warehoused as urban green-poverty, or land is used as a dump for people a city does not want. |
| Any | Reaching on Kumbaan, where the doors are barely a concept, or reaching in a year she has already spent. |

At the table, strain looks like a threshold that should have caught and did not, so the drowning just drowned, or a Strike that felt like a grab rather than a catch, or a Bound Whisper that lies more often because the Terms are being kept as theatre.

A break is a household rupture. It is rare, it is the size of a campaign, and it is not a war between gods. A Hand breaks allowance by granting a Given-only Condition, which means Long-Lived, Two-Bodied, Answered, or Phoenix, or by Giving at a Tree or during the Leaf-Fall, or by striking where there is no true door, or by taking another Hand's door, or by answering an ask that is a Gift in disguise, such as "make my child Long-Lived," or by seizing a will. The rule that nobody's will is bent is bedrock, and a Hand that tried would be outside the household, not a scarier member of it. Persisting after she has refused a particular catch, or after she has narrowed a door for a year, is a break as well. What the table sees is a door that sticks, so the next drownings stay dead, or a bargain that answered with a Gift-shaped clause and a Leaf-Fall that went argumentative, or Struck people who come through and do not feel received. Keep it near that small frightening share. This is a family argument. She can narrow a door, which means fewer Struck that season, or refuse one catch, and neither of those is a break. That is her allowance still working. The Hands are not owed a quota.

### What this does to the schism

Narrow-Hand is right that she Gives only at the Tree, and that the Struck were received by some other means. Wide-Hand is right that those means are still hers: her household, her allowance, and her catch at the edge. Neither reading holds the whole.

The Watching remembered a household from before it was named Mother, and that memory is half-right. The Fair Hand is right that a Counterparty exists, and wrong that every becoming is a bargain, because the Mother's Gifts are still gifts. The Old Ways are right that the land-doors are real, Vaelorn and Ornhael and Eloren among them, and wrong that she is only a latecomer claiming credit, because she authorized those guests. The Open Table is not of this household. Do not absorb Kumbaan's staying-kin into Threnvaeth or into anyone else. Their table is their own.

A household does not turn the Leaf-Fall into a committee. Giving is still love. The Tithe is still honest weight. The Other Hands are not a dark pantheon. Threnvaeth is why the Returned are respected, not why they are a plague. Narrow-Hand does not win. She authorized the catch. The stigma that follows a Struck person is social. It is not a cosmological abandonment.

### How the household surfaces

Keep this ladder behind the keystone's own clues. Do not lead with a pantheon.

1. Struck testimony splits. Some people felt a mother. Some felt a clerk, a term, a deep, a holding, or a green. The Motherfaith files all of that as "she caught them." The Old Ways and the Fair Hand file it as their own doors. Both filings are half-true. The handout that shows one stamp, and Struck people who will not sit under it, does not complete the roster.
2. A Returned who settles sometimes speaks of a book closing, and not of a lap. If you are listening, that is Threnvaeth.
3. Bound faces disagree, and the terms do not. Demon, vast, or unknown, the clause has the same shape, because Orledd is wearing masks.
4. The Watching's saying, "we kept the watch before the name," is about a household, and not only about a nameless Tree.
5. Kumbaan works as a control. There is almost no Struck life there, and no Given. If the household needed only doors, and not doors that people keep, the isle would be full of Struck people. Purchase matters. The storm-wall is a reach-edge and a name-edge together.
6. The confirming beat is late, and it is yours to place. If the campaign ever names the Other Hands, the fact in the room is that the family was already there. Pair it with the keystone's own confirming beat, or let it land after that beat. Never fire it first.

## Yumboes and Kumbaan

Kumbaan has no Tree because a graft has never crossed the storm-wall, not because the good people of the isle refused one. The isle is almost impossible to reach. The culture there is centred on the Open Table, the moon, the hill, and hospitality, which are different ideals and a different sacred.

There are no Gifts on the isle. Her reach does not get there, there is no Leaf-Fall, and Yumboes do not have Given Conditions as a people. They do not worship her or her household, so the Other Hands have little purchase. The doors are not kept in their names, and the rites are not theirs. This is not a rule that gods starve without prayer. A Hand reaches through a door that is used, and on Kumbaan those doors are barely a concept.

Struck becomings can still happen, at far smaller rates than the mainland's about fifteen percent over a lifetime. A Yumboe who dies with work unfinished, or who drowns, or who is taken by the deep green of the Paps, can be caught, and the catching is rare. It is enough for a story, and it is not enough to make a social class. There are no Returned neighbourhoods on the isle, and no Bound guilds.

A Yumboe abroad, standing under a mainland Tree, is a live campaign beat, and it is not a population fact. The Tree is her hand, so she could Give, and it almost never arises. Do not write a racial immunity to cover that rarity. Yumboes do not mix. They need your leave, and if the table says yes they are the complete Kind, Hollow-Hill and Moon-Waked and the Unseen Hands together. A child of a Yumboe and a mainlander is a full Yumboe or the other parent's Kind. The unseen hands are helpers. They can fetch and fumble, and they cannot deal damage or restrain a determined creature. The tremor-sense under the hill is a feeling for gait and number. It is not an immunity to being caught off guard.

The source myth calls Yumboes ghosts, or ancestral spirits. They are a living people, a playable Kind. The ancestral flavour is the faith's picture of itself, a people who venerate hill, moon, and lineage and speak of themselves in the old ancestral idiom. They are not literally undead. That lane belongs to the Returned.

The isle remains the sharpest experiment on the map: a people outside her Giving, barely inside her household's reach, with a faith that was never hers. Yumboes are unreached, not refused. A graft that cannot travel is not a judgment. Do not find an old scion on the isle. An old cutting that took would break the isolate and the Open Table. Dead wood is not a Tree. A wrecked pot in a sailor's story is allowed. A taking is not. Road-hands do not bring the Mother across the storm-wall.

Whether the storm-wall can be crossed, and how, stays open. Choose for the story in front of you. A wall that can never be crossed removes the expedition. A wall that thins every week removes the isolate. A seasonal thinning, a known passage that kills, or a Kind or a Condition that might survive a night on the reef, a Selkie or a Far-Voiced among the maybes, keeps a crossing available when that story wants one. This book does not record any one choice as the fact. The wall is weather, current, and reef. Do not set a larger power in the water to keep her, or the Hands, from crossing. That choice would pick the nature of her limit, which is still open, and it would turn a crossing into a seal.

Kumbaan is not one of the fifteen powers. A later campaign may reach it. It is not a great power. Play Ndenjoo as a home. The first nights are dishes, wrecks, and guests, and the confirmation that the isle is the control stays late. On one night of the moon sailors call Yoltal, the hall eats the heel of the loaf first. Every other night the heel is last, or it goes to the goats. The valley has a sentence about the crust and the mainland being loud. Njunda will not correct that sentence, and will not say it is the reason. They will not say why, and a guest who asks is given more bread. Do not make that night a ward, a liturgy, or a Leaf-Fall in disguise.

## The map as the edge

The reach-gradient, from the dense core to the thin frontier to the Treeless isle, is the ground-level face of the Mother's bounded reach. In the world it reads as plain distance and history. The ages explain today's thin edges as places that are Tree-poor, young or sick or simply few grafts, and as the front of a wave that is still moving on Heskoren. The skeptics take that account as the whole truth. It is most of the truth. The part that stays on this side is why the grafts thin with distance from Thaeloren: her reach itself has an edge. No player-facing region note states that deeper cause. It reads as geography.

Maiethorn is the control end of the natural experiment. The Trees are healthiest there, and the Given are densest. Keep the cause on this side, that her reach is fullest at the source. In the world it is simply the oldest, best-tended Trees.

Strandoren has Trees as healthy as Maiethorn's and wears the faith lightly. The Given are dense here for the same reason they are dense anywhere healthy grafts stand, and the faith is thin for a social reason, because commerce erodes devotion. Keep those two facts separate. The cool faith is not a sign of thin reach, and the reach is not a sign of the cool faith. The eroded language is the tell: smooth because secular, not because the place is Tree-poor.

Heskoren is the sharpest in-world test of the edge. The Waiting Lands are the place a devout population most wants an explanation for the thin gift. The true answer, that her reach is bounded and thins where the Trees are thin, is exactly the answer that would land hardest and cruellest here, because it would confirm the guilt-theology's worst fear. No player-facing note states it. In the world the thin reach is young, sick, or scarce grafts, which is true as far as it goes, and history, a wave of grafts still moving. The gap between "true as far as it goes" and the whole truth is where the play lives.

The Sundering Isle is the strongest experiment. A land with no Tree, whose people become what they become without the Leaf-Fall, is the cleanest natural test of whether the Mother is behind any of it. There are no Gifts, because no graft has crossed and her reach does not get there. Struck are rare, because the household has little purchase where it is unknown and the doors are not kept. Their Kind and their table are their own, not a subtler Tree. Whether a lone Yumboe is Struck is not settled in session one. It is still a late beat.

East and west are not settled cosmology. Only the relative positions matter. If a later map wants the gradient to run north to south, or along some other axis, that is free. The gradient is the canon. The compass is not.

The Watching sits at the sacred centre so that an old faith can hold old memory, what was believed before the Trees were called hers. It is the natural in-world source that could corroborate the keystone from an unexpected direction: not the skeptics, and not the devout, but the heretics.

The Open Table joins the Watching and the Old Ways as the world's three keystone-adjacent faiths, each grown far from the Motherfaith's account, or before it, each possibly preserving a fragment of what the Trees really are. The Isle's is the purest control, a cosmology with no Motherfaith in it. If a campaign triangulates the keystone, these three are the corners.

The Old Ways may be half-right for the wrong reasons. They revere the Struck and the Taken-In as the land's own, made without the Tree, and on a Tree-poor frontier people genuinely do become things without a healthy Tree nearby. They are the frontier mirror of the Watching, two old faiths at opposite edges, both remembering something the Motherfaith smooths over. Whether they are right about the Mother stays behind the keystone. Host-rights do not dissolve when the keystone lands.

The Fair Hand is a rival reading of the true gifts, not a false religion pasted over nothing. The Conditions are real gifts. The cult calls them bargains, the Motherfaith calls them blessings, and both are interpretations laid over the same real thing. The cult is wrong about who, because no bargain was struck at the Leaf-Fall, and its adherents are as sincere as the Motherfaith's. It is not villain-theology. Leddvael is that reading made civic.

The solstice is a two-reading fact. Believers say the Mother times her choosing to the year's hinge. Skeptics say the Trees colour with the length of the day, and that the choosing is biology. She is real, so the believers are right about who, and the skeptics are right about the mechanism. She works through the Trees' real nature, not against it. Player text does not settle it. The calendar works the same either way.

Kumbaan's lunar calendar is the sharpest small tell in the world. The Yumboes keep the moon, not the solstice, because the Trees never reached them. A whole people reckon time with no Leaf-Fall in it. In the world that reads as cultural difference. It is also the keystone experiment, the one calendar the Mother's Trees demonstrably never shaped. Reserve the significance, and let it look like colour.

Whoever keeps the dates holds power. There is no printing press, so calendars are hand-kept by clergy and by clerks. The tree-wardens set Turning-Week. The Tallied Crown's clerks record it. A campaign can turn on a disputed date, a miscounted cohort, or a child Turned a year late because someone kept the calendar wrong, or made them. Cut-years set against house-years set against "the year the graft took" is the same lever aimed at history.

The sky from the ground is enough to play. One sun. One moon on the mainland. Kumbaan keeps twelve named moons of that same sky and does not owe the mainland round a translation. Two solstices run the rite. This world does not have other planes. The storm-wall is weather, current, and reef, not a door. Whether a ship can cross it stays open. A second mainland moon is not available. Eclipses and comets are not written. A later story that needs one names it in play, and naming one does not add a moon, a plane, or a door. The household is not a place you can walk to, and the nature of her limit is not "she cannot cross planes."

Every scion is continuous tissue of the one Tree, so the First Cut really did move a Hand. Player-facing, that continuity is doctrine. The First Cut is the first living graft that took. Who cut it stays unpicked. The growth of reach with the grafts is the quiet argument for a mind. It is not a licence to narrate her standing over the first knife. Skeptics still win the mundane reading, which is horticulture, ships, and soil. Both readings share the same wave.

## The ages, GM-side

Two things change over time, and they are kept apart. The first is how a person Turned: they walked to a Tree, or they stood under a town graft. The second is where the wood has reached: the wave of living grafts, still moving. Keeping them apart protects the keystone without confirming it. Where the wood has reached is what players can see of "reach grew with the grafts," the quiet argument that a hand, and not a climate, is behind the thinning. Skeptics still win the mundane reading, young Trees, sick Trees, few grafts, distance, and history. Both readings share the same map. No player-facing era note states that a mind is behind it.

The present is this year, especially on Heskoren. Ornthael, the Years of Hands, is not a modern age that began after the Grafting finished. That telling would stack one finished age on another. The wave is live. A sick Tree in the core puts a Hands-town back on the neighbour's week. That changes how that town's children Turn. It is not an age ending. A hamlet sending this year's ten-year-olds to a neighbour's Tree is showing where the wood has reached, today. A hamlet sending them all the way to Thaeloren is still Turning them by walking, today. Neither is a re-enactment.

The Grafting is not a redemption arc for society. Local Trees did not abolish Struck stigma, Tithe-leverage, guild conscription, or the pitied Kept. They changed who gets a local witness and who waits on a queue. The scion-queue is the Hands-era injustice grown from a Walking-era mercy. Confirming she is kind does not make the queue fair. The Seat's phrase, that her will is travelling, is a claim about the wood, not a verdict on the list.

The colonial version is wrong. "We brought Trees to the benighted far" is the Seat's sermon after the fact, and it is also Lestrand's story of a supply line. What happened is shorter and worse. Children died on the road, someone cut a branch, the branch took, people copied it, and the Seat claimed the right to authorize the copies. Heskoren still lives the spread as waiting, not as being given a civilization. The Old Ways live it as guests arriving late. Keep all three of those tellings.

The Walking Years did not make far people spiritually deficient. Distance did. Vaethorn's guilt-theology is a Hands-era reading of a Walking-era fact. An Old Ways refusal is not a missed gift. It is a different door. History does not agree with the guilt. Keep the Given-door to one week. If eleven-year-olds Turn Given because they tried, the split household and the held-over year lose their force. Struck remains the later door. That is enough mercy.

Protect the two-sided memory. Devout storytellers of the Hands years will want Brenvaeth, the Walking Years, as holy traffic, every child a pilgrim and every summer a hymn. Skeptics will want it as biology plus bad roads. Both of them are using the era. The graves make the hymn expensive, and the hymn makes the graves a meaning. Play both. The fables refuse to pick.

The gap from Cut-year 160 to 200 stays. It is not empty time, and it is not a punishment. It is dead wood on the Long Mile, a chain of Trees that had to mature before they could give cuttings, and a queue that preferred a next town that could pay. The Grey Summer, the quay-fever of Cut-year 171, stacked a fourth friction on that wait, and it did not become the reason for the gap.

Who made the First Cut stays unpicked. A named first Road-hand, a named parent, or a named saint is available only if a session needs a tomb. Naming one face makes one person the cause. What carries the age is the wood and the waiting. The five attributions are the play. If you collapse them in your own voice, you have written a gospel. Do not narrate her standing over the first knife, and do not smuggle a cause of her limit into the reason the first cut worked. The riders of the Closing fought a pot, and they never learned a face.

Rithnali's minute says Tree. The later Cutting-leave says Mother. The early paper has not finished laying the later name over the older one. A household that keeps both practices, an old Speaker, or a college still arguing the name, is playable, and none of it is a trap. Do not correct the minute.

Before the Walk stays unfilled. People will not give it a start, and this book does not. The Watching and the Old Ways already hold the two usable memories, seeing without a mother, and doors without a graft. A third account, offered as the true prehistory, would flatten both. The Tree standing before it was called hers is compatible with she is real, and with the Mother-name being later. This era does not decide what she is. Cut-year 0 is a copy of wood, not the day Conditions began. The Giving, and the organized walk, are older than that shelf and have no start year. Becoming at a door is older than the walk, and Kinds are older than both. Long-Lived people are Given only. They can remember a walk they witnessed. They are not a library of centuries from before the Giving. A clean switch at the Cut, she appeared when the chronicle starts and before that there were no Conditions, is the overlay this era refuses. The three stones a party can walk into, the Low Wall, the Seeing-Ring, and the Dry Stair, do not date the Tree.

Long-Lived memory is the era-marker the calendar refused to be. In Cut-year 387, the 387th summer since the First Cut, plenty of deathless people remember the late Walking Years from their own lives. They are walking archives, and they do not agree. Memory is perfect. Meaning is not. A Long-Lived who walked as a child, and a Seat chronicle that starts at the Cut, will fight about the same summer. That fight is play. There is no single Long-Lived account.

Several changes of fortune have already happened, and they are not proposals waiting to be rolled. Road-houses became deathless banks. Unsanctioned cutting became Cutting-leave and the queue. Required pilgrimage became optional pilgrimage. The Closed Seat became the Inner Close. One hymn-country became two papers. Quay-fever became Trenledd's roll and Brenledd's compact. The Hinge War became the Hush-rate. Present-day pressure is visible, and it has not been rolled.

| Who | Where the pressure sits | What you can already see | What you do not do |
|---|---|---|---|
| The First Seat | Peak, and rising | The queue has run 368 years, since Cut-year 19, with Threnmaieth close by, and "thin soil" as a sentence towns can recite. | Do not collapse the college, give it a pope, convert it at the end of a Road-hand story, or let the crown own it. |

The road-house had two fates, and both are true. The White Note collected. The Third Hearth kept making the bed. Both are honest. Lestrand will only count the first as success. Play both. There is no single deathless account of the road. Mataero, junior at the White Note, cannot see that house custom is the credit. Thilim, inland, still makes the bed. The prestige-walk runs from Netstrand's berths, to the White Note's terms, to the berth Orentel holds, and Orenbren houses the year at the origin. ## Secrets and reveals

What the table learns, and in what order. The keystone and the household are already in this chapter. This is not a second cosmology.

The Phoenix rules sit behind the keystone. A campaign does not open with a second Phoenix, and does not confirm her in session one. The fire plot may run without the keystone. The claim that she Gave the leaf stays behind the keystone.

### When the fire is caught

There is never a second Gift. The Tree Gives its white-fire to one living person. While that person can still Rise From Ash, spending a Hope slot to come back from that death, no second leaf falls. What can look like two Phoenixes is the remnant of an ended Gift standing next to a new Gift. A captive who still has a Hope slot they can spend to Rise still holds the Gift, so no leaf falls while they can.

The original self ends at the death they cannot Rise from, the death that would cross out their last Hope slot. That death ends the Gift. A new white-fire leaf may fall at the next High Solstice any living Tree can reach. Two Gifts never overlap.

Each Rise burns a body to nothing and remakes it. If the death was prepared, the fire of that burn can be caught and banked. Banked fire has two jobs, and only two.

While the Gift lasts, someone can spend a measure of the bank to force a Rise the Phoenix did not choose, and take the memory of that death with it. The Hope slot still scars. The named piece of the old life still does not come back. The player of a Phoenix player character is never forced to Rise. Forcing the Rise is what is done to a captive.

After the Gift ends, the bank can be spent to keep the last risen body assembled. That upkeep is not a Rise. No Hope is crossed, because none remain. The body walks, it burns, and it can still take the air. It is the fire's memory of a person, and it is not a Phoenix. If the store runs out, the remnant goes to cold ash and stays there.

The remnant cannot Rise, cannot be Given, and cannot Give. Immunity and wings can linger, because those are properties of the fire, not of the Gift. Kindle and the Dwindling do not linger. A remnant that still thinks it is the Phoenix is a tragedy, not a second Condition.

The slot is empty from the true death, so the leaf may fall. The wider world does not see that fall, because the remnant is still called the Phoenix and is kept out of sight. Doctrine says there cannot be two, so a Spoken white-fire, a colour a warden said aloud, is filed as a mis-Speaking or a hush for as long as a sanctuary still claims to hold the Phoenix. A far graft with no Speaker never enters the record at all. The new child is real. The stories people tell are still about the old name.

The Protectors catch the fire. They use these rules. They do not rewrite them. Worship is what they use to hide the taking. They remove each Phoenix from public life, cause controlled deaths, bank the fire, and take the memory of those deaths with the forced Rise. They turn the bank into institutional power, which shows up as heat, as light, as a remnant that still walks, and as a hush that holds. They think they are caring for a relic. They cannot afford to see that they are harvesting a person. Play them as reverent, and sure they helped. The Care stays warm when the street is not. Do not give them a weapon that ends a city, and do not make the bank a second Gift. Player text knows the Care as a hospice that will not produce the one. The public sentence is "the one is in care." The harvest stays here. Do not store the Spent Leaf in the Care, and do not pour years into it. The Protectors are not the Pourers, and they are not the Walled Book.

Clues, in an order you can use, and the first four may run without confirming her. The opening already puts them on the table, so you do not lead with a second bird.

1. Hope scars without the memories of the deaths that made them. Something that burns the way a Phoenix burns has more crossed Hope slots than lives they can name.
2. A sanctuary that will not produce the Phoenix. The world is told the one is being kept safe, and no town has seen them in a generation.
3. A white-fire fall that was never Spoken. The evidence is a far warden's letter, or a hush, or a runner who did not arrive. A letter about such a leaf does not come back answered.
4. Fire that answers a child and an old name at once. The stored fire knows both, and that double recognition is the tell, not a second Gift. The object is the Closed Lamp, which leans, answers two people, and then there is tea. It is not a leaf, and it is not the Spent Leaf.
5. Late, and yours to place: the remnant goes to ash when the store is spent, or the new Gift stands in a room with the old name and the Tree does not argue. What lands is warmth with a theft under it. It is not a reveal that the god was a harvester. Do not fire this in session one.

Misreadings to refuse, in the voice you use with yourself before you answer a player. There were not always two. Leftover fire is not a Gift, however completely it burns. The Tree did not make a mistake. The slot was already empty when the leaf fell, and the leaf was on time. The useful lie, that the remnant is the real Phoenix and the child is a fraud, is backwards, and it is the lie the harvesters need people to believe. She did not stop Giving white-fire. She Gave, or the Tree Gave if the table is still unconfirmed on her, and the record was kept from seeing the fall. The Closed Lamp is not a Spent Leaf, and it is not harvested fire passed off as a leaf. The lamp is brass and a banked coal. The colourless remainder is a different pin. Do not braid them.

### A hidden Phoenix (player agency)

The open-card Phoenix is never anonymous. This is the other seat. Whole-table consent is the same rule the public card already uses, because a Phoenix campaign keeps arriving at fire. The Transformation card does not change. If anyone at the table does not want that fire, pick another Condition, or run the Phoenix as a non-player character.

Two legal shapes. Pick one. They are not two Phoenixes.

| Shape | What you are | Typical start |
|---|---|---|
| **The walker** | The Gift that left a room where deaths had been prepared. You can still Rise. You are the one. | Hope scars you cannot account for. Missing deaths. A world that says the one is safe indoors. The working band is one to three scars, and you do not start on the last Hope slot. |
| **The unseen leaf** | A new Gift that fell where no Speaker wrote it down. You can still Rise. You are the one. | Few scars, or none. Zero is the right count, or one if a later Rise already happened in a quiet year. The stories are still about an older name. Something in a far house still burns the way you do. |

If the next Rise would cross out the last Hope slot, that person cannot Rise, and they are not a Phoenix player character on this card. Agree a count of scars before play, not a biography. You and the player name that the deaths are missing, not what they were. "Three deaths I cannot see" is a start you can play. A plot in which the murders are already filled in is a story someone else wrote.

When play offers a fragment, a room, a smell, a name, or a scar that suddenly has a face, the player chooses. They may take it and name what came back. They may leave it, and a refused fragment can return later without getting louder because they said no. They may rewrite the meaning, not the fact. A prepared room existed. What it means that they walked out is theirs. You offer the fragments. You do not assign their moral, and you do not write the character's lie for them. One fragment a session is plenty. A session with none is legal.

Stored fire can show facts: a count of how many times a body burned in that room, the layout of the prepared room and the door that was not on the slate, a name the fire still answers, that a death was arranged, and that the same fire knows a child and an old name at once. It cannot assign a self, a forgiveness, a purpose for the Gift, a second Gift, or her. If the fire tries to tell the player who they are, a keeper is the one talking, and whether the player believes them is a player choice. Do not let the bank name the Protectors' harvest in narration the table can hear until the table is ready for that fight. Until then, the public sentence is still that the one is in care.

If this player character can still Rise, they are the one Gift, and a remnant in the same campaign is leftover fire, not a second card. If you want a remnant and a player character together, the remnant is the non-player character and the player character is the new leaf, or the player character is the Gift that walked and the remnant is what the house still shows its guests. Never put that the other way around on a player-character sheet. This opening, specifically, is a remnant beside a new leaf. Furled wings can pass. Flight and Kindle cannot. Do not run the public recognition and the hidden recognition both at full and call that fair. If the table wanted the public legend, where everyone knows the Phoenix and every town already has an opinion, use the card and skip this sheet.

A Brenod child, or the player character in that seat, whose hands hold heat, who asks ordinary questions, and who has been told not to be a story. Haelin will not name the colour from last Eolthael, and Tora needs someone who was there to walk the witnessing home. An upper-room guest who burns the way a story burns and cannot account for their scars, wings furled, passing for an ordinary guest until they do not. Rosire's flinch when a room has been cleared and laid with sand, grey under the nails, water in their hands, and no talk of where they travelled. A closed lamp that leans toward the new Gift and still knows an old name. The public sentence, if anyone is carrying it, that the one is in care. Not the harvest. Not the house named as harvesters.

Five moments, any order the table reaches, and the order below is the one to recommend. Play all five. Do not open on the lamp, and do not open on two burning people in one shot.

1. **Two tenses in the square.** Someone asks, in the ordinary talk, whether the one is coming for Leaf-Fall. A neighbour says the Phoenix is in care. Another says they have never seen one. Haelin's answer is the one they actually have: they have a Tree, and they do not have a legend. Curiosity, not dread. The square is not hiding a secret from the party. It has stopped looking. Then let the same people argue about the stone, so the necessity-day keeps running.
2. **White that nobody Spoke.** Last Eolthael, a Brenod child stood at Harrow's Tree, and something white showed. Haelin did not Speak it, so the colour was never said aloud, and a colour that is not civic cannot be walked home as a witnessing. Heat in the hands they do not show. If a player character occupies this seat, the moment is theirs, and you do not run Taeren beside them. Linger on the canopy, the hamlets in one sightline, and how small the square is against what the stories said a Phoenix-fall would be. Then stop. Do not confirm the Leaf-Mother. If a neighbour says she Gave, that is a neighbour talking.
3. **Scars that do not match the lives.** The walker is in the upper room, with more crossed lives than they can put in a row. They cannot Rise. Do not stage a death this session to prove the rule. Rosire brings water and does not explain the guest. The table is holding two burning facts that will not sit in one story, a child the hymns are not about, and a legend who cannot account for their count.
4. **The lamp that answers two people.** One glimpse of wrongness, and no more. Rosire kept a closed travelling-lamp. The coal should be cold. It is not. When the new Gift is on the stair, the lamp leans. When the walker speaks an old name, out of habit and not as a title, the lamp answers that name too. Narrate the lamp, the lean, and the two names. Say nothing of harvest, bank, remnant, Protectors, or "this is not a real Phoenix." One linger, then let someone pour tea.
5. **The door that was not on the slate.** Rosire on-screen as a person, not as a confession. A cleared room. Sand. They flinch, and they will not sit. What they will say is that someone walked, and that they walked the other way, and that this is all the travel they have. What the party does with the person who opened the door is theirs. Session one can end with the lamp still lit, the stone still unmoved, the witness still unwalked, and nobody named as the one. The remnant does not go to ash, and Vimein does not arrive.

After the lamp, decompress. Water, bread, the stone still there. No Leaf-Mother reveal. No fortress of the Care. No hunt in the first hour. No second Gift. Move people by the stairs, the cistern, and the dust. Do not cut from Tora's water straight across to harvested fire.
