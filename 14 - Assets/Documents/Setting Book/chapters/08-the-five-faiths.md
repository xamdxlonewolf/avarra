# The Five Faiths

The Motherfaith is the dominant faith of the Turning lands, and it is not the only one. Five lived faiths each answer a different experience the Trees, or their absence, actually produce. This is not a list of gods.

| Faith | Home | A Condition is… | Everyday name | Own name |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **the Leaf-Mother** | everywhere the Trees stand; born on Maiethorn | a **gift chosen** | the Motherfaith | *Maieth* |
| **the Watching** | Maiethorn's oldest districts | a **seeing**, later named a gift | the Watching | *Haelthael* |
| **the Fair Hand** | Strandoren's ports and houses | a **bargain struck** | the Fair Hand | *Leddoren* |
| **the Old Ways** | Heskoren's groves and doors | the **land making its own** | the Old Ways | *Vaeloren* |
| **the Open Table** | Kumbaan, the Sundering Isle | a mainland hunger **they never had** | the Open Table | *Ndeyaan* |

The three mainland rivals sit *inside* the settled world. The Open Table is the isolate: grown with no Tree and no Mother-name.

Smaller household rites, quay-charms, and Kind-customs exist and stay local. They are not the public faiths. Clergy of the five houses: the tree-wardens (Motherfaith), the Watchers, the Book-Hands, the Door-Keepers, the Table-Keepers, and the Shore-Sitters of the isle.

### The argument (what a becoming *is*)

Every faith in this world has to answer the same fact: most people *become* something they were not born as. They do not agree on the verb.

- **Given.** The Motherfaith says *chosen*. The Watching says *seen*. The Fair Hand says *signed*. The Old Ways say the Tree is a *late guest* at a door the land already kept.
- **Struck.** The Motherfaith argues whether she reached the threshold. The Watching says the threshold was always a place of seeing. The Fair Hand says a later contract, struck in extremity. The Old Ways say this is the *oldest* becoming: the land's own children.
- **Kept.** Gift of wholeness / passed-over / no bargain taken / the land had no door for you that year. Same hug; four readings.
- **Tithe.** Honest weight of a gift / the cost someone once paid / the term of the contract / the land's due. Same upkeep; four ethics.

None of these is a villain's creed. Each is a good-faith answer to a real seam: the fittingness of gifts, the thinning at the edge, the Bound's written terms, the Struck who change with no Tree nearby, a whole isle that never Turned. Dual practice is common. It is not a scandal.

### How they live together

**Orthopraxy is the peace:** belonging by the rite you keep, not the creed you recite. Because the Motherfaith counts you by what you *do* at the Tree, a Watcher who brings their child, a Settler who stands as witness to the colour, and a Kin-speaker who also walks the old doors can all belong at the same Leaf-Fall. Doctrine fights. The festival usually does not.

**Conflict is local, not crusading.**

- **Maiethorn.** The fight is *at the centre*. The Watching and the Motherfaith share the same Trees and argue over the blessing spoken there. Threnmaieth files Watchers on the Threnhael as a known, counted dissent (the Grown-Over). Pressure, not a hunt of households.
- **Strandoren.** Faith is worn light, so the fight is *for the Bound and the books*. The Fair Hand and the Motherfaith compete for the same oath-keepers in the same port. Lestrand prices both and polices neither.
- **Heskoren.** The fight is *over the grove*. The Old Ways were there before the grafts; the Motherfaith burns hottest where the gift is scarcest. A dying Tree has two claimants. Vaethorn's piety makes this tender and sharp. It is not a loud holy war.
- **The Sundering Isle.** Almost no contact. A Yumboe on the mainland is a guest at everyone else's rite. They keep table and find the noon-day faiths *loud*.

**Syncretism (mixed practice: what actually happens, most years):**

| Pair | The common blend | The hard edge |
|---|---|---|
| Motherfaith + Watching | Same Tree, two blessings; families inherit which words they use | Threnmaieth's roll wants one name for what you are |
| Motherfaith + Fair Hand | Leaf-Fall as festival *and* signing-watch; Tithe paid as both tending and settling | Gift-language vs. debt-language over the same Bound soul |
| Motherfaith + Old Ways | Pilgrimage to a graft *and* threshold-walking the old doors | Who tends a sickening Tree: warden or door-keeper |
| Any mainland + Open Table | A rare shared supper; "no one is a stranger twice" | Almost none. The storm-wall is the boundary |

A shared teaching-story, the Child at Four Doors, is told in all five houses with a different last line. That is how the world argues without a council.

The Leaf-Mother remains a *belief* in public life. The other faiths stay beliefs. The Watching *claims* to remember a cost; that claim is fuel, not an answer.

## The Watching

The heartland heresy of Maiethorn. An older Tree-faith the Motherfaith grew over: a *centre* dissent, not a frontier one. They keep the Tree and refuse the Mother-name. They say the colours fall because the Tree *sees*, not because a she *chooses*.

Everyday: **the Watching.** Their own name: **Haelthael** (*hayl-THALE*), from *hael* + *thael*, "the Tree kept whole." They mean the Tree before anyone split it into a Mother and her hands. The Motherfaith's soft name is **the First Watching**; Threnmaieth's file-name is **the Grown-Over**.

### Shape of the faith

- **A witness-religion, not a gift-religion.** The founding fact is that the Tree *looks*. A colour is a seeing made visible, not a present wrapped in a leaf. To live well is to *name what was seen*, not to thank a giver you cannot prove.
- **Orthopraxy, older than the Motherfaith's.** They stand at the same Leaf-Fall. They will not say the Tender's blessing as the wardens say it. Practice binds them to the town; the withheld word is the whole dissent.
- **Divine character: they will not give it one.** The Tree is the Tree. It is not a mother, a bargain-partner, or a land-spirit in a new dress. Naming it *Maieth* is, to them, the overlay: a comfort the core invented when the seeing needed a face.
- **Identity marker:** they keep **Thaeloren** ("the Tree that witnesses") and refuse **Hand of the Mother**. Same wood; the title is the schism.

### Doctrine (as believed)

**The Before.** In the beginning the world was Kind and Kind alone. This they share with the Motherfaith. Then the Tree was already there, watching. The Giving came *after* the Watching. The Mother-name came after that. Each layer is a story laid over a seeing.

**She chooses well is a later sentence.** Fittingness, they say, is what a witness produces: the Tree sees what you can carry and what the town will need, and the leaf matches the seeing. Intent without a mother. The skeptics of the Motherfaith are, to a Watcher, *almost* right, and still too quick to call the Tree a phenomenon with no mind. There is a mind. It does not owe you a lap.

**The cost is remembered.** Haelthael teaching holds that the Giving was not free. Something was spent, someone was lessened; the Tree (or whatever stands behind it) does not reach twice by chance because *reaching costs*. They will not name a Mother who pretends otherwise. What the cost *was* they will not agree on. They agree it was real.

**The Kept were seen.** A hug and no colour is a seeing of wholeness, or a seeing that did not reach. They argue this as fiercely as the Motherfaith does, without the consolation of a mother's lap. The hug is still a witness. It is not an oversight and not (they insist) a slight dressed as love.

**The Struck were seen at a harder door.** A drowning, an oath, a death: the Tree is the ordinary place of seeing; the threshold is an older one. The Watching does not need the Wide-Hand / Narrow-Hand split. Seeing was never confined to a grafted scion.

### Practice

- **The Night Watch.** The night before Turning-Week, Watchers keep a silent vigil at the town Tree. No blessing. No colour-reading. The point is to *be seen seeing*: to match the Tree's attention with their own.
- **The withheld blessing.** They attend the Leaf-Fall (orthopraxy; their children Turn). When the warden says the Tender's line, a Watcher household speaks their own, quietly:

  > *"What falls, was seen. What is kept, was seen. The Tree does not reach twice by chance."*

  Same cadence as the Motherfaith's blessing; the Mother is not in it. Towns that have lived together a long time barely notice the difference. Threnmaieth notices.
- **Name what you saw.** After a Turning, a Watcher does not say *tend what you're given*. They sit with the child and *describe* the fall: the colour, the way the leaf came, what the year will ask. Devotion is accurate speech.
- **Pre-Mother names.** In Haelthael liturgy the origin Tree is only **Thaeloren**. "First Hand" is tolerated as poetry. "Hand of the Mother" is the overlay they will not say.

Watchers are not priests. They keep the Night Watch, teach the withheld blessing, and (where a town will have it) offer a second reading of a rare or mixed fall: not authoritative over the tree-warden, a *witness alongside*. In Threnmaieth they are a known column on the roll.

The Watching has no seat in the Awakening Tree's shadow. That seat was grown-over by the First Seat. Their oldest rooms are under older chapels, or in houses that never took a warden's lintel (the beam over the door). The rooms are the argument: the Watching was here first. Watchers keep two acts, not four offices.

### The live schism

1. **The Masked.** The Mother is a later face the Witness learned to wear, or a name people needed, and the Tree allowed. Almost Motherfaith; common in mixed families. A Masked Watcher will stand for the Tender's blessing and add the withheld line under it.
2. **The Bare Tree.** There was never a she. The overlay is a comfort the core invented, and comfort is not the same as truth. Harder, smaller, the ones Threnmaieth files most carefully.
3. **What was spent?** Both branches agree the Giving cost. They do not agree *what*: a wound in the Tree, a year the watching failed, a someone who became the wood. This is the question they will not close. It is also the one the Motherfaith most wants left unasked.

### A lintel-inscription

Recut, found when a warden-chapel on Maiethorn was re-floored. The new beam faces the Tree; this face was turned to the stone.

> *We kept the watch before the name. The wood was already looking. If a mother came after, she came to a work begun. Do not thank the latecomer for the seeing.*

On its face: heresy, stubborn, local. Underneath: a claim that the Tree *predates the Mother-name*, which is exactly the memory the sacred centre cannot afford to be curious about.

## The Fair Hand

The fortune-and-contract faith of Strandoren. A mercantile theology that reads Conditions not as gifts given but as **bargains struck**. The world is a ledger of deals; the Tithe is a *term*, not a devotion; when the book is closed you are *even*, and owe no gratitude forever. Direct doctrinal rival to the Motherfaith's gift-language, at home on the trade continent and especially among the Bound.

Everyday: **the Fair Hand.** Their own name: **Leddoren** (*led-OR-en*), from *ledd* + *oren*, "the witnessed account." Lestrand slang: **the Settling.** The Motherfaith's name for them: **the Bargainers.** A Bound who has paid their year's Terms will say they have **a closed book**.

### Shape of the faith

- **A settling-religion, not a gift-religion and not a rescue.** No one is being saved from a fall, and no one is being *given* something they cannot refuse. A gift you cannot put down is an imposition. The founding ethic is **fair terms**: know what you owe, pay it, walk.
- **Orthopraxy of the book.** What makes you of the Fair Hand is that you *keep accounts honestly* (Tithe, contract, witness), not that you deny the Mother in the street. Many Settlers still bring a child to the Tree. They attend as witnesses to a signing.
- **Divine character: a counterparty, or none.** The faith splits on whether anyone sits across the table: a counterparty, the other party to the deal. Both branches agree the *table* is real. The Leaf-Mother, if she exists, is a giver who will not show her terms, which, to a Settler, is the one unforgivable clause.
- **Identity marker:** a small personal **book** (not a census; that is Threnmaieth's sin) in which a Settler writes colour, Tithe, and what they are owed. The Bound did not invent this; they are simply the ones whose book is also a contract.

### Doctrine (as believed)

**Nothing is given.** The Leaf-Fall is a *striking of terms*. The colour is the grant; the Tithe is the price; the town's need is the other side of the deal. "Tend what you're given" is how a creditor teaches you to be grateful for the interest.

**The Bound prove the model.** A Bound *chose*, named a grant, named Terms, named a Pledge. The Fair Hand says this is not a special horror. It is the same grammar as every becoming, written large enough to read. The Motherfaith's fear of the Bound is, to them, embarrassment: the Bound make the ledger visible.

**The Kept declined, or were not offered.** No bargain taken; nothing owed; nothing owed *to* them. This is a cooler dignity than spared-or-slighted. On Lestrand it rhymes with calling the Kept *the free*. The Fair Hand did not invent that slight; they gave it a theology.

**The Struck signed later, in a storm.** Extremity is when people accept bad terms. A drowning, an oath, a death: of course the book opens then. The Struck are not suspects; they are people who bargained without a clerk. The faith's kindness is to *settle the year fairly* after the fact, by a hall or a warrant: vouching that remakes the year as a new agreement (novation, in a clerk's mouth). A Bound's *Terms* are the exception: those belong to the Counterparty, not to a clerk. Its cruelty is the same motion: a Struck who cannot settle fades, and the quay steps around them.

**Grace is a closed book.** Not a pardon, and not a mother's lap. You paid. You are even. Walk.

### Practice

- **The signing-watch.** Settlers attend the Leaf-Fall as *witnesses*, not petitioners. They do not speak the Tender's blessing. They speak, if they speak:

  > *"Grant and term, seen and spoken. The book is open. Keep it fair."*

- **Public settling.** Tithe paid in a way a neighbour can see: not confession, *clearing*. A Bound's oath-day is the high holy day: Terms recited, the year's book closed or marked owing. The Motherfaith calls this showing off. The Fair Hand calls it the only honest worship.
- **Quay-blessings.** Short, worn, eroded-tongue: *fair wind, fair term.* Said over a hull, a contract, a marriage. The sacred and the commercial use the same sentence, and that is the teaching.
- **The personal book.** Colour, Tithe, debts, what the year owes you. Private. Not the Threnhael. A Settler will die to keep a crown-clerk out of it. That fury they share with every other faith on the subject.

Book-hands (also Settlers) are notaries who are also theologians. They witness signings, teach children how to keep a book, and settle a Struck's *civic* year (a hall, a warrant). They recite a Bound's Terms on oath-day; they do not rewrite those Terms (the Counterparty's clause, not a clerk's). On Lestrand they are often the same people as commercial clerks. The faith *is* the profession, worn lightly.

The Fair Hand has guild-rooms and quay-shrines, not a mother-church. A "high house" in a rich port is just the oldest book-room. No membership roll. A roll would be a Threnhael.

### The live schism

1. **The Counterparty.** Something sits across the table: a mind, a power, a hand that is *fair* rather than mothering. The Bound's unknown is this branch's whole theology. They will not call it Mother (her terms are hidden); they will not call it demon (that is the Motherfaith's scare-word). They call it **the other hand**.
2. **The Book Alone.** The world is the ledger. No one is across the table. This is the secular twin of the Motherfaith's skeptics, and it lives easily on Lestrand. Practice is identical; the argument is whether gratitude has an address.

Both branches agree that *hidden terms* are the sin. That is why they cannot join the Motherfaith even when they share a festival: "the Mother does not reach twice by chance" is, to them, a clause with no number on it.

### From a Bound's working contract

The form-words a Lestrand book-hand uses when the grant is ordinary and the Terms are not:

> *I was not given. I asked, and I was answered, and I named what I would pay. If a mother had wanted me she would have sent a leaf, not a clause. I prefer the clause. I can read it.*

On its face: a Bound's pride, a little cold. Underneath: the Fair Hand's whole case against gift-doctrine, and a blindness the document cannot afford: that a clause you can read is not the same as a love you cannot price.

## The Old Ways

The ancestor-and-land faith of Heskoren. It predates the grafts. It says the Mother's reach is new here, and the land was already making children of its own, the Struck, the Taken-In, at rivers, drowning-pools, oath-stones, and groves. The Trees are *guests*, not the source.

Everyday, and their own common name: **the Old Ways.** Liturgical: **Vaeloren** (*vay-LOR-en*), from *vael* + *oren*. They use **vael** in an older sense: not "a Condition Given" but **what the land yields**. The Motherfaith, they say, *narrowed* the word to a leaf-colour. Vaethorn-pious speech calls them **the Before-Faith**; the Taken-In among them say **the Green Kin**.

### Shape of the faith

- **A land-and-kin religion.** The founding fact is that *place* makes people. A river that has drowned and returned, a hill that took someone in, an oath-stone that heard a vow: these are **the old doors**. The grafted Tree is a new door, welcome if it behaves.
- **Practice is walking, not watching a colour.** You keep the Old Ways by going to the doors, feeding the kin who stayed, and standing for the Struck when a town will not. Belief about the Mother is secondary; many Kin-speakers also bring a child to a healthy graft when they can reach one.
- **Divine character: the land is many, and the dead stay.** Not one mother, and not a ledger. Hills, waters, old trees (the wild kin of motherwood, not the grafts), and the people who went into them. Ancestors are not ghosts to be feared (the Returned are a different door); they are *kin who remained in the ground*.
- **Identity marker:** a worn token from your door, a river-stone, grove-twig, or pinch of hill, carried the way a Settler carries a book. No two look alike. The mismatch is the teaching: no two doors are the same.

### Doctrine (as believed)

**The land was first.** Before grafts, before pilgrimage, Heskoren's people already became things. The Struck are not a problem the Motherfaith has failed to place. They are the *oldest* rite, the land doing what the land does. The Taken-In are the grove's own children, not a frontier embarrassment.

**Vael meant yield.** Grain, game, a child returned from water, a person the hill kept. The Motherfaith's *vael* (a Given Condition) is a *special case* they mistook for the whole word. To call a leaf a *vael* is fine. To say only a leaf is a *vael* is a theft of speech.

**Grafts are guests.** A healthy Tree is a new kin-house. You may tend it. You may Turn under it. You may not pretend it invented becoming, or that a town without one is empty of the sacred. A sickening Tree is a guest who is dying in your grove. The fight with the tree-wardens is *who is host*.

**The Kept are a year the land had no door.** Not spared, not slighted, and not free labour. Some years a door does not open for you. You are still the land's. Next year, or the drowning-year, or never. The waiting that Vaethorn sanctifies as pilgrimage is, here, just weather.

**The Mother is a late name.** Either the land wearing a face the newcomers needed (the Guest-reading) or a claiming (the First-reading). Both agree she did not *arrive first*.

### Practice

- **Threshold-walking.** At the year's hinges, and not only High Solstice, people walk the old doors of their district. You greet the water, the hill, the stone. You leave food. You do not ask for a colour. You ask to be *known*.
- **Ancestor-meals.** A place at the board for the ones in the ground. Quiet, domestic, easy to miss as religion if you came from Maiethorn looking for a chapel. The courtesy is the rite.
- **Standing for the Struck.** When a person changes later and alone, the Old Ways do not take them to a Tree to be *acknowledged as Given*. They take them back to the door that made them, if it can be found, and *name them kin*. The Motherfaith's quiet receiving-rite and this one sometimes happen in the same week, for the same person, and the argument is which name goes first.
- **The contested grove.** An ailing graft in an old Taken-In green is the fight everyone knows: warden against door-keeper, guest against host, pilgrimage-scarcity against "we had ways." Neighbours who both love the wood.

Door-keepers (also Kin-speakers) know which doors still open. They speak at ancestor-meals. They stand for the unvouched Struck when they can. Not a hierarchy; a reputation. A famous door-keeper is just the one three valleys will walk to. Two jobs, often one mouth.

The Old Ways cannot have a mother-church. The land is the seat. Vaethorn's tree-wardens find this infuriating and, in a dry year, quietly useful. Door-keepers walk doors; they do not carry scions.

### The live schism

1. **The Guest-reading.** The Mother is the land in a new dress. Grafts are kin. Common in Vaethorn towns that finally got a Tree and did not want to throw their grandparents out of the rite. They will pilgrimage *and* walk the doors.
2. **The First-reading.** The grafts are a claiming. The old doors were enough. Harder, thinner-reach districts, the ones still waiting on a scion that will not take. They will tend a dying graft as a *guest in their house* and still refuse the Tender's blessing.

Both branches revere the Struck and the Taken-In. That courtesy is what the faith stands on. A reading that started pitying them as "unreached" has already become Motherfaith.

### Said at a drowning-stone

On Heskoren's wet margin, the year after a graft sickened and a child came back from the river Changed:

> *The tree is young. The water is not. If a mother sent the water she sent it before she sent the leaf, and she has not told the wardens. We will feed both. We will not let either pretend it was first.*

On its face: frontier courtesy, a little barbed. Underneath: the Old Ways' whole case, and the seam they cannot see. They have correctly felt that becoming happens at doors with no healthy Tree.

## The Open Table

The isolate faith of Kumbaan. Grown with no Tree and no Mother-name. The good people keep the night, set a place for hands that have no bodies, and hold that a stranger at the table is the holiest thing in the world: not a colour, a graft, or a bargain.

Mainland everyday: **the Open Table**, or sailors' **the Moon-Feast.** Their own name: **Ndeyaan** (*ndey-AHN*), the night-keeping. In full courtesy, **Bakhna Ndeya** ("the good night"). The opening of every meal that matters is already their creed: *"No one is a stranger twice."*

### Shape of the faith

- **A hospitality-religion.** The founding fact is the table. To feed and be fed is how the living sit with the staying-kin and with anyone the storm-wall did not keep out. There is no Leaf-Fall because, they say, there was nothing missing to give.
- **Practice is the meal.** Ndeyaan has almost no clergy and no chapel. The rite *is* supper under the moon, a place set for hands you can see and a body you cannot, daylight reserved for sleep. A Yumboe who keeps this on the mainland is still of the Open Table. A mainlander who sits once is a guest; one who sits twice is kin.
- **Divine character: the hill, the moon, the ones who stayed.** Not a mother, and not a ledger. The dead do not leave; they stay as hands. Whether those hands *are* the dead (the Staying) or the hill's own courtesy (the Serving) is the house-argument. Neither reading makes the good people ghosts. They are a living Kind. The idiom is ancestral; the bodies are warm.
- **Identity marker:** an extra place laid, even when the household is poor. The unseen hands make this possible. They do not mean it as a metaphor. It is the furniture.

### Doctrine (as believed)

**Wholeness is the default.** The mainland invented a hunger and called it a gift. On Kumbaan you are born Yumboe and that is enough. If the good people become anything else, they do not become it at a Tree (there is none). The how is not a doctrine they owe strangers; it is a family matter, and they are not in a hurry.

**The dead stay to serve.** Folklore on the mainland calls Yumboes spirits of the dead. They find this rude and, in a sideways way, nearly true: the *hands* are the staying-kin. The living host. The staying pour. A feast that outshines its means is a theological statement. The household is larger than the bodies you can count.

**The moon wakes both.** Night is the shared day of the living and the staying. Daylight is a Sabbath of sleep, a rest-day, not a fallen hour. Mainland noon-faiths are *loud* the way a guest who will not sit is loud.

**A stranger is the sacrament.** The storm-wall keeps the world out. Anyone who crosses it has already been through drowning-water. You do not test them with a colour. You feed them. The second supper is the rite of belonging. This is why a lone Yumboe on Strandoren becomes a host wherever they land: they are still keeping Ndeyaan, with whatever table they can make.

**The Mother is a story about a quiet table, told too loud.** They do not hate her. They have never met her. They do not understand why a people would need to be *chosen* in order to be wanted.

### Practice

- **The moon-feast.** The ordinary high rite: night, hill-hall or open turf, the extra place, the unseen hands fetching and pouring. Songs. No colour-reading. No terms recited.
- **The place set.** Even a poor house lays it. To omit it is the one real impiety: not because the staying starve (they do not), but because you have counted the household too small.
- **No one is a stranger twice.** Spoken at the second sitting. The first night you are a guest (and a guest is already sacred). The second, you are of the table. Mainlanders who hear this as a proverb have missed that it is a *legal* sentence in all but parchment.
- **Daylight sleep.** The glare is neither holy nor cursed. It is when the good people and the staying-kin rest. A Yumboe abroad will still take their long rest when the moon is down if they can; they will not make a scene about it.

Table-keepers (also hill-hosts) are barely clergy. The person whose hall the valley comes to on the bright nights. Reputation, not office. One job: keep the table.

Shore-sitters (also wall-waiters) are the other isle job. They sit the storm-beach and wait. They do not swim out. The living go uphill to a table.

A Yumboe abroad is a table-keeper by necessity. One person can keep Ndeyaan in a Lestrand attic. That is how the faith travels, when it travels at all. Recruits by faith (who will lay the extra place), not by Kind. No Kumbaan mission.

### The live schism

1. **The Staying.** The unseen hands *are* the dead: grandparents, the drowned, the ones the hill kept. Ancestral-literal. Older tellings; still common in deep-hill halls. Not a ghost-cult: the dead are *family who took the serving-work*.
2. **The Serving.** The hands are the hill's own courtesy, the good people's oldest wonder, not a roster of names. Gentler, majority in the surface-facing halls, easier to explain to a mainland guest who has gone pale.

Both branches set the extra place. A Serving host who will not say the dead's names and a Staying host who will not stop saying them will still pass the same cup. The argument is for after the dishes.

### The sentence at the mainland lintel

On Kumbaan, *no one is a stranger twice* is how you belong. On the mainland, you belong because a town watched you Turn.

This is the faith's edge on the mainland. It does not need a mission and it does not need a chapel on Strandoren. It needs a room, a tenth year, and a clerk who has a different grammar.

**The everyday collision (citizenship).** A guest sits a second night in a Lestrand attic. The host says the sentence and means it. A quay clerk, a Book-hand, a warden passing through, wants a Turning-witness before they will treat that person as *of a place*. Inside the room: kin. Outside the door: guest. Neither office is cruel. The sentence does not cross the lintel intact.

**The week that can close (the Given-door).** A child of the table — isle-raised, convert-raised, a sailor's tenth summer in a port — reaches Turning-Week. The host will not walk them to a Turning Tree. They were never lacking. A neighbour or a town-warden will. The Given-door is **one week**. If the argument outlasts Eolthael, the child is unTurned. Struck doors still open later. That is not a comfort the host asked for, and not a victory the warden can file as kindness.

This is not a Kumbaan crusade. The child is already on the mainland. The storm-wall stayed where it was.

**Mbela** *(MBEH-lah)* keeps an attic off a Lestrand salt-stair and will not spend a child's week on a colour. **Sorel** of the quay will not file a second supper as belonging, and will walk a tenth-year neighbour to the Tree if the host will not. They are not a party. They are two grammars that share a landing.

Romanticizing the Moon-Feast is itself a slight. Printing the sentence on a luck-card is how Lestrand files the whole faith. The woman in the attic still sets the place.

### A place-setting blessing

Said to the empty plate before the hands arrive. Taught to children. Heard once, badly copied, by a Strandor sailor who did come back.

> *We have counted you. Sit. If you have a name we have not learned it yet; if you have a colour we have no tree to read it. The moon is enough light. Eat, and be the household.*

On its face: hospitality, almost too sweet. Underneath: a whole cosmology that has never needed the Leaf-Fall. The sailor who copied it thought it was a grace. It is a claim that *wanted* was never a gift.

Kumbaan keeps the moon, not the High Solstice. A mainland priest may notice that their holiest week is, here, just weather.

## The Child at Four Doors

A teaching-story told in every house. The bones stay the same; the last line does not. Children hear their own ending first. Travellers hear the others and do not sleep well.

### The story (as told)

A child came to the year of their Turning and found **four doors** where the town said there was one.

The first door was a Tree, and a leaf hung over it, coloured, waiting.
The second door was a book, open, a line already drawn, a space for a name.
The third door was a river-stone, wet, older than the road.
The fourth door was a table with a place set, and no one sitting in it.

The child asked which door was theirs.

No one would say. Each keeper only said: *this one opens.*

The child sat down in the middle and waited to be chosen, or seen, or signed, or fed.

They waited a long time.

When they looked up, all four doors were open, and the child was still in the middle, and the year had gone on without a colour, or with one (the tellings do not agree), and the child had to stand, and pick, and did, and the story ends before you see which.

### The last lines (by house)

The bones above are common. What a house adds last is the faith:

- **Motherfaith (Tender):** *The Mother had opened the Tree before the child sat down. The waiting was the hug. They were wanted either way.*
- **Motherfaith (Wanting):** *The Mother opened one door and the child was looking at the other three. That is how a person is passed over: by having too many doors.*
- **The Watching:** *The Tree had been looking the whole time. The other three doors were people talking. The child was seen. That was the Turning. The leaf was optional.*
- **The Fair Hand:** *The child signed by sitting. Waiting is a term. Someone should have told them the price of a year in the middle.*
- **The Old Ways:** *The stone was wet before the Tree was planted. The child was already the land's. The waiting was weather.*
- **The Open Table:** *The place was set. The child was the guest, then the household. There was never a test. The other doors are how hungry people decorate a supper.*

Skeptics of every faith tell a seventh ending, which is not kind and is very short: *There were not four doors. There was a child, and a year, and a town that needed a story.*

### Variants

- On Maiethorn the four doors are carved on the underside of old lintels; wardens call it decoration.
- On Strandoren it is a quay-song with a chorus the Bound like: *name the term or get up.*
- On Heskoren the river-stone door is told first, and the Tree is the third, and some tellers omit the book entirely.
- On Kumbaan there is only the table. The other doors are "mainland verses," told as a curiosity after the dishes, if a guest insists.
- A Threnmaieth school-version adds a clerk who writes all four doors on a roll. Children are not meant to laugh. They do.
