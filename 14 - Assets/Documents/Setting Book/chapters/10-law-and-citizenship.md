# Law and Citizenship

You belong to a place because someone who already knew you watched you become who you are. In your tenth year, during Turning-Week, the midsummer week a town keeps for the rite, you stand under that town's Turning Tree. Kin come, neighbours come, and the tree-wardens come. It is still the most-attended week of the community's year. Your **Kind** is what you already are by birth, the body and the bloodline you arrived with, and the week does not decide that. What the week decides is a **Condition**, the change three of every four people come to carry across a life and do not put down. A leaf falls, and the warden Speaks the colour, which is to say aloud which colour came down and so which Condition you were **Given**. Or the Tree hugs you and there is no colour, and you are one of the **Kept**, witnessed in the same hour as everyone else and carrying no Condition at all. Citizenship is settled then. You are of that place because a living circle watched, not because a clerk wrote the colour into a book.

In a village the circle is the whole settlement. In a city it is a **hearth**, a street, a quay-gang, a courtyard, or a guild-stair, and not twenty thousand people pretending to remember one child. The legal fact does not change. How a city actually gets through the week is the next part of this chapter.

So citizenship in the Turning is witnessed, not recorded. The law is the memory of the people who stood there, and it does not become a register when the crowd grows. There is no general list of the people. No town and no church keeps everyone and the gift they carry. A warden's week-slate only says which hearth stands at which bell, so it is a festival schedule, the same kind of board that says who has the square for the fair. A guild-roll is only the roster of people who practise a dangerous art. Neither paper is a census. The Walled Book keeps an heir-roll inside its own walls, and that roll lists only its own heirs. Two crowns are the exception and do keep a roll of persons. Threnmaieth, the Tallied Crown, calls its roll holy. Trenledd, the Worn Count, calls it the roll and does not call it holy. The rest of the world finds both unsettling.

That morning is also why a stranger can be known without papers, why a town can licence a dangerous practice without listing every neighbour, and why the person with no one to stand for them is the person the law leaves exposed.

This is how belonging works in the **Years of Hands**, the present age, when a town's own Tree is the ordinary place to Turn. The road under it is older. In the **Walking Years**, everyone who was Given had walked to Thaeloren, the Awakening Tree, or had been carried there. The crowd that saw you was the crowd under that one Tree, or there was no crowd at all if you never arrived. Someone who stood there, or a word from that someone, still has to be able to carry the witness home. The law on this page is the present. The custom of answering for a person who is far away is what that older walk left on the road.

### Turning in a city: the hearth stood

A village of three hundred can remember every child who stands. A salt-city of tens of thousands cannot, so witnessed citizenship does not turn into a register at that scale. The memory nests. It moves down into hearths, and the city does not open a book to replace them.

The Tree is still at the centre, Turning-Week is still the door the city comes in by, and the square is still full. The legal fact is that your hearth stood, not that the whole of Orentel, the salt-city on the Ledger Coast, saw you as if a city could be one witness.

A hearth, in a city, is eighty to two hundred people who already know the child. They bring their ten-year-olds in a named slot of the week, the warden Speaks the colour or names the hug, and the city, standing there as a crowd, sees only a crowd. The hearth is who sees the child.

One **pillar** of that hearth carries the memory when a desk far from the city asks. If a Long-Lived stood, the memory does not corrupt and it runs back centuries. If a Bound stood, the sworn word is literally binding, so a false vouch is not a lie they can cheaply tell. If a Far-Voiced stood, the voice betrays real feeling under strain, so they cannot smoothly lie on your behalf. Kin and neighbours carry the memory first. The pillar is how that stand travels once the child is no longer in front of the people who saw it. An orphan is still brought by kin, by the household that feeds them, or by the warden's own hearth. A child no hearth will bring has missed the Turning, the same as in a village.

To prove you are of a city, you name a hearth and produce someone of it, or a pillar who stood. "I am of the third quay" is a citizenship sentence. "I am of Orentel" only says where that hearth sits on the map.

Threnmaieth and Trenledd write the stand down. That writing is still the aberration. Everyone else keeps the week's slate as hours, which hearth at which bell, and the slate does not record the gift.

### How you prove who you are: by *people*, not papers

If no one is written down, a stranger still has to prove citizenship in a far city. The Turning never had to invent a state of papers and registers, because trust runs through people who cannot be faked. The work those people do, and the witness they can give, exists because they carry a Condition. You are known by witnesses, not by a document a state issued you. Everyday speech calls this **standing-for**.

A Long-Lived remembers you, and that memory cannot be forged the way a clerk's copy can, because it does not corrupt and it runs back centuries. When a Bound vouches, the sworn word is literally binding, so a false vouch is not a cheap lie. When a Far-Voiced speaks for you, the voice betrays real feeling under strain, and they cannot smoothly lie on your behalf.

To establish yourself in a new place is to be vouched into it. You acquire people who will stand for you. That is warmer than an identity check, and it has a sharp edge. The friendless and the rootless are the truly vulnerable, because they have no one to vouch, and the law's exclusions fall on the **unvouched**, a person no one will stand for, rather than on anyone you would call undocumented. A traveler is not, by default, that person. The next section is how they stay vouched on the road.

The same act wears a different name from town to town. In Vaethorn, the Waiting Lands, where the faith runs hot and the Trees run thin, someone vouches for you at worship, and they call that *the speaking*. On Lestrand, the Ledger Coast, where guilds do the governing a crown would do elsewhere, a commercial guarantee is *warranting*. In Threnmaieth you are named to the crown, and they call that *the naming*.

### How a traveler stays vouched

The unvouched remain the vulnerable class. A legitimate traveler moves on someone's word, not on a paper a state handed them. The road still works the way it did when the witness had to come home from Thaeloren. The person who saw you, or their word, still has to be able to answer.

A watch that stops a stranger asks three things: your name, your last hearth, and who stands for you. Answer any two and the road continues. Answer none and you are held until dawn, or until someone will stand, or until you are walked to the next gate. The watch saves its pressure for the person who can give none of the three. A company that can name a last host and show one mark is how this law wants people to move, and an adventuring party is included.

The table is the set of words and marks a watch will actually take. A **Gaze** is the Stilled practice, the one surgeons, riot-lines, and demolition use, and the one a later charge means when someone has held a citizen still. A **Cry** is a Far-Voiced voice let out in earnest, in a hall or over a crowd. An **element** is what one of the Answered works, fire or water or stone or wind, in a yard or on a boat. A mark that names one of those uses is permission to practise it. It is not a list of who carries which Condition.

| Custom | What it is | What it is not |
|---|---|---|
| **Road-word** | A named person at your origin or your last town, the one you mean by "Ask Halim of the third quay." A Far-Voiced can call that person, a Bound can send a word, and a letter is only a token of the person who vouched. | Not a passport. The scrap is worthless if the person who vouched is dead or will not stand. |
| **Company-vouch** | A party stands for each other, so one locally known or ticketed member covers the rest for the passage. | Not citizenship. You may sleep in the town. You are not of the town. |
| **Guild-mark** | A stiller's ticket, a Crae journeyman-mark from the element-guilds, or a Voice-ticket. Craft marks travel with the person who earned them. The Crae are the element-guilds under one umbrella name. | Not a census of Conditions. The mark says you may Gaze, work an element, or Cry in a hall, and nothing further. |
| **House-note** | Paper from a deathless house, a bank a Long-Lived founded and is still personally running. The credit also stands for the bearer, lightly. | Not required. Most travelers carry none. |
| **Guest-right** | A host who takes you in stands for the stay, whether that host is an inn, a table, or a road-house. "I ate at X" holds for about a week. | Not a vouch for sale. The host must have taken you in. |
| **Walk-custom** | Someone on a named walk, the Near Mile, the Salt Walk, the Long Mile, or a First-Hand year, is vouched as a walker by the road itself, through mile-shrines, road-houses, and a berth-slate. | Not permanent belonging. When the walk ends, the word ends. |

People Struck on the road, meaning they took a Condition where no one from home was watching, are vouched anew at the next desk that will take them. If the change is already dangerous, that desk is the Intake, the guild shed that takes a raw Struck person in hand because the hazard is live. Changing without witnesses is still a seam in the law. It is not a crime to have changed.

People still sell a vouch under no name. That sale sits outside the customs in the table, and the economy chapter is where the market has a face. If the bought speaker will not answer when a Far-Voiced calls, the sale fails where everyone can hear it.

Kumbaan, the Sundering Isle, is not a jurisdiction under this law, so you do not arrive there already vouched. A road-word, a company's vouch, or a guild-mark can still help you leave a quay on Strandoren, the continent across the water. None of those file you on a hill of the isle, and none of them make the isle a place this law administers. On the isle, guest-right is a seat at the table, and eating there a second time makes you kin of that table. The meal does not become a last hearth you can offer when a mainland watch asks where you slept. Hand that watch the hall's sentence instead of a person who saw you, and the watch still wants the witness.

### The Struck: those who changed *unwitnessed*

The seam this whole body of law grows around is not that someone fell off a list. A Struck person acquired their Condition later, and alone, by a drowning, a death, or a sworn contract, long after their tenth-year Turning. The town watched everyone else become who they are. No one watched the Struck. They changed with no witnesses.

So a Struck person must be vouched anew. The community has to learn them again as what they have become, on the word of the people who will stand for them. That is the plain root of the old unease that the Struck are "a little suspect," and the unease is not only prejudice, because their becoming happened where no one could see it. A change everyone witnessed is trusted. A change that happened in the dark has to be spoken for. Every polity has people whose transformation went unwitnessed, and every polity feels differently about them.

Two things make that seam sharper. The first is that the dangerous Struck arrive raw. A Struck Stilled or a Struck Far-Voiced manifests untrained, with the hazard already live and no guild that caught it while they were young. For these, being vouched in is bound up with mandatory guild-intake. You are taken in hand and apprenticed at once, for everyone's safety, whether you wanted the vocation or not.

The second is that some doors sit next to crimes. The Bound are Struck by a sworn contract, and the Returned are Struck by dying. To vouch a Bound into a community is to acknowledge a contract no one witnessed being sworn, which is why contract-law and the acceptance of the Bound stay tangled together in the edge cases below.

The second word shows the stance. Vaethorn says *the late-come*, met at a later door; Lestrand says *the turned*, a fact said plainly; Threnmaieth says *the unwitnessed*, suspect until the crown has named them.

### The hour someone is Struck

The first hour is care. To be Struck is to be changed with no leaf in anyone's hand, before a town has had time to decide what sentence fits. The person has changed, and nobody has a colour for them yet. Later the town may acknowledge them at a Tree, settle their civic standing, or walk them back to an Old Ways door, the older faith's own threshold. Those are other days. This hour is the coat, the breaths, and the pen taken out of a hand.

At Orentel, under the pilings, on a tide that ought to have been an ordinary tide, the gang hauls up a person who went into the water as one of them and comes back with a voice that does not fit the chest, or with a calm the morning did not have. They get their shoulders under the person, get the water off, and get a coat around them, and somebody counts the breaths. They do not walk the person to Tallo, which is the name the quay uses for its Tree, and they do not ask which story the new voice belongs to. If a book-hand opens a book over someone who cannot yet refuse a line, whoever is holding the head shuts the book. Tomorrow is soon enough, if the person can refuse by then. A civic settling, if one is going to happen, is an errand for daylight, not for this hour.

Before the coat is even fastened, someone on the gang will say the person was Struck, someone else will say the old rite reached them, and someone else will say a term opened in the storm. The person inside the coat is still coughing, so the argument waits until morning. A hall may later write slips about people who came in with no colour and would not agree on one account. Those slips are called the Uncoloured Intake, and they are not this hour.

On a thaw-street in Maiethlir, the Tallied Crown's seat, the family takes the person into a house and does not take them to the root of the Tree tonight. One sibling is already planning the walk for the morning, so the person can be acknowledged and not Given, because that walk is not a request that the Tree hand over a Condition. The other sibling says the Tree does not Strike, and that a morning walk is a kindness which can wait until the person is able to decline it. Both of them are in the family. What the hour comes down to is the blanket and the shutters. The sibling who needs to say Maieth, the short name devout people use for the Leaf-Mother, may say it over the blanket. The person under the blanket is not required to say it.

In Eolvaeth, the waiting-lands seat, they get the person dry, and if the person can sit they take them to the pot in the gift-hall. If a door-keeper is in the fold, one of the people the older faith trusts with a door, that is who you fetch. If no door-keeper is there, the house does the same things it already does when the weather turns. Nobody names a colour over the steam. The wet leaf on the town spring is not a sign for this hour. A warden who has arrived early, however kind the warden is being, is still early.

It happens rarely on the hill. Once the hall at Ndenjoo has a name for what happened, it treats the news as family news. Njunda feeds the person. Nobody reads a test over the food, and nobody tells the loud story, the mainland telling of the Mother. How a becoming works on Kumbaan is not a lesson the valley owes a guest. The place at the board stays set for whatever it was already set for.

If the change came by an oath said in a back room, the people there take the pen out of the person's hand until that person can hear the clause they are still saying yes to. If it came by a death that did not finish, they keep the person warm, and they do not name the person's Purpose tonight. The Purpose is the unfinished thing a Returned person, someone death did not keep, is still here to tend. A family that knows them can tell later, by a touch, whether that Purpose is being tended, because a Returned who is settling goes cool. Someone who was turned to stone and then came back, or someone who came out of the deep wild changed, is fed, and is not brought under a canopy tonight.

If the person is still inside their tenth year, the problem is a different one, and it is short. While Turning-Week is still open, people bring them to be welcomed. They do not stand the child under the Tree as if the Tree were giving them a second life.

### The Inviolate Will

Every polity holds one principle as the floor of law, and it holds because it is true of the Conditions. No Condition can compel, control, or bend another person's will. The Bound's Whisper cannot, the Far-Voiced's Cry cannot, and the Long-Lived's regard cannot. Every Condition's danger stops at property, at pain, at fear, and at the edge of another person's choosing. That limit is the **Inviolate Will**. Every child is taught the maxim:

> **"The will is one's own."**

The fears a horror story needs are void under that maxim. There is no such crime as being mind-controlled by a vampire, because that cannot happen, and the people a tale would call monsters live as neighbours, because the one thing that would make them impossible to live beside is impossible, and everyone knows it. The law has no charge for one Condition seizing another's mind. The Conditioned, the people who carry a change, are charged as neighbours on that ground.

A **Tithe** is the upkeep a Condition keeps requiring of the person who carries it. If you let it lapse, the dangerous side shows where other people have to live with it. Because a Condition cannot seize a will, harm done with one splits into two charges. **Negligent Tithe-lapse**, letting your element vent, your Cry break, or your green go to seed, is a matter of regulation or of public nuisance. **Willful weaponizing**, deliberately turning a gift on someone, is ordinary violent crime, and it is no gentler because the instrument was a Condition. In practice only the Stilled's gaze supports a charge the hearing can speak as "you held a citizen still." Most of what the law deals with here is negligence. Assault is the smaller share.

The same maxim is the shield the feared hide behind. A Bound can say, truthfully, "my contract cannot make you do anything." Whether a given polity listens is where the polities diverge.

Vaethorn keeps the maxim as *the Mother's own line*, and reads it as scripture. Lestrand cites *the free-will clause*, and treats it as ordinary commercial law. Threnmaieth cites *the crown's assurance*, and uses that citation to justify the roll.

### The safety-licence: how hazard is regulated (and by whom)

The one place the law takes hold of a particular person is public safety, and it takes hold narrowly. A **ticket** attaches to a hazardous use, not to the fact of being Conditioned. Across the world, a licence attaches where a practice is dangerous to other people. It does not attach where a gift is merely powerful, and it is not a way to keep track of people. Everyday speech calls this leave **the ticket**. In Vaethorn a practitioner asks for *the blessing-to-practice*. On Lestrand they show *the guild-ticket*. In Threnmaieth they show *the crown-warrant*.

The three ticket-Conditions are the Stilled, the Far-Voiced, and the Answered. Together that is about three people in ten, and the shares of each Condition are in *The Conditions*. They are a large minority who can be asked for a ticket if they practise the hazardous use. They are not a third of the world written on a crown's list. A ticket names a competence, stiller, hall-voice, or Crae-hand, which is the work you may do. It does not name the Condition as an identity, and it does not function as an identity paper.

| Who | Ticket names | Who is not on it |
|---|---|---|
| **the Stilled — personal Gaze-ticket** | Anyone who Gazes in public: surgeons, riot-lines, demolition. Most stillers hold one, because the vocation is the Gaze. | A Stilled who never Gazes, which is rare. The guild still wants that person in the yard. |
| **Far-Voiced — Voice-ticket / hall-hour** | Those who Cry in a crowd or keep a scheduled hour: criers, performers, sworn witnesses. The hall-keepers stamp that you are competent and sell you the hour at the same desk. | A field-voice who lets the Cry out at the hedge. The hall-slate is a list of hours, not of every blue-leaf in the city. Blue-leaf is the ordinary name for a Far-Voiced. A Far-Voiced who never takes an hour is not on it. |
| **the Answered — shop charter / journeyman-mark** | Those who let an element speak in a crowded yard, where a crowd can be hurt. The Crae charter the shop. Journeymen work under that charter. | A Tide-Answered on their own boat, under a master's eye. They answer to the shop. The shop does not keep a person-file, and they carry no paper of their own. |
| **Bound — a table, not a body-ticket** | No licence for the body. The honest table oversees the Terms, the clauses a Bound is still keeping. The Whisper cannot command. | A ticket. A Bound is not counted among the licensed. |
| **No ticket** | — | Two-Bodied, Long-Lived, Returned, the Taken-In, the Unbound, Phoenix, and all the Kept. Most people hold no ticket. They are simply neighbours. |

Active papers in a working city are fewer than the people who could be asked for one, and fewer again on the frontier, where a feared Condition is as likely to meet a rope as a stool. The stool is the ticket-stool, the master's seat that stamps the paper. The share is who might be asked. It is not a census of people under supervision.

What keeps this from becoming a general watch on neighbours is the way the pens are held. Three guilds keep the papers, and they do not keep a shared roll. A stiller cannot ask the Crae for a list of all the Answered. A hall-keeper cannot ask the Stillers for "all pale-stone," and pale-stone is the ordinary name for the Stilled. There is no union of the lists except in Threnmaieth and Trenledd. The state watches the charter, not the person. It can pull a guild's leave to operate, and it cannot ask for a list of this city's Far-Voiced. The slates name hours, plants, and chairs, not gifts. A bed-slate is rows of beds. A hall-slate is bells. A guild-roll is the practitioners of an art. Only the two crowns that keep a roll of persons write down the gifts those persons carry.

Most people never hold a paper at all. The Kept do not. The Two-Bodied do not, and they are the plurality, the largest share of any Condition. Nor do the Taken-In, the Returned, the deathless, or the Hollow, which is the ordinary name for the Unbound, a Bound released from the contract and still missing what they paid. Among people who do carry a Condition, only that large minority can be asked for a ticket, and only if they practise. This is a world of crafts, not a world that keeps a register of neighbours.

Licences are guild-issued, not state-issued, which is the arrangement a craft town of this age already knows. A guild knows its own members the way any craft guild does. The state charters the guild and lets the guild police its own, under the threat of losing the charter. No single authority thereby holds the Conditioned. The arrangement makes its own friction. Guilds guard their monopolies, tickets become property, and an unlicensed practitioner threatens the guild's charter as much as the public's safety. A mill, a hull, and a road keep fellowships of their own, and those fellowships are not these papers.

### Harm, the watch, and the hearing

The Inviolate Will has already sorted the charge into negligent Tithe-lapse or willful weaponizing. The ladder below is the path that charge walks. There is no crime on it for merely being a Condition.

1. **Neighbour-word.** Most harm dies on the street. Someone pays back what they took, or they apologize, or a Bound gives a sworn word if the street has one to give. The watch is not called.
2. **The watch.** Night-watch and ward-watch are the ordinary civic hold. The people who stand them are often Kept, whose nights are not themselves a Tithe, and Two-Bodied, for whom the ranging is the Tithe, with a Far-Voiced along so the shout will carry. They hold a body until morning. They do not investigate a Condition, which is a different question from whether someone was harmed tonight. When the matter needs someone who can speak for a hearth or a craft, they fetch a pillar or a guild. Where another page says "the watch," it means this hold, not a secret police, except where a Watchful crown, one of the two that keep a roll of persons, has told the watch to hold someone the roll wants.
3. **Guild-hearing.** Tithe-lapse comes here, and so does an unlicensed Gaze, and an element let go in a shop. The guild fines, or it suspends the ticket, or it binds the person to an apprenticeship. The state is not in the room unless the charter is at risk.
4. **Open hearing.** Willful harm is heard in the open, before witnesses, and especially before the three pillars. The charge is the act. "You Gazed a man" is assault. "You are Stilled" is not a charge. A Gaze used to hold a person still and leave them helpless is the rare charge the Stillers' own code already names.
5. **What the hearing can do** without writing you down is a short list of real consequences. It can order restitution in coin or in labor, or order you to furnish a Tithe you broke. It can strip the ticket, so you may not practise the dangerous act. It can require a Bound's word, or a term. It can exile you from the hearth, so that you become unvouched in your own city. That exile is the civic death, and it does not require a file. For murder, and for the rare holding of a citizen still, it can send you to the rope or the wall, which is used more often on the frontier and is rare in the Warm, the devout end of the world's theology.

The two Watchful crowns write you down on top of whatever else the hearing did. That extra record is their extra punishment, and it is why a Watchful town feels like a different country.

### The watch and the cohort

A town keeps a watch for the night. In a bad year it sends a cohort down the road with the children who still have to be Turned. Two old wars are finished. What they left, and what a power still collects, is a wall and a rate. In an ordinary year the work is a bench until morning.

When the watch is called, it is the hold already named: until morning, and not an investigation of anyone's Condition. At a gate the three questions are the same ones a traveler has to answer. Your name, your last hearth, and who stands. Two answers, and the road goes on. No answer, and the person sits a bench until the second bell, until someone comes to stand for them, or until the watch walks them to the next gate. The bench is not a cell. They do not keep the three answers past dawn.

In a village the watch is whoever is awake. In Maiethlir it is the Down Gate and the path along the flood-wall. In Orentel it is the night-gate on the quay. In Eolvaeth the square watches. In a year when a cohort goes out, the camp-streets watch instead, because the people who would have stood have gone toward a luckier Hand. A Hand is that town's own grafted Tree, the local wood that should Turn its children this week. Threnmaieth's roll belongs to the clerks, not to the people on the bench. The watch in a Watchful town can be told to hold someone the roll wants. The holding is still until morning unless a clerk comes, and the roll stays the clerk's.

A **cohort** is the group a town sends when its own Hand cannot Turn its children this week. The group is kin, the children who are ten, and a warden's word. It is not a company kept under arms.

Vaethod, the town-warden at Eolvaeth, has sent one toward Saelvaeth in years when the wood did not do the week's work. The gospel in that town says wait, but the children are ten now, and the Given-door, the one week in which the wood can give them what it gives, is that week, so they go. They come back Given, or not, or unvouched, with no one who watched the standing. One unvouched arrival in the gift-hall is a year the town still argues. Saelid's bread stall feels that winter, because the people who buy bread are the people the cohort takes away. There is no barracks to come back to. When the week is over the cohort breaks up, and people remember it by the summer it went.

The Mill-hold sends its ten-year-olds toward Brenthael when its Hand is unsound. Talen writes them on the neighbour-slate. That walk is the same kind of cohort. A bed-guild in Orenbren may take the slate for a town that failed. Talen writes it down as one more summer's walk. The children are not soldiers, and the slate is not a muster-roll.

Between those sends, the people are at their stalls and their races. A power does not keep a cohort in the field as its ordinary year. A sent cohort from Eolvaeth stops at the thorn outside the vale and eats before they go on, even when the path is clear. They will not say why.

The Closing ran from Cut-year 19 to the Retreat in Cut-year 38, in the year-count the chronicles use. Riders tried to pull up copied wood, the trees towns had grown as copies of a Tree. The towns hid the slips, the cuttings those copies were grown from, and lodging-hearths would not starve their beds. By the end, Hildal closed a wall and saved a book, and the riders went home. The wall is the Inner Close, Rothallo, the walled capital inside Orenbren. The Book still ranks who owes. That ranking is a class rule, not a garrison on the wall. Nobody kept the riders as a host that could be called again.

A house on the Near Mile may still hang a kitchen knife on a ribbon beside a scorch on an old pot-ring. The people in the house know which summer that was. It was the Closing, and they do not turn out for it. The Low Wall is another stone, from another dig. A person who says the two walls are one wall is making a claim about the past. They are not raising a company.

The Hinge Hush is the peace on the Old Crossing. From Cut-year 299 to 304, the Hinge Shore and the Ledger Coast tied up each other's hulls over who set the crossing's price. People died in a fight about a toll. In Cut-year 304 they hushed. The hush is a rate, a west-water clause, and a refusal to name a winner. Taerso spoke for the hinge-houses. Sirtol spoke for a charter-pile on the Coast, one charter-holder among many, not the White Note and not a crown. Both could stop a berth. Neither sat a throne, and neither left an admiral's fleet. When the hush held, the quays went back to cargo.

What the hush still collects is the rate, and the present year is Cut-year 387. Dock-bells on the Hinge Shore pause in Hale-month, the spring month, also called Haelren, because that pause is the fifth summer, and then the bells go back to loading. Netstrand still keeps hulls for hire under the west-water clause, so a charter is one crossing with a number on it, and Orentel holds berths the hush made easier to keep. The White Note did not sign, and it does not keep ships. A necessity-family, people who have to make the crossing, pays the rate and is not told which summer made the number. When a landing argues the clause, the argument is the old peace still doing the job it was written for.

*A door-custom, pinned inside a Lestrand night-gate, with no crest on it. The back of the page is a week-old rain note, and the writing on the front is what a stranger reads before the watch speaks.*

> *Name. Last hearth. Who stands. Two will do. None, and you sit the bench until the second bell, or until someone comes to stand for you, or we walk you to the next gate. We do not ask your colour. We do not keep the three answers past dawn. If you are on a walk, say which. If you ate at the bed-house, say so. If you have a ticket, show the stool-mark, not a story. The bench is not a cell. The bench is until morning.*

The stool-mark is the stamp from the ticket-stool. The watch that pinned this up thinks "we do not ask your colour" makes the gate unlike Threnmaieth. The three questions are the whole of how citizenship works, run at a gate, and the watch does not write the answers down. That is how a city of tens of thousands still runs on hearths.

### The three pillars: why the Conditioned *are* the legal system

Walk into a hearing and three Conditions are already the court. The law is not a set of rules imposed on the Conditioned from outside. These three are the legal apparatus, which is why a register is unnecessary.

A Long-Lived's memory does not corrupt, and it runs back centuries, so the archivists, the notaries, and the judges are often Long-Lived. A matter of record is whatever they say they witnessed, even when the witnessing was a century ago. Where a forged document would be the worry, a Long-Lived simply remembers.

A Bound's sworn oath is literally binding, and they hear a lie as it is told. That is why they keep the oaths, arbitrate the debts, and stand as the witnesses. When a treaty must truly hold, a Bound is the one who swears it.

A Far-Voiced cannot fake the feeling in the voice. Under strain the voice betrays it, so they cannot lie well once they are on the stand, and a court trusts them for that reason.

Evidence and contract in the Turning are therefore made of people. Memory that does not corrupt, an oath that hears a lie, and testimony that cannot fake a feeling are enough. The people who do those jobs were Given their Condition at the Tree or Struck with it later, so the courts do not need a paper bureaucracy to do the work. A world with witnesses like these does not need to write everyone down, which is why the two crowns that insist on writing everyone down look so strange to everyone else.

### Rights of the Kept

The Kept are the quiet centre of citizenship, and their legal position is gloriously unremarkable. They were witnessed at their Turning like everyone else. They carry no Tithe, they need no licence, and they answer to no guild. In most polities the Kept are the default legal person, the frame the rest of the law is drawn around. In one surviving compact they are also a ruling class. The Walled Book ranks the tithed, the people who carry a Condition and so owe a Tithe, and it will strike a Closed Heir for taking a colour.

The second word shows how the quiet position is felt. Vaethorn says *the Waited*, or *Mother-kept*, with pity, as if the Tree passed them over. Lestrand says *the free*, labor with no Tithe attached, and says it with approval. Threnmaieth says *the unmarked*, or *the passed-over*, and the clerks note the gap.

Two facts keep that quiet position from being a footnote. Kept status is not a permanent immunity. About a third of the children the Tree hugged are still Struck later, so a person who is Kept now lives untithed and unlicensed without ever standing wholly outside the safety rules. The day they are Struck, they too must be vouched anew. In law, "Kept" describes your present, and not a promise about your future.

Where reverence for the gift runs hot, the Kept can be quietly pitied or pressured, treated as people who missed out and leaned on to explain why the Tree passed them over. That is a social harm the law rarely names, and it runs hardest in the most devout polities.

The Given keep their plain name everywhere, and the second word is the stance again: *the gifted*, said as a blessing in Vaethorn; *the Given*, said as a plain fact on Lestrand; *the marked* in Threnmaieth, because the name goes on the roll.

### Edge cases the law must hold

A few people do not fit the ordinary hour, and the law has to hold them without pretending the fit is easy.

The Hollow, the Unbound, raise a question of capacity and of protection. They were Bound, and at the signing they named a Pledge, a part of themselves the counterparty collects when a term-limited contract ends. Freed, and missing that piece, an isolated Hollow is pulled to sign again. The law has to decide whether they still have the capacity to be held to a new contract, and whether someone must protect them from one. Kinder polities extend a protective status, which can be a guardian, a claim on connection, or a bar on presenting them with fresh contracts. Harsher polities leave them to the pull.

The Given-Over, also called the Forfeit, is the sharpest wound this law has. It is a Bound who kept re-signing until the counterparty effectively owns the person, a living case of legal personhood erased by accumulated contract. The contract-guilds have a term of art for it precisely because the law has never fully decided at what point a person has bargained away enough of themselves to stop being one. The question keeps returning, and it has not become a settled rule. The pipeline that feeds those signings has a lived face. People call that desk the Holding Desk. Sold vouching, a paid person who will stand rather than a paper, is the Standing Trade.

Phoenix is not a licensing problem. One legendary person is alive at a time and cannot hide, so the question is political, a symbol no ruler controls, and whoever currently wants the one alive tries to own them or to protect them.

A child who missed the week, refused to stand, or never stood is **UnTurned**. They have no hug and no colour. Folk will still call that child Kept. The law files them as the person the town raised, vouched if someone will stand, and unwitnessed at the Tree. A second Leaf-Fall is not a remedy. The doors that Strike a person later still open in their own time.

### Three polity stances

The rules above hold in every polity. The tone does not. Three choices carry most of the difference, and every polity sets each one separately.

Theology is how gift-devout the place is. It runs from an ardent Motherfaith, the faith of the Leaf-Mother, in which a Condition is a blessing and belonging is a birthright, through civic religion worn lightly, to frank secular pragmatism, and the odd local heresy. That choice sets how the Struck, the Kept, and the feared Conditions are read before the law even speaks.

Reach is whether the place is a Tree-rich core or a Tree-poor edge, which follows where the Trees grew out from the first one by graft, a living cutting set in new ground. Where healthy Trees stand, reach is full and the Given are dense. Where Trees are few, young, or sickening, the population runs higher on the Kept and on the Struck.

Governance is how far the state reaches onto the person. It runs from light guild self-rule, the ordinary arrangement, in which the craft polices its own and the crown charters the guild, all the way to a crown that writes its subjects down. That choice sets how far a safety licence becomes a way of watching people.

The three choices combine, and the combinations are places, not a single ladder. A devout frontier can hold ardent faith, thin reach, and almost no bureaucracy. A secular merchant republic can be rich in Trees, lightly governed, and worn thin of faith. A pious core kingdom can also be Watchful, so that the watching of people is dressed as sacred duty. A cold trade-city can have no reverence at all. Each has real supporters and a real cost. Three stances recur. The Waiting Lands, the Ledger Coast, and the Tallied Crown are three whole polities set on those choices at once. A polity can be Warm and a frontier at the same time, and lightly or heavily governed, because theology is only one of the three.

**The Warm** *(high theology).* Law here flows straight from the Motherfaith's gift-theology, so a Condition is spoken of as a blessing, the Struck are vouched in graciously as people the Mother met at a later door, and a licence is framed as help in tending the gift rather than as policing. The stance is warmest near the Awakening Tree. Its injustice is soft and hard to name. Everyone is expected to serve their gift, so a guild's "vocation" shades into pressure, the Kept are pitied for missing out, and declining your Condition's obvious use reads as ingratitude to the Mother herself.

**The Watchful** *(governance at its extreme: a deliberate aberration).* At the far end of governance are crowns that break the world's norm and do write their people down. They do not trust living memory, so they keep a roll of every subject and of that subject's Condition. Written records are not what makes this unusual. Every polity has guild rolls, notarial acts, and tax ledgers. The aberration is a census of persons and their gifts, a record held on people, and two crowns keep one.

Threnmaieth calls its roll holy. The census names itself the **Threnhael** *(THREN-hayl)*: *thren*, sacred counting, set beside *hael*, whole and hale, so the name means "the keeping-whole." It sounds like care, the keeping of every subject hale, and it is surveillance. Clerks say they "keep the realm hale." Subjects are "kept whole." Outsiders hear "the Counting." On that roll the second name is the house, an old given name that stuck to a line, said where other lands would use a surname. Witness-lands, which still name you by who saw you, do not do this. Trenledd calls its list the roll and does not call it holy. At home each crown frames the roll as prudence and order. Elsewhere it is felt as surveillance.

Under either roll the unvouched are hunted, and the Struck are pursued so they can be catalogued. Tithe-needs become points of leverage. The Bound especially are watched far past their real hazard, since the Whisper cannot command and the clerks know it, so the extra watch is stigma carried out as safety. Most of the world finds the two rolls chilling, because nobody else keeps a roll of everyone. The Walled Book's heir-roll lists only its own heirs. When a Watchful kingdom is also devout and Tree-rich, that watching is dressed as sacred duty.

**The Frontier** *(low reach).* Far from any healthy Tree, even the witnessing runs thin, and custom outruns law. These lands run higher on the Kept and higher on the Struck: more hugs, more later doors, fewer coloured falls. In town, people read that as distance and as young wood. They are freer in the good sense, because no one asks for a licence, and the Taken-In, whom cities keep at a social arm's length and still employ on the lots, are simply neighbours here and the backbone of the frontier economy. They are harsher in the bad sense, because an unvouched Struck person may have no one to stand for them and no guild to train them, and a feared Condition may meet a rope where a city would have issued a licence. Reach and governance often move together. A thin-reach edge tends to be thinly governed too. They need not. A devout frontier exists, and so does a lightly governed core.
