# Law and Citizenship


You belong because your town *saw* you Turn.

Every child passes through their town Turning Tree in their tenth year, and the whole town is there to watch. The Leaf-Fall is the most-attended day of a community's year — kin, neighbours, the tree-wardens, all gathered. They *see* the leaf fall on a child, or see the Tree hug them.

So citizenship in the Turning is **witnessed, not recorded.** You are of a place because that place *remembers you* — it watched you become who you are.

There is no register of the people. No town, church, or crown keeps a list of everyone and their gift. That would be to hold a record *on* people, and it is not how this world works. Belonging is living communal memory, the way a real village knows its own — not a ledger. The one kingdom that *does* keep such a list is a notorious exception — Threnmaieth, the Tallied Crown — and it reads as unsettling precisely *because* no one else does it.

This is also a Hands-era default. In the Walking Years the town that saw you Turn was the crowd under Thaeloren — or no one, if you never arrived. Vouching-at-a-distance exists to *carry that origin-witness home.*

### How you prove who you are — by *people*, not papers

The Turning never needed to invent the papers-and-registers state, because trust runs through people who cannot be faked.

You are known by **witnesses**, not documents. Common tongue: **standing-for**. Vaethorn calls it *the speaking* (vouched at worship). Lestrand calls it *warranting* (a commercial guarantee). Threnmaieth calls it *the naming* (you are named to the crown).

- A **Long-Lived** remembers you — and their memory is incorruptible and runs back centuries.
- A **Bound** *vouches* for you — and a Bound's sworn word is literally binding, so a false vouch is not a lie they can cheaply tell.
- A **Far-Voiced** speaks for you — and their voice betrays real feeling under strain, so they cannot smoothly lie on your behalf.

To establish yourself in a new place is to **be vouched into it** — to acquire people who will stand for you. This is warmer and more human than an ID-check, and it has a sharp social consequence: **the friendless and the rootless are the truly vulnerable**, because they have no one to vouch. The law's exclusions fall not on "the undocumented" but on **the unvouched** — a person no one will stand for.

### The Struck — those who changed *unwitnessed*

A Struck person acquired their Condition *later, and alone* — a drowning, a death, a sworn contract, long after their tenth-year Turning. The town watched everyone else become who they are. **No one watched the Struck.** They changed with no witnesses.

So a Struck person must be **vouched anew** — the community has to re-know them as what they've become, on the word of those who'll stand for them. That is the plain root of the old unease that the Struck are "a little suspect": not (only) prejudice, but the simple fact that *their becoming happened where no one could see it.* A change everyone witnessed is trusted; a change that happened in the dark must be spoken for. Every polity has people whose transformation went unwitnessed, and every polity feels differently about that.

Two things make the seam sharper:

- **The dangerous Struck arrive raw.** A Struck Stilled or Far-Voiced manifests *untrained*, with the hazard already live and no guild to have caught it young. For these, being vouched-in is bound up with **mandatory guild-intake** — you are taken in hand *and* apprenticed at once, for everyone's safety, whether you wanted the vocation or not.
- **Some doors are crimes-adjacent.** The Bound are Struck by a sworn contract; the Returned by dying. To vouch a Bound into a community is to acknowledge a contract no one witnessed being sworn — which is why contract-law and the acceptance of the Bound are tangled together.

Vaethorn's second word for the Struck is *the late-come* (met at a later door). Lestrand's is *the turned* (neutral, a fact). Threnmaieth's is *the unwitnessed* (suspect until named).

### The Inviolate Will

Beneath all local variation sits a single principle so firm it functions as constitutional bedrock, and it is bedrock because it is *true*: **no Condition can compel, control, or bend another person's will.** Not the Bound's Whisper, not the Far-Voiced's Cry, not the Long-Lived's regard — every Condition's danger stops at property, at pain, at fear, at the edge of another's choosing. The law mirrors the metaphysics in a maxim taught to every child:

> **"The will is one's own."**

Vaethorn holds it as *the Mother's own line* (scripture). Lestrand cites *the free-will clause* (sound commercial law). Threnmaieth cites *the crown's assurance* (to justify the roll).

Its consequences are large:

- **The horror-story fears are void in law.** There is no such crime as "being mind-controlled by a vampire," because it cannot happen. This is the legal floor under the world's neighbours-not-monsters tone — the one thing that would make them un-neighbourly is *impossible*, and everyone knows it.
- **It sorts the criminal code.** Because a Condition can't seize a will, harm done with one splits cleanly into two very different things: **negligent Tithe-lapse** (letting your element vent, your Cry break, your green go to seed — a regulatory or public-nuisance matter) versus **willful weaponizing** (deliberately turning a gift on someone — ordinary violent crime, no gentler for the instrument). In practice only the Stilled's gaze even supports a true "you petrified a citizen" charge; most of what the law fears is negligence, not assault.
- **It is the shield the feared hide behind.** A Bound can point to the Inviolate Will and say, truthfully, *my contract cannot make you do anything.* Whether a given polity *listens* is exactly where they diverge.

### The safety-licence — how hazard is regulated (and by whom)

The one place the law *does* attach to individuals is public safety — and it does so narrowly. Because every Condition carries a Tithe, there's a natural hook: **the handful of Conditions whose lapsed Tithe or lost temper can hurt bystanders are the only ones that require a licence to practice.** The rule of thumb is consistent across the world: **a licence attaches where a Condition is *dangerous to others* — never where it is merely powerful, and never as a way to keep track of people.** It is the same logic by which any world licenses a few dangerous trades, not a census.

Common tongue: **the ticket**. Vaethorn: *the blessing-to-practice*. Lestrand: *the guild-ticket*. Threnmaieth: *the crown-warrant*.

By that rule, only these need a licence — and it's held by the person as a mark of competence, like a master's ticket:

| Licence weight | Condition | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Strictest** | the Stilled | The one gaze that can hold a person still forever. Real oversight and a strict code — the model the others imitate. |
| **Heavy, training-first** | Far-Voiced, the Answered | A Cry in a crowded hall, an element "letting go" in a workshop — real bystander hazard, mastered by guild apprenticeship and scheduled outlets. |
| **Contractual, not bodily** | Bound | Overseen less for bodily hazard (the Whisper can't command) than because a Bound *is* a walking legal instrument. |
| **None** | Two-Bodied (~18%), Long-Lived, Returned, and all the Kept | No bystander danger worth a licence. **The great majority of the Conditioned carry nothing at all** — they are simply neighbours. |

Licences are **guild-issued, not state-issued**. A guild *knows its own members* the way any craft guild does; it isn't a registry of the population, it's a roster of practitioners of a dangerous art, kept by the art's own masters. The state *charters* the guild and lets it police its own under threat of losing the charter. So the Stilled's guild licenses stillers and the element-guilds channel the Answered, while the state watches the guild, not the individual. This diffuses power (no single authority holds the Conditioned) and breeds its own frictions: guilds guard monopolies, licences become property, and an unlicensed practitioner threatens the guild's charter as much as the public's safety.

### The three pillars — why the Conditioned *are* the legal system

The deepest fact about Condition-law is that it is not law imposed *on* the Conditioned from outside — three Conditions **are the legal apparatus itself**, and they are exactly the people who make a register unnecessary:

- **Long-Lived** — incorruptible memory. The archivists, notaries, and judges; a matter of record is whatever they say they witnessed a century ago. Where documents would be forged, a Long-Lived simply *remembers*.
- **Bound** — binding word. A Bound's sworn oath is *literally* binding and they hear a lie as it's told, so they are the witnesses, oath-keepers, and debt-arbiters. **When a treaty must truly hold, a Bound swears it.**
- **Far-Voiced** — unfakeable feeling. Trusted as sworn witnesses precisely *because* their voice betrays real emotion under strain, so they cannot lie well on the stand.

Evidence and contract in the Turning are therefore *made of people.* Its late-medieval reaches trust — perfect memory, lie-heard oaths, unfakeable testimony — that other worlds needed paper bureaucracies (and centuries) to approximate, because here the machinery is alive and Given. **A world with witnesses like these does not need to write everyone down** — which is exactly why the one kingdom that insists on doing so looks so strange to everyone else.

### Rights of the Kept

The Kept are the quiet centre of citizenship, and their legal position is best described as **gloriously unremarkable.** They were witnessed at their Turning like everyone; they carry no Tithe, need no licence, answer to no guild. In most polities the Kept are the **default legal person** — the frame the rest of the law is drawn around.

Vaethorn's second word is *the Waited* / *Mother-kept* (pitied — passed over). Lestrand's is *the free* (approving — unencumbered labor). Threnmaieth's is *the unmarked* / *the passed-over* (noted as such).

Two wrinkles keep them from being a footnote:

- **Kept status is not permanent immunity.** About a third of the Kept are still **Struck** later. So a Kept person lives untithed and unlicensed but never wholly outside the safety system's reach — the day they're Struck, they too must be vouched anew. Legally, "Kept" describes your present, not a promise about your future.
- **Where reverence for the gift runs hot, the Kept can be quietly *pitied* or pressured** — treated as having "missed out," leaned on to explain why the Tree passed them over. This is a social harm the law rarely names, and it runs hardest in the most devout polities.

The Given keep their plain name everywhere. Vaethorn's second word is *the gifted* (blessed). Lestrand's is *the Given* (plain). Threnmaieth's is *the marked* (on the roll).

### Edge cases the law must hold

- **The Hollow (the Unbound)** raise a *capacity and protective-status* question. Freed from a contract but missing the Pledge they paid, an isolated Hollow is pulled to sign again. Kinder polities extend a **protective status** (a guardian, a claim on connection, a bar on presenting them fresh contracts); harsher ones leave them to the pull.
- **The Given-Over (the "Forfeit")** is the setting's sharpest legal wound: a Bound who kept re-signing until the counterparty effectively *owns the person*. This is a living case of **legal personhood erased by accumulated contract** — and the contract-guilds have a term of art for it precisely because the law has never fully decided *at what point a person has bargained away enough of themselves to stop being one.* A recurring, unresolved question rather than a settled rule.
- **Phoenix** is not a licensing problem at all — a single legendary person who cannot hide is a *political* problem (a symbol no ruler controls), handled by whoever currently wants to own or protect the one alive.

### Three polity stances

The grammar above is universal; the *tone* is not. Variation comes from three independent axes every polity sets separately:

- **Theology — how gift-devout is it?** From ardent Motherfaith gift-integration (a Condition is a blessing, belonging is a birthright) through worn-lightly civic religion to frank secular pragmatism, and the odd local heresy. Sets how the Struck, the Kept, and the feared Conditions are *read* before the law even speaks.
- **Reach — Tree-rich core or Tree-poor edge?** Where healthy Trees stand, reach is full and the Given are dense; where Trees are few, young, or sickening, the population runs higher on the Kept and the Struck.
- **Governance — how far does the state reach onto the person?** From light guild self-rule (the world's warm default — the craft polices its own, the crown charters the guild) all the way to a crown that writes its subjects down. Sets how much the safety-hook becomes a surveillance-hook.

Three recurring points on these axes:

**The Warm** *(high theology).* Law flows straight from the Motherfaith's gift-theology: a Condition is a blessing, the Struck are vouched-in graciously as people the Mother met at a later door, licences are framed as helping you tend your gift rather than policing you. Warmest near the Awakening Tree. **Its injustice is soft and hard to name:** everyone is *expected* to serve their gift, so guild "vocation" shades into pressure, the Kept get pitied for missing out, and to decline your Condition's obvious use reads as ingratitude to the Mother herself.

**The Watchful** *(governance turned to its extreme — a deliberate aberration).* At the far end of the governance axis sits a crown that breaks the world's norm and *does* write its people down — a ruler who does not trust living memory and keeps a **roster of every subject and their Condition.** Note precisely what makes this aberrant: it is *not* that they keep written records at all — every polity has guild rolls, notarial acts, and tax ledgers — it is that they keep **a census of persons and their gifts**, a record held *on people*, which no one else does. Threnmaieth's census calls itself the **Threnhael** *(THREN-hayl)* — *thren* (sacred counting) + *hael* (whole, hale): "the keeping-whole." It *sounds* like care and *is* total surveillance. Its own clerks say they "keep the realm hale"; its subjects are "kept whole"; outsiders hear "the Counting." Framed at home as prudence and order; experienced as **surveillance**, because that is what it is. The unvouched are hunted, the Struck are pursued to be catalogued, Tithe-needs become points of leverage, and the Bound especially are watched far past their real hazard — pure stigma dressed as safety, since the Whisper can't command and the roster's clerks know it. A Watchful kingdom that is also devout and Tree-rich is the darkest corner of the whole space.

**The Frontier** *(low reach).* Far from any healthy Tree, where even witnessing is thin and custom outruns law. The giving thins where the Trees are thin, so these lands run higher on the Kept and higher on the Struck — more people the rite reached faintly or not at all. Freer in the good sense (no one asks for a licence; the Taken-In whom cities keep at arm's length are simply *neighbours* here, the backbone of the frontier economy) and lawless in the bad (unvouched Struck with no one to stand for them and no guild to train them, feared Conditions met with a rope rather than a licence). Reach and governance often *drift* together — a thin-reach edge tends to be thinly governed too — but they need not: a devout frontier and a lightly-governed core both exist.
