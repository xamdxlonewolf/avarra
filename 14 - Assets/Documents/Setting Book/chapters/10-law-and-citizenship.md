# Law and Citizenship


You belong because your town *saw* you Turn.

Every child passes through their town Turning Tree in their tenth year, and the whole town is there to watch. The Leaf-Fall is the most-attended day of a community's year: kin, neighbours, the tree-wardens, all gathered. They *see* the leaf fall on a child, or see the Tree hug them.

So citizenship in the Turning is **witnessed, not recorded.** You are of a place because that place *remembers you*. It watched you become who you are.

There is no register of the people. No town, church, or crown keeps a list of everyone and their gift. That would be to hold a record *on* people. Belonging is living communal memory, the way a real village knows its own, not a ledger. The one kingdom that *does* keep such a list is a notorious exception: Threnmaieth, the Tallied Crown. It reads as unsettling because no one else does it.

This is also a Hands-era default. In the Walking Years the town that saw you Turn was the crowd under Thaeloren, or no one, if you never arrived. Vouching-at-a-distance exists to *carry that origin-witness home.*

In a village the witness is the whole settlement. In a city it is a **hearth** — a street, a quay-gang, a courtyard, a guild-stair — not twenty thousand mouths pretending to remember one child. The legal fact does not change.

### Turning in a city: the hearth stood

A village of three hundred can remember every child. A salt-city of tens of thousands cannot. Witnessed citizenship does not become a register at that scale. It becomes **nested**.

The Tree is still at the centre. Turning-Week is still the week's door. The square is still full. The legal fact is not "Orentel saw you." The legal fact is **your hearth stood**.

A hearth, here, is eighty to two hundred mouths who already know the child. They bring their ten-year-olds in a named slot of the week. The warden Speaks the colour or names the hug. The city as a crowd saw a crowd. The hearth saw a child.

One **pillar** of that hearth — a Long-Lived, a Bound, or a Far-Voiced who stood — carries the memory when a far desk asks. Kin and neighbours carry it first; the pillar is how the stand *travels*. An orphan is still brought by kin, by the household that feeds them, or by the warden-hearth. A child no hearth will bring is a miss, same as in a village.

To prove you are of a city is to **name a hearth** and produce someone of it, or a pillar who stood. "I am of the third quay" is a citizenship sentence. "I am of Orentel" is geography.

Threnmaieth writes the stand down. That is still the aberration. Everyone else keeps the week-slate as hours, not as gifts.

### How you prove who you are: by *people*, not papers

The Turning never needed to invent the papers-and-registers state, because trust runs through people who cannot be faked.

You are known by **witnesses**, not documents. Common tongue: **standing-for**. Vaethorn calls it *the speaking* (vouched at worship). Lestrand calls it *warranting* (a commercial guarantee). Threnmaieth calls it *the naming* (you are named to the crown).

- A **Long-Lived** remembers you, and their memory is incorruptible and runs back centuries.
- A **Bound** *vouches* for you. A Bound's sworn word is literally binding, so a false vouch is not a lie they can cheaply tell.
- A **Far-Voiced** speaks for you. Their voice betrays real feeling under strain, so they cannot smoothly lie on your behalf.

To establish yourself in a new place is to **be vouched into it**: to acquire people who will stand for you. **The friendless and the rootless are the truly vulnerable**, because they have no one to vouch. The law's exclusions fall not on "the undocumented" but on **the unvouched**, a person no one will stand for.

A traveler is not, by default, that person.

### How a traveler stays vouched

The unvouched remain the vulnerable class. A legitimate traveler is **on a word**, not on a paper-state. The Hands-era road inherited this from vouching-at-a-distance: the origin-witness had to *travel home*. It still does.

A watch that stops a stranger asks three things: **name, last hearth, who stands.** Answer any two and the road continues. Answer none and you are held until dawn, or until someone will stand, or you are walked to the next gate. Routine harassment is for the nameless. A company that can name a last host and show one mark is how this grammar wants people — including a party — to move.

| Custom | What it is | What it is not |
|---|---|---|
| **Road-word** | A named person at origin or last town. "Ask Halim of the third quay." A Far-Voiced can call; a Bound can send a word; a letter is a token of a mouth. | A passport. The scrap is worthless if the mouth is dead or will not stand. |
| **Company-vouch** | A party stands for each other. One locally known or ticketed member covers the rest *for passage*. | Citizenship. You may sleep. You are not of this town. |
| **Guild-mark** | A stiller's ticket, a Crae journeyman-mark, a Voice-ticket. Craft marks travel. | A Condition-census. The mark says you may Gaze, work an element, or Cry in a hall. |
| **House-note** | Deathless paper. Credit that doubles as a light stand. | Required. Most walkers have none. |
| **Guest-right** | A host who takes you in stands for the stay. An inn, a table, a road-house. "I ate at X" holds about a week. | A sold vouch. The host must have taken you in. |
| **Walk-custom** | Someone on a named walk (Near Mile, Salt Walk, Long Mile, First-Hand year) is vouched *as a walker* by the road: mile-shrines, road-houses, a berth-slate. | Permanent belonging. When the walk ends, the word ends. |

Struck on the road are vouched-anew at the next desk that will take them (the Intake if the door is hazardous). Changing unwitnessed is still a seam. It is not a crime to have changed.

Sold vouching still exists as a nameless underside. It is not this custom. A sold mouth that will not answer a Far-Voiced call is how the market fails in public.

Kumbaan is not a jurisdiction on this grammar. You do not arrive vouched. A road-word, company-vouch, or guild-mark still helps you *leave a Strandoren quay*. It does not file a hill. On the isle, guest-right is the table. A second sitting makes you kin *there*. It does not become a last-hearth you can spend on a mainland watch.

### The Struck: those who changed *unwitnessed*

A Struck person acquired their Condition *later, and alone*: a drowning, a death, a sworn contract, long after their tenth-year Turning. The town watched everyone else become who they are. **No one watched the Struck.** They changed with no witnesses.

So a Struck person must be **vouched anew**. The community has to re-know them as what they have become, on the word of those who will stand for them. The old unease that the Struck are "a little suspect" is not only prejudice. Their becoming happened where no one could see it. A change everyone witnessed is trusted; a change that happened in the dark must be spoken for. Every polity has people whose transformation went unwitnessed, and every polity feels differently about that.

Two things make the seam sharper:

- **The dangerous Struck arrive raw.** A Struck Stilled or Far-Voiced manifests *untrained*, with the hazard already live and no guild to have caught it young. For these, being vouched-in is bound up with **mandatory guild-intake**: you are taken in hand *and* apprenticed at once, for everyone's safety, whether you wanted the vocation or not.
- **Some doors are crimes-adjacent.** The Bound are Struck by a sworn contract; the Returned by dying. To vouch a Bound into a community is to acknowledge a contract no one witnessed being sworn, which is why contract-law and the acceptance of the Bound are tangled together.

Vaethorn's second word for the Struck is *the late-come* (met at a later door). Lestrand's is *the turned* (neutral, a fact). Threnmaieth's is *the unwitnessed* (suspect until named).

### The Inviolate Will

Beneath all local variation sits one principle every polity treats as the floor of law, and it holds because it is *true*: **no Condition can compel, control, or bend another person's will.** That is the **Inviolate Will**. The Bound's Whisper, the Far-Voiced's Cry, and the Long-Lived's regard cannot cross it: every Condition's danger stops at property, pain, and fear, at the edge of another's choosing. The law mirrors the metaphysics in a maxim taught to every child:

> **"The will is one's own."**

Vaethorn holds it as *the Mother's own line* (scripture). Lestrand cites *the free-will clause* (sound commercial law). Threnmaieth cites *the crown's assurance* (to justify the roll).

A **Tithe** is the upkeep of a Condition, not a tax to a temple.

- **The old mind-control fears are void in law.** There is no such crime as "being mind-controlled by a vampire," because it cannot happen. That is the legal floor under treating the Conditioned as neighbours, and everyone knows it.
- **It sorts the criminal code.** Because a Condition cannot seize a will, harm done with one splits into two very different things. **Negligent Tithe-lapse** (letting your element vent, your Cry break, your green go to seed) is a regulatory or public-nuisance matter. **Willful weaponizing** (deliberately turning a gift on someone) is ordinary violent crime, no gentler for the instrument. In practice only the Stilled's gaze even supports a true "you petrified a citizen" charge. Most of what the law fears is negligence, not assault.
- **It is the shield the feared hide behind.** A Bound can point to the Inviolate Will and say, truthfully, *my contract cannot make you do anything.* Whether a given polity *listens* is where they diverge.

### The safety-licence: how hazard is regulated (and by whom)

The one place the law *does* attach to individuals is public safety, and it does so narrowly. **A ticket attaches to a hazardous *use*, not to being Conditioned.** The rule of thumb is consistent across the world: **a licence attaches where a practice is *dangerous to others*, never where a gift is merely powerful, and never as a way to keep track of people.** It is the same logic by which any world licenses a few dangerous trades, not a census.

Common tongue: **the ticket**. Vaethorn: *the blessing-to-practice*. Lestrand: *the guild-ticket*. Threnmaieth: *the crown-warrant*.

The three ticket-Conditions are the Stilled, Far-Voiced, and the Answered (~7 / ~12 / ~10). Together that is **~29% of people, ~39% of the Conditioned** — a large minority who *can* be asked for a ticket if they practice the hazardous use. It is not "~90% of the Conditioned carry nothing." It is also not a third of the world on a crown list.

**The pool is not the roll.** A ticket names a competence (stiller, hall-voice, Crae-hand), not a Condition-ID. Three guilds keep no shared roll. The state watches the charter.

| Who | Ticket names | Who is not on it |
|---|---|---|
| **the Stilled — personal Gaze-ticket** | Anyone who Gazes in public: surgeons, riot-lines, demolition. Most stillers hold one, because the vocation *is* the Gaze. | A Stilled who never Gazes (rare). The guild still wants them in the yard. |
| **Far-Voiced — Voice-ticket / hall-hour** | Those who Cry in a crowd or keep a scheduled hour: criers, performers, sworn witnesses. Hall-keepers stamp competence and sell the hour at the same lintel. | A field-voice who vents at the hedge. The hall-slate is hours, not a list of every blue-leaf in the city. |
| **the Answered — Crae-ticket / yard-leave** | Element used in a shop, a yard, a hull. The Crae stamps the craft. | An Answered who never works the element in public. |
| **Bound — a table, not a body-ticket** | No licence. The honest table oversees Terms. The Whisper cannot command. | Do not add Bound's ~5% contract-lane to the licence rate. |
| **No ticket** | — | Two-Bodied, Long-Lived, Returned, the Taken-In, the Unbound, Phoenix, and all the Kept. About two people in three. They are simply neighbours. |

Active papers in a working city are smaller than the 29% pool — perhaps half of it, fewer on the frontier, where a feared Condition is still as likely to meet a rope as a stool.

Licences are **guild-issued, not state-issued**. A guild *knows its own members* the way any craft guild does. It is not a registry of the population. It is a roster of practitioners of a dangerous art, kept by the art's own masters. The state *charters* the guild and lets it police its own under threat of losing the charter. So the Stilled's guild licenses stillers and the element-guilds channel the Answered, while the state watches the guild, not the individual. This diffuses power (no single authority holds the Conditioned) and breeds its own frictions: guilds guard monopolies, tickets become property, and an unlicensed practitioner threatens the guild's charter as much as the public's safety.

### Harm, the watch, and the hearing

The Inviolate Will already sorts the charge: **negligent Tithe-lapse** versus **willful weaponizing**. This is the ladder the charge walks. There is no crime of *being* a Condition.

1. **Neighbour-word.** Most harm dies on the street. Restitution, apology, a Bound word if they have one. The watch is not called.
2. **The watch.** Night-watch, ward-watch. Often Kept (untithed nights) and Two-Bodied (the ranging *is* the Tithe), with a Far-Voiced for the shout. They hold a body until morning. They do not investigate Conditions. They fetch a pillar or a guild.
3. **Guild-hearing.** Tithe-lapse, unlicensed Gaze, an element let go in a shop. The guild fines, suspends a ticket, binds to apprenticeship. The state is not in the room unless the charter is at risk.
4. **Open hearing.** Willful harm. Witnesses — especially the three pillars. The charge is the act. "You Gazed a man" is assault. "You are Stilled" is not a crime. A Gaze used as a forever-hold is the rare charge the Stillers' own code already names.
5. **What the hearing can do** without writing you down:
   - Restitution — coin, labor, furnish a Tithe you broke
   - Ticket-stripping — you may not practice the dangerous act
   - A Bound word, or a term
   - **Exile from the hearth** — you become unvouched in your own city. This is the civic death. It does not require a file.
   - The rope or the wall — murder, and the rare forever-hold. Commoner on the frontier. Rare in the Warm.

The Watchful write you down. That is their extra punishment, and why they feel like a different country.

*A door-custom, pinned inside a Lestrand night-gate. No crest. The verso is last week's rain.*

> *Name. Last hearth. Who stands. Two will do. None, and you sit the bench until the second bell, or until a mouth comes, or we walk you to the next gate. We do not ask your colour. We do not keep the three answers past dawn. If you are on a walk, say which. If you ate at the bed-house, say so. If you have a ticket, show the stool-mark, not a story. The bench is not a cell. The bench is until morning.*

On its face: a watch being practical. Underneath: the three questions *are* the citizenship engine, run at a gate. They think "we do not ask your colour" makes them unlike Threnmaieth. They also do not write the answers down, which is how a city of tens of thousands still runs on hearths.

### The three pillars: why the Conditioned *are* the legal system

Condition-law is not imposed *on* the Conditioned from outside. Three Conditions **are the legal apparatus itself**, and they are the people who make a register unnecessary:

- **Long-Lived**: incorruptible memory. The archivists, notaries, and judges. A matter of record is whatever they say they witnessed a century ago. Where documents would be forged, a Long-Lived simply *remembers*.
- **Bound**: binding word. A Bound's sworn oath is *literally* binding and they hear a lie as it is told, so they are the witnesses, oath-keepers, and debt-arbiters. **When a treaty must truly hold, a Bound swears it.**
- **Far-Voiced**: unfakeable feeling. Trusted as sworn witnesses because their voice betrays real emotion under strain, so they cannot lie well on the stand.

Evidence and contract in the Turning are therefore *made of people.* Perfect memory, lie-heard oaths, and unfakeable testimony give a late-medieval world a trust other worlds needed paper bureaucracies, and centuries, to approximate, because here the machinery is alive and Given. A world with witnesses like these does not need to write everyone down.

### Rights of the Kept

The Kept are the quiet centre of citizenship, and their legal position is **unremarkable.** They were witnessed at their Turning like everyone. They carry no Tithe, need no licence, answer to no guild. In most polities the Kept are the **default legal person**, the frame the rest of the law is drawn around.

Vaethorn's second word is *the Waited* / *Mother-kept* (pitied: passed over). Lestrand's is *the free* (approving: unencumbered labor). Threnmaieth's is *the unmarked* / *the passed-over* (noted as such).

Two wrinkles keep them from being a footnote:

- **Kept status is not permanent immunity.** About a third of the Kept are still **Struck** later. So a Kept person lives untithed and unlicensed but never wholly outside the safety system's reach. The day they are Struck, they too must be vouched anew. Legally, "Kept" describes your present, not a promise about your future.
- **Where reverence for the gift runs hot, the Kept can be quietly *pitied* or pressured**: treated as having "missed out," leaned on to explain why the Tree passed them over. This is a social harm the law rarely names, and it runs hardest in the most devout polities.

The Given keep their plain name everywhere. Vaethorn's second word is *the gifted* (blessed). Lestrand's is *the Given* (plain). Threnmaieth's is *the marked* (on the roll).

### Edge cases the law must hold

- **The Hollow (the Unbound)** raise a *capacity and protective-status* question. Freed from a contract but missing the Pledge they paid, an isolated Hollow is pulled to sign again. Kinder polities extend a **protective status** (a guardian, a claim on connection, a bar on presenting them fresh contracts). Harsher ones leave them to the pull.
- **The Given-Over (the "Forfeit")** is the setting's sharpest legal wound: a Bound who kept re-signing until the counterparty effectively *owns the person*. This is a living case of **legal personhood erased by accumulated contract**, and the contract-guilds have a term of art for it because the law has never fully decided *at what point a person has bargained away enough of themselves to stop being one.* A recurring, unresolved question rather than a settled rule.
- **Phoenix** is not a licensing problem at all. A single legendary person who cannot hide is a *political* problem (a symbol no ruler controls), handled by whoever currently wants to own or protect the one alive.
- **UnTurned is not Kept.** A child who missed, refused, or never stood has no hug and no colour. Folk will call them Kept. The law should file them as the person the town raised, vouched if someone will stand, unwitnessed at the Tree. A second Leaf-Fall is not a remedy. Struck doors still open later.

### Three polity stances

The grammar above is universal; the *tone* is not. Variation comes from three independent axes every polity sets separately:

- **Theology: how gift-devout is it?** From ardent Motherfaith gift-integration (a Condition is a blessing, belonging is a birthright) through worn-lightly civic religion to frank secular pragmatism, and the odd local heresy. Sets how the Struck, the Kept, and the feared Conditions are *read* before the law even speaks.
- **Reach: Tree-rich core or Tree-poor edge?** Where healthy Trees stand, reach is full and the Given are dense. Where Trees are few, young, or sickening, the population runs higher on the Kept and the Struck.
- **Governance: how far does the state reach onto the person?** From light guild self-rule (the world's warm default: the craft polices its own, the crown charters the guild) all the way to a crown that writes its subjects down. Sets how much the safety-hook becomes a surveillance-hook.

Three recurring points on these axes:

**The Warm** *(high theology).* Law flows straight from the Motherfaith's gift-theology: a Condition is a blessing, the Struck are vouched-in graciously as people the Mother met at a later door, licences are framed as helping you tend your gift rather than policing you. Warmest near the Awakening Tree. **Its injustice is soft and hard to name:** everyone is *expected* to serve their gift, so guild "vocation" shades into pressure, the Kept get pitied for missing out, and to decline your Condition's obvious use reads as ingratitude to the Mother herself.

**The Watchful** *(governance turned to its extreme: a deliberate aberration).* At the far end of the governance axis sits a crown that breaks the world's norm and *does* write its people down: a ruler who does not trust living memory and keeps a **roster of every subject and their Condition.** What makes this aberrant is not that they keep written records at all. Every polity has guild rolls, notarial acts, and tax ledgers. It is that they keep **a census of persons and their gifts**, a record held *on people*, which no one else does. Threnmaieth's census calls itself the **Threnhael** *(THREN-hayl)*: *thren* (sacred counting) + *hael* (whole, hale), "the keeping-whole." Clerks say they "keep the realm hale." Subjects are "kept whole." Outsiders hear "the Counting." Framed at home as prudence and order; experienced as **surveillance**. The unvouched are hunted, the Struck are pursued to be catalogued, Tithe-needs become points of leverage, and the Bound especially are watched far past their real hazard: pure stigma dressed as safety, since the Whisper can't command and the roster's clerks know it. A Watchful kingdom that is also devout and Tree-rich is the darkest mix of the three axes.

**The Frontier** *(low reach).* Far from any healthy Tree, where even witnessing is thin and custom outruns law. The giving thins where the Trees are thin, so these lands run higher on the Kept and higher on the Struck: more people the rite reached faintly or not at all. Freer in the good sense (no one asks for a licence; the Taken-In whom cities keep at arm's length are simply *neighbours* here, the backbone of the frontier economy) and lawless in the bad (unvouched Struck with no one to stand for them and no guild to train them, feared Conditions met with a rope rather than a licence). Reach and governance often *drift* together. A thin-reach edge tends to be thinly governed too. They need not drift together: a devout frontier and a lightly-governed core both exist.
