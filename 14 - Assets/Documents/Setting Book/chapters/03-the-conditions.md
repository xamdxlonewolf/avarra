# The Conditions

A Condition is what a person becomes, set beside the Kind they were born, the ancestry they came into the world already wearing. For about three people in four there is also a Condition, one of the old monsters living in town as a neighbor, with a job the town needs. The Turning Tree gives most of them at the Leaf-Fall, a child's tenth-year solstice, when the colour of a leaf names the Condition and that child is Given. The rest are Struck later, by a threshold the Tree does not hand out at that ceremony. Either way the Condition carries a Tithe, an upkeep you meet by ordinary play and an ordinary rest, and the Tithe must never seize control of the person.

On the sheet, a Condition is a Daggerheart Transformation card. The card opens with a line of flavor and then its features. At minimum there is a boon, the thing the Condition lets you do, and a Tithe, the drawback that keeps the boon honest. Both of them hook to resources the game already uses, Hope, Stress, Hit Points, Fear, weapon tiers, and Experiences, rather than to a private meter that only the Condition understands. A player character holds one transformation. It sits in the loadout the way a heritage card does, and it does not count against the limit on domain cards. How many features a card carries is allowed to vary. A Condition that takes the table's focus, or a complicated one, carries more features than a workhorse card. What stays constant is the shape of the card, a clean boon and a Tithe with its name written on it.

The card does not level, and it does not grow because the character does. A becoming is not a class. The card you hold at level 1 is the card you hold at level 10, while class features, domain cards, Experiences, weapon tier, and Hope scars are what change around it. A few things look like the card is growing, and they are not. A Two-Bodied may attach an animal-form Experience when they would gain an Experience, and a second signature capability is a once-ever story milestone, not a level to pick. Returned and Phoenix spend Hope slots, and that spending is a countdown of the returns they have left, not a power that climbs as they level. A Bound's Terms, the standing obligations written into the contract, and the Pledge, the piece of themselves named at signing as the price, move when the story moves them. Attacks that use Proficiency rise because Proficiency rises. Those attacks are Kindle, the Phoenix's burst of fire, Reach, the moment an Answered element's answer leaves the hands, the Green Word, the Taken-In's sudden growth, and the Two-Bodied natural weapon.

A Tithe's tokens drain or fill when the day is over, at a long rest, unless the card names another time. Two short rests in one day do not count as that rest. Check each line once.

There are nine Conditions a person can be Given or Struck with, and the tenth, the Unbound, is not a starting Condition. Common speech calls those people the Hollow, for the hole a finished contract leaves. A Bound enters that state when a term-limited contract ends and no further contract is signed, so you become Unbound only through Bound, in play or arranged with the GM before the first session. There are nine to choose from, and ten in the world. The Given-Over is a title rather than a Condition, the world's name for a Bound who signs contract after contract until the being on the other side effectively holds them. The contract-guilds call that fate the Forfeit. It is not a card anyone plays, and the account of it sits with the Bound.

Over a lifetime, about 60 percent of people are Given a Condition at the Tree, about 15 percent are Struck later, and about 25 percent are the Kept. The Given and the Struck together are the three-quarters who carry a Condition. The Kept are the people the Tree hugged at the ceremony and left as they are, wanted in their own right, who carry no Condition for life. At the ceremony itself, about 40 percent of children are hugged and receive no colour, and a little over a third of those children are still Struck someday.

The share named with each Condition below is the count in the roster at the end of Kind and Condition, and that table is the one count of how many people carry each Condition. The colour of the leaf is the Tree's palette, copied here so a colour can be read without leaving the chapter, and it does not replace that roster.

The Tree never Strikes, so the Returned, the Bound, and the Unbound have no colour. The hug that names one of the Kept is ordinary summer-green, and that green is not a colour that names a Condition.

## Long-Lived

A Long-Lived is an ageless keeper of memory, and they live on what they learn. The vampire of folklore feeds on the living, and a Long-Lived who is starved does not hunt anyone. They fade, they forget, and if the hunger runs all the way out they empty, the memories gone and the life with them. What is left of the old blood-hunger is an echo, and only an echo. A Long-Lived can read a memory in a single drop of blood, and by an ancient courtesy they will not cross a threshold uninvited. You can still recognize the old story in those two habits, and the predation has been taken out of both.

Aging simply stops, and nothing a Long-Lived has witnessed is ever forgotten, which is why a court will trust them as a record that does not corrupt. They are scarce, about 2.5 percent of people, and every one of them is Given, never Struck. The leaf that names them is a rare deep red, the colour of wine, and there is no later door. A world with too many deathless people would lock its courts, guilds, and archives into the shape they already have, so their scarcity is part of the world's shape. They are revered, they are consulted, and they stand a little apart from the neighbors who will not live as long. Courts, guilds, and houses of record build themselves around these people, and use them as archivists, judges, notaries, and libraries that happen to be alive.

A Long-Lived will not cross the threshold of another's home uninvited, and they wait to be asked in, always. People hold that waiting as the deepest good manners, a mark of trustworthiness, because a keeper of records does not intrude, and no one quite remembers the custom as anything else, though it is old and it is universal among them. A Long-Lived who is refused the door feels the refusal more than they will say. It reads, faintly, as the last shadow of the folklore vampire, the one who could not enter unbidden, but nobody calls it that, and it is simply how the Long-Lived have always been.

The leaf is wine-dark, and a neighbor who has seen the ceremony can already read it. The Motherfaith is the faith built around the Turning Tree. Some hold that the Leaf-Mother blessed the world with the Conditions, and the faith treats that as a belief, not as a fact anyone has proved. In a deep-red leaf, that faith hears a chosen keeper, a memory fitted to a town that needs a record. The Watching is the older Tree-faith the Motherfaith grew over. They keep the Tree, they refuse the mother-name, and they say a colour falls because the Tree sees, not because a mother chooses. They call a Long-Lived a witness rather than a mother's pick. Those two readings do not get to close the argument between them.

From the ceremony they go to courts, guilds, and archives. Every Long-Lived is asked once to join the Pourers, who give memory into a cup they say holds a god they made, and they call that god the Unspent. A member gives one year of memory at a time, each year on its own night with its own minutes, and may stop before the next. Five years is what the circle asks of a member in all. For each year given, that year's papers go onto the shelf at the long-house that keeps the circle, and the circle counts the member one year younger. When a member leaves, the years already given stay in the cup, and the papers are what come back. Most Long-Lived say no to the ask, and a person who says no is received everywhere as before. The Pourers are described with the other houses in Orders and Houses.

Meeting the Tithe is why they hold the jobs they do. A Long-Lived wants to keep learning, so scholarship, judging, and the keeping of records are the careers that feed the hunger while the work gets done. Deprivation is the danger: a prison, a siege where nothing new ever happens, exile in a place with nothing to witness. Isolating a Long-Lived is a recognized cruelty.

They can have children, and they can have families, but no child of theirs is guaranteed the Condition, and most children do not inherit it. They watch a spouse grow old and die, and they watch the children do the same, and then they either stop, living on alone with the memory of that house, or they begin again and outlive the next family too. Beneath the hunger for knowledge, they outlive the people they love. That repeating funeral is the deeper Tithe. They are deathless, and they are not unkillable. Violence ends them, accident ends them, and a failed Tithe ends them, which is what keeps the numbers stable across the centuries.

### Rules

> *Ageless keepers of memory, law, and record: they live on what they learn.*

- **Unfading Mind:** Your aging has stopped, and you forget nothing you take in. You have **advantage** on rolls to recognize, appraise, or recall what you have personally witnessed. Spend a **Hope** to recall a witnessed detail complete: the vow as it was spoken, the face, the exact count. The Hope pays for that perfect record. It does not pay for the ordinary work of a librarian.
- **Blood-Read:** Memory rides in blood. Taste a single drop of a creature's blood, freely given or taken from a wound that is already there, and you read a true memory carried in it, a fragment of what that person has lived. The GM chooses the fragment so that it fits what the blood remembers. It is never predation, and it never harms the donor. One drop gives one glimpse, and the older or more guarded the memory you reach for, the less of it you may get. You feed on knowledge, and blood is the oldest record of all.
- **The Hunger to Know** *(Tithe):* Place **3 Memory tokens** on this card. At a long rest, refill all of them if, since your last long rest, you spent Hope on Unfading Mind, used Blood-Read, or spent a downtime move in study or in first-hand witness. Otherwise remove a token. The advantage you always have does not refill the clock. Using the keeper's tools does.
  - **2 tokens:** nothing has begun to slip.
  - **1 token:** the fade begins. You have **disadvantage** on rolls to recall, recognize, appraise, or use Presence, and you start losing older memories, which you describe as they go. Other action rolls and reaction rolls are untouched.
  - **0 tokens:** you are starving. You are **Vulnerable**, and at the start of each long rest that you are still at 0 tokens you mark a **Stress**. If you would mark your last Stress this way, mark a **Hit Point** instead. If you would mark your last Hit Point this way, you do not make a death move. You go still. The memories are gone, the mind is emptied, and that stillness is the true death of the Long-Lived. You may clear Hit Points and Stress as usual while you still have them. Getting a token back, by a Hope spent on recall, a Blood-Read, or a downtime spent in study, stops the starve before the next long rest.

## Two-Bodied

A Two-Bodied carries a second, animal self, and that self is always theirs to command. The werewolf of the old stories is a beast that overtakes a person when the moon is up. There is no moon-curse here, no rage, and no moment in which the person stops being the one who decides. What used to be told as a monster is a useful animal partner you carry inside you, and it is the common Condition, the one towns put to work.

A Two-Bodied has two bodies, the shape they were born in and one specific real animal, and shifting between them is voluntary and clean. That is what makes them the most common labor in the world, and the most versatile, about 18 percent of people, Given only, when the Tree drops a storm-grey leaf at the Awakening and a child of ten comes away with a second self. Rescue, herding, work in the wild, war, and the runs of couriers and scouts all use that second body, and the point of the Condition is that utility, a shape suited to the job, rather than the spectacle of the change. After the ceremony there is no guild waiting and no college to enroll them, so they do the work with no ticket, on civic land and on the ranging-commons, the ground at a town's edge where a Two-Bodied can be their other self.

The animal shape is not an empty suit you climb into. It carries its own personality, temperament, and inner voice, distinct from the born self, and you converse with it. Not aloud as a person, because the animal mouth cannot form human speech, but in your own head, a running second opinion: the wolf that wants to run the scent now, the hawk that trusts height over talk, the otter that finds the whole meeting tedious. It is named and known, so townsfolk say "that's Bramble, her otter," and they come to recognize its moods. It never takes the wheel, because the change and every action stay yours to command, and what it does is nag, want, and color the day, a live companion you happen to be.

The Condition runs in families, so the children of a Two-Bodied parent are far more likely to be Given it at their own Awakening, and usually as the same animal, which is why whole bloodlines are known as wolf-folk, hawk-folk, or otter-folk. A player character still picks their own animal, and someone who is first in the line, or who breaks the family type, is notable to the people around them.

The other self is a real, natural animal, roughly between a housecat and a large wolf, a bear, a boar, or a horse in size, and nothing larger than that band, and nothing out of myth. There is no giant hawk, no dire wolf, no roc, and no kelpie. A real animal does exactly what that animal really does, so a hawk flies, an otter swims and holds its breath, and a bear is strong. The lines between this Condition and the other peoples hold because of what the animal body lacks. In the other self you have no hands, no person's mind at work, no speech as a person, and no tools, and the person still chooses every action. A flying Two-Bodied is a superb scout and a superb courier, and is still not a Tengu, because a Tengu is a person aloft, with hands to carry and to fight and a human mind and a human voice in the air. An aquatic Two-Bodied swims brilliantly and works the water as that animal, not as a tool-using Answered, whose element already answers their hands, and not as a true amphibious person. The body is a magnificent animal, and it is not a person wearing fur.

Storm-grey is already a proverb by the time a child watches it fall, because this is the common leaf and people know what it means before the warden speaks. The path is Given only, with no later door. The Motherfaith hears the common gift, a second body the town can put to work, and the Watching says the Tree saw two selves and made that seeing visible, a witness rather than a mother's pick.

Because the upkeep is simply being the animal sometimes, most Two-Bodied satisfy it without having to think about the rule, since their jobs already demand the animal form. The risk is the restless shift that comes when the day was skipped. It arrives at the worst moment, in the middle of a negotiation or in the middle of a stealth, and it costs a Stress, but it never takes the wheel: you stay in command of what the body does, and only the timing belongs to the Tithe.

### Rules

> *A person who carries a second, animal self, and not a beast that overtakes them.*

- **The Other Self** *(boon):* You have a second body, a specific real animal, chosen when you become Two-Bodied, inside the band above. You may shift between your two selves at will, as an action. The change is always yours to command. It costs no Stress and needs no roll, your gear melds with you, and the gear returns when you change back. In animal form you gain the natural weapon below, plus the beast's own movement, a wolf's run or an otter's swim or a hawk's flight, which is why the shift is worth making in a fight and not only in order to leave one. You are one body or the other, and you cannot equip another Primary while you are in this form.
  - **A signature capability,** chosen with the GM to fit the animal. A hawk might see a mouse from the sky, a wolf might run a scent for miles, a bear might shrug off a blow. That is **advantage** on the fitting rolls, or a small effect once per scene that the GM sets. You start with one signature.
  - **An animal Experience.** Choose one **Experience** that reflects the animal's nature, "Wolf's Nose" or "Cat's Balance" or "Bear's Strength," and use it only while you are in animal form.
  - **A voice, not a driver.** Your other self has its own name, its own temperament, and an inner voice you can converse with. The voice is pure roleplay. It flavors the scene and it nags, it grants no bonus of its own, and it can never take an action you did not choose.

**Natural weapon** (animal form only):

| Category | Tier | Trait | Range | Damage | Burden | Feature |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary | 1 (does not advance) | **Instinct**, or **Strength** if the animal's signature is brute force (bear, boar) | Melee | **d8 phy** (housecat-sized: **d6 phy**; bear or boar: **d10 phy**) | One-Handed | **Natural:** cannot be disarmed; usable only in animal form; you cannot equip another Primary while in this form |

Roll damage dice equal to your **Proficiency**, as with any other weapon. You do not add a second weapon's dice on top. The tier stays 1, so the die on the card stays the die you started with. Proficiency is what raises the number of dice.

- **Both Must Be Lived** *(Tithe):* If a full day passes without time spent as your other self, it grows restless. The next time you roll with **Fear**, you shift into it whether you meant to or not, and you mark a **Stress**. You stay fully in control, and only the timing was not yours. You shift back in the ordinary way. This Tithe is not a clock that ticks at a rest. It rides the duality dice on purpose, the roll that comes up Hope or Fear.

**Growth.** You start with one animal-form Experience and one signature capability. As you live longer as Two-Bodied, growth deepens the character of the animal, not the raw power of the body. Whenever you would gain an Experience, you may attach a new animal-form Experience to your other self, usable only in animal form. Your signature capability stays at one, with a single exception. A second signature is a once-ever milestone, never a routine pick. The GM grants it as the payoff of a genuine story, a season lived almost entirely in the other self or a defining trial passed in that shape, and that grant is the only way the count of capabilities ever grows. The limit is what keeps a long-lived Two-Bodied from quietly stacking scout, tracker, and bruiser into one body.

## Returned

A Returned is a person who died with something unfinished, and whose purpose keeps them walking. They are calm, they are deliberate, and they are unafraid of death because they have already been through it once. The corpse in the old stories rots and does not think. The Returned persist, mind intact, and the towns hand them the work that kills the living: the plague wards, the mine collapses, the recovery of a battlefield, and the tending of the dead, and the speaking for them.

The Returned are Struck only. You cannot be Given this at age ten, because the Tree gives a Condition at the Awakening by the colour of a leaf, and this Condition requires a death. Someone dies with a thing left undone that is strong enough to hold them, and they get back up. Their standing is ordinary and quietly respected, about 7 percent of all people. They are solemn and dependable, the ones you send where the living cannot safely go. Gore does not shake them and death does not shake them, and a death that takes them may still not be the last. A Returned has no colour, because the Tree never Strikes and a death leaves no leaf. What comes next is the Purpose rather than a guild, because no desk can issue one.

The Motherfaith brings them to the Tree after the fact, for acknowledgment rather than for a gift the Tree is handing out. The Wide-Hand, the reading that says the Leaf-Mother meets people at every door and not only under the Tree, says she met them at the death-door, and the Narrow-Hand, which holds that she gives only at the Tree, is uneasy. The Watching says the threshold was always a place of seeing, and the Old Ways, the land-and-ancestor faith that keeps rivers, groves, and oath-stones as older doors than any grafted Tree, name them kin at the door that made them rather than calling the return a late gift. None of those faiths can close the question between them.

The Tithe is a feeling as much as a rule on the card. A Returned is steady only while they have a reason, and if they neglect the reason they cool, in the temperature of the skin and in the way the living step back from them. The living can tell a settling Returned at a touch. That is why the Returned build a life around the Purpose with a care that looks almost religious. The returns themselves are counted. Each time they Refuse to Fall, they cross out a Hope slot, so a Returned who has walked a long time is a person whose remaining comebacks you can count. Blaze of Glory stays available as the death they can choose. After a life of refusing rest, they can go out on their own terms, spectacularly, rather than on the last scar.

When the unfinished thing is finally done, they make the choice with the GM: lie down and take the rest they skipped, lay the Condition aside and live out the rest of their days as a mortal with no Condition at all, or take up a new Purpose and keep walking. That choice is a major beat for the character.

### Rules

> *You died with something unfinished. It keeps you walking.*

- **Unfinished Business** *(boon):* You have already died once, and death does not hold you the way it holds other people. When you make a death move, you cannot choose **Avoid Death**. Your options are **Blaze of Glory**, **Risk It All**, and a fourth option this card gives you, **Refuse to Fall**. Avoid Death is the safe road in Daggerheart: you fall unconscious, you may take a scar, and you come back when an ally clears a Hit Point or the party finishes a long rest. Offering it here would make Refuse to Fall a worse version of a move every character already has, so it is off the list. When you Refuse to Fall, you go down and leave the current scene, and you pull yourself back together by the next long rest, which you describe. Coming back leaves a mark. You cross out a Hope slot as a scar. If that crossing would take your last Hope slot, you cannot Refuse to Fall. The unfinished thing finally lets go, and that death is true and final, the same scar rule Daggerheart already uses: crossing out your last Hope slot ends the character's journey. You may still choose Blaze of Glory, or gamble on Risk It All and die if the gamble fails. Refuse to Fall is the steady road back, and it is finite, because the scars count it. You are also unshaken by the dead and the dying. You automatically pass rolls to resist fear, horror, or despair that come from corpses, gore, and deathly places. That exemption belongs to this card alone. It is not the Unbound's missing capacity, and it is not Quiet.
- **The Tether** *(Tithe):* Name your **Purpose**, the unfinished thing that holds you here, and write it on the card. While you can still act toward it, you are steady. If a long rest passes in which you did nothing in service of that Purpose, you begin to settle. You mark a **Stress** that cannot be cleared until you next act toward the Purpose, and your touch runs cold. A living creature you touch, or that touches you, feels a bone-deep wrongness and marks a Stress, and you have **disadvantage** on rolls to comfort, persuade, or charm the living until you act toward your Purpose again.

When the Purpose is fulfilled, you settle and take the rest you skipped, or you live on with no Condition, or you name a new Purpose and keep walking.

## Bound

The Bound are people who, in some extremity, swore a contract with something on the other side and are still keeping the terms. They are the only people carrying a Condition who chose it. Every other Condition was Given when the Tree offered it, or Struck when a threshold took the person whether they had asked or not. The Bound asked, and something answered. The power they carry is the power they named, and what their neighbors fear is not a demon in the blood but the debt, and the counterparty who watches the ledger and does not look away.

Daggerheart already has the Infernis, people born demon-blooded. That is a Kind, the ancestry you come into the world wearing, and Bound is not that. Bound is acquired, and you are never born into it. An Infernis may still be Bound, because Kind and Condition stack on the same person. The blood is not a contract, and the contract is not a bloodline.

The Tree gives a Condition at the Awakening, or it withholds a colour and gives nothing, and it does not Give a contract. The Bound are Struck only. The threshold is a bargain: someone in dire need swore, at whatever age, and something answered. A Bound has no colour, because a contract leaves no leaf. People fear them because the contract-lane is about 5 percent of everyone, the power is real, the terms bind, and a Bound who falls behind is collateral the contract can collect. Towns need them in the same breath, because a Bound's sworn word is literally binding, and many of them can tell a lie when they hear one, so the world puts them to work as enforcers, oath-keepers, witnesses, and the people who arbitrate a debt. When a treaty absolutely must hold, a Bound swears to it. That 5 percent is the whole contract-lane, and it already holds both halves. People currently Bound are about 3.5 percent, and the Unbound already released are about 1.5 percent inside the same figure. You do not count the released ones a second time on top.

The faiths do not read a Bound the same way. The Motherfaith fears them and still needs them, and it receives them back at the Tree so the town can acknowledge what has already happened. That receiving is not a Gift, because the Tree is not handing the contract out. The Fair Hand, the fortune-and-contract faith that treats a becoming as an account a person can close and walk away from, says this is the same shape as every other becoming, written large enough to read, rather than a horror set apart. The Watching names a seeing at a harder door, and the Old Ways take a Bound to the oath-stone that heard the vow and name them kin. The Book-Hands, the Fair Hand's notaries, witness the signing, and on oath-day they recite the Terms where a neighbor can hear them, and they do not rewrite those Terms. A newly Struck Bound is sent first to the Intake, the shed that takes a raw Struck person in hand before the town reaches for a rope, so the contract can be read and written down.

You choose the counterparty when the character is made, or you do not know it yet and the discovery is a thread of play. What holds the note may be a demon or a lesser power, a named thing that bargains, or an intelligence that is vast, indifferent, and wrong. It may also be unknown even to you, so that you genuinely do not know what holds your note.

The only sure release is to kill the thing that holds the contract. A patron that can grant this power can often sense the intent while it is still forming, so plotting the kill is its own peril. A term-limited contract does not need that ending. It comes due, the counterparty releases you and collects the Pledge, and what that leaves behind is one of the Unbound.

A Bound may contract for true flight, but the sky is already spoken for. It belongs to the Tengu and, in legend, to the Phoenix. A contract for flight is uncommon and never clean. It grants literal, visible wings, or some stranger tell, a shadow that lifts or feet that will not touch the ground, and a Bound cannot furl those wings or hide that tell. The bargain marks them as plainly as a Phoenix's fire marks a Phoenix, so anyone looking can see that a deal was made. Most people who want the sky are more simply served by a glide or a saving slow-fall. True sustained flight is a GM's call, priced with heavier Terms and a Pledge to match, and it is not one of the ordinary boons a Bound can carry in a crowd.

The world has a name for a Bound who keeps signing, power after power and pledge after pledge. They start because they are afraid of paying what they already owe, and another contract defers the debt, and then it becomes too much to stop. Bit by bit they give themselves over until the being effectively holds them. This is a fate for people the table meets, not a Condition a player takes, and a player character who has signed away nearly all of themselves is no longer really playable. The plain name is the Given-Over, the contract-guilds call that same fate the Forfeit, and the pipeline that feeds it is the Holding Desk, the back room that will still write a next term after the honest stamp has already closed.

### Rules

> *You swore something to something, and it answered. Now you both keep the terms.*

- **The Granted Power:** When you became Bound, you named what you wanted, and the contract granted it. With the GM, choose one small, defined boon your class doesn't give you. The boon might be that you can never become lost, so you always know true north and the way back. It might be that you don't need to breathe, or that you need no food or water, or that you can speak and understand any tongue, or that you always know when someone lies to you. If the boon is something you actively call on, it costs a **Stress** to use. If it is simply true of you, it works with no cost at all. Flight, if granted at all, follows the visible-wings terms above, and it is not one of these ordinary boons.
- **The Terms** *(Tithe):* Your contract carries **Terms**, written on the card, a standing obligation the counterparty exacts. You might never refuse a sincere plea, or you might be bound to collect what is owed, or to tell no lie on a holy day. Keep the Terms and the power stays clean. Break a Term, or miss a payment, and the contract collects. The GM gains a **Fear**. Until you make restitution, which is a defined act the GM sets, your Granted Power costs a **Hit Point** instead of Stress, and the Whisper begins. While you owe that debt, whenever you roll with Fear the GM may voice an intrusive thought, or a plausible lie the counterparty wants you to believe. You may act against it freely. Shutting it out costs a Stress, and the debt compounds each time you fall further behind. The Whisper suggests and never commands, and the counterparty cannot take control of you. You choose every action.
- **The Pledge:** At signing you also named what you would give when the contract ends, a part of yourself: an eye, a leg, your capacity for fear, your memory of home. It is written on the card, and you keep it while the contract runs. When a term-limited contract comes due, the counterparty releases you and collects the Pledge, and you become one of the Unbound, whom people colloquially call the Hollow.

## The Unbound

The Unbound are what a term-limited Bound contract leaves behind when the term ends. The counterparty releases the person and collects the Pledge, the part of themselves that person named at the signing, and they walk away unbound: free of the contract at last, and left with a hole where that piece used to be, exactly as the contract agreed. The state is Struck only, and it chains off Bound, so there is no other door into it. It is rare, about 1.5 percent of all people, and that figure sits inside Bound's 5 percent contract-lane, because only the Bound whose contracts were term-limited, rather than open-ended, ever arrive here. An open-ended contract does not come due, and people still call them the Hollow, for the absence the freedom cost.

The Condition takes its title from the release, because the contract is over and the person is unbound, while common speech fixes on the cost and calls them the Hollow. Both names are true, one of them a freedom and the other an absence.

The Pledge was chosen at the signing, before any of the power had been spent, so you knew what you would owe. It might be an eye, or a leg, or your capacity for fear, or your memory of home. The loss is grief and gift at the same time, because the hole where that part was is also an immunity to whatever used to come in through it. People are not afraid of the Unbound so much as unsettled by the absence in them, and the standing that follows is pity mixed with unease. Where the Long-Lived have to keep learning if they want to stay whole, the Unbound have to keep connecting if they want to stay quiet. The fuel runs the other way.

An Unbound has no colour, because the state chains off Bound and the Tree never named them, and you cannot start a character here. The Motherfaith pities a person who paid with a part of themselves, while the Fair Hand hears a closed account, the debt paid in full so the person walks away. The Watching names a seeing of what is missing, and the Old Ways still count them as kin of the oath-door rather than as someone the Tree refused. Nothing institutional comes next, no guild and no school, so family keeps an Unbound person company through a long rest, or a hired companion does. The Book-Hands settled the contract in the year the person became Bound, and they do not help with the loss afterwards.

You become Unbound only after you have played through an actual Bound contract, or arranged one with the GM before the first session: what the contract was, what you Pledged, and the moment the term came due and the Pledge was collected. The Absence has to be a specific loss the table has agreed, with real weight, and it is not a free immunity you pick off a list, so talk it through with the GM before anyone writes it on a sheet.

### Rules

> *You paid what you promised. Something is missing now, and the missing place is quiet, and strong.*

- **The Absence:** Name what the contract took, which is the Pledge from Bound. You lose it permanently, in the fiction and, where the loss matters, on the sheet. The hole is also a narrow immunity. You are immune to effects that would mark Stress, or that would grant the GM Fear, when those effects work through the capacity you gave up. You are not immune to every roll that sits in the same broad lane. Agree the details with the GM at the moment you become Hollow. The loss falls in one of three lanes, and the lanes do not borrow from each other.
  - **A capacity of the mind** (fear, grief, love, the ability to lie, or the memory of a person or a place) becomes a lack in the fiction, and the immunity covers only the Stress-marking or Fear-granting effects that use that particular capacity. If you gave up fear, a terror that would mark Stress does not mark it. You still have to treat a scared crowd as a crowd. If you gave up deceit, you cannot lie, and a lie that would have marked Stress on you if it landed does not mark Stress. You do not automatically know every lie you hear.
  - **A sense of the body** (an eye, hearing, or taste) is a fictional loss with a sharp edge in play. Give up an eye and you have **disadvantage** on rolls that need depth or distance. You are immune to Stress-marking effects that require the sense you lost, including the Stilling Gaze aimed at you and a blinding light that would otherwise mark Stress. An illusion you have to see still works on you, unless the illusion would mark Stress through that missing sense.
  - **A part of the body** (a leg, or a hand) is a fictional loss plus a standing penalty the group accepts, offset by immunity to Stress-marking pain and to other Stress-marking effects that work through that part. In the fiction there is often a prosthetic, or a Condition-crafted replacement.
- **The Quiet** *(Tithe):* Place **3 Quiet tokens** on this card, and begin play with them filled. Spend a Quiet token to act through the hole. You take an action the missing thing would have stopped, or you gain **advantage** on a roll where the empty place makes the hard thing easy. You still mark Stress from effects that sit outside your Absence immunity. Quiet is not a second immunity to the same lane, and it is not the Returned clause that leaves a Returned unshaken by corpses, gore, and deathly places. If you pledged fear, Quiet does not let you automatically pass a charnel house. At a long rest, refill all the tokens if you rest with at least one other person who knows your name and shares the meal or the watch. If you are alone, or among strangers who do not know you, the tokens stay where they are. At 0 tokens the pull turns loud. While the card is empty, whenever a bargain, a favor, or an offer of power is on the table, the GM may present that offer as unusually reasonable, and refusing to reach for it costs a **Stress**. The offer is a suggestion and never a compulsion, so you can always say no, and saying no aches. Taking the offer is how an Unbound becomes Bound again, and how a Bound moves toward the Given-Over.

## Far-Voiced

A Far-Voiced carries a voice with total emotional fidelity. It travels exactly as far as they mean it to travel, and it arrives exactly as true as they mean it, with no extra feeling poured in and none shaved off. The siren of the old stories sings the unwilling onto the rocks. This is not that song, and a Far-Voiced does not override anyone's will. The danger runs the other direction, into the singer, when a feeling they have been holding finally comes out of them whether they chose the moment or not. Towns build their relay-singers, their sworn witnesses, and the performers who fill a hall out of people who can do this.

Distance does not drown the voice, and neither does a storm, the noise of a battle, or water over the singer's head. The voice also never says less than the Far-Voiced means. That is why the signal network runs on them. Relay-singers take posts along coasts, roads, and border-towns, and a warning, an alarm, or an order in that voice travels faster and farther than any horn or drum. Courts and treaties want them as witnesses for a reason that cuts against the glamour. The moment a Far-Voiced is under pressure, their own voice betrays the feeling they actually hold, so a sworn word from them is trusted precisely because they are bad at lying once the strain is on, where many Bound can instead hear that someone else is lying. Concert halls want the same fidelity, because emotion delivered so the room can hear it, and delivered exactly, is the whole of the art. They are revered rather than feared, and a Far-Voiced who has not mastered the channel is a genuine hazard to everyone in earshot, which is why the guild exists and why its training is what it is.

About 12 percent of all people are Far-Voiced, and about eleven parts in twelve of them were Given, roughly 11 percent of everyone. They drew the sea-blue leaf at the Awakening and spent childhood in the guild, learning breath, projection, and how to vent a feeling on purpose before it vents itself, so they come to the table controlled, professional, and poised. About one part in twelve was Struck, roughly 1 percent of everyone, and the door is surviving a drowning: they were pulled under, breath and voice both stopped, and both came back changed, raw and untrained, with the undertow already pulling before anyone had taught them how to swim it. It is the same Condition the guild trains, and it is not the same person at the table. There is no leaf and no ceremony, so the people watching call it a miracle, or call it faintly uncanny, depending on who is watching, and a Struck Far-Voiced usually needs a guild's help quickly, before they learn in public what happens when the tokens run out.

When the leaf falls it is sea-blue, and people who have stood at a ceremony know that colour, though a Far-Voiced who came up from a drowning has no leaf at all. The Motherfaith hears a gift of voice, and if the person came up out of the water instead of receiving a leaf, the faith holds a quiet receiving-rite after the fact, the Wide-Hand saying she met them there and the Narrow-Hand uneasy. The Watching says the Tree, or the threshold itself, saw a voice that would carry, and the Old Ways do not treat a drowning as a late gift from the Tree, so they name that person kin of the drowning-door. The Hall-Keepers keep the hour in which a Far-Voiced may feel on purpose, and they stamp the Voice-ticket, the licence that lets that voice be used where other people can hear it. A newly Struck Far-Voiced is sent to the Intake for help, before anyone has to restrain them.

The pleasant face of the Tithe is why the jobs exist. Deliberate, controlled venting, in a performance, a ritual song, or a relay-call, is how a working Far-Voiced manages the channel, so the careers that need a big voice are also the careers that keep one healthy. The guild drills the breath, schedules the performance, and holds people to the discipline of singing it out before a shift ends, because an un-vented Far-Voiced is not merely unpleasant to stand beside. When the feeling breaks out anyway, that outburst is a Cry, and a Cry in a crowded hall or a command tent is a real and documented hazard, the risk sitting under the reverence.

### Rules

> *Your voice carries exactly as far, and exactly as true, as you mean it to.*

- **The Carrying Voice:** Your voice is never lost to distance, weather, noise, or water, and it lands with the emotional weight you intend. Spend a **Hope** to be heard clearly and understood by everyone you intend, across any distance or chaos in the scene, or to land a rally, warning, plea, or calming word with exact emotional force, and gain **advantage** on that roll.
- **The Undertow** *(Tithe):* Place **3 Breath tokens** on this card. The clock is triggered by the scene, not drained by rest. A long rest leaves the tokens where they are. When you mark Stress from feeling, or when the GM names grief, terror, rage, or joy as what is in you this scene, you must vent it before the scene ends. Sing, cry out, laugh, weep, something the room can hear. If you do not, remove a token.
  - **2 tokens:** nothing about the voice has changed yet.
  - **1 token:** the undertow pulls. Your voice starts leaking whatever you are actually feeling underneath the words you chose, and you have **disadvantage** on rolls to persuade, deceive, or perform a feeling you do not hold.
  - **0 tokens:** the next strong emotion you feel escapes as an involuntary **Cry**. Everyone within earshot marks a **Stress** as your raw feeling crashes into them, or the GM may instead gain a **Fear** when the scene is a crowd or already hostile. The Cry empties you. Mark a Stress yourself, then refill to **2 tokens**. It is out now, one way or another.

The Cry costs the listeners a Stress and costs the Far-Voiced a Stress as well, and it never compels an action, a belief, or a roll. What hits the room is an emotional blow rather than a command, so the listeners feel the feeling and still choose what they do.

## The Stilled

One of the Stilled can hold a moment still. A wound that is bleeding stops bleeding, a poison that is spreading stops where it is, and a blade already swinging hangs in the air a beat longer than the arm meant. The gorgon of the old stories turns a person to stone and leaves them there. That curse, inflicted on someone else as the point of the power, is not how this works. The stillness has to be paid for out of the Stilled's own body, and if they do not keep moving, the stillness starts to settle into them instead. Towns put them to work as peacekeepers, as surgeons, and as the menders who have to hold a struggling patient steady under the knife.

Peacekeepers want that gaze on a riot line, because a brawl that has been stilled is a brawl that did not turn into a massacre, and surgeons and medics want it in the theatre, because a patient held perfectly still is a patient who survives the cut. They are feared, and they are regulated by a guild, rather than simply revered. About 7 percent of all people live this way. The same gaze that stops a wound can, if someone misuses it, hold a person helpless in a fight, so a licence and a strict code decide who may still a thing, and for what reason. The snake-hair of the old folklore survives as something you can see, not as a rule on the card. The hair stirs, faintly, when something nearby has gone dangerously still, a held breath or a nocked arrow or a predator about to spring, and the people who do this work have learned to read that tell.

The two paths do not produce the same person. About six parts in seven are Given, roughly 6 percent of all people. They drew the pale-stone leaf, marble-white, at the Awakening, and they trained young under the guild, so they know how much stillness they can spend and how to keep moving until the spent stillness sheds, and they come to the table composed and professional. About one part in seven is Struck, roughly 1 percent of all people, by surviving being turned to stone. They were held rigid and unbreathing for however long the reversal took, and they came back with the stillness living in them, unpracticed and closer to the surface than any guild training would have left it. The stone came from a trap, a curse, or an accident, and then they returned to flesh, however the reversal was managed. There is no leaf and no ceremony, so the people watching call it a miracle, or a mercy, or a little unnerving, depending on who did the reversing. A Struck member of the Stilled usually needs a guild's help quickly, before an unmanaged stillness teaches them, in public, what a seizing feels like, and that collected stillness is the Grey.

The leaf is pale stone, marble-white, a colour people know and fear, so the warden names it carefully when it falls, and a Struck Stilled has no leaf at all. The Motherfaith hears a hard gift, and the gift is licensed because the same gaze that saves a life can leave a person helpless in a fight. The Watching names a seeing of stillness that the town then made civic, a witness rather than a mother's pick, and a Struck Stilled is a threshold the Motherfaith still argues over. The Old Ways take them back to the door that made them and name them kin. From there they go to the Stillers, the guild that keeps the strictest ticket, the infirmary, and the drill-yard, and a newly Struck Stilled goes to the Intake.

The Stilled drill hard, they spar often, and they stay physically busy almost as a matter of who they understand themselves to be, because every use of the gaze banks a little Grey. The guilds build constant motion into the job itself, patrols and practice bouts and dance used as therapy, so the Grey gets worked back out before it can settle. An unlicensed Stilled, or a careless one, who lets the Grey fill is a documented hazard, to themselves first of all. A doorknob turned to stone is one kind of accident. A hand gone still on a weapon is another, which is why the guild's oversight is strict rather than a ceremony people attend and forget.

### Rules

> *You can hold a moment still. The stillness has to come from somewhere, and if you don't move it along, it settles into you.*

- **The Stilling Gaze:** You may still a wound, a spreading harm, or a struggle you can see. Nothing you still in this way is harmed by the stilling itself. Each use adds a Grey token, as The Grey describes below. You can keep one stilling at a time, and a new use lets the last one go.
  - A harm you can see, whether bleeding, poison, or disease, needs no roll. The harm pauses until you look away, use the Gaze on something else, or the scene ends.
  - A willing creature, or a creature that is down, needs no roll. They gain **Stilled**, described below.
  - An unwilling creature requires an attack roll using Instinct or Presence. On a success they gain Stilled. A miss still adds a Grey token, because the stillness has already left you.
- **Stilled** *(a special condition, not Restrained):* A Stilled creature cannot take actions or reactions. They can still speak, they can still perceive, and they can still be targeted. They are not Restrained. A Restrained creature would stay in place, while a Stilled creature can be moved by other people, and the effect does not by itself stop them from talking or from seeing. They may mark a **Stress** to clear Stilled at the moment they would act. The effect also clears if you look away, if you turn the Gaze onto another target, or if the scene ends. The same creature cannot gain Stilled twice at the same time.
- **The Grey** *(Tithe):* The stillness you spend collects in you, and if you do not work it back out, it settles. Place Grey tokens on this card, starting at **0**. This clock fills, and it does not drain. Add a Grey token each time you use the Stilling Gaze, and add one when you take a long rest that did not include vigorous exertion: a downtime move spent on labor, a spar, a drill, or a fight. Clear one token at the end of a scene in which you marked Stress from physical effort, or in which you dealt damage or took it.
  - **1 token:** nothing has stiffened yet.
  - **2 tokens:** the grey creeps in. Your joints stiffen, and you have **disadvantage** on Agility rolls and on rolls to evade.
  - **3 tokens (full):** the stillness seizes. The next time you use the Stilling Gaze, or the next time you take damage, one of your own limbs locks stone-still and stays useless until it is tended and rested off. You describe which limb: an arm, a leg, your jaw. The GM may instead gain a **Fear**, and something small and nearby, a doorknob or a cup or a patch of ground, turns permanently to stone, collateral of the seizing. Afterward, clear back to **1 token**.

The seizing only ever locks the Stilled's own body, or harmless scenery around them. It never petrifies another creature, and it never takes a player character's turn away from the player, so nobody's will bends and nobody else pays the debt.

## The Answered

An Answered is a person one of the four elements answered, and the element has gone on answering ever since. The djinn of the old stories is a wish trapped in a lamp, bound to whoever holds the vessel. There is no wish here, no master, and no bottle. At the Awakening an element spoke to this person, and it talks back to their hands the way a native tongue talks back to the person who grew up in it. The glasswrights, the shipwrights, the masons, and the makers of instruments are Answered when the craft needs that element coaxed rather than forced.

The leaf that marks them is lamp-amber, or brass, and the shade of that colour names which element answered: Ember for fire, Tide for water, Root for earth and stone, and Gale for air. From that day the element is theirs to speak to, though they do not summon it and they do not command it. They coax it the way a potter coaxes clay, and they never conjure it the way a story conjures a spirit. That is why the guilds are built on the coaxing. Ember-Answered glasswrights and smiths do not need a furnace at full heat, and Tide-Answered shipwrights and dyers can bend a hull's planks or set a colour without steam and without mordant. Root-Answered masons and potters coax stone and clay with no chisel and no wheel, while Gale-Answered sailmakers and instrument-makers cut the cloth and voice the instrument with the wind helping. The path is Given only. An element answers at the Awakening or it does not, and there is no bargain in the leaf, whereas a Bound becomes Bound by swearing to a counterparty and an Answered did not swear.

Their standing is the wildcard, and they are about 10 percent of people, because an Answered is never one kind of neighbor. Which element answered, and what temperament arrived with it, changes from person to person. An even-tempered Root-Answered potter and a short-fused Ember-Answered smith share the name and very little else. Some guilds prize them above any other artisan, while some households are wary of keeping, under their roof, a person whose element runs hot. The leaf tells you the element and does not tell you the temperament, which you learn only by meeting them. That is why "wildcard" sticks as a label.

The Motherfaith hears an element that answered because the gift fitted a craft. The Watching says the Tree saw which medium would talk back, and calls that a witness rather than a mother's pick. The four element-guilds, one charter each for Ember, Tide, Root, and Gale, license an Answered to one standard and give them steady work, so the element has somewhere to go and does not break loose in public.

Real, purposeful use of the element keeps an Answered steady, so the apprenticeships run long, and the workshops exist to give them work. Just as often, the outlet is a watch rotation, an escort, or a firefighting crew. One of the Answered kept idle, grieving, or benched too long is a genuine hazard to be near, and every guild hall has a story about the time someone's untended element finally let go in a crowded room.

### Rules

> *An element answered you at the Awakening. It still does, and it still expects to be spoken to.*

Choose your element at creation: **Ember**, **Tide**, **Root**, or **Gale**. The choice sets the Reach in the table, and it sets what you are allowed to ask.

- **The Answering Hand** *(boon):* Your element answers your hands without a tool, a forge, or force behind it. Glass blows with no furnace, a hull bends with no steam, stone is coaxed with no chisel, and you have **advantage** on any action roll to shape, repair, wield, or read something made of your element. In the moment it answers too. Spend a **Hope** to use your element's **Reach**, in the table below. The Reach is always there to spend. What wears down, if you go too long without speaking, is the Tithe.
- **Spoken, not commanded:** Once per long rest, you may ask your element one question it can feel through its own medium. Ember can tell you about heat, fuel, and what would burn. Tide can tell you about flow, depth, and what the water has touched. Root can tell you about weight, fault, and what the stone holds. Gale can tell you about pressure, scent, and what the air is carrying. The GM gives you a clear impression, not a map you could navigate by. Your element does not obey another creature's order, and it does not take their bargain. If someone tries to treat the element as theirs, it answers them with silence. You may spend a Hope to turn that refusal into a Reach effect aimed at the interruption: the fire flares, the stone holds, or the water pulls away.
- **The Untended Element** *(Tithe):* Place **3 tokens** on this card. At a long rest, refill all of them if you have spent Hope on Reach since your last long rest. Otherwise remove a token. Asking the element a question does not refill the clock. Spending Hope on Reach does. At 2 tokens nothing has changed, and at 1 token and at 0 the element starts speaking for itself, which the table describes.

| Element | **Reach** *(spend a Hope)* | **1 token** (restless) | **0 tokens** (it lets go) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ember** *(fire)* | Send a burst of fire at a target within **Very Close** range. Make an attack roll (Instinct or Strength). On a success, deal **magic damage in d6s equal to your Proficiency** and the target is temporarily **On Fire**. | You run **hot:** disadvantage on action rolls needing a steady hand or a cool head (stealth, precision, composure). | You **flare:** mark a **Stress**; searing heat rolls off you and anyone within **Very Close** must give ground or work through it (scorched cloth, singed hair; no direct damage). Refill to 2. |
| **Tide** *(water)* | Move like water: breathe and swim freely as though water were air, flow out of a fall or a grapple unharmed, **or** slick the ground within **Very Close**. Creatures there must succeed on an **Agility Roll** to move out of it. | You run **thin:** disadvantage on action rolls needing brute force or holding your ground. | You **flood:** mark a **Stress**; water and mud spread under you (**Very Close**), and you *and* everyone in it must make an **Agility Roll** to move out until it drains. Refill to 2. |
| **Root** *(earth/stone)* | Turn skin to bark and stone until the spotlight leaves you: **reduce the severity of the next damage you take by one threshold**, and gain **advantage** on rolls to resist being moved, knocked down, or Restrained. | You run **slow:** disadvantage on Agility rolls. | You **calcify:** mark a **Stress**; you become **Restrained** by your own stiffening body until you or an ally clears it with a successful action roll. Refill to 2. |

Every consequence at 0 tokens lands on the Answered's own body, or on the air, the water, and the ground immediately around them. It never targets another creature directly, it never harms another creature directly, and it never bends anyone's will. Other people keep their own will, and nobody else pays the debt.

## The Taken-In

A Taken-In is a person the wild took partway into itself and then gave back changed. Bark shows at the knuckles, moss grows in the hair, and somewhere on the body there is a spread of antler or a knot of root, and green things lean toward them when they stand still. The leshy of the old stories is a trickster that leads travelers in circles until they die. There is no haunting here, and no luring. The wild lives in them now, and it answers them, which is why the hard land gets worked at all. Farms, forests, herds, and the wardens of a frontier run on people the wild has already taken in.

One of the Taken-In can coax a thing into growing, read the wild, and move through it without being seen and without losing the way. That is why the frontier runs on them. A Taken-In steward makes thin soil yield, keeps a herd alive through a bad winter, walks a forest without ever losing north, and knows a day ahead which way the weather will turn. They are respected, and they are looked down on, and they are about 13 percent of all people. Cities and their guilds need the grain, the timber, and the wardens the Taken-In provide, and the cities eat well because of that work, but a Taken-In comes to town dirt-handed and backcountry, and no longer quite human to look at. The bark and the antler unsettle people who have never left the paved districts. Farm country that is prospering reveres them. Polite society in the city finds them a little coarse, a little uncanny, and keeps them at arm's length.

Both paths lead here, and they do not make the same person. About twelve parts in thirteen are Given, roughly 12 percent of all people. They drew the copper-green leaf, the metallic green called verdigris, at the Awakening, and they grew up inside the change. A farm child or a forest child is comfortable in that half-wild skin, because the change came on gently and everyone around them already knew what it was. About one part in thirteen is Struck, roughly 1 percent of all people. They were lost in the deep wild, and they were dying there, days without a path and ready to lie down, and the wild took them in rather than let them die, remaking them partway so they would live. They come back more uncanny than a Given cousin, more overgrown, and quietly unsure whether they walked out as a person or as something the forest decided to send.

The leaf is copper-green, and people know it only if they have learned not to confuse it with the hug. The Tree's own summer-green is the colour of a child it holds and does not name, and verdigris is not that colour, while a Struck Taken-In has no leaf at all. The Motherfaith hears a gift of green, which people in the city call a little coarse. The Old Ways call them the grove's own children, the land making its own people whether or not the Tree had a hand in it, and a Struck Taken-In is, to them, the oldest door rather than a late embarrassment. The Watching names a seeing, and does not call it a taking-in performed by a mother. What happens next is civic work rather than a college. The towns treat that green as a utility they use, not as a school that trains it, and the Greens-Keepers, who keep living land inside a stone town so a Taken-In does not wilt on a parlour floor, will hire the skill for the lot while refusing a Taken-In a seat at the high table.

They also live in the city, and that life is not a contradiction of the frontier one. A copper-green leaf falls at a city Tree, where family and a hearth-witness, someone who stood when that child Turned, keep the child, and leaving the city would mean leaving the people who saw it. Grain-hands, seasonal Taken-In who came in with the harvest, winter on the quay, and not all of them go home. A rare Struck door can open in a walled grove, a city lot at night, or a river-garden after a flood. City pay covers what it costs to keep the Tithe and does not cover what it costs to leave, which is the gap that makes them easy marks for the Slide, the people who rent a week of green when the upkeep is about to fail in public and who make the failing last. Polite districts keep them at arm's length for the bark, the antler, and the dirt. Their share of the world is about 13 percent, a stone city has fewer of them, and most of those work on the greens. The fuller account of the money sits with the economy of the Tithe.

The work is the upkeep. A life spent on soil, among things that are growing, under open sky, keeps the green in them fed without anyone having to think about it. The Tithe bites when a Taken-In is pulled off the land: a long voyage by sea, a stretch in a gaol of stone, a season stuck inside a walled city on a lord's business. Old custom on the frontier is to send them home before they wilt. A city host who must keep one indoors learns to fill the room with potted green and to open the windows, a courtesy that is also how you keep your guest from going to seed on the parlour floor.

### Rules

> *The wild took you in and gave you back green. It still lives in you, and it needs living things around it.*

- **The Green Word** *(boon):* Growing things heed you. You have **advantage** on action rolls to forage, track, navigate wild land, calm or read an animal, or find your way, and you never become lost in a natural place. In the moment, the green answers, and each of these costs a Hope.
  - Spend a **Hope** and roots, vines, and brush erupt from any natural ground within **Close** range. Make an attack roll with Instinct or Strength against a target there. On a success, deal **physical damage in d6s equal to your Proficiency**, and the target is temporarily **Restrained**.
  - Spend a **Hope**, while you are in any natural cover, brush or trees or tall grass or roots or soil, to draw that cover around you and become **Hidden**.
  - Spend a **Hope** to force a sudden useful growth: a branch that bridges a gap, a screen of thicket, a ladder of root, a burst of forage that is already ripe. The GM sets how large the growth can be.
- **The Rooting** *(Tithe):* Place **3 Green tokens** on this card. At a long rest, refill all of them if you can touch a living plant where you rest, whether that plant is soil, a tree, a field, or a green in a pot. Otherwise remove a token. The test is yes or no. Open sky with no plant in reach does not count, and a window-box does.
  - **2 tokens:** nothing has wilted.
  - **1 token:** you wilt. Your green browns and sheds, and you have **disadvantage** on action rolls to resist fear, despair, or exhaustion, because the wild in you is starving and it drags at you.
  - **0 tokens:** you go to seed. Mark a **Stress**. You are **Vulnerable** until you next touch living land, and the wild reaches for any living thing it can find. Weeds crack the flagstones, a beam sprouts leaves, roots buckle the floor, and the room around you goes riotously overgrown. The GM may instead gain a **Fear** if the scene is already tense. Reach living land and refill to **2 tokens**.

The growth that comes when you go to seed erupts from the ground and from the structures around the Taken-In, and it stays there. It never entangles another creature, it never targets one, and it never harms one directly, and it never bends anyone's will. Each person keeps their own will, and nobody else pays the debt.

## Phoenix

A Phoenix is a person who burns down to ash and rises again, but not unchanged and not forever. The bird in the old stories is reborn eternal, and that rebirth costs nothing. Each death here takes a piece of who they were, and each life sits a little further from the first. Where the Long-Lived never die and the Returned refuse to stay dead, a Phoenix does die, completely, and comes back a new turn of the same soul, carrying fewer and fewer of the old one's memories. There is only ever one of them at a time in the entire world, so almost nobody has met them, and the world knows that one person through stories.

There is exactly one Phoenix in the world at any moment, and never two. When the current one finally ends, having spent their last life, the Tree gives the white-fire leaf, the colour of opal, to a single child, somewhere, and the next one begins. A Phoenix is not rare the way a good harvest is rare, because they are singular, the one living person the whole world's phoenix-stories are currently about. The path is Given only, and no threshold can Strike one into being. The Tree names them once a generation or once an age, and then not again until they are gone. Their share of the people is less than 0.1 percent, that trace is still only the one life the stories are about, and the standing is legendary.

Every other Condition wears a plain title, the Long-Lived, the Returned, the Far-Voiced, because those becomings pass unremarked in a crowd, and the quiet name is their camouflage. The Phoenix cannot pass and cannot hide, and the whole world already knows the story, so they are called what the stories call them, and the naming itself marks them as singular.

That singularity is why a whole city can form around them. In a single town they may be, all at once, revered, worshipped, hunted, and hated. The people who revere them parade a living miracle on holy days and take that parade as proof the Leaf-Mother is real, while the people who worship them believe that touching the Phoenix, or touching the ashes, blesses or heals. Hunters want the ashes, the blood, the feathers, or the death itself, some because they believe a Phoenix's ash grants a life and some because they want the one irreplaceable person gone. Beside the hunters are the people who hate and fear them: skeptics who call the Phoenix a fraud, faiths who call them heresy, and rulers who cannot stand a symbol they do not control. They cannot hide in a crowd, because a Phoenix is the Phoenix and everyone has heard the stories, and that is the true weight of the Condition, heavier than any Tithe. You are the most known person alive, and the world has already decided what you mean.

A Phoenix is a spotlight Condition, and it belongs to the whole table, not to one player who liked the card. The group and the GM agree to it together, the way you would agree to a campaign about a chosen one. The Phoenix player commits to playing the legend, the constant recognition, the followers and the hunters, and the impossibility of an ordinary life, and the other four players commit to a campaign that will keep arriving at their door. If anyone at the table does not want that weight, pick another Condition, or run the Phoenix as a legend the party meets. What those other four players receive is a living legend in the party, so the town already has an opinion before anyone speaks, and the Hope slots left on the card, which are how many lives are still left, are a countdown the whole table can see. The factions come looking for the group, not only for the Phoenix. What the table gives up is stealth as the way you enter a place, and the opening in which everyone still has an ordinary job and a face nobody knows. The Phoenix player has to pass the scene and hand the spotlight across the table, and if they will not pass the scene, do not take the card. The fire on the card is a full immunity to fire, flight on wings of flame, an attack that costs a Hope, and a fourth death move. The package is strong on purpose, and the tax that pays for it is social and finite, so a table that wants the myth without that tax does not want this card. A hidden or captive Phoenix is a different seat at the same card, and what that player still gets to decide belongs to that seat rather than to the open legend.

Clergy know the white-fire, the opal, from their texts, and almost no living town has seen that leaf fall, so a warden who names it at a ceremony has just made history and a problem together. The Motherfaith hears a living miracle, and the hearing then splits into proof, heresy, a hunt, or hate, depending on who is talking. The Watching describes the same fall as a seeing rather than a mother's pick, and still sends the runner. The faiths cannot confirm what the world is by what they saw, and they can only watch. A town-warden does not guess at the next step, and sends a runner to the First Seat of the Tree-Wardens, the clergy who tend the town Trees and speak a colour aloud, and if the Seat agrees, the child cannot hide.

The wings are living fire, so a Phoenix can furl them and pass for a moment as an ordinary person, but the moment those wings open and they leave the ground, everyone watching knows what they are. Nothing is owed on an ordinary day, because this Tithe is not the hunger that keeps a Long-Lived learning, or the living plant a Taken-In has to touch, or the feeling a Far-Voiced has to let out, and the whole cost waits until death, a little less memory and one fewer life. The real upkeep is social and does not let up, because they are the only one. A Phoenix cannot walk a market unrecognized, cannot refuse the meaning other people pour onto them, cannot stop the worshippers or the hunters from finding them, and cannot grieve a rebirth in private. The stories are heavy with loss for a reason. There are Phoenixes who rose so many times they forgot their own children, and Phoenixes who ended not by a hunter's hand but by choosing to stay dead rather than come back as still less of themselves. A Phoenix late in their cycle is a person made mostly of legend, with almost none of their first life left inside.

### Rules

> *You are the only one. You will die, as you have died before, and rise, a few more times, and no more.*

**The living-fire boons** (a Phoenix simply is these, no cost unless noted):

- **Deathless to Flame:** You cannot be harmed by fire or heat of any kind. You take **no damage from fire** and can walk through, stand in, and breathe within flame unharmed. Ash and cinder are home.
- **Wings of Fire:** You have wings of living flame and you can fly. **Once per rest**, while flying, you may mark a **Stress** after an adversary attacks you to gain a **+2 bonus to your Evasion** against that attack. You can furl the wings and pass for ordinary for a moment, but the moment you fly, everyone watching knows what you are.
- **Kindle:** Spend a **Hope** to send a burst of fire at a target within **Close** range. Make an attack roll (Instinct or Strength). On a success, deal **magic damage in d6s equal to your Proficiency** and the target is temporarily **On Fire**.

**The rebirth:**

- **Rise From Ash:** When you make a death move, you gain a fourth option, **Rise From Ash**. Your body burns to nothing and reforms by the end of the next scene, standing in your own ashes. You return with **Hit Points cleared**. **Stress is not cleared.** You are **Vulnerable** until your next rest. Rebirth burns part of who you were. With the GM, name a piece of the old life that did not come back: a memory, a face, a skill, a fear, a name you used to answer to. You come back you, but lighter, and a little more like something that has done this before. The count this costs you is the Dwindling, below.
- **The Dwindling** *(Tithe):* Rebirth is finite, and paid in self. Each Rise From Ash permanently crosses out a Hope slot, as a scar does, and your lives are counted in Hope. When you would cross out your last Hope slot to Rise, you cannot. That death is your true and final one, and this turn of the soul ends for good, the same scar rule Daggerheart already uses: crossing out your last Hope slot ends the character's journey. Then, somewhere, a leaf falls for the next Phoenix. The dread is the count. Everyone at the table can see how many risings are left, and so, quietly, can the Phoenix.

Rise From Ash acts only on the Phoenix's own body and Hope. Kindle is an ordinary attack, no different from a torch or a blade in what it may target. Nothing here compels another creature's will.
