# Climate, Ecology, and Travel

The continents already carry climate-at-a-glance. This chapter is the playable weather: named water and stone a table can point at, how a week feels in each band, and how long a walk actually takes. Deep forage-and-disease ecology is still thin. Use what is here; do not invent a second map.

Speak the common-tongue name. A liturgical reconstruction is a clerk's hobby. Stance still shows: a devout mouth keeps *th* in the land-names around the water; a dock mouth does not.

## Named water, range, and rivers

| Everyday | What it is | Who says something else |
|---|---|---|
| **the Old Crossing** | the busy water between Maiethorn and Strandoren | Lestrand: *the crossing.* Vaethorn: *the waiting-water.* Sailors: *the Crossing.* |
| **the West Water** | the wider sea between Strandoren and Heskoren | already the Night Shore's word |
| **the Rain-Wall** | Maiethorn's central range | Lirorn-folk: *the Thaw-Wall.* Clerks: *the Divide.* |
| **the Noon Pass** | the old high road over the Rain-Wall (lost its stone in C.Y. 233) | the courier's *noon-pass* |
| **the Shelf-gate** | the pass taken after the Break | Threnmaieth named *the new one*; Lirorn charges **Break-rate** |
| **the Core-thaw** | the river Maiethlir sits on | clerks: *snowmelt.* Watchers will not. |
| **the Well-wash** | Rain-Shadow seasonal water | dry most years; silt in a bad thaw |
| **the Rise-water** | the stream from Harrow's Green to the hamlets' ford | the Seat still says *the ford* |
| **the Chart-run** | Strandoren's interior river to Orentel's estuary | Lestrand files it as inland tickets reaching salt |

**the Old Crossing.** The oldest trade route in the world. Narrow enough that a fair-weather hull does it in a handful of days; busy enough that a late storm in Hale-month is still a Kept child. The Hinge Shore is the Motherland face. Orentel is the Shore-lands face. The Salt Walk was this water, then the Near Mile. The two continents are the Old World pair because this water made them one argument. The Grey Summer (C.Y. 171) tied the quays. The Hinge Hush (C.Y. 304) is the peace still charged as the **Hush-rate**.

**the West Water.** Wider than the Crossing. This is the Long Mile's sea-leg, the graft-pot's worse berth, the Night Shore's leftover. Heskoren's frontier coast faces it back toward Strandoren. Beyond the last capes, the storm-wall. Do not book Kumbaan on this water. A wrecked pot in a sailor's story is allowed. A taking is not.

**the Rain-Wall.** A highland spine down Maiethorn's middle. West-face weather is wet and four-seasoned; the leeward east is the Rain-Shadow. The range is why Saelthael sows at wells. The range is why Lirorn can levy a thaw. Fox-of-the-Snows and Tengu hearths sit densest on the high snow. That is terrain, not a flag. Heskoren's highland spines are a different weather. Do not hang the Rain-Wall on the frontier.

Two passes a table needs: the **Noon Pass** (the old high road; the Thaw-Break put a water-line over the ribbon-notch) and the **Shelf-gate** (the road that was left).

**Four rivers.** The **Core-thaw** is snowmelt off the Rain-Wall's west face, running toward the Old Crossing. Maiethlir sat down where the water slowed enough to hold a Hand. In a high Liren the lodging-road meets a near branch of the same water. The **Well-wash** is a seasonal run east of the range: a river in a kind Liren and a silt-line in a cruel one. Ornsael drinks a well, not this wash; the wash is why the well matters. The **Rise-water** is a low stream off Harrow's rise; downstream it is the ford the Seat used as a geography because the Seat will not learn Brenod, Vaelun, Ornath. The **Chart-run** is Strandoren's interior river-plain, running to Orentel's estuary. Coasts took by sea; inland waited on a cart and this run.

## Climate bands

Enough to know what a week feels like.

| Band | Where | Weather |
|---|---|---|
| Crossing-shore | both faces of the Old Crossing | mild, wet-temperate; Hale-month storms decide a Salt Walk |
| Mother-core | Maiethorn west of the Rain-Wall | four true seasons; cathedral-country; winters that make High Solstice mean something |
| Rain-Wall / Thaw-Wall | Lirorn | snow, shelves, a thaw that is a civic year |
| Rain-Shadow | Saelthael east | dry hills, wells, the Well-wash or nothing |
| Shore-lands | Strandoren coasts and the Chart-run | maritime, indented, the sea as highway |
| Night Shore | Netstrand | open-ocean; west-weather; watches for hulls that do not arrive |
| Sundered upland | Heskoren | cooler, wilder, harder winters; civilization is a map of surviving Trees |
| Storm-wall / Kumbaan | the Sundering Isle | mild wet under cloud; bright nights; the wall is the climate |

## Maiethorn

West of the Rain-Wall the Motherland keeps four true seasons. Winters are real. High Solstice means something because the year has been cold. Grafts take in cathedral-country and in the Motherwood; a cutting wants living earth and a drink, not a miracle. Stilled infirmaries and Returned plague-work cluster in the rich core because that is where the chairs and the endowments are. Tithe-green is commons and lot, not a desert problem.

On the range, last year's snow is this year's civic year. Lirorn levies the thaw. A thaw-week can turn the Shelf-gate into a lake. The Thaw-Break closed the west-road for two summers; people still remember which notch holds the water-line.

East of the Wall the land is wells, goats, and the Well-wash. A Taken-In Tithe is cheap on terrace-green and expensive if the well is dropping. Two-Bodied range the dry hills at night. Returned work sits downwind of the drink. A travelling stiller is a pass-story, not a college.

On the Old Crossing face the weather is the berth. Hale-month storms decide a Salt Walk. Far-Voiced are dock infrastructure. Selkie hearths are common on the quays. The Hinge Shore classifies the hull before anyone across the water prices it.

## Strandoren

Maritime, indented, the sea as highway. Coasts took the wood first; the Chart-run carried it inland. Food on the Salt Quay is this plain, the Crossing's catch, and whatever the hinge pays. Tithe-provision here is a market: you buy a hall-hour, a lot-night, a novelty page. The Taken-In who live in stone cities are a working minority on lots, orchards, and river-gardens — arm's length is manners, not an empty lot.

The Night Shore watches for hulls that do not arrive. West-weather is a local climate. Chart-crews strip what the sea gives. Prestige and necessity use the same water and do not pay the same rate.

A Hale-month storm on the Old Crossing can turn a Salt Walk into a missed year. A Chart-run delay can make a Grey-verso due. The table of days is weather, not a promise.

## Heskoren

Cooler, wilder, harder winters. Civilization is a map of surviving Trees. Young grafts face wind, thin soil, and a queue written in the Motherwood. A Taken-In Tithe is the land itself, honoured, until a bad winter. Two-Bodied range against gap-cats and weather, not against a second werewolf. Returned work the far sheds. Stilled medicine is one tired stiller or a rope.

The live front is Saelvaeth's luck: Harrow's Green, the Rise-water, the hamlets' ford. Vaethorn waits in a vale that cannot see that canopy and does not want to. Ornled has the same thin wood and no congregation. Between mile-shrines the next stone is a rumour. Empty road-houses still make a bed. The leftover walk is people who did not go home.

## Kumbaan

Mild wet under cloud. Bright nights. The wall is the climate. There is no Tree-ecology. A slip planted here would die. Dead wood is not a Tree. Food is terrace, shallows when the wall is kind, and whatever unseen hands fetch. Fuel is peat and what a wreck sometimes gives the hall.

You do not book the isle. A thinning is a moon shore-sitters know, not a ferry. Most hulls wreck, turn, or do not return. If you live: sand or not; a shore-sitter decides *alive*; walked uphill; first night as guest at a hall; a table-keeper decides *kin* at the second sitting. The second sitting is how you belong **on the isle**. It does not travel back to a mainland watch. Playable square: Ndenjoo, the Feeding Hill.

## Travel times

The unit is a **day's walk** — the interval a mile-shrine already marks. A horse does not invent a different world; it buys you the same road with fewer blisters. A ship buys you the water the walks already named.

| Walk | What it was | Fair-weather band |
|---|---|---|
| **Near Mile** | Maiethorn inland to Thaeloren | **3–12 days** |
| **Salt Walk** | Old Crossing, then the Near Mile | **3–5 days' sail** plus the inland days; typically **1–3 weeks** |
| **Long Mile** | West Water, then Heskoren inland | **6–12 weeks** for a coast-to-grove attempt |

Kumbaan is not on this table. No walk crossed the storm-wall.

A Hale-month storm on the Old Crossing can turn a Salt Walk into a missed year. A thaw-week on the Rain-Wall can turn the Shelf-gate into a lake. The table is weather, not a promise.

The surviving Kept regime is not a sixteenth flag and not a replacement of the Hinge Shore. The Closed Seat was an origin-gate on the grove. The Retreat closed walls one day's walk from the Motherwood, inside Orenbren lodging-country. That is the Inner Close. The Hinge Shore is the hinge of a different war. Leave both jobs where they are.
