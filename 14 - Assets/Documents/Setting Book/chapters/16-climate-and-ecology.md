# Climate, Ecology, and Travel

Maiethorn is the old heart, Strandoren the shore continent, Heskoren the outer land, and Kumbaan the storm-walled isle. This chapter names the water and the stone, says how a week feels in each band, and says how long a walk takes. Say the everyday name. A devout speaker keeps the *th* in the land-names around the water. On a dock, that *th* wears off. Clerks enjoy the long old form. A Cut-year, written C.Y., is a year counted from the First Cut, the first successful planting. The Ages chapter keeps that count.

## Named water, range, and rivers

| Everyday | What it is | Who says something else |
|---|---|---|
| **the Old Crossing** | the busy water between Maiethorn and Strandoren | Lestrand: *the crossing.* Vaethorn: *the waiting-water.* Sailors: *the Crossing.* |
| **the West Water** | the wider sea between Strandoren and Heskoren | the Night Shore's own name for this sea |
| **the Rain-Wall** | Maiethorn's central range | Lirorn-folk: *the Thaw-Wall.* Clerks: *the Divide.* |
| **the Noon Pass** | the old high road over the Rain-Wall (lost its stone in C.Y. 233) | the courier's *noon-pass* |
| **the Shelf-gate** | the pass taken after the Break | Threnmaieth named *the new one*; Lirorn charges **Break-rate** |
| **the Core-thaw** | the river Maiethlir sits on | clerks: *snowmelt.* Watchers will not. |
| **the Well-wash** | Rain-Shadow seasonal water | dry most years; silt in a bad thaw |
| **the Rise-water** | the stream from Harrow's Green to the hamlets' ford | the Seat says *the ford* |
| **the Chart-run** | Strandoren's interior river to Orentel's estuary | Lestrand: the inland run that brings goods to the salt quay |

**the Old Crossing.** The oldest trade route in the world. Narrow enough that a fair-weather hull does it in a handful of days, and busy enough that a late storm in Hale-month can still keep a child from the Turning. The Hinge Shore is Maiethorn's face. Orentel is Strandoren's face. The Salt Walk was the way Strandoren reached a Turning: this water, then the Near Mile, the inland walk on Maiethorn. Maiethorn and Strandoren are the Old World pair because this water joins them. The Grey Summer (C.Y. 171) tied the quays. The Hinge Hush (C.Y. 304) is the peace still charged as the **Hush-rate**.

**the West Water.** Wider than the Crossing. This sea is the water-leg of the Long Mile, the weeks-long road from Heskoren toward a Tree. A graft is a cutting of living wood planted so a town can Turn. A pot of those cuttings has a harder berth here than on the Old Crossing. The Night Shore faces this water and sells the far run. Heskoren's frontier coast faces the same water back toward Strandoren. Beyond the last capes is the storm-wall. No house on this water sells a passage to Kumbaan. Sailors tell of a pot that went into the storm and was wrecked. No graft took.

**the Rain-Wall.** A highland spine down Maiethorn's middle. West-face weather is wet and four-seasoned; the leeward east is the Rain-Shadow. The range is why Saelthael sows at wells. The range is why Lirorn can levy a thaw. Fox-of-the-Snows and Tengu hearths sit densest on the high snow. A hearth is the home-ground of a people, and the snow is why these hearths are thickest here. Heskoren's highland spines are a different weather, on the outer continent. The Rain-Wall is Maiethorn's range.

Two passes cross the range. The **Noon Pass** is the old high road. It lost its stone in C.Y. 233, and the Thaw-Break left a water-line over the ribbon-notch. The **Shelf-gate** is the pass taken after the Break, and it is the road people use now.

**Four rivers.** The **Core-thaw** is snowmelt off the Rain-Wall's west face, running toward the Old Crossing. Maiethlir sat down where the water slowed enough to hold a Turning Tree. A Turning Tree is a town's planted Tree, the one children Turn under. Seinbrun stands off that water. In a high Liren, the thaw-month, the lodging-road meets a near branch of the same water. The **Well-wash** is a seasonal run east of the range: a river in a kind Liren and a silt-line in a cruel one. **Larbril** is where the west road meets that wash, short of the pass. **Ornsael** drinks a well, farther into the dry, with open country between it and Larbril. The town sits inland. The wash is why the well matters. The **Rise-water** is a low stream off Harrow's rise. Downstream it becomes the hamlets' ford, the crossing of Brenod, Vaelun, and Ornath. The Seat calls that crossing the ford. **Natai**, the march-town, stands on other ground, west of Harrow's Green, between Sanbreo and that grove. The **Chart-run** is Strandoren's interior river-plain, running to Orentel's estuary. Lunbra sits on the wealthy stretch of it. The coasts received the wood by sea. Inland towns waited on a cart and on this river.

## Climate bands

A week in each band feels like this.

| Band | Where | Weather |
|---|---|---|
| Crossing-shore | both faces of the Old Crossing | mild, wet-temperate; Hale-month storms decide a Salt Walk |
| Mother-core | Maiethorn west of the Rain-Wall | four true seasons; old sacred country; winters that make High Solstice mean something |
| Rain-Wall / Thaw-Wall | Lirorn | snow, shelves, a thaw that is a civic year |
| Rain-Shadow | Saelthael east | dry hills, wells, the Well-wash or nothing |
| Shore-lands | Strandoren coasts and the Chart-run | maritime, indented, the sea as highway |
| Night Shore | Netstrand | open-ocean; west-weather; watches for hulls that do not arrive |
| Sundered upland | Heskoren | cooler, wilder, harder winters; civilization is a map of surviving Trees |
| Storm-wall / Kumbaan | the Sundering Isle | mild wet under cloud; bright nights; the wall is the climate |

## Maiethorn

West of the Rain-Wall the Motherland keeps four true seasons. Winters are real. High Solstice means something because the year has been cold. Grafts take in the old sacred country and in the Motherwood. A cutting needs living earth and water. Stilled infirmaries and Returned plague-work cluster in the rich core because that is where the chairs and the endowments are. A Tithe is the upkeep a Condition needs. In the core, the greens that upkeep asks for are commons and garden lots.

On the range, last year's snow is this year's civic year. Lirorn levies the thaw. A thaw-week can turn the Shelf-gate into a lake. The Thaw-Break closed the west-road for two summers; people still remember which notch holds the water-line.

East of the Wall the land is wells, goats, and the Well-wash. A Taken-In Tithe is cheap on terrace-green and expensive if the well is dropping. Two-Bodied range the dry hills at night. Returned work sits downwind of the drink. People on the pass tell of a travelling stiller. The dry country has no stillers' college.

On the Old Crossing face the weather is the berth. Hale-month storms decide a Salt Walk. Far-Voiced are dock infrastructure. Selkie hearths are common on the quays. The Hinge Shore classifies the hull before anyone across the water prices it.

## Strandoren

Maritime, indented, the sea as highway. The coasts received the wood first. The Chart-run carried it inland. Food on the Salt Quay is this plain, the Crossing's catch, and whatever the hinge pays. Tithe-provision here is a market: you buy a hall-hour, a lot-night, a novelty page. The Taken-In who live in stone cities are a working minority on lots, orchards, and river-gardens. They work at a polite distance, and the lots are in use.

The Night Shore watches for hulls that do not arrive. West-weather is a local climate. Chart-crews strip what the sea gives. Prestige and necessity use the same water and do not pay the same rate.

A Hale-month storm on the Old Crossing can turn a Salt Walk into a missed year. A Chart-run delay can make a Grey-verso due. These spans are fair weather. A storm rewrites them.

## Heskoren

Cooler, wilder, harder winters. Civilization is a map of surviving Trees. Young grafts face wind, thin soil, and a queue written in the Motherwood. A Taken-In Tithe is the land itself, honoured, until a bad winter. Two-Bodied range against gap-cats and against the weather. Returned work the far sheds. Stilled medicine is one tired stiller or a rope.

The live front is Saelvaeth's luck. **Harrow's Green** is the grove the neighbours can see. The **Rise-water** runs from that rise to the hamlets' ford. **Natai** is the march-town west of Harrow's Green, between Sanbreo and that grove, on its own ground. The hamlets' ford is the crossing of Brenod, Vaelun, and Ornath. Harrow's square is that grove-town's own ground. The canopy the hamlets can see is Harrow's. A complaint can name Natai. Vaethorn waits in a vale with no view of that canopy, and the people there wait without looking for it. Ornled has the same thin wood and keeps no congregation. Sanbreo keeps the slate on that shore. Vaelhesk is old greens. The land is the seat. There is no city on it. Between mile-shrines the next stone is a rumour. Empty road-houses still make a bed. Some of the people still on that road did not go home.

## Kumbaan

Mild wet under cloud. Bright nights. The storm-wall is the climate: weather, current, and reef. Kumbaan never took a graft. There is no Tree-ecology. A slip planted here would die. Dead wood never became a Tree on this isle. Food is terrace, shallows when the wall is kind, and whatever unseen hands fetch. Fuel is peat and what a wreck sometimes gives the hall.

There is no ferry to the isle, and no ticket you can buy. Shore-sitters know a moon when the storm-wall thins. The thinning is weather. Nobody sells it as a sailing date. Most ships wreck, turn back, or do not return. If you live, this is the order: you are on the sand or you are not; a shore-sitter decides that you are alive; you are walked uphill; the first night you are a guest at a hall; at the second sitting, a table-keeper decides that you are kin. That second sitting makes you kin on the isle. A mainland watch will not take that sitting as papers. The town sailors come into is Ndenjoo. Sailors call it the Feeding Hill.

The unit is a **day's walk**, the interval a mile-shrine already marks. A horse takes the same road and spares the blisters. A ship takes the water those walks already name.

A Hale-month storm on the Old Crossing can turn a Salt Walk into a missed year. A thaw-week on the Rain-Wall can turn the Shelf-gate into a lake. These times are fair weather. A storm rewrites them.

The Closed Seat was an origin-gate on the grove. The Retreat shut the walls one day's walk from the Motherwood, inside Orenbren, in the lodging-country. That city is Rothallo. People still say the Inner Close. Fifteen powers stand. Rothallo is inside Orenbren, one of them. The Hinge Shore is the coast of a different war. Its peace is the Hinge Hush, still charged as the Hush-rate. Rothallo's wall and the Hinge Shore's quay keep their own names.

## What a house keeps in the field

An ox ploughs, hauls timber, and pulls a cart. The herds are on the core farms, the Chart-run plain, and the broad vales of Heskoren. A breeding animal is worth more to a house than a week's wages. People are more afraid of losing that ox than of the ox itself. The ox will not take a ford when the water is over the axle. On a ford that is already shallow, the driver still gets down and leads. They will not say why.

A goat gives milk, meat, hide, and dung, and it browses scrub other stock will not take. The herds are on the Rain-Shadow, the rough ground of Heskoren, and the terraces of Kumbaan. The house is afraid of a terrace wall knocked loose, and of a well-share argument when the herd comes in. The goat is not afraid of the wall.

A dog herds, guards a door, and keeps a quay or a hall honest about who is coming in. The dog is afraid of floodwater. It refuses a flooded path. Handlers who know the dog stop with it.

A person in an animal's body is a person. An ox, a goat, and a dog are animals, and a house keeps them as animals.
