# Playing in the Turning

Follow this page, in this order, when you make a character and when you start play. The stories and the laws live in their own chapters. This page is the sequence at the table, so it tells you what to do and where the fuller ruling sits, and it does not become a second copy of those chapters.

**Tone.** The people of this world are neighbours and colleagues, and most of what a session spends its time on is ordinary life, with a real danger underneath it. A Tithe, the upkeep a Condition card asks of the person who carries it, never takes the player character over. Keep the frightening part rare.

## Making a character

You run this once, in this order, and you do not invent a second way to build a character beside it.

1. **Kind.** Choose what the character was born, which this book calls an ancestry. That can be a stock ancestry from Daggerheart, one of the four written for this world (Kitsune, Selkie, Tengu, or Yumboe), or a Mixed Ancestry in the way the System Reference Document already allows. The rulings sit in *The Kinds*. A stock ancestry grants two features, and each of those four grants three, so a character whose two features both come from stock ancestries, whether that is one stock Kind or a mix of two, takes a **Hearth-Mark** to make up the difference. Name the place that raised you, a town or a quay or a road, written as a place and not as a Kind. Once per session, when you make an action roll or a reaction roll that leans on how that place taught you to live, you add +2, and you do not spend Hope to add it. A character who takes any feature from the four ancestries written for this world takes no Hearth-Mark, because that third feature is already the hearth. A Yumboe needs the GM's leave, and the character is the full Kind, all three features, never a mix and never a Hearth-Mark.
2. **Community.** Take a Community the way the SRD has it, the card for the sort of place that raised the character. A Hearth-Mark names a place. It does not fill the Community line, and it is not a second Community.
3. **Class, Experiences, equipment.** Take class, equipment, and Experiences as the SRD has them. Write two Experiences, the phrases on the sheet that say what the character has lived. A Kept character has those same two and no third, and the tradeoff for that empty card is written below.
4. **Turning.** Pick one, and write it on the sheet. The Leaf-Fall is the week a child stands under the town's Turning Tree in their tenth year, and what the Tree did then, or did not do, is the Turning. How many people are Given, Struck, and Kept is in *The Conditions*.
   - **Given** means a coloured leaf fell on you when you were ten, and you take one Given-path or Both-path card from *The Conditions*.
   - **Kept** means the leaves closed around you in a hug and no colour fell, so you take no Condition card. What that empty card is worth is below.
   - **Struck** means a later threshold, one the roster already names, already happened in the backstory. You died and came back, you swore a contract, you survived a drowning, you survived being turned to stone, or you were lost and dying in the deep wild and the wild took you in. You take a Struck-path or Both-path card. Someone the town will believe stood up and said what you had become, and you say who.
   - **UnTurned** means you never stood under a Turning Tree, so there was no hug and no colour, and you are not Kept. Neighbours may call you Kept. The law and your sheet say UnTurned. You can still be Struck later.
5. **Choices for the whole table.** Some characters are a conversation the whole table has, not a private pick.
   - **Phoenix** is a spotlight Condition, a campaign that will keep arriving at that fire. If anyone at the table does not want that, pick another card, or the GM runs the Phoenix as a person in the world and the player characters are someone else.
   - **Hidden Phoenix** uses the same consent, because the fire still keeps arriving. There is still one Gift, the single living Phoenix, and the shape of a Phoenix who is not the public legend is worked out between that player and the GM.
   - **Yumboe** needs the GM's leave, and the character is the full Kind only.
   - **the Unbound** is not a starting pick. A Bound character, someone who swore a contract and is still keeping the terms, becomes Unbound when the term ends, in play, or because the table agrees at the start that the term already ended.

Kind and Condition stay two things on one character, so the ancestry you were born and the Condition you carry are both on the sheet. A Selkie can be Given Two-Bodied. A stock Human can be Struck Returned. A character of mixed heritage is Given, Kept, or Struck the same way anyone else is.

They stay two things when the stories sound alike. An **Infernis**, a stock ancestry whose features are already on the sheet from birth, who swears a contract is Bound the way anyone else who swears one would be. The blood they were born with stays Kind, and the contract is the Condition. The two do not merge, they do not cancel, and the character does not become Given-Over by default. Given-Over is the world's name for a Bound person who signs contract after contract, and it is a title rather than a Condition this page hands out.

### The Kept deal

The Kept are a deliberate tradeoff, and they are not a consolation Condition invented to make up for a missing card. They have no boon and no Tithe, so the card is empty and there is no clock on it to tick. A Condition card asks other characters to mark a Tithe clock, and the mark usually comes at a long rest. The Kept clock is empty on purpose, and that emptiness is the tradeoff.

What they have is a legal standing and a free rest. The law treats them as the ordinary person: the hug was witnessed, they owe no guild, and they hold no Condition licence. At every long rest they take their downtime with no Tithe checklist, while everyone who carries a Condition may be marking a clock. That free evening is the advantage at the table. They can still be Struck later. Kept is the life they have now, and it is not a promise that the life will stay empty.

They do not receive a tenth Condition, a bonus Experience, a Hearth-Mark they did not already qualify for, or a Kept ability of their own. An ability that is really a Tithe belongs on a Condition card. If a table later wants a house rule, that house rule is theirs, and this page does not give one.

An UnTurned life looks similar from the outside, because there is no Tithe and no guild. The difference is that a Kept person's hug was witnessed, and an UnTurned person never stood. Do not file an UnTurned neighbour as Kept just to be kind, and do not write an UnTurned character as Kept on the sheet.

### Struck in play

A Kept or UnTurned character can be Struck when the story crosses a threshold this book already names. Dying, with something left undone, can make them Returned. Swearing a contract can make them Bound. Surviving a drowning, breath and voice both stopped and both come back changed, can make them Far-Voiced. Surviving being turned to stone, held rigid and then returned to flesh, can make them one of the Stilled. Being lost and dying in the deep wild, until the wild takes them in rather than let them die, can make them one of the Taken-In. Long-Lived, Two-Bodied, the Answered, and Phoenix are Given only, so no later door can Strike them into being.

The player agrees to take the card. The GM does not hand out a becoming to punish a failed roll. A Tithe never takes the character over, and becoming is not a moment where the player loses the character.

When the player takes the card, add the Condition, and the Kind stays what it was. Someone stands for them again at the next desk that will take them, which means someone says what they became so the town can know. A hazardous becoming goes to the Intake, the quiet week a town keeps for a person just Struck. Mark them Struck. They were not Given, so there is no leaf and no second Leaf-Fall. They do not take a second Condition, because the Tree does not put one becoming on top of another, and a later door does not either.

A Bound character whose term ends becomes the Unbound, as that card says. No other Condition turns into a further Condition.

### Advancement

A Condition card does not level, so the card you took at level 1 is the same card at level 10, while class features, domain cards, Experiences, weapon tier, Proficiency, and Hope scars change around it. Hope scars are the marks that cross a Hope slot off the sheet. A few things look like the card is growing, and they are not a new level of the Condition. A Two-Bodied character can gain an Experience, a Bound term can move, and a Returned or a Phoenix counts down Hope slots, while attacks the card already grants can roll more Proficiency dice as that Proficiency rises. The card underneath those changes is the card you started with. The full ruling is in *The Conditions*.

A domain card is a job someone in a town is already paid to do, and you use it as the SRD printed it, without a second roll on top of the card. A Condition clock ticks only from its own card. A Spoken colour, the sentence a warden says at the Leaf-Fall, is not a spell, and Hope does not buy a colour. If a scene has a thing that keeps working with no person in it, name the maker and name the problem that thing is still causing, and do not give it a Kind, a Condition, or a domain.

### Travel

You move on someone's word. A paper that names what you are does not replace the answer. When a watch stops a stranger, they ask your name, your last hearth, and who stands for you, and two of the three will do. If you can give them none, you sit until morning, or they walk you to the next gate.

The customs that count as that word are a road-word, a named person who will stand for you; a company's vouch, the party standing for one another; a guild-mark, a craft mark that travels; a house-note, paper from a house that will answer for you; guest-right, a host who took you in; and the custom of a walk, the word the road itself gives someone who is on a named walk. Distances are in *Climate, Ecology, and Travel*. Kumbaan, the Sundering Isle, does not use this road-word. The crossing is in *Other Places*, under Ndenjoo, and in *The Four Continents*. People do sell a vouch. That sale is a separate trade, and it does not answer this watch.

### City witnessing

A village remembers the child who stood. A city is too large for that memory, so it remembers a **hearth**, and the legal sentence is "I am of the third quay," rather than "I am of Orentel."

Turning-Week is still **one week** in a city. People stand by hearth, in named slots. The warden says the colour or the hug aloud, and they do not write a list of gifts. Threnmaieth writes the stand down, and that writing is the exception, because the other towns leave it spoken.

### Crime

There is no crime of *being* a Condition. The Inviolate Will, the doctrine that a Condition cannot seize another person's will, already sorts the charge into a Tithe that lapsed through neglect, or a power used on purpose as a weapon.

The charge walks up a ladder from a neighbour's word, to the watch, who hold the person until morning, then to a guild hearing, and then to an open hearing. Exile from the hearth is the penalty the town treats as civic death, the end of belonging to that place. A ticket names a hazardous use, the Gaze, the Cry, or an element let loose in a yard, and it does not name the Condition. A Bound hearing sits at a table and covers the Terms of the contract. The three licences on the body stay the Stilled, the Far-Voiced, and the Answered. Bound is not a fourth.

### Leaf-Fall edge cases

When you cannot open *The Turning Tree*, hold to this. The door that Gives a Condition is open for **one week** in the tenth year, and a completed standing, whether a colour fell or the leaves hugged, under sound wood, happens **once**. A miss, a refusal, or a week spent under unsound wood makes the child **unTurned**, and it does not make them Kept. Struck remains the mercy that can still come later. You may correct a Speaking, the warden's sentence about what the leaf did, and you may not rerun the fall itself. White-fire, the leaf that would name a Phoenix, is sent to the First Seat, and a town-warden does not guess that reading.

## Starting situations

Start in one of these places, and stay with the one job the old walk left on that street. After a town has its own Tree, that job is one of three. **Devotion** is people keeping a walk they no longer need, either as an extra mile or as a wait at a Tree that may not speak. **Prestige** is a household paying for a winter, or a berth, for a walk they do not need. **Necessity** is a local Tree that is missing, young, or sick, or a week that cannot hold everyone who comes, so people use a neighbour's standing, water, or bed. Give the place one of those jobs. A guest may bring a second into the same week, and the second is not the place's theme.

**Eolvaeth.** People come here to wait, and the wait is the job. Vaethod, the town-warden, will not invent a colour. Saelid's stall keeps feeding its winter guests, and those guests are the same households a sent cohort takes away. A stranger in the gift-hall has no list to stand on. Some years the wood says send the children on, toward a luckier Hand, and the gospel in the vale says stay. In a year when the Tree does not speak, a wet leaf lies on the spring, pilgrims from the core ask if it is a ribbon, and Eolvaeth will not say.

**Orentel.** The quay sells a winter to households who can pay for a walk they do not need, and that sale is the job. A First-Hand year, the prestige season at the origin, is assembled at three desks: a Night-Shore hull chartered at Braetu, the city on the shore that sells the ship, a term from the White Note, and a hold at Orentel, where Sorim keeps berths 1–4 empty because they are held. Orenbren houses the sleep once the year reaches the grove, and does not sell the year. Tesara works the stillers' shed, and the shed does not face the street. Two streets off the third quay, a person too poor to meet a Tithe fades while the city steps around them. Salt sits on the Tree's roots in Eolthael, the month that holds Turning-Week. People say it is the tide, the tide does not reach the rise, and Orentel will not say why the salt is there.

**Maiethlir.** Start the week before Leaf-Fall, when the thaw is loud. Clerks file that rise as snowmelt, the watchers do not, and Maiethlir will not say why. Rithim, the Speaker, will not give the clerk the line, and will Speak over the water. The wardens have recut a chapel, and a Watching room is under it, where Laevila keeps the only copy of the beam's hidden face. The crown keeps a counted channel for a Far-Voiced hour and for the city's green, and that channel can be shut. The extra mile is still walked here. The roll writes on top of that walk, and the walk remains.

**Harrow's Green.** Start in Hale-month, the spring month when the land is counted sound. The year is Cut-year 387, the 387th summer counted from the First Cut. The job is a neighbour's week that can still be walked home. Haelin wants the stone moved so the crowd can stand, and the water is still on it, because the stone is the day's drink for anyone using the road as a mile. The upper room has extra guests. Children here Turn under this Tree. The hamlets can see the canopy and are still sending theirs. The cistern-dipper is hung with the bowl toward the stream, and Harrow's Green will not say why.

**Ornsael.** The well is dropping, and the job is water. Theisva keeps the share, chalked on the lip, Lesna keeps the planting, and Bovaer still sends people west when the drink will not stretch through Hale-month. Pilgrims from the core arrive expecting a holiday walk and find a necessity-column already on the road. The well-rope is knotted at last year's water-line, and this year's knot is wet even when the water stands below it. Ornsael will not say why.

**the Three Hamlets.** Play the same week as Harrow's Green. In clear weather the canopy is a dark on the far slope, and in rain it is a rumour. Brenod sends the child, Vaelun may refuse the walk because the children are already the land's, and Ornath has looked at the other road, toward a slate and away from a Tree, without taking it. The job is the neighbour's week. The night before, someone leaves a cup on the ford-rock. If it fills, they send, and if it does not, the tellings do not agree, while the child still goes, or does not, according to the kitchen and not according to the cup.

**the Third Hearth.** Thilim has made Seine's bed and left it made. The extra mile is optional, and the house still keeps the bed. That is the job. Meirim, Brenthael's warden, wants the stone taken down so the extra mile does not look like a requirement on the town's approach. Seine's name is on the lintel. The pot needs the stone's water at dark, and the stone at the road is the day's water. At dark, Thilim sets the yard-broom across the doorway and steps over it, and they will not say why.

**Ndenjoo.** Play the hall's hospitality, and play the storm-wall as the weather. A wreck has walked uphill with the extra place already set, and a guest is loud at noon, when the hall sleeps. There is no Tree, so you seat the wreck, keep the kin who will not leave, and let someone want a crossing out. The session is the hall, and it is not spent proving what the Trees are.

## Tone at the table

Play neighbours, colleagues, and family. The Tithe is where the danger sits, and Leaf-Fall is a warm week, so keep dread out of the Tree. When you need one moment of wrongness, use the weather, a host still keeping a guest from a week that ended, a week that already closed, or a person who missed the standing.

The Conditions are already the people in the world. There is no second vampire, no cursed wolf-kin, and no hungry dead who is not a Returned. The cart-ox, the terrace goat, and the path dog are livelihoods people keep, and they are not adversaries you drop into a fight.

## Sample frames

Use one of these for a run of sessions. In each mainland frame, some people Turn under the Tree in town, and this year someone is still walking because living wood has not reached every hearth. Play both as the present. The Years of Hands are underway, which are the years a town Tree is the ordinary way to Turn.

**A town where the planting is still arriving.** Harrow's Green, or the hamlets looking at its canopy, where the job on the street is a neighbour's week. A pot of living wood may arrive, and it may not. Someone here is still sending this year's children, while someone who thinks the planting is finished lives two weeks away and cannot see this canopy.

**A quay that sells a winter.** Orentel. A household that wants the prestige of a First-Hand year goes to three desks, a Night-Shore hull, a term from the White Note, and a hold at Orentel, and one desk does not finish the winter. Necessity still sleeps in the loft. Ledan, at the White Note desk, will convert a house-year, the house's own count of summers, or a Cut-year, the wardens' count from the First Cut, or a third date a court invented, if you say which. They will not invent the count for you.

**An origin town with a roll.** Maiethlir. The extra mile is still walked, and the clerks write on top of it. Rithim will not copy the clerk's line. A Watcher will describe what they saw, and will not confirm it. The First Seat is in the wood beside the city, and can refuse what the city asks.

**The hall on the isle.** Ndenjoo, where hospitality is the rite, an extra place and a second night and a guest who can sleep the day, and the storm-wall is the weather. Play kin and a wreck. The session is not spent proving what the Trees are.

The first campaign has its own opening, in the GM's chapter. It is not one of the starts above.

## Dangers on the road

Settlements already have the things that can enter one of their scenes, a cohort, a crane, a well-mouth, so use this section for the country between those towns, the Long Mile, the wild of Heskoren, and the named waters. What threatens a traveler out here is people who missed their week, the animals on the road, and the weather, which is a real obstacle under ordinary travel.

Start battle points from the SRD: `[(3 × PCs) + 2]`. Spend: Minion group 1 · Support/Social 1 · Horde/Ranged/Skulk/Standard 2 · Leader 3 · Bruiser 4 · Solo 5. Benchmarks: Tier 1 Difficulty 11, thresholds 7/12, ATK +1; Tier 2 Difficulty 14, 10/20, ATK +2.

Tier is how hard the fight is. The type is the Daggerheart role. What it wants is what it is trying to do when the party meets it.

| Name | Tier / type | Where it lives | What it wants |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gap-Cat** | T1 Skulk | Rain-Wall shelves, Heskoren ridge, mile-gaps | A quiet kill, and then the high rock back |
| **Chart-Crew** | T1 Horde | Chart-run, Night Shore, West Water wrecks | The hull that did not arrive, or yours if that one is gone |
| **Bed-Keeper** | T1 Leader | Empty road-houses on the Long Mile | The bed occupied, and you staying in it |
| **Spent-Week Walker** | T2 Standard | Long Mile, west-road, live-front tracks | To finish a tenth-year week that has already closed |
| **Colourless Host** | T2 Support | Dead pots, unsound grafts, waiting ground | To Give a Condition the way a Tree would, which it cannot do |

None of these stat blocks are Conditions. A walker may carry a Condition the way any person might, and the card they carry is not this block. The Spent-Week Walker and the Colourless Host are Tier 2 blocks, so in a Tier 1 scene you use the Tier 1 Difficulty, thresholds, and attack bonus from the benchmarks above in place of theirs.

You use one of these places as the scene, and you put a single adversary into it. An impulse is what the place does when you spend Fear, or when someone fails a roll to travel.

**Mile-gap** (T1 Traversal, Difficulty 12). This is the day's walk between two mile-stones, and the next shrine depends on the weather rather than on a promise that the stone will be there. The place will stretch the day, hide the next stone, offer a false bed, or make a Tithe expensive. A Taken-In who cannot touch living green, or a Long-Lived with nothing new, feels the gap, but their next long rest runs the card's check as usual and the gap adds no extra tick. Travel rolls to find the next shrine are Difficulty 12. Failure with Fear ends the day in the gap.

**Thaw-gate** (T1 Traversal, Difficulty 13). This is a shelf-gate or the Noon Pass in a thaw-week, when last year's snow is the road you are walking this year. Crossing without a guide is Difficulty 13. On a failure, someone is swept, or a cart is lost, and one roll does not do both. Anyone who lived the Thaw-Break, in Cut-year 233, has Advantage on the next travel roll here if they say what they remember. Anyone who did not has to trust someone who did.

**Empty road-house** (T1 Exploration, Difficulty 11). This is a road-house the walking years left on the mile, a brenhael *(BREN-hael)*, and it kept the upper room after the walk ended, with someone still making the bed. Guest-right is a week. If you eat, a host stands for the stay, and leaving before morning without a word is a slight the next shrine will hear.

**Night-berth** (T1 Exploration, Difficulty 12). This is a Chart-run or West Water quay at first dark, where a hull that does not arrive is counted as local weather. The place wants to wait, to take what the sea gives up, and to sell a stand, and it does not want to file a hill.

Pair a place with one danger. On the Long Mile inland, use a mile-gap and a Gap-Cat, and add a Spent-Week Walker if the table wants more. On the Long Mile where a house still keeps a bed from a week that ended, use an empty road-house and a Bed-Keeper. On the Rain-Wall, use a thaw-gate, and let the weather be enough. On the Chart-run or at Netstrand, use a night-berth and a Chart-Crew. At a dead pot on the live front, use one Colourless Host as Support, with walkers or a Gap-Cat, and never a second Host, because what a Host does is make the place worse. Near a town on the Near Mile, use what that town already sends into a scene. The storm-wall belongs with the isle, in *Other Places*, and it is not one of these road dangers.

Use a Colourless Host when a planting has gone wrong, or a pot never took. Skeptics and believers can both be loud about what that means, and the argument stays open.
