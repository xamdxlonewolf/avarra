# Daily Life


Most of family in the Turning is utterly ordinary. Two people marry; some of their household carries a Condition and some doesn't; the children haven't Turned yet and won't until their tenth year. A born-Selkie wife, a husband Returned from a drowning, a Kept grandmother, three children who are simply children so far — this is not a remarkable household. It is *the* household. Because Conditions **stack** onto Kinds and arrive across a whole life, nearly every family is "mixed" in some way, and precisely because nearly every family is, **mixed families are not a category anyone thinks about.** There is no "interracial marriage" tension here in the Condition layer, because there is no unmixed baseline to marry away from.

### Family — the whole spectrum

**At the warm, mundane end** (most families): a Condition is just a texture of the household, the way a trade or a temperament is. A Two-Bodied parent's other self has a *name* and the children know it — "that's Bramble, mother's otter" — and grows up as much a part of the family as a pet that is also a person. A Far-Voiced household is a *loud* one, because the Tithe means feeling must be aired daily; you do not get to sulk quietly in a home with a Far-Voiced in it, and families learn to treat the evening's singing or weeping or laughing as ordinary weather. A Taken-In parent keeps the windows open and the rooms green. These are quirks, not tragedies — the small daily accommodations of living with people who are also *becomings.*

**In the middle**, the Tithe becomes a shared family labor. Where a Condition is provided-for, keeping a family member whole is a household expense and a household duty: the green for the Taken-In, the books for the Long-Lived, the outlet for the Far-Voiced. Families organize around it the way others organize around a member's chronic need — mostly without drama, sometimes with strain, and with the quiet knowledge that **to let a loved one's Tithe lapse is to watch them slide toward the scary edge.** A family too poor to furnish a Tithe carries a particular grief: they can see exactly what their kin needs and cannot buy it.

**At the aching end** sits the marriage the whole setting is quietly organized around: **loving someone whose becoming and yours don't share a timespan.** Chief among these is marrying a **Long-Lived.** Their aging simply stops; their spouse's does not. A Long-Lived can have children and a full family, but the Condition is **not reliably inherited** — most children age and die like anyone — so a Long-Lived does not build a deathless dynasty; they **outlive it.** They watch a spouse grow old across a marriage that stays, on one side, unchanged. They bury their children. The old saying is that *the cost of the Long-Lived is measured in funerals*, and it is a cost paid inside the family, at the dinner table, over decades. A Long-Lived who has done this once faces the choice their whole existence turns on: **stop** — live on alone, keep no more households — or **begin again**, marry again, love again, and outlive that family too. Neither choice is cowardly and neither is free. This is the setting's deepest, quietest ache, and it lives in the most ordinary place there is: a marriage.

A few threads:

- **The threshold courtesy in the home.** A Long-Lived never crosses another's home threshold uninvited — held as the deepest good manners, the mark of someone safe to let in. Inside a *family*, this softens into something tender: to be welcomed across a Long-Lived's own threshold, or to welcome them across yours without a thought, is a real intimacy. A door held open is how this world says *you are mine and I am yours.* (A door refused is felt more than a Long-Lived will ever say.)
- **The Returned come *home.*** A person who died and Returned is, contrary to every outside expectation, **not cold** — a Returned is calm, deliberate, unafraid, and *quietly respected*, and their loved ones get them back. The chill only comes if their Purpose lapses: a Returned neglecting the unfinished thing that holds them "runs cold," and the living can feel the wrongness at a touch. So a Returned's family learns a hard and intimate literacy — **you can tell, by an embrace, whether the one you love is tending what holds them here.** A warm embrace means they are well. A cold one is a family emergency dressed as a hug.
- **Two-Bodied bloodlines are family lines.** Because the Condition runs in blood (children of a Two-Bodied parent usually Turn the same animal), "wolf-folk," "hawk-folk," and "otter-folk" are real extended families, and the family animal, the Condition, and often the family trade descend as a single inheritance. Marrying into such a line is marrying into a shape your children will likely wear.
- **Raising a child before they Turn.** Every parent lives the same open question until their child's tenth year: *what will they become, or will they be Kept?* In devout homes this is held as a hopeful mystery (the Mother will give what the child can carry); in anxious ones it is a decade of quiet dread or ambition. Parents who carry a heritable Condition (Two-Bodied especially) can half-guess; most cannot. The Leaf-Fall is therefore not only a civic day but the emotional hinge of a family's life — the day you finally *meet* who your child has been becoming all along. In the Walking Years the question had a prior: *can we even get there?* A household that could walk only one child still lives in the present as a Given/Kept sibling pair.

### Medicine — miraculous, and strange to outside eyes

Medicine in the Turning is genuinely astonishing for a late-medieval world, and it is astonishing for one reason: **the Condition-labor advance shows up hardest at the sickbed and the deathbed.** Two Conditions turn medicine into something a world of this technology should not have — and to anyone from outside, a Turning clinic is a deeply *uncanny* place, wonder and wrongness in the same room.

**The Stilled are the surgeons.** A stiller's gaze **arrests motion** — it holds a wound from bleeding, slows a poison's spread through the blood, keeps a patient perfectly still under the knife without holding them down. It does not petrify and does not harm; it simply *stops* what is moving for a beat. In a world with no anaesthetic worth the name and no way to halt a hemorrhage, this is the difference between a survivable operation and a death. The best surgeons in the world are Stilled, and a great infirmary is built around them. But sit in that operating room as an outsider and watch what it *is*: a surgeon whose flat, patient gaze freezes the blood in your veins on command, working fast because the Grey is filling in them as they work (their Tithe — using the Gaze fills a clock that, left full, locks *their own* limb to stone). They drill and spar between patients to burn it off. The most gifted healers in the world are also the most tightly **licensed** and quietly **feared** — the same gaze that saves you on the table is the one the law watches hardest. Wonder and dread, one person, one room.

**The Returned do the work that kills the living.** Plague wards, mine collapses, the recovery of bodies from places the living cannot safely go, and the tending of the dead — this is Returned work, done by those death cannot take a second time until their Purpose is met. A Turning mortuary is staffed by the dead, calmly and well; a plague ward that would empty a town's living physicians is walked by Returned who are unshaken by rot and contagion. To an outsider this is ghoulish. Inside, it is simply **respected** — the Returned are the reason a plague does not take the doctors too, and everyone knows it. There is a real tenderness in it, too: no one speaks for the dead, or tends them, as gently as someone who has been dead.

**The rest of medicine is ordinary — and ordinarily unequal.** Midwives, bonesetters, herbalists, the Answered's craft turned to apothecary and instrument-making — the everyday care of a 1400s town, better staffed than others because more of the population carries a useful becoming. But the Condition-medicine that makes this world exceptional is **not evenly available.** The great Stilled infirmaries and the deathless-endowed hospitals cluster in the rich, Tree-dense core; the frontier makes do with a travelling stiller if it is lucky and a rope-and-prayer if it is not. And the deepest inequity is Tithe-poverty already named: **you can be too poor to stay whole,** and the medicine that treats a lapsing Condition is exactly the medicine the poor can least reach. The miracle is real. So is the queue outside it.

### Living in a Turning town — city design

A city here is shaped by a fact no other world's city faces: **most of its adults have an upkeep, and some of those upkeeps are dangerous or expensive to meet.** So the town builds around the Tithe the way others build around water and waste — as **civic infrastructure, visible and load-bearing.**

- **The Tree at the centre.** Every town is *organized around* its Turning Tree — it is the civic and sacred heart, the site of the year's most-attended day, the thing the oldest quarter grew up around. A town's age and standing can be read in its Tree (a mature grafted Tree = an old, reach-rich town; a young Tree or none = a new or frontier settlement). Geography and civic layout both point back to it. Residues layer this: a stone in the square, an upper room, a road that ends at the boughs.
- **Tithe-provision as public utility.** Where the polity treats provision as a public good, the town furnishes it in the open: **venting-halls** (common tongue: **the ael-hall**; Vaethorn *the gift-hall*; Lestrand *the release-house*; Threnmaieth *the counted hall*) for the Far-Voiced — part concert hall, part release valve, often doubling as the town's performance venue; **potted groves and garden-commons** (common: **the greens**; Vaethorn *the Mother's green*; Lestrand *the leaf-lot*; Threnmaieth *the tended green*) so the Taken-In don't "go to seed" indoors; **endowed libraries** (common: **the long-house of books**; Vaethorn *the deathless gift*; Lestrand *the endowment* / *the house*; Threnmaieth *the crown archive*) feeding the Long-Lived's novelty-Tithe and often *built* by a Long-Lived; and **ranging-commons** (common: **the ranging**; Vaethorn *the wild-leave*; Lestrand *the beast-yard*; Threnmaieth *the ranging-roll*) at the town edge where the Two-Bodied can be their other selves. These are as normal a part of the streetscape as a well or a market cross.
- **The dangerous trades on the edges.** The work that carries a bystander hazard sits where such work always sits — at the margins and the edges. Stilled infirmaries and Answered element-workshops (fire, forge, tide) are guild-quartered and set slightly apart; the Returned's mortuaries and plague-work sit at the town's edge, near the dead. Not from disgust — from the same logic that puts a tannery downwind.
- **Homes that accommodate becomings.** Ordinary domestic architecture quietly bends to the Conditions: windows that open and rooms kept green for a Taken-In guest, a household's evening rhythm built around a Far-Voiced's vent, a threshold a Long-Lived waits to be asked across. A Turning home is legibly a home *for becomings.*
- **The reach-edge writes the map, again.** Dense, Tree-rich core towns are thick with all this infrastructure; frontier settlements have little of it and improvise. You can read a town's place in the world off how much of its Tithe-infrastructure is public, private, or simply absent.

### How polities vary (domestic faces)

**The Warm** *(high theology).* Family, medicine, and civic life all carry the gift-theology: a child's Turning is a joyous sacrament, mixed households are blessings compounded, Tithe-provision is a public good offered in the open, and the sick are tended as people whose gifts must be kept whole. **Its soft injustice reaches the hearth:** the Kept child is *pitied* for being passed over, marrying "beneath your gift" is quietly frowned on, and a family is expected to spend its members' Conditions as the community sees fit — love and pressure wearing the same warm face.

**The Watchful** *(governance at its extreme).* The register-keeping crown reaches into the family: marriages of heritable Conditions may be *noticed* and encouraged or discouraged, a child's Turning is an event the state records, and the medicine that furnishes a Tithe runs through channels the crown controls. **Its cold injustice is domestic surveillance** — a household whose upkeep and bloodline are both on file is a household that cannot easily refuse the crown anything.

**The Frontier** *(low reach).* Thin infrastructure, thin medicine, thin ceremony. Families improvise their own Tithe-provision or do without; a travelling stiller is a rare blessing; the Taken-In whom core cities keep at arm's length are *neighbours and kin* here, the backbone of the place. Freer (no one polices your marriage or your gift) and harsher (a lapsing Condition with no infirmary to reach, an untrained Struck child with no guild to catch them). Warmer in feeling, poorer in safety-net.

A devout frontier holds joyous Turnings with almost no infirmary behind them; a secular Tree-rich republic has superb medicine and a marriage market that shrugs at theology; a Watchful-and-pious core turns the sacrament of a child's Turning into a state census with a hymn over it.
