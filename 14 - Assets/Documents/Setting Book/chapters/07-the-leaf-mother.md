# The Leaf-Mother


Some hold that the **Leaf-Mother** blessed the world with the Conditions, and that the Turning Trees are her hands reaching down to *choose* — each Leaf-Fall an act of a mother's love. This is a belief held by many, not confirmed cosmology: skeptics are sincere and full members of society, and the faith itself is built to make room for them. Everyday: **the Motherfaith**. Their own name: **Maieth**.

### A gift-religion

No one is being rescued from a fallen state. The founding fact is that the world received *gifts* (the Conditions) and goes on receiving them each solstice. The faith is a long answer to *how do you live worthy of a gift?*

**Orthopraxy over orthodoxy.** What makes you of the Motherfaith is what you **do** — you bring your children to the Tree, you tend your Tithe honestly, you keep the festival — far more than what you privately believe about *who* does the choosing. This is the structural reason a devout believer and a quiet skeptic can stand at the same Leaf-Fall and both belong. Right practice binds; right belief is argued about.

**Divine character: a mother.** Benevolent, attentive, generous, and *quiet* — she does not thunder, command, or demand worship. She gives, and (the faithful say) she watches. The maternal frame is the whole tone: you don't fear the Mother, you try not to waste what she hands you.

The Motherfaith is the dominant faith of the Turning lands but not the only religion. The Leaf-Mother is not written as the universe's sole power; she is *this* people's Mother.

### Doctrine (as the faithful hold it)

**The Giving.** In the beginning (the faithful say) the world was only ever *born into* — people were their Kind and nothing more. The Leaf-Mother's gift was **becoming**: the capacity to be given something beyond your birth, a Condition the world would need. The first Tree, the Awakening Tree, is where she first reached down; every town Tree is a grafted Hand of the Mother, so that no child is too far from her reach.

**She chooses, and she chooses well.** Doctrine holds that the colour a child receives is *chosen* — looked-at, intended, fitted to the child and to the town's need. The gifts "fit too well" to be accident (believers point to this; skeptics call it hindsight).

**The Tithe is the weight of a gift, not a punishment.** Every Condition costs upkeep. The faith reads this as *honest weight*: a real gift has a real charge to carry, and tending it well is the ordinary devotion of a lifetime. **"Tend what you're given"** is the closest thing the faith has to a first commandment.

**The Kept are given wholeness.** To be Kept — hugged by the leaves, no colour — is doctrinally a *gift of being left whole*, not an oversight. This is where the schism bites hardest.

**She has no throne to visit and no voice that answers on demand.** Accessibility is *mediated and quiet*: she is met at the Tree, in the turning of a life, in the fittingness of things — never summoned. This quietness is doctrinally *intended* (a mother lets her children live), and is exactly what leaves room for honest doubt.

### Practice

- **The Leaf-Fall** — the central rite, a child's tenth-year solstice Turning. It is at once sacrament, coming-of-age, and the town's great festival.
- **The Tending** — the ongoing, private devotion of *paying your Tithe well*. Framed religiously: to tend your Condition honestly is to honour the gift. A Two-Bodied who runs their other self, a Long-Lived who keeps their vigils — the faith calls this worship-by-living, not just upkeep.
- **The Turning-week festival** — the solstice week around the Leaf-Falls: markets, feasts, the reunion of families, the naming of the year's new Given and new Kept. The emotional high point of the calendar.
- **Threshold rites for the Struck** — because the Struck receive a Condition *away* from the Tree, the faith has grown quiet rites to receive them back: a Struck person is brought to the Tree *after* the fact, not to be Turned (the Tree doesn't Strike) but to be **acknowledged** — folded into the community of the Given rather than left as an anomaly. How warmly this is done is itself contested.
- **Tree-tending** — the civic-sacred care of the town's Turning Tree (watering rites, warding against blight, the graft-rites when a new scion is planted). Handled by the tree-wardens.

Towns house the warden-hearth; Turning-Week is the festival share; Cutting-leave carries a fee framed as carrying-earth; pilgrimage keeps the Seat. The tree-wardens are not the Tithe-infrastructure sector.

### The live schism

The faith is not monolithic. Its central argument is a genuine, good-faith one that no one can settle, which is exactly why it endures. It turns on three questions the doctrine can't prove.

**1. The Kept — spared, or slighted?** If the Mother blesses everyone, why are about a quarter Given no colour at all?

- **The Tender reading (majority/orthodox):** the Kept are *given wholeness* — a real gift, chosen for them. To be Kept is to be trusted to need nothing added.
- **The Wanting reading (minority):** the Kept were *passed over* — loved, but not chosen, and the doctrine of "wholeness" is a kindness the faithful tell them. This reading quietly stings, and shapes how some Kept feel about the festival.

**2. The Struck — still hers, or not?** The Struck receive a Condition the Tree never handed out. Did the Mother reach through the threshold too, or did *something else* give the gift?

- **The Wide-Hand reading:** she meets people at every door — the drowning, the oath, the death — the Tree is only her *ordinary* hand. The Struck are fully hers.
- **The Narrow-Hand reading:** she gives only at the Tree; the Struck received their Condition by *some other means*, which is why the Struck are "a little suspect." This reading is the seed of real social friction toward the Struck.

**3. Does a mind choose at all?** — the believer/skeptic line proper.

- **Believers:** the fittingness of the gifts, the care of the Kept, the way the Struck feel *received* — these add up to a Someone.
- **Skeptics:** "it's just what the Trees do." Biology, or a phenomenon so old it wears a face we gave it. The grafting is observable horticulture; the *choosing mind* is an inference they decline to make.

Because the faith is orthopraxic, a skeptic who tends their Tithe and brings their children to the Tree is a *good member of the Motherfaith* — belief in the Mother is honoured but not policed. This keeps the skeptic sincere and un-persecuted.

Every branch is a good-faith position. No faction is secretly right-and-evil.

### Two sayings

**The Tender's blessing**, spoken over each child at the Leaf-Fall:

> *"What falls to you, wear well. What is kept from you, keep. The Hand that reaches is the Mother's own — and the Mother does not reach twice by chance."*

On its face: comfort and the "tend your gift" ethic. The line asserts intent behind the fall — the very thing skeptics deny.

**A Kept-child's saying** (folk, half-consoling, half-barbed):

> *"The Tree that gives to all its neighbours held me instead. I have decided that is not the same as being forgotten."*

On its face: the Tender reading of the Kept, made personal and a little defiant. Underneath: it dramatizes the schism — *"I have decided"* is a choice made against doubt, not a certainty.
