# The Leaf-Mother

Some people hold that the **Leaf-Mother** blessed the world with the Conditions, the gifts of a becoming beyond the life a person was born into, and that the Turning Trees are her hands reaching down to choose. On that account each **Leaf-Fall**, the town's gathering when a child of ten stands at the solstice and a leaf falls or the leaves hug, is an act of a mother's love. Many people hold this, and it remains a belief, because nobody in the world has proved it. The skeptics who decline it are sincere and full members of society, the faith having been built to keep a place for them. Everyday people call the faith **the Motherfaith**. The faith calls itself **Maieth**, and a devout square that says the name means a mother, quiet, who gives, and who does not come because someone called.

## A gift-religion

The faith begins from gifts the world received, the Conditions, and from the gifts that still come at each solstice. Nobody here is being rescued from a fallen state. The long question is how a person lives worthy of a gift.

What makes you of the Motherfaith is what you **do**. You bring your children to the Tree, you tend your **Tithe** honestly, the upkeep a Condition asks of the person who carries it, and you keep the festival. That practice counts for far more than what you privately believe about who does the choosing. A devout believer and a quiet skeptic can stand at the same Leaf-Fall and both belong, because the practice is what binds and the belief is what the faith argues about.

The faithful speak of a mother who is benevolent, attentive, generous, and quiet: she gives, and they say she watches, and she does not thunder or command or ask to be feared. People come to her by trying not to waste what she hands them.

The Motherfaith is the dominant faith of the Turning lands, and the other faiths are lived beside it. To the people who hold her, she is their Mother.

The houses do not share one face for her. What they share is the wood. A pressed leaf kept in a chest is a common keepsake, and it is only a leaf, and it does not speak, and some hearths keep no image of her at all. The faith does not picture her standing at a drowning, or at an oath, or out in the deep wild, because it has not agreed that she is in those places, and that argument stays open. Some Long-Lived people, the ageless keepers of memory, keep a cup they call the **Unspent**. Among themselves they say the Cup We Made, because they will tell you they invented whatever is on the other side by pouring in memories they could no longer carry, and on a quay people say the Poured God, with the smile they keep for a rich uncle's hobby. No town Tree is involved in it, no leaf falls for the cup, and a warden does not set that cup on the sill.

## Doctrine (as the faithful hold it)

What follows is what the faithful hold. Nobody in the world has proved it.

In the beginning, they say, a person was only what they were born. That born half is a **Kind**, the people they were by birth, and nothing more. The Leaf-Mother's gift was **becoming**: the capacity to be given something beyond birth, a Condition the world would need. They call that first gift the Giving. They say she first reached down at the Awakening Tree, and that every town Tree is a grafted **Hand of the Mother**, a living cut of that first Tree, planted so that no child is too far from her reach.

Doctrine holds that she chooses, and that she chooses well. The colour a child receives is chosen, looked-at, intended, and fitted to the child and to the town's need. Believers point to gifts that fit too well to be accident. Skeptics call that hindsight.

The Tithe is the weight of a gift, and the faith does not read the cost as a punishment. Every Condition asks upkeep. The faith reads that cost as honest weight: a real gift has a real charge to carry, and tending it well is the ordinary devotion of a lifetime. "Tend what you're given" is the closest thing the faith has to a first commandment.

To be Kept, hugged by the leaves and given no colour, is in the doctrine a gift of being left whole. The faithful do not call the hug an oversight. This is where the argument bites hardest, and the schism below is that bite.

She has no throne a person can visit, and no voice that answers because someone demanded an answer. The faithful say a person meets her at the Tree, in the turning of a life, and in the fittingness of things, and never by summoning her. They hold that the quiet is intended, because a mother lets her children live. The same quiet is what leaves room for honest doubt.

## Practice

The Leaf-Fall is the central rite, a child's tenth-year solstice Turning, and people keep it as sacrament, coming-of-age, and the town's great festival in the same afternoon. The questions a warden actually gets, a missed week, a refusal, an orphan, a child who is already Struck, and whether anyone stands twice, belong with the Tree. The rite's cited shadow is the **Wrong Green**. In Cut-year 248, at Harrow's Green, a warden named a hug as copper-green, and the child spent two summers in a garden that had no gift to teach, until the correction came as a second word and the child was not stood under the Tree again.

The Tending is the ongoing, private devotion of paying a Tithe well. To tend a Condition honestly is, in this faith, to honour the gift. A Two-Bodied person, who carries a second animal self and still commands it, works in that other body. A Long-Lived person keeps the vigils and the learning their years require. The faith calls that life worship, lived as the upkeep itself.

The Turning-week festival is the solstice week around the Leaf-Falls. Towns hold markets and feasts, families reunite, and the year names its new Given and its new Kept. A colour fell on the new Given. The leaves hugged the new Kept. It is the emotional high point of the calendar, the week the year calls the High Solstice, and also Turning-Week.

The Struck receive a Condition away from the Tree, at a drowning they survived, an oath they swore, or a death they crossed, so the faith grew quiet rites to receive them afterward. A Struck person is brought to the Tree to be **acknowledged**, and folded into the community of the Given, rather than left as an anomaly. The Tree does not Strike, so this is not a Turning. How warmly a town does it is itself part of the argument.

Tree-tending is the civic and sacred care of the town's Turning Tree: the watering, the watch against blight, and the planting of a new scion, a living cut carried in earth. The **tree-wardens** do that work. They are one order with offices inside it, not a set of rival chapters. They officiate the Leaf-Fall, and they hold the authoritative reading of the colours. Common colours are already folk-known. The wardens confirm them, and they rule on the rare, the subtle, and the mixed falls, which is why a town needs them as a civic office and not only as people who pray.

The **First Seat** is a college in the Motherwood about the Awakening Tree, the old wood around that grove, and it is not a throne. Pilgrims go there. The Seat authorizes graft-scions, whether the cut comes from the origin or from a healthy town Tree acting on the origin's behalf. It confirms **Road-hands**, the people who carry a living cutting, and **Speakers**, the elders who hold the last word on a rare colour. It does not appoint every town-warden. A hearth is taken by apprenticeship, and the town that watched the new warden through a year is what confirms them.

The keeping of the order is ordinary and local. Towns house the warden-hearth, the warden's house on the square, and the stipend is bread, wood, and a place, not a tax on Conditions. During Turning-Week the warden eats from the festival, the way the town feeds any office it needs that week. **Cutting-leave**, the paper that says who may take wood and for where, carries a fee the Seat describes as carrying-earth, the cost of the living soil the cutting travels in and of a Road-hand's year. Pilgrimage keeps the Seat: offerings, lodging, and the devout who settle in the Motherwood. The wardens point a new Given toward the work the colour named. The halls that help a person meet a Tithe are other people's work.

## The live schism

The faith holds more than one reading. The central argument is sincere on every side, and no one has settled it, which is why the faith endures. It turns on three questions the doctrine cannot prove.

The first question is the Kept: if the Mother blesses everyone, why does the Tree hug so many children and give them no colour at all? The **Tender reading** is the majority, and it is the teaching the faith usually gives. The Kept are given wholeness, a real gift, chosen for them, and to be Kept is to be trusted to need nothing added. The **Wanting reading** is the minority. The Kept were passed over, loved but not chosen, and the doctrine of wholeness is a kindness the faithful tell them. This reading quietly stings, and it shapes how some Kept feel about the festival.

The second question is the Struck, who received a Condition the Tree never handed out. Did the Mother reach through the threshold too, or did something else give the gift? The **Wide-Hand reading** says she meets people at the drowning, the oath, and the death, and that the Tree is only her ordinary hand, so the Struck are fully hers. The **Narrow-Hand reading** says she gives only at the Tree, and that the Struck received their Condition by some other means, which is why this reading finds the Struck a little suspect. That suspicion is the seed of real social friction toward the Struck.

The third question is whether a mind chooses at all, and it is the line between believer and skeptic. Believers say the fittingness of the gifts, the care of the Kept, and the way the Struck feel received add up to a Someone. Skeptics say it is just what the Trees do, biology, or a phenomenon so old that it wears a face people gave it. The grafting is horticulture a person can watch, a cut of wood set into new ground, and a choosing mind is an inference they decline to make.

Because a person belongs by the rite they keep, a skeptic who tends their Tithe and brings their children to the Tree is a good member of the Motherfaith. Belief in the Mother is honoured, and it is not policed, so the skeptic can speak the doubt and remain that member. Every branch above is held in good faith, and no house is a villain. Being a skeptic costs nothing day to day, because the practice is what binds. The questions stay open because no one in the square can close them.

## Two sayings

The Tender's blessing is spoken over each child at the Leaf-Fall.

> *"What falls to you, wear well. What is kept from you, keep. The Hand that reaches is the Mother's own — and the Mother does not reach twice by chance."*

The blessing is comfort, and it carries the ethic of tending the gift. Believers hear intent in it, skeptics hear a proverb about not asking the wood twice, and both can say the words.

A Kept child's saying is folk, half a consolation and half a barb.

> *"The Tree that gives to all its neighbours held me instead. I have decided that is not the same as being forgotten."*

It takes the Tender reading of the Kept and makes it personal, and a little defiant. "I have decided" is a choice made against doubt, and not a certainty.
