# The Leaf-Mother

Some hold that the **Leaf-Mother** blessed the world with the Conditions, and that the Turning Trees, the trees that stand in the towns, are her hands reaching down to choose. Each **Leaf-Fall**, the tenth-year solstice when a leaf falls or the leaves hug a child, is in this belief an act of a mother's love. Many people hold this. Skeptics hold otherwise, and they mean it. They are full members of society, and the faith keeps a place for them. Everyday people call the faith **the Motherfaith**. Its own name is **Maieth**.

### A gift-religion

The faith begins from the gifts the world received, the Conditions, and from the gifts that still come at each solstice. Its long question is how a person lives worthy of a gift.

**The rite counts ahead of the creed.** What makes you of the Motherfaith is what you **do**. You bring your children to the Tree, you tend your **Tithe** — the upkeep a Condition must meet — honestly, and you keep the festival. That counts far more than what you privately believe about who does the choosing. You belong by the rite you keep. This is why a devout believer and a quiet skeptic can stand at the same Leaf-Fall and both belong. The practice is binding. Belief is argued about.

**The faithful speak of a mother.** Benevolent, attentive, generous, and quiet. They say she gives and watches, and that she does not command them. For them, worship is the effort not to waste what she hands you. People do not come to her in fear.

The Motherfaith is the dominant faith in the Turning lands. Other religions are lived beside it. To the people who hold her, she is their Mother.

### Doctrine (as the faithful hold it)

**The Giving.** In the beginning, the faithful say, a person was only what they were born: their Kind, and nothing more. The Leaf-Mother's gift, they say, was **becoming**: the capacity to be given something beyond your birth, a Condition the world would need. They say the first Tree, the Awakening Tree, is where she first reached down. Every town Tree is a **graft**, a cutting of that Tree planted in new ground. The faithful call each one a Hand of the Mother, so that no child is too far for her to reach.

**She chooses, and she chooses well.** Doctrine holds that the colour a child receives is *chosen*: looked-at, intended, fitted to the child and to the town's need. The gifts "fit too well" to be accident (believers point to this; skeptics call it hindsight).

**The Tithe is the weight of a gift.** Every Condition costs upkeep. The faith reads this as honest weight: a real gift has a real charge to carry, and tending it well is the ordinary devotion of a lifetime. The faith's nearest thing to a first commandment is the line, "Tend what you're given."

**The Kept are given wholeness.** Doctrine calls a Kept child, hugged by the leaves and given no colour, a gift of being left whole. This is the question the schism argues hardest.

**People meet her, the faithful say, at the Tree, in the turning of a life, and in the fittingness of things.** She is quiet, and she is not summoned. Doctrine calls that quietness intended: a mother lets her children live. That quiet leaves room for honest doubt.

### Practice

- **The Leaf-Fall.** The central rite, a child's tenth-year solstice Turning. It is at once sacrament, coming-of-age, and the town's great festival.
- **The Tending.** The ongoing, private devotion of paying your Tithe well. The faith frames it so: to tend your Condition honestly is to honour the gift. A Two-Bodied person works in their second body. A Long-Lived person keeps their vigils. The faith calls that life worship, lived as the upkeep itself.
- **The Turning-week festival.** The solstice week around the Leaf-Falls: markets, feasts, the reunion of families, the naming of the year's new Given, the children a colour fell on, and new Kept, the children the leaves hugged. The emotional high point of the calendar.
- **Threshold rites for the Struck.** The Struck receive a Condition away from the Tree, so the faith has grown quiet rites to receive them back. A Struck person is brought to the Tree after the fact, to be **acknowledged** and folded into the community of the Given. The Tree does not Strike, so this is not a Turning. How warmly this is done is itself contested.
- **Tree-tending.** The civic and sacred care of the town's Turning Tree: watering rites, warding against blight, and the rites of planting a new **scion**, a living cutting. The **tree-wardens**, the people who tend the town's Tree, do this work.

The town keeps the **warden-hearth**, the warden's house on the square. During Turning-Week the warden is fed from the festival, the way the town feeds any office it needs that week. **Cutting-leave**, leave to take a cutting, carries a fee. The Seat describes that fee as the cost of carrying-earth, the living soil the cutting travels in. Pilgrimage supports the First Seat. The tree-wardens tend the Trees. The halls that help people meet a Tithe are other people's work.

### The live schism

The faith holds more than one reading. The central argument is sincere on every side, and no one has settled it. That open argument is why the faith endures. It turns on three questions the doctrine does not prove.

**1. The Kept: spared, or slighted?** If the Mother blesses everyone, why are about a quarter Given no colour at all?

- **The Tender reading** (the majority, and the faith's usual teaching): the Kept are given wholeness, a real gift, chosen for them. To be Kept is to be trusted to need nothing added.
- **The Wanting reading** (the minority): the Kept were passed over, loved, but not chosen, and the doctrine of "wholeness" is a kindness the faithful tell them. This reading quietly stings, and shapes how some Kept feel about the festival.

**2. The Struck: still hers, or not?** The Struck receive a Condition the Tree never handed out. Did the Mother reach through the threshold too, or did *something else* give the gift?

- **The Wide-Hand reading:** she meets people at every door, the drowning, the oath, the death. The Tree is only her *ordinary* hand. The Struck are fully hers.
- **The Narrow-Hand reading:** she gives only at the Tree. The Struck received their Condition by *some other means*, which is why the Struck are "a little suspect." This reading is the seed of real social friction toward the Struck.

**3. Does a mind choose at all?** This question is the line between believer and skeptic.

- **Believers:** the fittingness of the gifts, the care of the Kept, the way the Struck feel *received*. These add up to a Someone.
- **Skeptics** say, "It's just what the Trees do." They mean biology, or a phenomenon so old it wears a face people gave it. The grafting is observable horticulture. A choosing mind is an inference they decline to make.

Because you belong by the rite you keep, a skeptic who tends their Tithe and brings their children to the Tree is a good member of the Motherfaith. Belief in the Mother is honoured, and no one is made to hold it. A skeptic may speak their doubt and remain that member.

Each branch is held in good faith.

### Two sayings

**The Tender's blessing**, spoken over each child at the Leaf-Fall:

> *"What falls to you, wear well. What is kept from you, keep. The Hand that reaches is the Mother's own, and the Mother does not reach twice by chance."*

The blessing comforts the child, and it says to tend the gift. It also says the fall was intended. Skeptics do not grant that intention.

**A Kept-child's saying** (folk, half-consoling, half-barbed):

> *"The Tree that gives to all its neighbours held me instead. I have decided that is not the same as being forgotten."*

The saying takes the Tender reading of the Kept and makes it personal, and a little defiant. The words "I have decided" are a choice the speaker makes while the doubt is still there.
