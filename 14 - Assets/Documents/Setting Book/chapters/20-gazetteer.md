# Gazetteer

## Gazetteer types

Every place the Turning has already built shares a grammar, rich town and waiting kitchen alike. A Turning Tree stands at the centre, the living wood a town gathers under when a child comes of age, or else the neighbour's Tree does that work, or the place is still waiting for a Tree of its own. Where a polity can afford it, the Tithe is treated as something the town has to furnish, the upkeep a Condition asks of the person who carries one. A Condition is what a person becomes, at the Tree or later in life, and a Kind is what they were born, so a town does not sort its streets into Kind-nations. Dangerous trades sit out on the edges, houses are built so a becoming has room in them, and the edge of the wood's reach is what draws the map.

The Years of Hands left a road that used to go further than it goes now, a stone that used to mark a day's walk, an upper room that used to sleep pilgrims, and a house that used to be an inn. People reused what the walk left behind, and that reuse is how Ornthael, the years a town is living in now, comes to look like a present.

Kumbaan has no towns of this kind. The storm-walled island keeps a different past, the storm-wall and the moons and the Open Table, which is the faith of its one hall at Ndenjoo, and it does not keep a mile-shrine. It has no Tree, and it is not a mainland power.

Once a town had a Tree of its own, the long walk to the origin stopped being required, and a place can still be doing one of the jobs that walk used to do. Devotion is the extra mile, the walk you take to the Motherwood when you already have a Tree at home. Prestige is the origin-winter you can pay for, a First-Hand year, bought by a household that does not need the walk. Necessity is the neighbour's week, the walk you still have to make because your Tree is missing, or young, or sick. A Held bed is a bed kept for a named person who has not come. An inn on that road can sleep more than one kind of traveler in the same week. The town around the inn still has a job of its own.

## Towns a party can walk into

These are towns a party can walk into. Each entry says where the place sits, what work it does, and who you can meet there. A block set off in quotation marks is a page someone in the place wrote, or a thing someone there said, and the sentences around it tell you what that page is. Where an entry says the wave, it means the slow spread of living wood outward from the Motherwood, the first grove. That spread is still moving. This year's children still walk to a Tree that can speak, or a road is still in use because the walk is not over. It is the present. The Ages tells the spread at length.

### Harrow's Green

Harrow's Green is a live-front Tree-town, the luckiest kind of square Heskoren has. People call Heskoren the Sundered Reach, the frontier continent, and they call this square the neighbour's canopy. A scion, a young cutting of the wood, arrived in the Cut-year 200s, the clerks' numbered count of years, spoke a generation later, and has now Turned local children for a few generations. New towns everywhere still say the Harrow-sentence, *we Turned at Harrow's Tree before we had our own*. Here that sentence is the decade, not an old story. Everyday speech already had *Harrow's green* as a Seat sentence on a Cutting-leave, the paper that says where the wood may be taken. Haelin *(HAY-lin)* is the warden, the person who speaks the fall when the leaves come down. The town is a few hundred hearths around one canopy. It is not the capital of Saelvaeth, the live-front march, and the next scion is not a reward. Natai is the march-town, west of this square on the roads between Sanbreo and the green, and the days between them are not recorded here. This square's water is the Rise-water, the stream off the rise, and the ford is downstream.

The town is a grafted pocket on the Sundered Reach. On Heskoren, settlement clustered where the wood took, because civilization here is a map of surviving Trees, and Harrow's is old enough, for the front, to be a town: a canopy at the centre, a square that used to be a road, and borrowed Turnings coming in from the Three Hamlets Past the Ford every Eolthael, the month the Tree is asked to speak. It is not the end of the wave. It is a Hand that grew up, a grafted Tree a neighbour can already count on. The three hamlets can see the green from the ford, and the Seat has already written that the wood will take here and will not, this year, take there. Harrow's lives inside that sentence whether it asked to or not.

Vaethorn's gospel, the faith of the wait, is spoken on many hearths, and not always as a flag. This square lives in Saelvaeth's orbit, and it is not that march's capital. The Old Ways, the older practice of doors and first meals, are neighbours, not a museum. When a cut from this Tree is set down in an older green, the first meal at that later planting is a live argument.

What the town does each day is the neighbour's week, the week other places send their children here because their own Tree cannot serve. Devotion leaves a ribbon on the stone. Nobody books a winter here.

**Site.** The houses sit on a low rise above the Rise-water. The graft took on the rise, and the old pilgrim-road climbed it because that was the day's water. Towns on Heskoren do not pick a pretty hill and then invent a Tree. The wood picked, and the hearths followed.

Water is the stream, a cistern by the warden-hearth, and the stone in the square, which is still the day's drink for anyone using the road as a mile. Food is the pocket's gardens, ranging beyond the last house, and whatever the neighbour's week brings in Hale-month, the month the roads fill with crowds, dust, and a complaint. Fuel is the same woods the Two-Bodied use as ranging, people who also carry an animal body and work the edge in it. The Taken-In, people whose life is the living green, keep the greens around the Tree from going to seed indoors. There is no resident stiller, one of the surgeons who sit with a dangerous body, and a travelling one is a summer's luck.

The square is the last holy mile. Beyond the boughs the old way is a cart-track to the ford. Children are told the road goes to the Motherwood. It goes to the mill, and then to three hamlets that can see the canopy. Both are true if you keep walking.

**The square.** The town grew around the canopy, house by house rather than on a grid. The oldest houses face the Tree, and later houses filled the wedge between the road and the stream. There are no walls. The boundary is the ranging and the last garden.

The Tree is mature enough to Turn the local children, and young enough that grandparents still remember the borrowed years, when this town's own children had to go elsewhere. Leaf-Fall, the day in a child's tenth year when the Tree gives a colour or a hug, is held in the square because there is nowhere else a crowd fits. A colour is a Condition. A hug leaves the child Kept, unchanged, with no Condition to carry. You can read the town's age in the wood. This is a Hand, not a First, not the original Tree in the grove.

The stone is the last mile-shrine of the road that used to go further. Children have scuffed it climbing, the Road-hands still take water here on the way through with wood, and some years there are ribbons. Haelin wants the stone moved before the next Leaf-Fall, because it stands in the way of the crowd. A hamlet child will climb it and ask how far it is to the Tree. The cistern-dipper is hung with the bowl toward the stream, and Harrow's Green will not say why.

The upper room still has too many beds for the trade the town has now. It opens for the neighbour's week, and it has not become a merchant-loft. A Road-hand sleeps here on the way to a town that was not chosen for the next pot, sometimes with the pot itself, and a pot off Sorim's earth-berth on Orentel's quay may be the one upstairs. Hale-month is dust and extra people. Ledger-month, the quieter month after the crowd, is local. Haelin can tell a pot from a pilgrim, and will not always say.

The greens are potted groves and a garden-common around the Tree, enough to live from, and not a lot you buy a ticket for. They will not follow you home. The Taken-In here are neighbours, not a quarter of the town. The ranging sits at the edge, not in a beast-yard, and the Two-Bodied work the woods that feed the hearth, with no Kind-flag on the trees. The far shed is Returned work, the plague-and-body labour, set downwind on the same logic as a tannery. It is not an infirmary, and it is not a district.

Homes have room for a becoming: a window that opens, a room kept green, a threshold a Long-Lived waits to be asked across. The Long-Lived are the ageless keepers of memory. Mixed Kinds live here. A Fox of the Wilds at the ranging is unremarkable, and a Tengu on a ridge-visit is a guest. What the town can furnish for a Tithe is frontier-warm and thin, the greens, the ranging, and the upper room as a seasonal outlet for whoever is loud that week. There is no counted hall, and no stiller on a licence-wall. You can be too poor to stay whole here, and the neighbour's week will not fix that.

The square is the last mile. The road ends at the boughs, the children of a Hands-town Turn at home, and the hamlets can see the canopy and still send this year's ten-year-olds. A Road-hand is going the other way with wood, because the walk is not over. The Harrow-sentence is this decade.

A warden who pulls the stone down is doing theology with a hammer. They are not wrong that Leaf-Fall needs the space. They are wrong if they think the graves went with the requirement, because the next column that has to walk will miss the water. Haelin knows this and still wants the stone moved.

**Offices.** Haelin's office is practice-first. It is not reserved to a Kind. Haelin speaks the fall truly enough for a front-town, believer-heavy the way a live Tree makes a person, and still has to lodge the neighbour's week. The queue of scions is not Haelin's to fix, and it is the sentence the town lives in. The hearth still keeps the Seat's letter from the Wrong Green, Cut-year 248, in which Nathdo Spoke a hug as if it had been the colour copper-green. Haelin Speaks slowly, and Hale-month does not always forgive the slowness.

Tora *(TOR-ah)* of Brenod is the second person on this square. They walk a neighbour's fall home when they can, carrying the witness of a Turning done here back to the hamlet, and they are here when the stone is to be moved, because the next column still drinks at that stone. Haelin has a Tree. Tora has a ford. It is the same week. Vouching at a distance was invented for a fall at the origin. Here it is a walk back to the ford. If the witness will not walk, the child is seen at the canopy and unvouched at breakfast.

Road-hands sleep in the town that was chosen, on the way to the one that was not. A Door-Keeper at a later planting holds the host's rights if a cut from Harrow's is set in an old green. The later planting is the First Bowl, called Lonasir, in the Far Yield. What that ground still wants is the first meal, or else the planting is a claiming. When the queue is ugly, Saelvaeth's march-voice can be blamed for it. That voice is Dumu, and Dumu stands at Natai, not on this rise.

*A notice chalked on the cistern-wall in Hale-month, Cut-year 387, in Haelin's hand. The stone is not named.*

> *The crowd will need the square. The stone will be walked around until Eolthael, and then we will see. Water stays. Names stay if they were names. Climbing is for after.*
>
> *Beds above: the neighbour's week first. A pot is not a pilgrim. A pilgrim is not a pot. I can tell. I will not always say.*
>
> *If you came from the ford, you are seen here. If you must be seen at home, take someone with you who was here. I will not walk it for you. I have a Tree.*

The notice shows Haelin making room for the crowd, and refusing to walk anyone home. In the square it reads as crowd control. Past the ford it can arrive as a slight.

### The Three Hamlets Past the Ford

The Three Hamlets Past the Ford are a waiting cluster, three hearths rather than one village, and none of them is a capital. The Seat's Cutting-leave has already filed them as a remainder: *the wood will take at Harrow's green. It will not take this year at the three hamlets past the ford — the soil is thin there.* They can see Harrow's Green from the water. They send this year's ten-year-olds to the neighbour's week, or further if Harrow's year is too full, or still the Long Mile, the old walk when the neighbour was a continent away, if the neighbour is only a stick in a pot. The folk hearth-names sit in worn Maiethren, the old tongue as people still speak it. The Seat still does not use them. Its paper still reads "the three hamlets past the ford." Three hearths, a few dozen houses each. You walk from kitchen to kitchen, and you do not walk through a town.

The three hearths sit close enough to share a complaint and far enough apart that a scion cut for "the hamlets" would still start a fight about which green received it. The reach is thin as weather. This year, for this cohort, the hamlets are Kept-heavy and Struck-heavy: a child may stay Kept at the ford while a sibling walks, and a Kin-speaker, someone who speaks for a household's kin, may stand for a Struck person the square will not. Struck means the change came later than a child's standing, by a drowning, an oath, or a death. That thin year is not a re-enactment of the old walking age. This is the present.

They have learned not to say *queue*. They can recite the horticulture-sentence, the one that names the thin soil. Folk-right cutting is a kitchen-knife kept in a drawer, not a rebellion with a flag. Most slips die. The ones that take are how the First Seat, the college of tree-wardens in the Motherwood, learns that it does not own the Tree's tissue, only the authorization to cut it.

Old Ways hearths among them may refuse the neighbour's week on purpose, because their children are already the land's. Motherfaith neighbours read those children as Kept for want of a gift. The disagreement changes who walks, who stands witness afterward, and who will share a kitchen on the return. History does not agree with Vaethorn's guilt, the teaching that the wait is a kind of unworthiness.

They sit on a seam. Saelvaeth's luck is the canopy they can see. Vaelhesk's refusal is in the kitchen. Vaethorn's gospel is on the tongue. Ornled's slate is what they would meet if they walked the other way, toward Sanbreo. The hamlets are making the week themselves. A visitor who calls that devotion has misread the ford, and nobody with money stops here.

**Site.** The ford is the Seat's geography, the place from which you can see Harrow's green, water a mile-shrine would have marked if anyone had set a stone on the waiting side. No one set that stone. Necessity still drinks there, and children still ask how far it is.

The Rise-water comes down from Harrow's rise. In clear weather the canopy is a dark against the far slope. In rain it is a rumour you have to take on trust. The three greens sit on slightly different ground, a wetter shelf, an old taken plot, and a thinner rise past the last hedge. A scion planted "for the hamlets" would still be a fight about which of those grounds is soil.

Food is gardens, a shared mill-path, and ranging that is simply the woods. There is no hall where a person can vent a Far-Voiced hour, the hour someone whose feeling carries in the voice needs to let it out, and there is no ticketed lot of green. What a lapsing Condition can be given is the yard and the neighbour. Here that lapse is a family problem before it is a guild's.

The names are worn and short, stressed on the root, and everyday speech comes first. There is no liturgy in them. To name a hearth in the old holy shape would be to pick a Seat-hearth, and a Seat-hearth would pick a scion.

#### Brenod *(BREN-od)*

Brenod is the sending hearth. Motherfaith in this kitchen is a practice. They walk the child to Harrow's, they want someone who was there to walk back and stand for the fall, and they can recite the thin-soil sentence without using the word *queue*. The kitchen-knife sits in this drawer more often than in the others, which does not mean they have used it. It means they have looked at it.

Brenod keeps the neighbour's week as a duty. A visitor from the core will hear faith. Brenod hears a date in Eolthael. Tora of Brenod is the one who sends. They walk a neighbour's fall home when they can, and they are at Harrow's when the stone is to be moved, because the next column still drinks there.

#### Vaelun *(VAY-lun)*

Vaelun is the yield-hearth. The older word *vael* is what the land keeps, and it is not *vaeth*, the waiting. The Old Ways sit this kitchen. They may refuse the neighbour's week on purpose, because their children are already the land's. A first meal is the host's claim, not a courtesy. A later cut from Harrow's, acting for the origin, still wants the bowl if this ground is an old green.

Vaelun's work is host-rights, not a second Harrow. The Door-Keepers play here, people who keep an ancestor's door and speak for the kin that door is for. The later planting they point at is the First Bowl, a day's walk into the Yield, sometimes two, and not this kitchen. Motherfaith neighbours in Brenod will call Vaelun's Kept children passed-over. Vaelun will not correct them in the Mother's language. Nethiro *(neth-EE-ro)* of Vaelun is a Door-Keeper's cousin. They ask about the bowl before they ask about a leave, they may refuse the neighbour's week on purpose, and they walk to the First Bowl when the bowl is set.

#### Ornath *(OR-nath)*

Ornath is the furthest hearth past the ford. The soil is thinnest here as a feeling, not as a horticulture anyone has locked. Ornath can see Harrow's when the weather is kind, and Ornath can walk the other way, toward Sanbreo, where Ornled keeps the slate, if the congregation's net fails. They have not gone. The road is still a temptation that looks like sense: no gospel, a ledger, and the same thin Trees.

Ornath keeps the wait, and the other road is an exit that is not a Tree. Some people there still wait. Some have stopped saying the Mother's name, and have not started keeping a list either. Baelon *(BAY-lon)* of Ornath has not taken the slate-road, and has looked at it. The cup this kitchen leaves empty on purpose, or does not, is Ornath's.

A pot in a yard, a young failure, or nothing yet: any of the three greens can hold the sick stick for a night. The fight about which green would receive a scion is the cluster's civic life. Naming the hearths did not end that fight.

Harrow's has a Tree, and the square down the slope is Hands, a town that Turns under its own wood. This year's ten-year-olds still walk. A household can split on a shorter road than the old mile: one child goes to Harrow's, one stays Kept at the ford, and a sibling will not walk the same side of the road. Beside those kitchens there is a Kin-speaker who will stand for a Struck person the square will not, and someone who owns a knife they will not call nameless.

There is no stone on the waiting side. There is a cup, and the night before the neighbour's week someone leaves it on the ford-rock. If it fills, they send. If it does not, the tellings do not agree, and the child still goes, or does not, according to the kitchen and not according to the cup. Brenod calls the cup weather. Vaelun calls it the land answering. Ornath has started leaving it empty on purpose, or has not. In a flood the canopy is only a rumour, and the week is still a date, so someone has to decide without seeing Harrow's. If a folk-right slip takes, which green receives it is still undecided.

The Seat's leave still says *the three hamlets past the ford*. Clerks who learn Brenod, Vaelun, and Ornath have learned more than the minute wanted them to.

*Said at the ford, not written, in a Brenod voice, a Vaelun voice, and an Ornath voice, over the same water.*

> *We can see it. That is not the same as having it.*
>
> *The land had them before the cart came. I will not walk a child to a neighbour to be named a Waited.*
>
> *If the cup stays dry I still have to decide. The slate the other way does not ask about soil.*

The voices disagree, and nobody settles it. At dawn someone still decides whether to lift the cup, wake the child, and take the Harrow road.

### The Third Hearth (and Brenthael)

The Third Hearth is a road-house that stayed a hearth. It is the third lodging out from the Motherwood on the Near Mile, the short road in to the origin, and Thilim *(THIL-im)* keeps it. Thilim is Long-Lived, and has kept this house inland since before the First Cut, as later clerks would say. The First Cut is the first time a cutting of the Tree took in new ground, which is where those clerks start their years. Seine's bed is still made. The notes here are smaller than the White Note's, and the shrine is larger. People say *the third hearth*. The road-house cant is *brenhael*, the name any such house can be called. The neighbour-town is Brenthael *(BREN-thael)*. Thilim is not on the quay. Brenthael is not Orenbren's capital, and it does not hold the First Seat. That capital is Rothallo, which people still call the Inner Close. The college sits in the wood beside this country.

This is an old Near Mile house on Maiethorn, three days' walk out from Thaeloren, the grove-name of the Motherwood, toward the approach-country. It still has beds, porridge, a dry room, a green-lot, and a shed. In Brenvaeth, the years when a child's Turning was the walk to the one Tree, the house sold that walk, the one you could not miss twice. In Ornthael the house still opens for Eolthael.

It sits in Orenbren's lodging-country, the stretch of civic beds within a week's walk of the grove. The inns grew a country. This is one house, and the compact does not own it.

The house's older work is devotion, the extra mile walked by people who could already have Turned at home. The loft still sleeps necessity, the people who have to walk, and, rarely, prestige, the households who come because they can be seen coming. Thilim can tell which guest is which, and will not always tell you. The White Note sells the First-Hand year. This house makes the bed.

The Ledger Coast, the coast that prices a house by what its paper earns, calls this a bed-house that failed to become a bank. Thilim asks who will be here at dark. One description prices the building. The other decides whether supper is enough.

**Site.** The Near Mile is a week's door to the origin. Counted from the grove outward, the first hearth is a day out, then the second, then this third. Pilgrims walking in, toward the wood, hit the neighbour-town before they hit the inn.

Brenthael stands a day's walk further from the grove than the inn. It is a Hands-town that grew around a graft planted beside the road. Brenthael's own children Turn at home, under that Tree, and the Mile goes on past the town, through Raillath, then the Mill-hold, then the rest of Maiethorn. Children there are told the road goes to the Motherwood. It does, if they keep walking past the inn, two more hearths, and the wood. Most children of a Hands-town do not.

The Third Hearth kept the older job, which is beds for people still going in. Water is a well in the yard and the stone by the road. The stone is a day's-walk shrine that never got pulled into Brenthael's square, because the inn refused to let the town have it. Thilim uses the stone for water and will not join the argument about pulling it down. Brenthael's warden wants it moved. The stone stands in the way of a crowd that is not this house's crowd. Food is the pot, the green-lot, and whatever the extra mile pays in Hale-month. The shed is still a shed.

**Features.** The upper room still has too many beds, and it has not been converted. It opens in Hale-month and is quiet in Ledger-month. A devotion-column, a necessity-family, and a Road-hand going the other way with a pot can sleep in it in the same week, which is two kinds of need in one afternoon. A prestige household that missed the White Note's winter is rare, and when one lands here it is conspicuous.

Seine's bed is Held, kept made for Seine of the far cape, who was due in a tenth summer and did not come. The mother said next year. Next year was last year, and it has been last year for a length of time Thilim will not convert into Cut-years. It is not a shrine Thilim will admit, and it is a shrine everyone else can see. A name at the door that is not Seine and thinks it is, or that is Seine, is the day Thilim has to decide whether the bed is still Held.

The slate is still kept. The back of the board still does not say what the front says, and the full text is in the Walking Years. The stone stands by the road, not in Brenthael's square, and it has climbing-scuff on it anyway, because people still climb it. Road-hands still aim for the water. Brenthael wants the stone down so the extra mile does not look like a requirement on their approach. Thilim will not move a name.

The green-lot and the shed are still a pot and a shed, not the civic works a town builds so people can pay what a Condition asks. A cutting sits in the pot and is still not a Tree. Thilim missed a green once, and that miss is not the First Cut.

Brenthael's square is a neighbour, not a second kind of house. A Tree stands at the centre, the Mile goes on past the boughs, and Leaf-Fall is the town's civic season, with lodging-guild pressure coming in from Orenbren and no throne under it. Mixed Kinds live there. The town's own version of the extra mile is a holiday. From the last house they can see the inn's stone, and they resent it as a grave that is also an advertisement, a remembered name and a sign that still draws the walk.

Brenthael Turns at home. The Near Mile is optional now, and Orenbren houses the people who still want the origin. The stone still waters a mile. Seine's name is still cut over the door. A necessity-family from thinner soil still pays when they walk home. A Hand can fail. The Mill-hold, two days past Brenthael, put a Hands-town back on the neighbour's week this year. Brenthael's Tree has not sickened, and Thilim does not say the age is over.

The two fates of the same origin still stand next to each other. The White Note collected, on a Strandoren quay, and the Third Hearth kept making the bed. Mataero, from that other house, sees this one only as a warning.

At dark, Thilim sets the yard-broom across the doorway and steps over it, and they will not say why.

**People.** Thilim is Long-Lived, inland, and tired. When Thilim says *I walked*, the phrase can mean four different things, and the four are set out in the Walking Years. A Blood-Read, the memory a Long-Lived can take from blood, will give a true fragment of a walk. It will not tell you which meaning Thilim intends when they make the bed. What Thilim wants is the stone's water for the pot at dark. The document of this house is Seine's bed, made, and it is not a notice anyone can file. A pilgrim who asks where the college is will be pointed at the wood, not at the inn.

Meirim *(may-REEM)* is Brenthael's town-warden, and wants the stone. The office is held by the work, not reserved to a Kind. Meirim speaks a fall in a square that used to be a road. This Hale-month Meirim also has a necessity-column coming in from the Mill-hold, people still walking because they have to. Orenbren's lodging custom makes hospitality into rent in Eolthael. Thilim charges less than that custom, and is called a failure for the difference.

*A chalked complaint from Meirim's warden-hearth, carried to the Third Hearth and left under the stone's lip. It is Cut-year 387, after Hale-month. Thilim has not answered on paper.*

> *The crowd will need the approach. Your stone is a mile that is no longer owed. Water we have. Names we can keep on a ribbon in the square, which is where a remembered name belongs.*
>
> *You house the extra mile. We Turn at home. If you make a shrine of a bed, that is a house matter. A stone in the road is a town matter.*
>
> *We are not pulling a grave. We are pulling a requirement that has ended.*

The complaint shows that Brenthael wants the stone out of its crowd's way, and that it offers a ribbon in the square as the place for the remembered name. The Held bed is still made. If Seine comes, the stone will still be standing in the way of a crowd that is not hers.

### Ornsael

Ornsael *(ORN-sael)* is a Rain-Shadow walk-hold whose drink is leaving. The name keeps the conservative drift of the old tongue, *orn* for land and *sael* for sowing, the sown-ground. Everyday speech says Ornsael. A traveller from the core who says "the west-road well" is corrected, because the well is why anyone stopped and it is not why they stay this year. The town is a few hundred hearths, thinner than the core's, and it is not Saelthael's capital. The three corner seats are Eolvaeth, Orentel, and Maiethlir. Larbril is Saelthael's city, where the west road meets the Well-wash, short of the pass. Ornsael is the smaller well-town, farther into the dry.

East of Maiethorn's Rain-Wall, the range that leaves one side of the Motherland wet and the other dry, on the leeward side away from the Old Crossing, the country is dry hills and sand, and the towns sit at wells. Ornsael is one of those wells. A Tree stands beside it, young for the Motherland and old enough to have Turned local children for a generation. It Speaks most years, and a missed Eolthael counts as weather, since the crisis on this square is the drink.

Saelthael is the polity of people who still sow. Ornsael is a square of that habit, not a throne. Plenty sits on the other side of the range. Pilgrimage here used to be a walk the town could not retire, because the wood was thin. The wood grew up, and the well did not. Children still go west in the neighbour's week, not to hunt a colour, but because the Hale-month crowd needs water this well cannot host. Core pilgrims who arrive in Eolthael expecting a holiday extra mile find a necessity-column already on the west-road, and they are shocked. They thought waiting was a Heskoren feeling. They were not wrong about waiting. They were wrong about what is waited for.

Fox of the Sands hearths are densest here, and that is the terrain, not a flag. A town with no fox-born would be the thing worth asking about. Mixed Kinds drink the same share. The town's daily work is meeting need. The west-road is the neighbour's week lived as water. Devotion belongs to people who already have a healthy canopy and a wet well. Nobody books a dry hill.

**Site.** The well is why anyone stopped. The range takes the rain, and this side keeps the dust. The houses gathered around the water. The graft was married to the well on purpose, because living earth wants a drink in sand-country as much as a child does. The Hand took, and now the well is what is failing.

The west-road is the main street. It used to be how you reached a healthier canopy, then how you reached the origin, then how you reached the core's Hands-towns after those towns stopped coming east. It is still how this year's extra people go, when the share will not stretch through Hale-month. Beyond the last house the road is a pale cut toward the range-pass. East of the well it peters into tracks and Taken-In plantings that used to look like stubbornness and now look like a second drink.

Water is the well, a few cisterns, and the dew-catch on the north walls. The cisterns are the civic argument: who filled them, who may draw, and whether a planting may have a bucket before a child. Food is terrace-gardens, goats, and whatever the west-road pays when it still pays. Fuel is dung, and whatever the range-trade carries over the pass. The Taken-In are the backbone here, because thirsty land honours green, and they are not a nation. Their pots also need water. There are no walls. The boundary is the place where the well's water no longer reaches and the dust does.

**The square.** The Tree at the well is the civic and sacred heart, a reasonable Hand. Most Eolthaels it Turns. The town-warden Speaks when there is a colour and does not invent one when there is not, and that is ordinary work, not the town's plot. A missed week still shuts the Given-door, the one week in which a child can be Given a colour. Struck remains the later mercy. There is still no second-chance year. The square does not turn on that warden.

The well is the day's drink and the year's fear. A rope hangs in it with knots at the old water-lines, and the share is chalked on the well-lip, not on a warden's lath. Children who have heard a stone-story have scuffed the lip by climbing. The west-road carries mile-markers toward the pass, and one stone still stands in the well-square because the town grew around a day's water. Ribbons on that stone point west, which children from the core find backwards. The column that leaves at dusk is the people the share refused.

The well-market is stalls in the morning shade. Fox-born are densest in the crowd, and they are not gated. Stock ancestries drink here, a Tengu comes through on a pass-errand, and a Selkie who married in from the Hinge Shore is dry and funny about it. The greens are potted, hauled, and argued over, with no lush lot to retreat to. Lesna's planting is the only green for a day's walk in some weeks, and it drinks. The greens-keepers do not run a chapter of their craft here. The ranging is dry hills and night-work, where the Two-Bodied know the difference between a sand-fox and a Fox, and it sits at the edge. The far shed is Returned work, downwind of the well, because you do not put the dead on the drink. There is no infirmary. A travelling stiller is a pass-story.

Homes bend to the change a person is in the middle of: north-side cool rooms, green pots beside the doors, and shutters that open for a Far-Voiced hour because there is no hall to book. At street height this is Saelthael's own combination, thin infrastructure, a high theology, and very little government.

This is the Motherland, where a town Tree was supposed to be enough. The extra mile is supposed to be optional, and this canopy Turns at home. The well is dropping. Children and Hale-month guests still go west, and the road that used to go further still does. The word *sael* is the same word as in Saelvaeth on the live front, sowing said on two continents. If the wood sickened, a Hand could fail. It has not. The drink has.

Vaethorn's guilt, imported to sand that never asked for it, is the wrong sermon for this square. Waiting read as unworthiness was about a colour. This year the town empties itself for water. A core hymn about thin soil will be heard as someone who has never drawn a bucket.

The well-rope is knotted at last year's water-line, and this year's knot is wet even when the water stands below it. Ornsael will not say why. Theisva moves the knot, Lesna says the planting tastes the same when the knot is wet, and Bovaer puts sand in a west-facing shoe and does not call that a rite.

**Offices.** Three people keep the argument at the well. Theisva *(THAYS-vah)* keeps the share, speaks the conservative way, and will cut a guest's bucket before a child's and a planting's before a guest's. Theisva will not pretend the chalk is a hymn, and is tired of being asked whether this is fox-country. Theisva may be Sands-Fox, and may not. Either way the question is the slight. Lesna *(LES-nah)* is Taken-In, and keeps the planting east of the well. Lesna wants one more bucket than the share allows, because the green is also the town's food, and will not follow you home. Home is dust if the planting dies. Bovaer *(bo-VAIR)* still sends people west. Some years the Tree has Spoken and Bovaer sends anyway, because the drink is the limit. Core pilgrims hear faith. Bovaer hears a date and a water-mark.

Some years the witness of a fall is local. Some years a person has to walk home from a wetter town over the pass, not because the wood failed, but because the week could not be hosted. A vouching that has to travel, on the Motherland, is still the tell that the age is unfinished. Road-hands are rare on this road, because the circuit prefers the next town that can pay. A pot meant for Ornsael is a fight with the queue. Saelthael's custom is light, and the wells are civic fact. The well-share is the town-law. There is no list of persons. Threnmaieth would like one. Plenty on the other side of the range is Maiethvael's problem, not this well's.

At first light Theisva draws, the mark is lower than yesterday, and a crowd is already there with jars. A night-bucket given to Lesna's planting, and refused to a child, is a fight, because the planting is also breakfast. At dusk Bovaer's column is ten-year-olds, Hale-month guests, and a core pilgrim who came for a hymn and is being sent on. The lip can shear and take the rope with it, and for an hour the town has a hole and no share.

*Well-share marks, chalked on the lip in Hale-month, Cut-year 387, in Theisva's hand. The Tree is not mentioned.*

> *Dawn-mark: below last knot. Child-jars first. Guest-jars after the planting's one bucket, and I will hear about the planting.*
>
> *West-road at dusk if the noon-mark does not rise. Bovaer has the names. I have the rope.*
>
> *A man from the Reckoning asked to name the well on a roll. We named the well Ornsael, which it already was. He wrote a colour-year. The sand does not keep his hand. The knot is wet. I did not wet it.*

Theisva is keeping the water, and will not file a year. Lesna will read the marks as a slight on the green. Bovaer will already be on the road.

## The three seats

### Eolvaeth (Vaethorn's seat)

Eolvaeth *(EOL-vaeth)* is the Waiting Lands' seat, a pilgrim-edge town and not a live-front luck. The name joins a hinge to the waiting, and folk-speech shortens it to Elvaeth *(EL-vaeth)*. People walk here in order to wait, rather than because the canopy is a certainty. Vaethod *(VAYTH-od)* keeps the square. A couple thousand souls live here in Ledger-month, and more in Hale-month. This town is not Harrow's Green, not the three hamlets, and not Saelvaeth's march, and this Tree does not have Harrow's luck. It is the capital a poor land gets when the capital is a congregation, and it is not a throne.

The town sits in a vale behind Heskoren's coast that faces Strandoren, and it is one of the earlier pots on this continent, a graft of the Cut-year 200s that took, spoke a colour in some years, and never grew into a neighbour's certainty. The live front moved on along the wave, and the waiting stayed, and congregation made a country of that staying. Vaethorn is the gospel of the wait. Eolvaeth is the square the gospel stands in.

Governance here is almost absent. What sits in the vale is a Tree, a spring, a gift-hall that is the town's worship, and the camp-streets of people who came for a hinge and did not leave. Custom outruns written law. The town-warden is Hands-folk, so the office is kept by practice and not by a crown.

Saelvaeth's luck is a different weather, further along the wave. Harrow's Green is a Hand that grew up. The three hamlets can see that canopy from a ford. Eolvaeth cannot see it, and does not want to, because wanting it would be admitting the wait is a queue. The gospel says stay. Horticulture, in some years, says send.

The town's older work is devotion, the wait kept as a pilgrimage you can walk to. Visitors from thinner country are usually here because they have to be, and the town will not call that necessity. Nobody books a thin vale.

**Site.** Two tracks meet at a spring in a fold of upland, and the graft was married to that water because living earth wants a drink and a child does. Settlement clustered where the wood took, and then the camps filled the fold, because a thin Tree that might speak is still the oldest Hand a long walk can reach without crossing into Saelvaeth's orbit.

The spring is the day's drink, and it is not a mile-shrine stone. Ribbons belong on other squares. Children leave a wet leaf on the lip the year the Tree does not speak. Travellers from the core will try to climb something, and there is nothing here to climb. The Long Mile used to pass this vale when the neighbour was a continent. The requirement to walk it ended, and the wait did not.

Food is the vale's gardens, ranging on the slopes, alms from the gift-hall pot, and whatever Hale-month guests bring. Fuel is the same woods the Two-Bodied use as wild-leave. There is no resident stiller, and a travelling blessing-surgeon is a decade's luck. The Taken-In are the backbone, Mother's green held in common, honoured, and not a nation. There are no walls. The boundary is the last camp-street and the ranging. You can walk in, someone will feed you, and no clerk will write you down.

**The square.** The town grew around the canopy. The oldest houses face the Tree, later houses filled the wedge between the tracks, and the camp-streets run up the fold. They are pilgrim-camps that learned to winter, set one against the next until they look like a town and still smell like a season.

The Tree is the heart of the place even when the wood is only a maybe. Grandparents remember a fall. A Seat clerk would not call it a First. Some years it Turns. Some years the colour is late, or thin, or Vaethod will not call a fall that is not one. A missed Eolthael still shuts the Given-door. Struck remains the later mercy, and there is still no second-chance year. The spring is why the vale is here. In a wet-leaf year the leaves are already on the lip, and children who have heard the stone-story have scuffed it anyway. The gift-hall is worship and venting in the same room, a hall kept as the congregation. A Far-Voiced hour here is a hymn whether they meant it to be. There is no counted hall, and no ticket at the door. Hale-month still doubles the people, and Ledger-month is local and guilty about the empty rooms. The green around the Tree is enough to live from, and it will not follow you home. The ranging is on the slopes, at the edge, with no Kind-flag on the trees. Returned work sits downwind of the spring, and it is not a district.

Homes have room for a becoming. A Fox of the Wilds at the ranging is unremarkable, a Tengu on a spine-visit is a guest, and a Selkie who married in from the coast is wet about the vale and kept anyway. What people need in order to stay whole is alms and congregation, real warmth with real want beside it. You can be too poor to stay whole here, and the gospel will console what it cannot furnish.

Some children did Turn at this Tree, so the Long Mile is supposed to be optional and the hinge is supposed to have arrived. The Tree is not a certainty. Camp-streets still fill for Eolthael, and some years a cohort still goes toward Saelvaeth's luck, or further if that luck is full. The gospel says wait, and the Seat's horticulture, on a thin year, says send. Vaethod has done both, and the town remembers which summers.

People walk to Eolvaeth to wait together, and that is how a poor land gets a capital without a crown. It is not Orenbren's extra mile, because those people already have a healthy canopy, and it is not the hamlets' necessity, because those kitchens can see Harrow's. It is the wait made into a destination, which is also how scarcity becomes unworthiness if you let the sermon land.

A wet leaf lies on the spring, and pilgrims from the core ask if it is a ribbon. Eolvaeth will not say. In a year when the Tree speaks, there is no leaf. In a year when it does not, there are too many to count, and no one writes the number down.

**Offices.** Vaethod is Hands-folk, and will not write a colour that did not happen, even when a visiting clerk wants the hinge tidy. The year they send a cohort toward Saelvaeth is the year the gospel and the wood disagree. They have not resigned. Some years the witness of a fall is local, and some years it has to walk home from a luckier Hand. A vouching that has to travel, on a gospel capital, is the tell that waiting is still a road. Road-hands sleep in the town that was chosen early and stayed thin, on the way to a town that was not chosen this year. Vaethorn's custom is a light state, a hot faith, and alms, and no list of persons. Threnmaieth would like a list. Ornled would not understand why anyone stays. The unvouched Struck, people the later mercy took who have nobody to stand for them, are the ache this seat meets with faith rather than with a guild.

The wants on the square are carried by people. Vaethod wants Saelid's stall to keep feeding its winter guests. Nedaen wants the gift-hall pot to feed a child through Ledger-month. Heiral wants a marriage witnessed without Mother-kept pity, the pity a Motherfaith neighbour spends on a child the Tree hugged. Daron wants a corpse named so a grain-debt dies. Hithaen is eating in the gift-hall this Hale-month, and Vaethod has not decided which kind of guest that is. A cohort that was sent can walk back in while the notice is still being chalked, some of them Given at a luckier Hand and one of them unvouched, and Saelid's stall feels it by evening. Hale-month can put a fire through the tents that learned to winter, and the spring is the drink, not the bucket-line. A clerk from the Reckoning can ask to name the spring while the leaf is already on the lip.

*A notice chalked on the gift-hall door at Eolthael, Cut-year 387, in Vaethod's hand. The Tree is not named as sick.*

> *The hinge is this week. Beds on the camp-streets first. Someone who came to wait is a guest. Someone who came to count us is a clerk, and I can tell.*
>
> *If the Tree speaks we stay. If it does not I will not invent a colour. I will not send until the week is over. I have sent before. I will not pretend I have not.*
>
> *The spring is not a stone. Leave the leaf if you must. Do not ask me what it buys. It buys nothing. That is why we leave it.*

The notice is Vaethod saying, in their own chalk, that a cohort has been sent from this square before. It is good for one week, and by the time the gospel repeats the wording, it reads as a slight.

### Orentel (Lestrand's seat)

Orentel *(OREN-tel)* is the Ledger Coast's seat, a salt quay rather than a desk wearing a crown. The name is the witnessing-Tree, with the *th* worn down to a *t*. Coast-folk say the Salt Quay and mean the city. Notes and berths meet here, and no house sits a throne. The White Note House is on the third quay, north side, placed and not crowned. Sorim *(SOR-im)* is the factor people ask for, the one who charters berths and files who occupies the water. Ledan is not the government. Tens of thousands live here, and the old Tree-town is the smaller half.

Orentel stands on an Old Crossing estuary, the oldest water in the world, on Strandoren, facing Maiethorn, on the shore where the Salt Walk used to land. When that walk stopped being required on this coast, the berths did not empty. They changed jobs. A Tree-town grew a quay, then a second quay, then a run of warehouses along the tide, until the old square was the smaller half of a salt-city.

Lestrand is guild self-rule carried as far as it goes, and Orentel is the square that proves it. A charter-council of houses and craft-guilds speaks for the city. There is no crown, and there is no Threnhael, the roll that writes every person down. Citizenship by witness runs smoothly because the reach is high, with a mature Hand and the city's own children Turned at home. In a city this size the witness is a hearth, the quay-gang that stood, not the whole crowd sharing one memory. The three pillars of that witnessing are a Long-Lived's memory, a Bound person's sworn word, which hears a lie as it is told, and a Far-Voiced person's feeling, which is hard to fake once it is in the room. An unvouched person is a transaction that cannot clear.

The city's daily work is prestige. A First-Hand year is held as a product and sold to households who can pay to walk a walk they do not need. Sorim assigns the empty Eolthael berths on the first quay. The White Note writes the terms. A Netstrand hull, from the Night Shore, may be the wood in the water. Orenbren takes the sleep at the origin. Three desks, one commodity. Necessity still eats in the lofts, and Mataero files that necessity as occupancy, the count of who is in a room.

**Site.** People stopped here because the estuary gave them deep water, a sheltered sound, and a tide that writes the working day. The graft took on the rise above the first quay in Cut-years 40 to 89, a wealthy coast and a paying next, and the oldest houses still face that Tree. The road used to come inland from the berth toward a healthier origin-canopy. Then this canopy grew up, and then the berth became the point.

The estuary faces east, toward the Old Crossing, and the Chart-run, Strandoren's interior river, comes in from the west. A sail from the Hinge Shore takes three to five days in fair weather and arrives at the Crossing-mouth. Denlad is a side inlet about a day's sail short of that mouth, on the way in, and it is not a berth in the mouth itself. Inland boats and the river-plain come by the Chart mouth. Lunbra, where Trenledd keeps its roll, is four to eight days upriver, and the food on the quay is that plain as much as it is the catch off the Crossing.

Water is the tide, the cisterns on the rise, and the paid wells in the warehouse stretch. Food is the Chart-run plain, the catch, and whatever the hinge pays. Fuel is drift, peat, and what the factor-houses book. The Taken-In rent leaf-lots by the season. They are here because they were Given here, because grain-hands winter, and because the lot is a job, not because the city is a forest. Selkie hearths are densest on the water, a lot of shoreline and not a gate. No walls here matter. The boundary is the last crane and the ranging-yard inland, and the council charters the guild, never the person.

The Rise is where the graft took, and the square still Turns local children for free. The Drop is the street from the Rise down to the first quay, and the tension of the city is that one hill. Free colour at the top, held berths at the bottom, and a family can stand on the Drop and see both prices in the same glance. Sorim's slate starts at the bottom. The Tree-tenders' free Turning starts at the top. Neither of them owns the street. The first quay is the Salt Walk's old landing: berths 1 to 4 are held in Eolthael, and berth 5 is earth. The third quay, north side, is the White Note, a desk and not the council. Hallowquay is a lesser inner quay, not a second city. A fox-born broker might still be called of Hallowquay. That byname was already in the world. The seat is Orentel. The inland yard is the ranging ground behind the Rise. Leaf-lots and the ticket-infirmaries sit downwind, off the Drop's seaward end. They are not the tension. The tension is the hill.

**The square.** The old town on the rise gathers around the Tree. The quays run along the tide. The warehouses jumped to the next stretch of bank wherever a fire or a silted berth made that stretch cheaper than rebuilding, and the interesting streets are the edges where the rise meets the salt.

The Tree is still the civic heart, even when the money has gone downhill, and the Hand is mature. Leaf-Fall is a civic and commercial milestone, because a family's colour is watched for the guild it opens, and the day is not a warm festival. The square still Turns local children for free, and the quay cannot price that without noticing itself. On the first quay, empty Eolthael berths are held as prestige, which is Sorim's occupancy, and wool through Hale-month covers a shrine. There is a smell of earth out of season when a pot, and not a child, is the passenger. A west-water hull sitting in an Old Crossing hold is a Netstrand charter. The White Note is Ledan's desk, with Mataero's loft in the rise behind it, twelve beds, the north bed not to let, and century instruments in the house. Lestrand will only count the house as success.

Downhill from that free Turning, the release-house tickets the hall as a playhouse and an outlet, superb if you can pay, and the queue outside it is the gap between having and not having, at eye level. The leaf-lots are rented green rather than the Mother's common, and they will not follow you home, home being a loft if they did. The beast-yard at the inland edge hires out ranging, so Two-Bodied haulage becomes a trade dynasty if the match was priced that way, while the Stillers' ticket-infirmaries sit on the downwind edge, excellent for those who can reach them, with the longest queue in the city. Returned work sits past the last crane, on the same logic as a tannery.

Homes have room for a becoming. Mixed Kinds live here, and a Sands-Fox at a desk is unremarkable if they have warranting. In the alleys the council does not charter, what moves is sold vouching and smuggled green. The Standing Trade is there if the alley needs a face.

The canopy Turns children at home, and the Salt Walk is optional. Empty Eolthael berths are still shaped like a landing, pots still take a child's space, and a First-Hand year can still be booked. If Orentel's Tree sickened, this quay would be the neighbour's week. It has not sickened, and the council does not say the age is over. It reports occupancy. The city sells a walk people do not need, and that is how a rich coast gets a capital without a crown. When a hymn lands here, the release-house schedules it. Necessity in the loft is Mataero's concern, and it is not the city's face.

Salt sits on the Tree's roots in Eolthael. People say it is the tide, and the tide does not reach the rise. Orentel will not say why the salt is there. A hall-keeper will schedule around it. A Book-hand will not put it in a term.

The charter-council turns one chair to the wall before a meeting and turns it back when the room is empty, and will not say why. From there the Drop is a cook's step, the pot set on the rise side and ladled toward the quay, which the cook will not explain either. The first quay's crane-crew taps the hook once against the beam before a lift, and once the release-house has shut its doors the last bench in the queue is left unroped. A leaf-lot renter knocks twice on the gate-post and does not knock again, and on a coughing morning the ticket-infirmary hangs a dry cloth on the downwind nail and leaves it after the queue has gone. Hallowquay sets the first basket of the morning with the lid underneath, and the inland yard leads the hired beasts out and back in before the first job. Asked, none of them will say why.

**Offices.** The charter-council watches the guild and never the individual. Sorim can be asked who chartered the empty berths this Eolthael, and the answer comes back as occupancy. Sorim is not Ledan. Tree-tenders are Lestrand's word for the wardens. They Speak the fall as a civic fact, and a colour that opens a guild is still a colour. At the White Note, Ledan writes and Mataero lets. The north bed is house custom. The Book-Hands are ordinary on this quay. A signing-watch here is prestige, if you book it. They do not rewrite a Bound term, and they do not sit a seat. Tesara sits the stillers' shed, where success is silence. Road-hands are rare. A pot on an Eolthael berth is cargo to the clerk. The Tithe-poor fade while the city steps around them, and that is the injustice. Nothing is policed. Everything is priced.

The wants on the quay do not agree with each other. Mataero is trying to get Sorel off a century-note that Sorel, still alive, is still on, while Sorim wants berths 1 to 4 held and counts the hold-fee as the house-share. In the stillers' shed Tesara, for whom success is silence, wants Valen stamped before the floor takes the master back. Lasole is asking the leaf-lots for one more season of credit, and Didel of Hallowquay is asking for a kin-berth for a wedding rather than a First-Hand year. Reimaethe lets a room on the rise, and Rosire works the ticket-infirmary downwind of the shed. A crane can fail on berth 5 while a family who wanted 1 to 4 watches the earth come up. The tide can fill Tesara's shed without pausing the catching-year, the week a person just Struck is kept there. Didel's wedding and a First-Hand boarding can want the same morning.

*A berth-slate, first quay, Eolthael through Hale-month, Cut-year 387. Sorim's hand. The Tree is not mentioned.*

> *Eolthael berths 1–4: held. Do not load wool. A First-Hand year is not cargo. I have explained this to the crane.*
>
> *Berth 5: earth. A pot is cargo. I have explained this to the family who wanted 5 for a child. They could not hold 1–4. I did not invent the hold.*
>
> *The rise still Turns for free. That is a Tree-tender matter. The quay is a hold matter. If you need both in one week you are at the wrong desk.*
>

The slate shows that Sorim says the hold was not Sorim's doing, and that the family without a berth is still without one. It does not mention the salt on the roots. Ledan reads a neighbour who cannot convert. Mataero reads occupancy. Their readings call for different action on the same berth.

#### The White Note House (placed, not crowned)

The White Note House is a road-house that became a desk, and it is still a house you can walk. It is a deathless house of the trusted note, kept by a Long-Lived so the paper does not die when a mortal clerk would. Ledan writes at the quay-desk. Mataero *(mah-TAY-ro)* lets the loft and is still junior to Ledan. People say *the White Note*. Lestrand, the coast that counts a house by its success on paper, still says *the bed-house* and means the bank. The founding is about Cut-year 80, about that year and not a sharper date. The present house-year is 307, the same summer as Cut-year 387. The conversion query headed "the 200th summer" is archival, from Cut-year 280, and it is not the count the desk is in now. The house stands on Orentel's third quay, on the north side, with the inland loft in the rise behind the salt. The desk is not the crown.

The fortune here is the same fortune as the Third Hearth, come later and to a quay. In the years when Turning was a walk you could not miss, the house was beds, porridge, and credit for that walk. When the walk stopped being required in the core, the Long-Lived still remembered every debtor, and the paper outlived the pilgrimage season. The desk sat down on this quay because that is where notes move now. Twelve beds stand in the rise behind the salt, and their window looks at a Tree that still Turns for free.

What the house sells now is century instruments, notes written to be trusted across a long run of summers, and the terms on a First-Hand year, credit for a stay at the origin the buyer does not need. It also sells loft-terms to wool through Hale-month, and it still writes the old kind of note for a necessity-family, people who have to eat now and pay on the home-walk. Same paper, two jobs. The house does not hold the berth, and it does not own the hull. A winter booked from this coast is one product with three desks. Netstrand supplies the ship, this house writes the term, and Sorim holds the first-quay berth. Orenbren takes the sleep at the far end. Ledan will convert the year from one count to another. They will not tell the crane which berth is empty.

The north bed is how a bank proves it used to be an inn. Empty is not a shrine, Mataero says, because empty is a bed they are not charging for. Ledan says the house is the house it always was. The founding-book has a name in it, and Thilim, inland, still holds a far-cape name the White Note does not. If you ask Mataero whose name the north bed is, you are told it is house custom. If you ask Ledan, they will want that name spoken once, aloud, and they will not speak it first. Ledan wants the founding-name spoken once over the north bed, so they can stop being the only memory of it.

There are two doors and one house, and you can use both in a morning if the rise is not wet. The street door opens on the salt, and there is no chapel-front. The counting-room smells of ink and of the tide. Ledan's stool faces the door, not the water, because they have seen the water. A second stool is there for a junior who is not Mataero, Mataero having the inland key, and a chest under the desk holds the founding-book. The Held names in it are credit-tokens, names the house still extends credit against, and they are not read aloud when a term is being written.

A customer comes in with a walk, a wool-season, a grandchild's debt, or a court-slip, and Ledan asks which count is to be written: the house-year, a Cut-year, or a third date that a Lestrand court invented last generation. Ledan can convert among them, and will make you say which one you want. If the paper is a First-Hand year, the term is written here and the berth is not. Ledan will send you to Sorim for the hold, and will not walk you there. If the paper is a necessity-note, the back still says *eat now* and the front says a house-year, and Mataero may already have given the family a bed. A smell of earth out of season means a pot, not a child, is the passenger on the next term. The clerk files that pot as cargo. At the next berth over, a First-Hand year is not cargo.

The way up from the third quay is a walk, not a secret stair. There are twelve beds, eight let and four open, and the north bed is one of those four. Three of the open beds are for Eolthael, and the north bed is not to let. The window beside that bed looks at the Tree on the rise. Mataero's slate hangs on the inland door, and it reads *let / open / not to let*. Wool, a First-Hand household, a necessity-family, and a Road-hand carrying a pot can all want a bed in the same week. Mataero files who is in which bed, and will not copy the north bed onto a term. Lestrand will only count this house as a success. Orentel's charter-council will only count it as one ticket among many.

On a usable afternoon in Cut-year 387, morning at the desk is a conversion. Ledan writes a Hale-month loft-term as a Cut-year because a court on the rise asked for that count. Mataero brings the occupancy numbers and does not sit. A necessity-family from a thin year that Vaethod sent is already in a bed, and their grandchild cannot point to the ford named on the back of the note. Someone else's note can be due in the same hour. Sorel's century-instrument is not due then, but it is still on the paper. A court-clerk can have a year converted, and cannot have the Held names read out as a performance. Two lets can be claimed against one bed, and wool and a neighbour's week do not share a mattress just because they share a job-name. If the north bed is occupied, the person in it may be a guest, a thief, a junior's mistake, or a name that came back, and the house is a house again, in public. Ledan stays until the tide-bell. Mataero walks the rise and checks the north bed. Empty is the custom. Occupied is a session, because the room has become a scene the moment someone is in that bed.

### Maiethlir (Threnmaieth's seat)

Maiethlir *(MAI-eth-lir)* is the Tallied Crown's seat, an origin pilgrimage-town under a roll, and not the grove. The name is the Mother's thaw. This is where the crown started counting a hearth that was already holy, not where it acquired Thaeloren. The First Seat sits in the Motherwood beside this country and is not its crown. Rithim *(RITH-im)* is the Speaker on the square, the person who names the colour. In a small town the warden Speaks. Here the office has its own person. The city is smaller than Orentel, and older. The clerks will tell you a number after every Eolthael, and the number changes. This is not the Third Hearth, and it is not Brenthael.

Maiethlir is a thaw-river town on Maiethorn, in the Sacred Core and not in the grove. Snowmelt comes down from Lirorn's Rain-Wall, and the Core-thaw carries that melt toward the Old Crossing. The city is an origin-town of Cut-years 0 to 40, one or two days' walk from the wood, and it sat down where the water slowed enough to hold a Hand, on a different road than Orenbren's Near Mile. Orenbren lodges the approach. This river counted the people who stayed.

The extra mile used to come through with the thaw. When Hands-towns made the origin-walk optional, the lodging emptied toward Orenbren's beds, and the rooms did not stay empty. Clerks filled them, and a pilgrimage-street learned house-names on a tablet.

Threnmaieth coils near the sacred centre and wants everyone on the Threnhael, the roll of names, colours, and marriages of heritable Conditions once they have been noticed. Maiethlir is the square that proves the coil without owning the Tree. The colour that falls is a hymn and a line. Maiethvael shares the Mother and refuses the list, the same hymns on the opposite paper. Its people live downriver, along the first day below the Down Gate. Their capital, Seinbrun, is more than a day from Maiethlir, off the water, and this gate does not lead to it. How much more than a day is not recorded.

The street's older work is devotion. The extra mile is kept as a civic season, and the roll sits on top of it. A family from thin up-range soil still sleeps in an uncounted loft, and a counted winter can be booked for a price. The Threnhael sits on that season and does not replace it.

**Site.** The Core-thaw is why anyone stopped. There was a ford, a mill, and a Hand planted beside the water, because the first carrying followed living earth and a day's drink. The Motherwood is a dark on one horizon. You can walk to the grove, and most children of a Hands-town do not. The college is in those clearings, and it is not this square.

Water is the river, the conduits the crown maintains, and the cisterns in the old pilgrim-streets. Food is the Core's plenty. Fuel is the wood's edge, which the Seat will not let a crown cut without a leave. Proximity is pressure, not ownership. The Taken-In work the tended green the crown furnishes, and they are not a nation. A wall exists, and it is old. It was for floods and for a generation of argument, not for a siege the Core no longer expects.

The Core-thaw runs west, toward the Old Crossing, and the city sits where that water slowed. From Thaeloren you come along the Grove Bank, one or two days on the river road, which is not the Near Mile, and you arrive with the Motherwood as a dark behind you. The Down Gate opens downstream onto the Down-Bank. The counted conduits thin within a day, and the Hinge Shore is still several days of bank, orchard, and mud beyond that. The Wall Path goes upstream toward Lirorn and the snow that feeds the thaw. Vehaela's thin-snow winters come this way. It is not the Noon Pass, the old high road over the range. It is the path the river's source already implies. Inside the wall, the Slow Water is the ford and the mill-reach, where a Hand could be planted because the carrying had stopped being a current. The Loft Row is the old extra-mile, one street from the Tree to the tablet-hall. The upper rooms that slept pilgrims are clerk-dorms now. The tension of the city lives on that street: a hymn at the square end, a copy at the hall end, and beds between them that the roll has not all found. Rithim Speaks at the Tree. The clerk waits on the hall side of the same street.

**The square.** The streets ring the canopy and then run out along the river and the old extra-mile. The order is the order a counted city grows into, an accretion that learned its numbers, not a grid drawn last week.

The Tree is a mature Hand, not the First. Leaf-Fall is a joyous holy day that is also a registration. The colour is Spoken, and a clerk waits to copy it. Rithim Speaks, and does not always let the copy happen in the same breath. The tablet-hall is the Threnhael's local office, beside the Tree and not the Tree. Outsiders hear the work called the Counting. The clerks say they keep the realm hale. The counted hall is the outlet and the attendance in one room. A Far-Voiced hour is furnished through the crown's channel, and to be cut off is framed as your own lapse. A Held bed in the old lofts would offend the roll, and the roll has not found all of them. The Watching keep a room under a chapel the wardens recut, filed as the Grown-Over. The tended green will not follow you home, and home is on file if it did. Two-Bodied leave at the edge is noticed, and that notice is not a Kind-flag. The crown's stiller is excellent medicine, downwind, through the channel, and the Bound are watched past their hazard, the watching dressed as safety. Returned work is kept past the last conduit, and filed there.

Homes accommodate a becoming, and the accommodation is on the tablet. Mixed Kinds live in the city. A Fox of the Snows, down from Lirorn, is a guest with a name. The clerks will tell you a number, and it changes after every Eolthael.

The canopy Turns children at home. The Near Mile is Orenbren's work, the extra mile is a civic season in Maiethlir, and the Motherwood is a walk, not a requirement. The thaw-road still brings a family in need when the up-range soil is thin, an uncounted loft still smells of Hale-month, and a Hand can fail. The roll would like those summers filed. People still come because the hinge is holy. The Threnhael is a tablet beside the Tree, not a record put there instead of it. That is how a pious core got a capital without taking the grove. The same layer has learned how to cut off a household's green.

The Core-thaw runs louder the week before Leaf-Fall. Clerks file that rise as snowmelt, and the watchers do not, and Maiethlir will not say why. Rithim will Speak over the noise, and a clerk will wait for the water to quiet before copying. The counted hall serves the Far-Voiced hour from cups that have already been used that morning, which Maiethlir will not explain, and on the day a marriage is copied the clerks tie a loose thread around the tablet-stylus and cut it off before a colour, which they will not explain either.

**Offices.** Rithim speaks under Reckoned pressure, and the office is practice-first, not a Kind. The copy is a second, lesser witnessing. Some summers Rithim lets it happen in the same breath, and some summers not. The crown has not replaced them. The Seat has not recalled them. Among the tablet-clerks are Long-Lived whose memory has been bent to the census rather than left free to check it. The named instruments of the roll are Menirein, Tarvae, Videm, and Sirtal. They are not a replacement for this hearth. Rithim is still the Speaker. A pilgrim who asks where the college is will be pointed at the wood, not at the tablet-hall. Some Speakers cooperate with the roll, because they believe they are keeping the realm hale. Some refuse. On the other road, Orenbren witnesses who slept, not what colour fell, and Threnmaieth would like those lodging-rolls. Laevila keeps the old room of the Watchers. They have no seat.

The wants are specific. Rithim wants Valein to stay unmarked on the tablet. Laevila wants Senithi's name good for a marriage. Vehaela wants an uncounted loft for one winter off Lirorn's thin snow. Baerith wants the crown to stop cutting green on a sister's street. The Kept are pitied and noted as unmarked. A household whose bloodline and upkeep are both on file cannot easily refuse the crown anything. In a bad week the river comes over the tablet-hall step, a shove starts at a cut pot and stays a neighbours' shove, or an uncounted loft gives way because the timber did, while the roll had not found the room.

## The twelve seats

The three corner capitals are already above: Maiethlir for Threnmaieth, Orentel for Lestrand, and Eolvaeth for Vaethorn. These twelve are the other seats of power. A seat here may be a capital, a city, a town, or a refusal to build a city at all. Harrow's Green stays a square in Saelvaeth's orbit, and it is not one of the twelve. Six charter-towns around Raitin, the shelves of Lirorn, and the pockets between grafts stay unnamed. No sheet paints a star on a capital. The First Seat, the college of tree-wardens, stays in the Motherwood.

In each entry the opening says what the place is and where it sits. The work is the job, and who does it. A neighbour uses a given name. In a land that keeps lists, a clerk says the house-name first. What comes last are habits. People do them, and they will not say why.

### Seinbrun (Maiethvael's capital)

*(SAYN-brun.)* Seinbrun is Maiethvael's capital, a large city in the warm core of Maiethorn, and the city that furnishes. People call the country the Gift-Realm, while the charters write *Maiethvael*. The city keeps no list of persons, because counting people is treated here as a failure of trust, and there is no Threnhael. The hymns are the same ones Threnmaieth sings, and the paper is not.

Smaller towns and villages sit around the city, and this book does not name them. Seinbrun stands more than a day from Maiethlir, down the Core-thaw and north of Maiethlir on the atlas, and how much more than a day is not recorded. A traveler who leaves by Maiethlir's Down Gate spends that first day on the Down-Bank, among Gift-folk, and has still not arrived. The bank goes on toward the Hinge Shore. Seinbrun stands off that water. During pilgrimage season the Motherwood is in sight, and the grove stays outside the city. Orenbren houses the people still walking toward the grove. Seinbrun can see that season. It is not an inn on the Near Mile, and it does not crown the First Seat. A pilgrim who asks for the college is pointed at the clearings.

A town Tree stands beside the furnished hall, and Leaf-Fall is kept there. The colour is spoken, and nobody copies it onto a roll. Water comes from wells and from the rain of the warm core, not from the counted conduits. Food is the core's plenty, tended as a green. The Taken-In work the tended lots, and they are not a nation. The hall's court and the older house-walls are what a crowd can point at. The weather the core still plans for is a flood.

**The work** is hospitality, in the furnished hall, with the green beside it. Congregation and guild tend what a person was Given: the pot, a bed when the hall has one, medicine, and the commons. There is no crown channel and no tablet-hall. To be inside the hymn is to be inside the net. Vuthbraen keeps the hall. Raermu keeps the medicine basket. A neighbour says those names, and a stranger hears *of Seinbrun*. There is no house to put first, and Maiethvael will not write one. The extra mile arrives as a meal and a bed. A person who has already left the hymn can still be given a bowl.

The warmth is real, and so is the bill. A household that leaves the congregation loses the green, the hall, and the medicine together, and there is no crown to appeal to. The Kept are pitied here, in plenty, and the pity measures what they missed. Breillai left the hymn, and the green went with it, and Breillai is still in the city. The hall can still set a bowl. The net stays where the hymn is.

Sit down in the furnished hall and an empty bowl is already at the end of the table, away from the pot, cleared when the others are cleared, and Seinbrun will not say why it was set. On the day a household leaves the hymn, a neighbour stacks their green pots in the lane with the plants still in them. Raermu opens the medicine basket's latch and only then knocks. After a hug at Leaf-Fall the hall turns the child's bowl so the chip in the rim faces the door, and the Kept are given the first drink from a new cask, the next cup coming from the old one. A lamp in the window burns through the afternoon and is put out while the pilgrimage road is still full. Vuthbraen tears the guest's bread and sets the first piece on the threshold before anyone eats. They will not say why.

Maiethlir's clerks, Orenbren's beds, and the college in the wood are other powers. They are not a staff for this hall.

### Rothallo (Orenbren's capital)

*(roh-THAL-oh.)* Rothallo is a walled city, one day's walk from the Motherwood, and the capital of Orenbren, which people call the Witness-Hearths. People still say the Inner Close, and the Close, and the walls answer to those names. The seat's name is Rothallo. It stays inside Orenbren, and it is not a second power. Smaller towns and villages lie around it in the lodging-country, and this book does not name them. The Third Hearth, the Mill-hold, and Nelath stand in that same country, and they keep their own jobs: a hearth, a sick-Tree lodging, and the road-end.

The First Seat is in the wood, beyond the wall. The wood is not crowned, and Rothallo is not the college. From the grove the walk is one day, and arriving does not make the college a room in the city. From the Near Mile you have already passed the outer hearths, the Third Hearth among them at three days out, and a person who thought the Close was an inn finds a gate. The river road to Maiethlir is a different road. Threnmaieth sends clerks to this gate with empty tablets, and they do not get the page.

A Hand stands inside the walls, and the city has no speaker of its own for Leaf-Fall. A warden of the First Seat may come in for Turning-Week, the festival week when the children stand, and Speak the fall there. If a colour is copied afterward, the copy is the Close's. The wall is old. Hildal's Retreat, the withdrawal that carried the Book behind a wall, put the lodging-beds outside and kept the Book inside, and the wall was closed then rather than raised to found a kingdom. Orenbren says the wall was meant as a guest-compact, and that the compact never opened to guests. The inns lodge anyone outside the wall. The Book rules inside it. The two share a country, and they do not share a page. Water inside is cisterns and a well the wall owns. Food is the lodging-country's surplus, and whatever the Book assigns bodies to grow. The Taken-In work lots the Book can write them onto, and they are not a nation.

**The work** is the Book of Tithes, kept inside the walls. Delamem *(del-AH-mem)* keeps it, and will tell you the Book is housekeeping, though the page that assigns labour is in the same hand as the page that assigns a hearth-tax. The pages assign taxes, restrictions, and turns of work, and the Tithe a person already carries is not that assignment. The assignment sits on top of it. Everyone who lives inside the walls owes the Close those turns, the way a town expects each household to take a turn on the watch: the green, the hall, the wall, the road, and the beds outside the gate. A Kept household's turns are mostly the watch on the wall and the keeping of keys. A household with a Condition is given the heavier turns, and more of them, and the Book picks the turn by the Condition. A person who does not want the turns can leave. The Book has no claim on anyone who lives outside, and it cannot send for someone who has left. It does not sell a person. Delamem and the Close will not call the Book a scripture, and they will not Speak a colour.

Talnin *(TAL-nin)* of the Inner Close is still the heir whose tenth year is not finished, so the Tree has not Spoken this child's colour yet. The name is on the heir-roll. A Closed Heir who is Given at Leaf-Fall loses the heir's place. The hug keeps the name on the roll, and the colour takes it off. A Strike later is treated the same way once the clerks learn of it. A neighbour in the city says a given name. A clerk says the house first. *Closed* on a roster is a rank, not a second name. The line can cast a person out and still keep the house on them.

**What a visitor sees.** A visitor does not need to read the Book to see the rank. At a meal in a Closed house, the Kept sit nearest the host, and a guest who carries a Condition is shown to the far end, past the last Kept guest. When the Book is read aloud, the Kept sit on the front benches and everyone else sits behind them. Nobody is turned away. The host says where each guest sits. When two people disagree in front of a clerk, over a debt, a boundary, or a broken pot, and one of them is Kept, the clerk writes the Kept person's account as what happened and writes the other account underneath as "also said." The person with a Condition can still win, and to win they need a second Kept person to say the same thing. Only a Kept person may hold a key to a gate in the wall, to the Book's house, to a cistern, or to a granary. A person with a Condition holds the key to their own door and to their own stall, and no others.

The children of the Closed houses learn their letters at a school beside the Book's house, and other families inside the walls may send a child if they pay. The teacher, Volnai *(VOL-nai)*, tells them that a hug is rare, and that a child the Tree hugged was kept back for something better than a colour. Volnai believes this and teaches it every year. The lesson is wrong about the count. Most towns have Kept neighbours, and being hugged is common. What is rare is a family that has stayed without a Condition on purpose, generation after generation, and treats that record as a birthright. The school does not teach the count. Orders and Houses has the Walled Book and the Inner Close in full.

A person with a Condition who lives inside the walls works for wages, keeps what they earn, owns their tools and their household goods, marries whom they choose, and can own a stall. The Book writes a licence for that stall, and a Kept stall-keeper needs no licence. They can walk out of the gate on any day it is open. Nobody owns them, and they are not slaves. What they cannot have inside the walls is the heir's place, a gate key, a vote in the Close's compact, or the first word in front of a clerk.

In Turning-Week the wall will not lodge a Given pilgrim, and the lodging-country is in the business of beds. Breolnir, house Tethnae, writes who slept. The Book writes who owes. The same person can be a guest on one page and labour on the other, and the pages will not be copied onto each other. Brimaen, house Lanbru, keeps a stall inside the walls on a licence the Book can revoke. Methei, house Sithlaen, is Given, on the Book's list for the beds outside the gate, and lives there while the turn lasts. Methei can walk away on any day the gate is open. A neighbour says Brimaen, Breolnir, Methei. A clerk says Lanbru, Tethnae, Sithlaen, and then the given name. Hithaen is a disinherited heir of the Closed, already eating in Eolvaeth's gift-hall. Hithaen is not Talnin, and not Methei.

On a morning with no crowd the outer gate is shut for the length of a kettle's boil, and Rothallo will not say why. At that gate Breolnir sands a sleep-line and writes it again before the ink would have needed sand, while bread for the beds outside is cut while it is still too hot to eat and the heel is set on the wall-stone, which the beds will not explain. Inside, Methei turns the blanket so the stripe faces the wall rather than the door, and Brimaen wipes the stall-board with the cut edge of a loaf and then sells that loaf. The week before a Closed child's tenth year, the child leaves one shoe by the Book's step and walks the lane in the other. The lane, the stall, and the bed will not say why.

### Larbril (Saelthael's city)

*(LAR-bril.)* Larbril is Saelthael's medium city, the place people call the Sown Tree, where the west road meets the Well-wash, short of the pass. It is not a great capital, and the well-town farther into the dry is still Ornsael. Walking west, the Shelf-gate is still ahead of you, the pass above the dry. Walking east, you have already come down, and the dry country is what stands in front of you.

Ornsael stays the smaller well-town, farther into the dry, and its drink is a well. Larbril's drink, when the city has one, is the wash. The days from here to Ornsael, and the days to the pass and to Tasain, are not recorded. The Dry Stair is another rise, and people climb it so they will know a dare from a Tree. The climb is not why this city is here. The city stopped where it stopped because travelers already wanted water and the walk west, and because the core stopped walking while this country did not.

The wash is a river in a kind Liren, a gentle thaw, and a silt-line in a cruel one, a ford in the one case and a pale line in the road in the other. A Hand stands at the meeting and Speaks most years. A missed week is called weather. The civic fact is the wash. Fox of the Sands hearths are dense in this country, and that density is terrain. Brormei is not chosen for being Fox-born, Larbril is not a Fox nation, and mixed Kinds use the road. There is no wall. The boundary is where the road stops being a street and becomes the climb, or the dust. Food is what the wash year allows, plus what a west-bound cart pays. The Taken-In plant where a bucket can be argued.

**The work** is the meeting, and the city's daily work is the walk west, the decision of whether a column goes on. Brormei says what the wash is this week, and whether a column goes west toward the pass. A neighbour says Brormei. A stranger hears Brormei of Larbril. Saelthael does not keep a person-list, and there is no house to put first. Ornsael's well-share is Ornsael's, and Larbril does not chalk a rope for that well. The pass-levy is Lirorn's, taken at Tasain and at the Shelf-gate. This city keeps the road and the wash in one place. Necessity is a column that still has to go. Devotion belongs to people whose well and canopy are both sound.

The city stands on water that is not a certainty. In a cruel Liren the carts still come, the wash is a line in the dirt, and the core will call that thin soil, and a slight. Larbril still has to say whether the week goes west. Plenty on the far side of the range does not shorten the road. Theisva, Lesna, and Bovaer stay at Ornsael. They are not moved here.

When the Well-wash is a silt-line, Brormei still wets the meeting-stone from a jar, and will not say why. A cart bound west is held while the driver sets a pebble on the dry line. Larbril will not say why.

### Votaer (the Hinge Shore's port)

*(VOH-tayr.)* Votaer is the Hinge Shore's port, one of the largest cities on the Motherland coast, because the crossing is why the people are there. It faces Orentel, and it is not a second Orentel. Hulls are classified on these quays, and the Hush-rate is charged here. Towns and villages along the rest of the shore stay unnamed.

Orentel prices the crossing, and Votaer sorts it. A child, a pot of living earth, a Given-Over whom a contract has come to hold, a recanter taking back a standing, the claim that a witness watched them, or a family on a neighbour's week can make the bell sound the same, and a clerk writes which docket that hull belongs to. That writing is the job. The White Note is not on this quay, and the Chart-run is not this water. The city is ancient and rich, and less frantic than the Ledger Coast, because the grove is still at its back. A fair-weather hull reaches Orentel in three to five days. From Thaeloren the inland way is the Core-thaw and then the Down-Bank, eight to twelve days in fair weather. The bank is the stretch before anyone asks what a hull is. The question is asked here.

The trees on this shore are healthy, and this is still the Motherland. Faith is worn thinner than in Maiethvael, because a dock teaches categories. A Hand stands back from the working quay, and the quay's work is the hull, not the canopy. Selkie families work these quays, as they do on any old water, and the docket does not belong to a hearth. The rate is old custom from the Hinge Hush, the peace the Old Crossing still charges for, and they will not call it a victory. Taerso spoke that Hush for the hinge-houses in Cut-year 304. Taerso is dead. The rate is remembered as Taerso's, and Taerso is not a living clerk.

**The work** is the classification quay. Tumair writes the hull, pilgrim or pot or person or cargo, and Lertho charges the Hush-rate on the same water. A neighbour says the given name. A stranger hears *of Votaer*. The quay does not file a family, and there is no house to put first. The Standing Trade uses this face more often than Orentel, because a standing has to travel and a hinge has two sides. That trade is not the city's office. Other harbours exist on this coast, and this book does not name them. They are not a second port for the Hush.

The blessing and the docket happen in the same breath. A Tithe-poor household can fail in sight of the road to the grove while a hymn is still in the air. Across the water, Lestrand will price what this quay has already sorted. Votaer will not become that price, and it will not put the blessing down in order to look unlike it. Water for the city is rain and the casks a quay keeps. A wall, where it exists, is for a surge and for the old argument about a fade, not for a siege the Crossing still thinks it can sail around.

Tumair chalks the hull's class under the waterline, where the next tide takes the mark, and will not say why. Lertho counts the Hush-rate aloud, sets the last coin in the box, and takes it out once before it stays. When a bell rings for a sail that is not in sight, a cook-fire on the quay is put out, and on a day when no one has asked for a hymn a dry net hangs on the blessing-rail. The quay will not say why.

### Tasain (Lirorn's town)

*(tah-SAYN.)* Tasain is Lirorn's walled town, in a sheltered valley below the Shelf-gate, where the west road comes down and the levy is taken. People who cannot keep the high road through a winter sleep here. The town is not a great city, and it is not a second Maiethlir. Villages and hamlets stay on the shelves and at the passes, and this book does not name them.

The town gate is the wall in the valley, where a cart comes down off the pass and can wait. The Shelf-gate is the notch above that wall. They are not the same gate. The Noon Pass is a third thing, higher and older, a water-line still sitting on its stone, and it is not a town and not a seat. The Shelf-gate is the road Narol of the Pass took after the Thaw-Break, the one-week melt of Cut-year 233, when the old stone sat under the water. Narol *(NAH-rol)* is dead. The house still levies, and the person does not. Break-rate, the toll still said in Narol's name, is asked even in a kind year, and a lowlander is not given a sermon with the coin.

Saelthael's west road crosses by this pass. Larbril is the city on the dry side, short of the climb if you are walking west. Tasain is where you are when the road has come down into shelter and someone asks for the year's due. Tengu hearths and Fox-of-the-Snows hearths are densest on the high snow. Mixed Kinds live in the valley, and the office at the gate is the levy, not a people. A sky-courier who hears a thaw first is doing a job. A Hand stands in the valley, where a graft can take in shelter.

**The work** is the levy, taken at the town gate and reckoned on how much snow fell the winter before. The household that inherited Narol's toll still collects, and it carries no house-name. It levies the thaw, the pass, and the high road. After Cut-year 233 the argument was which notch the road should use. A traveller at the gate meets a collector of that household. The collector has no name in this book, and says the toll is Narol's. Maiethlir drinks the Core-thaw downstream and files a loud week as snowmelt. That filing is not a receipt for this gate. Water the core will call a gift is taken here as a due. Devotion can still walk west once the gate has opened.

The valley takes the levy in shelter, and the shelves above pay in isolation for water the lowland files as a gift. A shelf whose graft sickens is a long way from a stiller. The town can charge, and it does not furnish every Tithe on a cliff. A toll that feels like weather feels like Cut-year 233 to anyone who was on the shelf when the stone went under.

In a kind year, before the levy is taken, a bowl of shelf-snow is left on the gate-stone until it is water. Tasain will not say why.

### Raitin (Brenledd's throne-city)

*(RAY-tin.)* Raitin is a river-city on Strandoren, inland of the Ledger Coast and south-west of Orentel on the atlas, and one of the largest on that shore. The days from Orentel are not recorded. People say the Hearth-League. Charters write *Brenledd*. The league stays a league of hearths. It does not become one house. This is where the council sits, and where the shared notes clear.

Six charter-towns sit around the city, and this book does not name them. Villages and hamlets along the same water are not on the shared list. The list is seven hearths, and Raitin is one of them. People still recite Nidtol when Lestrand offers to deal with one ruler instead of seven hearths. Nidtol is the hearth that made the league's first shared refusal, in Cut-year 174, after the Grey Summer, the fever that emptied the quays in 171, and the name is recited for that. Nidtol is not this city, and it is not a person in this hall.

Where Orentel prices a berth, Raitin clears a note among hearths. There is no crown and no Threnhael, and you are of your town first. The three pillars are the machinery a commercial shore already uses. An unvouched person is a transaction that cannot clear, and a vouched person can still be unknown in the next hearth. Faith is worn light, as on the rest of this coast, and a Hand stands beside the council hall, where Leaf-Fall is kept. Nothing is policed, and a charter is. Raitin's water is the river under the stair. The Chart-run, four to eight days upriver of Orentel, is Lunbra's water, and it is not this river. The city does not hold a First-Hand year.

**The work** is the council hall, at the head of the river stair, where the shared notes clear. Turvo keeps the notes, Sulnu carries a cleared ticket down to the stair, and Nubo is vouched in this city and still unknown where another hearth did not stand them. A neighbour says the given name. A stranger hears *of Raitin*. There is no house to put first, and the league will not write one. The six charter-towns keep their own hearths. This hall does not appoint them. Water for the city is the river and the cisterns above the stair. Food is what the hearths underwrite into the market, grain and river-fish and a ticket a charter will still honour. A rise in the river still lifts the lower doors, and the city plans for that week.

A note can clear in this hall and still die in the next hearth. The fade sits in that gap, and the league calls the gap local. Lestrand can still offer one ruler in place of the seven. Raitin is where the council sits. It is not that ruler.

Watch a meeting and you will see Turvo turn a cleared note face-down before the next hearth is called, Nubo move a dry thread from the left wrist to the right on the way out, and Sulnu set a river-stone on a shared note and put that stone into a bowl of water at dusk. The hall sets one cup at the end of the table and leaves it empty. Bread for the meeting is broken at the river door, and the heel stays on the sill until the meeting ends. On the morning a note is due, the stairs are swept from the water up toward the hall, a child who came through Leaf-Fall with no colour is handed a wooden token with no price on it, and the lamps on the stair are lit from the hall downward. Ask Turvo, or Nubo, or Sulnu, or the hall, and you will not be told why.

### Naenor (Leddvael's port)

*(NAY-nor.)* Naenor is Leddvael's port, on its own stretch of indented coast, down the coast south-west of Orentel and south of Raitin on the atlas. The days are not recorded. It is slightly smaller than Orentel, large enough to keep its own houses, and it is not a second Orentel. People say the Reckoned Gift, and the charters write *Leddvael*. Towns and villages along the rest of that coast stay unnamed. This port does not face the Hinge Shore. Votaer is the port that does.

Leddvael took the Fair Hand, a dockside theology of the gift, and made it a civic rite, so Leaf-Fall here is a festival and a signing-watch at once. A Tithe is a term serviced. The Bound are employed to witness a term, because a Bound's word still closed in the Grey Summer, when the other witnesses had died. The city will file a contract. It will not copy Threnmaieth's list, and it will not become a census of souls.

The port competes with the Ledger Coast, and it wins the signing. A term can close on this quay while Orentel is still pricing the hold. What Naenor loses is the berth, the White Note, and the scale of the deathless houses on the Old Crossing. A First-Hand year is not this city's product. The empty prestige berths are Orentel's. Naenor has working berths, and the work the city is for is the watch. The Book-Hands stamp and recite on this quay, as they do on other signing-quays. They have no seat of their own, and they do not rewrite terms. Inland filing is Lunbra and the far crossing is Braetu, so Naenor is the stretch between those jobs.

A Hand stands off the signing-square, which Motherfaith tree-tenders and Fair Hand clerks share. Gift-reading families live in the city. They are the minority that sounds devout, and the statute reads them as a credit risk. The city's daily work is the signing. If devotion lands, it is a hymn the festival will schedule. Necessity is a household that still calls the colour a gift.

**The work** is the signing-watch, on the book-table in the square. Derdil stands it. A neighbour says Derdil. A stranger hears Derdil of Naenor. There is no house to put first, because the Reckoned Gift does not keep a person-list. The Book-Hands who work this quay are not Derdil, and they are not a seat.

The city wins the term and loses the berth. A Bound's word can close a contract on this quay, and the hold that would make the contract a life is still priced on the Ledger Coast. Inside the win, a household that calls the colour a gift is filed as a bad debtor. The Bound are safe here, and they are over-read, because the sworn contract is being asked to stand for the whole person. The faith is not the villain. It is a sincere ledger laid on a real Condition. Water is rain, casks, and a paid well off the salt. A wall, where the town kept one, is for a surge. The statute is not a wall.

Derdil stamps the wax, then warms the stamp in a closed hand before the next page, and will not say why. The watch reads a term aloud and turns the page with the left hand only, and at Leaf-Fall the square sets the Bound witness's chair with its back to the Tree. Naenor will not say why.

### Lunbra (Trenledd's city)

*(LUN-bra.)* Lunbra is a large, wealthy city on the Chart-run, four to eight days upriver of Orentel and inland of the salt. People say the Worn Count, and the records write *Trenledd*. This is where the roll is kept. The governing office, or the council, stays unnamed. Towns through the filed country stay unnamed, and a road-end town that got written down is not the roll-room.

The city shows the money, in stone fronts, paid chairs, and guild doors left open onto the square so the coin is visible from the street. People come because a family already has the money, or because they want it, and both kinds of arrival are written down. Trenledd began as ticket-and-tax machinery and did not stop at property. You are on the roll so commerce and hazard can find you again. There is no hymn that makes the list a stewardship. Threnmaieth at least claims to keep you hale. The Worn Count claims to keep you findable.

After the Grey Summer, Dirrol's column was who is still alive to be found, and that column is the city's memory. Dirrol is not a living clerk in this room, and not the crown. Cutting a person off the furnished channels is the default, and it is not called impiety. The Struck are pursued so they can be catalogued, because an unwitnessed change is a row that appeared without a clerk. A Hand stands in the square, where the trees are mature and the faith is worn light, and the number on the back of a stone is a count, not a prayer.

In fair weather, grain, beans, and the inland tickets go down the Chart-run to the salt, and the food on the Ledger Coast is this plain as much as it is the catch. In winter the banks go to mud and the river still moves, so the week belongs to the boats. That shift is weather, and it is not a second government. The Taken-In work the river-gardens. Oxen pull the heavy carts, and a barge does the bulk.

**The work** is the roll-room, on the square where the money is shown. Vamar keeps a column of who owes, who Turned, and who can be found. The file is Melro, Vamar. A neighbour says Vamar. A clerk says the house first. A stranger hears Vamar of Lunbra. Vamar is not the governing office. Licence-guilds write their own tickets through the same room, and they are not a second crown. In Trenledd the column may file a person under the house that pays, including a house they dislike. Vamar's page is where that fight becomes ink.

The city shows the money, and the roll is how a person is allowed near it. A family that already has the money is already a house on the page, and a person who came to make it is written in order to try. There is no hymn to appeal to. The office that could strike the row stays unnamed, and the column does not become that office by being the page people can see.

Vamar sets a coin on the open page, and the coin is not the fee, which Vamar will not explain. On the morning a new house is written, the stones in the square are turned number-down, and a newcomer is shown the expensive chair and is not asked to sit until the house has been said. Lunbra will not say why.

### Braetu (the Night Shore's harbour)

*(BRAY-too.)* Braetu is a harbour city on Strandoren's west and south face, on the West Water. It is smaller than Orentel because the far run is thinner than the Old Crossing. People say the Night Shore, and the charters write *Netstrand*, which sells the hulls and the west-water berths. Towns along the rest of the shore stay unnamed. A crossing is quoted here the way another shore talks about a pilgrimage.

The city does not sell the First-Hand year as a finished thing, and that winter is not this city's to crown. The year is assembled on the old water: a Night Shore hull is chartered here, the White Note writes the terms, and Orentel holds the berth. Braetu is the first of those three desks, and it is not the other two. Coasting from Orentel to these west quays takes eight to fourteen days. You arrive with a hull to charter, or with a name to keep lit, and you do not arrive in a hold.

The far run, toward the Heskoren frontier, takes two to four weeks in fair weather. Beyond the last capes is the storm-wall, and almost nobody has crossed it. A story that says otherwise is a story, and Kumbaan is not booked from this quay. Along that run, Saelvaeth may take a pot without a waiting-due, Vaethorn may bless a landing and may not levy it, and Ornled is a beach-fee. The quote for the run is written here.

The harbour keeps a dark book of the quoted crossings that did not come back, and houses pay to keep a lamp on a name in it. A lamp on a name that will not come back is charged as watch. The book is not a Threnhael, and it is not a White Note. It is how this shore remembers that it is a night shore. One berth stays unlit, with no house attached to it, and the city does not add one. Selkie families are common on this water without becoming the flag, and the sky-couriers out of Lirorn and the Tengu families on the clock-towers are jobs in the harbour, not a district of it. Faith is mixed and worn light, quay-charms stay off the charter, and a Hand stands back from the working water. The harbour's worry is a hull past the last forecast anyone trusted.

**The work** is the quote-desk, where the dark book is kept and where a lamp can be bought for a name. Mursur writes the far crossing and takes the fee for the lamp. A neighbour says Mursur. A stranger hears Mursur of Braetu, because there is no house to put first and the Night Shore does not keep a person-list. Mursur is not a house on the unlit berth, and nobody is. A name can be kept lit, and the lighting is a charge. In the same harbour, one berth stays dark, with no one to bill. The city will not put a house on that berth to make the book tidy, and it will not pretend the far quote is grief. Watch is what the lamp is sold as.

Mursur lights a name's lamp with a spill from the cook-fire, not from another lamp, and will not say why. The far figure is written, blotted, and written again the same, and people leaving the harbour touch the unlit berth's ring without hanging a line on it. Braetu will not say why.

### Sanbreo (Ornled's town)

*(san-BREE-oh.)* Sanbreo is Ornled's town, on Heskoren's slate-shore, the west coast. It is on the brink of a small city, and it is still a town. People say the Outer Ledger, and Outer folk, while slates and learned maps write *Ornled*. The beach faces west, toward the last capes and the storm-wall beyond them. The frontier coast that faces the West Water is on the east side of Heskoren, so a hull that wants this beach has to come round the capes. Villages in the pockets around the town stay unnamed. They share the same thin grafts, the same barter, and the same absence. Eolvaeth is a vale behind the other coast, in the waiting-gospel, and it is not this gate.

The roofs have filled the pocket. A walk that used to pass through here left stones and upper rooms, and people still sleep in them. The brink did not grow a congregation, and it did not grow a counted hall. Custom outruns law, because there is almost no law and almost no hymn. Braetu can quote a far crossing, and the fee here is a beach, not that quote. Vaethorn may bless a landing, and may not levy it. Ornled was written into the Hinge Hush as a beach-fee, and they were not asked. They take the fee anyway, because a hull that wants the beach is a hull, and a hymn is not what they sell.

**The work** is the slate, on the wall inside the shore gate, where a pocket-road meets the beach. The first line is still the sentence Vathne cut, after the Thaw-Break, when a graft pot the town had waited for drowned in a flood that came from someone else's thaw. The line says the town will not wait with a hymn for a stick that drowned. The wood of the wall is newer than the words, because someone recopied the line onto it. Vathne is that line, and Vathne is not a person you meet here. When a hull arrives, whoever is on the shore gate that day cuts the fee on the same slate, and that person is not named. Barter, favor, and the wall are what a person puts up so they do not slide. An unvouched Struck is met with a rope, a deal, or a neighbour. There is no blessing queued behind the fee.

A hull can pay, and a neighbour who cannot barter a Tithe still slides. The roofs filled the pocket without becoming a net. Vaethorn hears godlessness in the missing hymn, and the wall hears a luxury that died in a cart. The loneliest unvouched on this coast are next door to a gospel, in a town where no one is waiting with them. The Outer Ledger does not keep a person-list, and there is no house to put first.

When a hull has paid, the coin stays on the slate's sill until the hull is gone. Sanbreo will not say why.

### Vaelhesk (the land is the seat)

*(VAEL-hesk.)* The land is the seat. The formal name sets *vael*, what the land yields, beside *hesk*, the far edge, and saying it that way keeps the spoken name clear of Vaethorn. People say the Far Yield, and the Yield, while charters and learned maps write *Vaelhesk*. There is no new city and no new name. Old greens, doors kept for ancestors, and a first meal before anyone is allowed to leave with something of the place: that is the country. The Mother's wood is a guest on that older ground. A Cutting-leave without the meal is a claiming. Villages and hamlets stay on the old greens. A traveler who asks for the seat is pointed at a door.

There is no settlement dot. The First Bowl stays the guest-grove, folk name Lonasir, a planting on this land and not a capital. Harrow's Green is the live-front square, and it is not this seat. The Door-Keepers walk here as Kin-speakers. They did not carry the scion, they do not confirm colours, and what they argue is who the host is. The Struck, and the Taken-In, are read as the land's own children. A neighbour's week to a Motherfaith Tree can be refused on purpose, and the refusal says the child is already the hill's. Vaethorn reads those children as Kept for want of a gift. That disagreement decides whether a child travels. No new person is seated on the refusal.

These were ancestor countries before the grafts. The grafts arrived across Cut-year 200 to the present year, 387, and they are still arriving. Some districts became Vaethorn, and these districts kept the door. The state stays light because the land does not need a clerk in order to be owed a meal. The Thaw-Break moved guests off flooded ground and up to high doors. The Hinge Hush does not write Vaelhesk into its clauses, because a first meal is not a landing-due. A warden who arrives with a pot is a guest if they sat down for the meal, and a thief if they took the wood without sitting. Refuse the meal, take the wood, and you are not a neighbour. The harm stays local, and it does not become a holy war.

### Natai (Saelvaeth's town)

*(NAH-tye.)* Natai is a march-town on Heskoren's moving edge, in Saelvaeth. People say the Sown Waiting, and March-folk, while the charters write *Saelvaeth*. A graft has taken here, and the canopy is a town's Hand, young enough that the neighbours still argue which town gets the next pot. It is not the luck at Harrow's Green. Other towns where a graft has taken stay unnamed, and so do the hamlets between them.

On the roads, Natai sits west of Harrow's Green, between the square and Sanbreo, and not on the ford. The days to either place are not recorded. Harrow's square is the canopy the hamlets can see, with Haelin's stone and the upper room, and it is not the march's seat and not a capital. The Three Hamlets Past the Ford stay the three hamlets. Brenod, Vaelun, and Ornath stay their kitchens. The Hinge Hush lets this march take a pot without a Vaethorn waiting-due. The due that remains is local. Someone has to stand where the queue can be shouted at, and that is why this town has the office. The neighbour's week of need still happens at Harrow's. What sits here is the blame.

**The work** is the march-voice, the office that answers for the queue of grafts. Dumu stands it. A neighbour says Dumu. A stranger hears Dumu of Natai. The town can be blamed for a queue it did not write. Harrow's has the canopy, and Natai has the person to blame. The town has enough state to be blamed, and not enough state to be a kingdom of the luck. The gate is the road between grafts. It is not Harrow's square, and it is not the ford.

While a complaint is said, Dumu leaves the gate-latch on the post, and puts it back in the catch when the speaker turns. They will not say why.
