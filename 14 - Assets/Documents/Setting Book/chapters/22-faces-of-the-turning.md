# Faces of the Turning

These are offices the towns already needed, made into people, and the job is why a party meets them at all. None of them were chosen. You can meet two in a week and miss the rest. They were not recruited by Kind, the ancestry a person is born with, so any Kind may hold the work. A neighbour says the given name. The second name, and what a clerk or a stranger gets instead, sits at the end of this chapter.

Read the last column of a table as an ordinary want, a stall or a bed or a name, and not as a want about the Tree.

## Offices already on the ground

Each of these six already stands where the work is. The seats stay the seats already named.

Thilim and Laevila both say *I walked*, and they do not mean the same walk. Thilim means the Near Mile, kept as an inn. Laevila means a child's walk under Thaeloren, the origin Tree, before this river had to count them. Mataero Turned at home, files the year as occupancy, and treats the spread of town Trees as finished. Vaethod sends people toward a luck they cannot see from Eolvaeth. The live front is Haelin's square at Harrow's Green, not Vaethod's, and Eolvaeth remains Vaethorn's seat, the gospel of the wait. Other offices already stand on that ground, and the later table keeps them: Haelin at the live front, Tora walking a witness home, Ledan at the White Note quay-desk, and Sorim holding a berth on Orentel's first quay.

## Positional pivots

These six matter because of the office they already hold.

### Vaethod — Eolvaeth

**Vaethod** *(VAYTH-od)* is town-warden of Eolvaeth, the vale where people come to wait, and there is no house to give a clerk. A neighbour says Vaethod, and a stranger gets Vaethod of Eolvaeth. They are Hands-folk, raised where a town Tree is the ordinary way to Turn, and they were Given at this Tree, a colour at their own Leaf-Fall, so the office is practice and not a Kind. They are the person who has to say whether the hinge happened, whether this Tree's week actually turned, and some years whether a cohort walks toward Saelvaeth's luck, the march where young wood takes more readily. They will not write a colour that did not fall. They have sent people away before, and the town remembers which summers.

What they want is **Saelid** *(SAY-lid)*'s baker's stall through Ledger-month, the late summer when contracts are counted. Saelid is the younger sibling, on the camp-streets. The winter customers who buy the bread are the same households a sent cohort takes away, and Vaethod has not told the square that this is why they wait until the week is over. They have sent people toward Haelin's live front without meeting them. A pot going that way is a Road-hand's job. They are fortyish, with salt in the hair already and chalk on a cuff more weeks than not. They will feed you, and they will not let you name the spring as a clerk's list. They cannot stamp a Cutting-leave, the First Seat's paper for who may take living wood, and they cannot make the Tree speak. They can refuse a colour that did not happen, and they can tell a hundred people to walk.

### Rithim — Maiethlir

**Rithim** *(RITH-im)* is Speaker of Maiethlir, the person who names the leaf-colour at Leaf-Fall so the town can treat it as a fact, while a clerk waits to copy that colour onto the roll. House **Nirsei** *(nir-SAY)* sits on the conservative side of the river, where a clerk still puts the house first, so a neighbour says Rithim and a clerk says Nirsei, Rithim. The crown has not replaced them, and the Seat has not recalled them. In a small town the warden Speaks, which is the act of naming the colour aloud. Here the Speaker is a separate office, and Rithim holds it under pressure from the Reckoned Hands, the crown's tablet offices. The office is practice, not Kind. They Speak truly, and they treat the copy as a second, lesser seeing. Some summers they let the Speaking and the copy happen in the same breath. Some summers they do not.

What they want is **Valein** *(vah-LAYN)*, Kept and unmarked in the same household, to stay off the tablet. Kept is what a square calls a person the Tree hugged and did not give a colour, and unmarked is the roll's word for that. Valein is a partner, not a child, the person who brings Rithim a cup after a Speaking, and the house is Nirsei if a clerk is allowed the second name. The verso of last Eolthael's copy-slip, the back of the paper, already noted an unmarked person in the household. Rithim has not said the name into a hall. Until they Speak, the colour is weather, and after they Speak it is a civic fact. They cannot unsay a colour, and they cannot keep a clerk from writing *delay noted*. They can make the hymn happen first, and they can refuse to read a household aloud. On the same morning Laevila describes the other colour in the same square, which is a different refusal from the line Rithim keeps off the roll. They eat together after the Night Watch, the silent vigil the night before Turning-Week, and they do not agree what that meal means. The First Seat stays in the wood beside the city, and the nearness is the pressure. Rithim's office is the square's Speaking. If a pilgrim asks where the college is, they will point at the Motherwood, and they will not walk the pilgrim there.

### Mataero — Orentel

**Mataero** *(mah-TAY-ro)* lets the inland loft of the White Note House, the deathless road-house on the quay that kept its beds when it became a desk. There are twelve beds, eight of them let and four standing open, and the north bed is one of those four. Three of the open beds are for Eolthael, the month that holds Turning-Week, and the north bed is not to let. Mataero files all of that as occupancy. They are of the rise behind the third quay, with no family to put first. A neighbour says Mataero. While a White Note paper is open the line may be headed White Note, Mataero, and it is the desk. Shut the paper and the quay says Mataero of the rise. They Turned on the rise, for free, in a city that still does that and does not like to notice. They did not walk the old mile. The job requires them to believe the house is a bank that happens to have rooms.

What they want is **Sorel**, their mother, clear of a century-note the house still holds, a hundred-year paper from a walk Sorel cannot point to. They think a clean loft-slate will buy that, and the cut of the occupancy money has not moved the paper. They are young for a house that does not age, with ink on the fingers and a key to the inland door rather than the quay-desk. They answer in occupancy even when you asked about a name. They cannot rewrite Ledan's count, and they cannot let the north bed. What they can do is lose a name in a slate that only says *let* or *open*, and if you press them they will tell you that empty is a bed they are not charging for. A household Vaethod sent may already owe a bed here. **Reimaethe** pays a rise-room Mataero writes *let*. Ledan, who is Long-Lived and writes the quay-desk, will not explain house custom unless the week makes them. Sorim, the factor on the first quay, will not convert the White Note's house-years into anybody else's calendar, and Mataero hears a neighbour who cannot file beds.

### Thilim — the Third Hearth

**Thilim** *(THIL-im)* keeps the Third Hearth, the third lodging out from the Motherwood on the Near Mile, and they are of the Held bed, a bed kept for a name that has not come. There is no house, and they will not borrow one. They are Long-Lived, deathless, and inland. The notes they write are smaller than the White Note's, and the shrine is larger. They have kept this house since before the First Cut, as later clerks would say. The First Cut is the hinge the wardens count from, the year living wood began to leave the one Tree. Thilim walked the mile as the inn, the same stretch every summer, until the stretch was optional, and they kept making the bed anyway. Lestrand, which prices a road-house by whether it became a bank, calls this a bed-house that failed to become one. Thilim asks who will be here at dark.

What they want is the stone's water still feeding the pot when it is dark. Seine of the far cape, due in a tenth summer, has not come. The mother said next year, and next year was last year, and has been. Seine's bed is still made, and it is not a shrine Thilim will admit. The stone by the road is the inn's water and the Road-hands' mile, so the name is also a well. Meirim *(may-REEM)*, Brenthael's town-warden, wants the stone moved so the extra mile does not look like a requirement on their approach. Thilim will not move a name that is also a well, and has not answered Meirim's chalk on paper. They can feed a necessity-family and a devotion-column in the same loft in the same week, and know which is which without saying. When they say *I walked*, a Long-Lived is saying when they were young, and the same sentence does work the people who say it do not agree on. They will not always tell you which of those they are doing when they pour. They wait to be asked across a threshold, even their own, if they have been out to the stone. It is courtesy, and they will not explain it. At dark they set the yard-broom across the doorway and step over it, and they will not say why. They cannot make Seine arrive, and they cannot make the White Note fail. A pilgrim who asks where the college is will be pointed at the wood, not the inn.

### Laevila — Maiethlir

**Laevila** *(lay-VEE-lah)* bakes on the old extra-mile, the street where people still walk past what the local rite already gave them, and keeps the room under the chapel the wardens recut. Before that recut they copied the hidden face of the beam, the side turned away from the room, and the copy is the only one. The roll files a Watcher of that room as the Grown-Over, its name for people whose old place sits under a chapel that was built over it. House **Thalonve** *(tha-LON-ve)* is the clerk's name, so a neighbour says Laevila and a clerk says Thalonve, Laevila. They are Long-Lived, and they walked as a child under Thaeloren before this river had to count them. A Watcher is the neighbour the Watching sends when a leaf comes down strange. They describe what they saw, and they do not confirm it, because confirmation is the Speaking. Laevila looks like the neighbour who stayed, flour on the cuff and no mark of any office, and you know them because someone said *ask the old room*, or because you saw them under the Tree the night before Turning-Week with no lamp.

What they want is **Senithi** *(seh-NITH-ee)*'s name good for a marriage. Senithi is their grandchild, Given a colour at the Leaf-Fall and of the same house, and a marriage is priced on a line that lists the wrong grandmother. The hidden face still has that grandmother under her own name. Laevila wants it read once, in a room a clerk will accept as a witness, not as theology but as a name a tablet will swallow. They will not Speak, and they will not say *Two-Bodied* if what they saw was two colours, though they will say the two colours. Rithim Speaks in the same square. They eat together after the Night Watch. Rithim will not say the line the clerk is waiting to copy, and Laevila will not say the Mother, the name a Watcher leaves out of the blessing. That is as close as peace gets on this street. The chapel wardens leave a plate during Turning-Week, and that plate is courtesy, not a wage. They cannot confirm a colour, and they cannot unsay the recut. They can sit beside the Speaker and say the second colour aloud, so it stays spoken in the square and the clerk cannot quietly drop it.

### Tesara — Orentel

**Tesara** *(teh-SAH-rah)* sits the stillers' shed that does not face the street, the catching-year, which is the week a newly Struck person is kept somewhere quiet before the town reaches for a rope. A key is kept off the slate, so if the week goes well, nobody hears of it, and they will not stamp a ticket this week. A ticket is the licence that lets one of the Stilled use the gaze where the town can see it. There is no family to put first. A neighbour says Tesara, and a stranger gets Tesara of Orentel. Years back Tesara was Struck, a Condition met at a later threshold rather than at the childhood leaf, and what they carry is a Gaze, the stilling look, taken raw in a week somebody else sat. The paying floor does not write that down. Tesara remembers the door, and that memory is why they are the one the floor can spare. They think the work is first aid. You catch the person, you steady them, and you hand them on to the year the guild would actually train.

What they want is **Valen** stamped with a real ticket, not left on a slip that can be burned. Valen was stone on the third night, a Struck member of the Stilled, the kind who survived being turned to stone, stood for by a sister and by an N. who would not give a family name. The week is almost over, and the face of the ticket is still empty, because the slip is written on the inside of a blank and Tesara will not stamp the outside this week. A burned slip is how a quiet week disappears. The paying floor wants the master back, because a quiet catching-year is a week the theatre lost. They cannot overrule a quay-watch, and they cannot stamp a ticket this week. They can keep a master in the shed one more night, and they can fail to burn a slip. If Valen lives and still has no ticket, Mataero is who they will need, because occupancy will not say *Struck*. Rosire used to bring water downwind of the shed, and is not on the floor this Hale-month. Tesara has not been told why.

## Named wants in the three seats

Wants sit on people. An institution can furnish a bed, a pot, or a paper, or withhold them. The want stays with the person.

**Eolvaeth.** Vaethod wants Saelid's stall to keep its winter customers, because in a year when a cohort is sent away the camp-streets that buy the bread go empty. **Nedaen** *(neh-DAYN)*, one of the Taken-In, the people the wild took partway into itself and gave back, keeps the commons around the Tree and wants the gift-hall pot to feed their child through Ledger-month. Mother's green is enough until it is not. **Heiral** *(HAY-ral)* is Kept and lives on the camp-street. They want a marriage witnessed without being pitied as Mother-kept. They will take a neighbour's word, and they will not take a sermon. **Daron** *(DAR-on)* is Returned, a person who died with something unfinished and is still walking because of it, and they keep the far shed. They want a specific corpse named so a grain-debt dies with it. The family will not come downwind to do it.

**Orentel.** Mataero wants Sorel off the White Note's century-paper, and they think a clean loft-slate will buy that. **Sorim** wants berths 1–4 to stay held through Hale-month, the spring month when the land is counted sound. Their house-share is the hold-fee, the fee for keeping the berth, and not the wool. Tesara wants Valen stamped before the paying floor takes the master back. **Lasole** *(lah-SO-leh)* rents a leaf-lot on the rise and wants the credit extended one season. The lot will not follow them home, and home is a loft if it did. **Didel** *(dih-DEL)* of Hallowquay is Selkie, sea-born, one of the skin-kin, and works haulage. They want a kin-berth for a skin-kin wedding in Eolthael. Sorim has held berths 1–4 for a First-Hand year, a berth kept for the prestige walk to the origin, and Didel cannot hold those.

**Maiethlir.** Rithim wants Valein unfiled. The verso already knows how to write *unmarked*. Laevila wants Senithi's name read off the hidden face of a recut beam, once, in a room a clerk will accept as a witness. **Vehaela** *(veh-HAY-lah)* came down from Lirorn's thin snow with two children and wants an uncounted loft for one winter. The extra-mile street still has rooms the roll has not found. **Baerith** *(BAY-rith)* is Far-Voiced, a voice that carries exactly as far and as true as the singer means it to, and they sing the counted hour. They want the crown to stop cutting green on the street where their sister keeps a pot. To be cut off is framed as a lapse, and what it is, is a household going hungry.

The squares touch by paper, beds, pots, and one week in Eolthael, and the crossings are not a party. A household Vaethod sent last thin year sometimes eats now on a necessity-note from the White Note, a paper for a household in need. Mataero files the loft, and Vaethod does not know the house-year, the count that house keeps for itself. Sorim's berth 5 is earth. The pot, a young tree in the container it travels in, is going toward a luckier Hand. Haelin will sleep the Road-hand, the warden walking with that wood, if the wood arrives. That is the live front, not a reunion. Rithim Speaks the colour, and Laevila describes the other colour in the same square, the same morning. They already share a street, and they do not say the same thing. Tesara's week, if it is not burned at the end of it, becomes a person who later needs a loft or a berth. Mataero will hear occupancy, and Sorim will hear a hold they cannot invent. Thilim still holds a far-cape name the White Note does not have. A court that wants a Cut-year set beside a house-year writes inland and to the quay. The two answers will not match, and the two desks do not travel to deliver them. Reimaethe lives in a rise-room Mataero lets, and has given four years of memory into the Pourers' cup. Hithaen arrived in the gift-hall with no list behind them. Vaethod can tell a person who came to wait from a clerk who came to write, and has not yet decided which this is. Rosire works a ticket-infirmary downwind of Tesara's shed when they are in Orentel. This Hale-month they are not there, and Tesara has not been told why.

**Ledan** *(LED-an)* still writes the White Note quay-desk. They are Long-Lived, and the Cut-year 280 conversion-query, the question of how to turn one count of years into another, is theirs. They will convert a house-year, or a Cut-year, or a court's third date, if you say which. They will not invent a berth, and they will not invent a hull. What they want is the founding-name on the north bed spoken once, aloud, so they can stop being the only memory of it, and they will not speak it first. **Four people a campaign can start from.** They are four separate people, not a crew, and they do not travel as one.

**Reimaethe** *(RYE-may-theh)* is Long-Lived on the rise in Orentel, of the rise and of no house. Every Long-Lived person is asked once to join the Pourers, the circle that sets a year of memory into a cup, and Reimaethe said yes. A member gives one year at a time, each on its own night, with a drop of blood as the record, because a Long-Lived already reads memory in blood. The year goes into the cup and is gone from the member's memory, the papers from that year go onto the shelf, and the circle counts the member one year younger. The circle says the cup holds a god it made, and the Unspent is the presence in it, a small household presence that stores what it is given and does not make plans. The circle asks five years in all. Reimaethe has given four, and a member may stop before any year, while the years already given stay in the cup. They want to stop before the fifth, and they want their papers back, thin as that copy is, because the papers are all the circle returns. They want Mataero to keep writing *let* and not *why*. Neighbours call the practice a rich uncle's hobby, and they do not look like a hobby. A second cup sits next to the first. They have not asked to drink from it, because asking would already cost them something with the circle.

**Hithaen** *(hith-AYN)* arrived in Eolvaeth's gift-hall this Hale-month with no list behind them and a name they will not put on a wall. They are Given, and somewhere behind the walls that are still standing, families that have stayed Kept for generations are still a class, and a child Given at Leaf-Fall can fall out of that line, which is what happened to Hithaen. The house is **Brethlumal** *(breth-LU-mal)*. The line cast them out and kept the name on them. They will not say it, and they will not take *of Eolvaeth*. They want a life that is not a wall-pass, a life of being let through the wall, and on worse nights they want the name back, which is not the same want. They are not Talnin, and they are not Methei. Vaethod has not decided whether Hithaen came to wait or to be counted. What is left of the arrangement they walked out of is the Walled Book, the Inner Close inside Orenbren, political rank and not a church. This is one person eating alms in a vale that has no list, which is why they walked here.

**Taeren** *(TAY-ren)* is of Brenod, not of Harrow's and not of a capital, and there is no house to put first. They are a hush, the filing word for a white-fire the clerks will not let become a civic fact, and a child who stood at the neighbour's canopy when something white happened. The wider world knows there should be a Phoenix, the one person the stories are about, and it has not seen one in a generation, while doctrine says there cannot be two. If a white-fire is Spoken while a sanctuary still holds the Phoenix, the clerks file it as a mis-Speaking or as a hush. Last Eolthael Taeren stood at Harrow's Green. Something white happened, and Haelin did not Speak it. A colour that was never named aloud cannot be walked home as a witnessing, so Taeren is still in the square when the Hale-month crowds arrive, with the tenth year just behind them and heat in the hands they do not show. They ask ordinary questions, about bread and about whether the week is over and about why an old hymn uses a name they do not own, and they do not know why a fire answers them when someone says an old name. The public sentence about a Phoenix is still that there is one, and this chapter does not change it.

**Rosire** *(ro-SEER-eh)* is of Orentel, with no house, and this Hale-month they are sleeping in an upper room at Harrow's Green. They once held a post in a house they do not name. They opened a door they were not scheduled to open, and someone walked out through it. Rosire walked the other way, took the ticket-yard work of bringing water at Tesara's shed, and does not use the byname from before that floor. They are not on Tesara's water this week. They walked inland with the person who walked out. They want that person, and any child a fire might answer, not to become a story, and they want themselves not sent for. They look infirmary-tired, with grey under the nails from other people's stone, and travel stays out of their talk.

A neighbour still gets the given name. Raetoth, Lithsur, Lonteith, Relmun, Huval, and Nurreith are given names the places already use, and a stranger hears *of Nelath*, *of Raillath*, *of Denlad*, *of Tunral*, or *of the garden-end*. None of them has a house. Maiethlir writes a house before the given name: Laevila and Senithi are Thalonve, Rithim is Nirsei, Valein is that same house left unwritten, and Baerith is Rairtei. Vehaela is of Lirorn, and has not taken a house. Eolvaeth asks *of where?* Vaethod, Saelid, Heiral, Nedaen, and Daron are of the town, or of the street they already stand on. Hithaen will not take *of Eolvaeth*. Orentel asks *of where?* as well. The quay does not file a family. The White Note is a house only while the paper is open, and then it is the desk. Valen is of Orentel, and Didel is of Hallowquay. Thilim is of the Held bed, Taeren is of Brenod, and Njunda is the given name, which is the whole of the name.

The six pivots stay the six. Reimaethe, Hithaen, Taeren, and Rosire stay four separate people.
