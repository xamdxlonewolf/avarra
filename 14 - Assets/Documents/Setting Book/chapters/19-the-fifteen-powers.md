# The Fifteen Powers

## The fifteen

Fifteen named powers stand across the three large continents. Each one has a common name you say at the table and, where the old form still survives, a written name a charter, a rite, or a learned map will use, so at the table you say *the Night Shore* and *the Far Yield*, while a factor's docket may write *Netstrand* and *Vaelhesk*. A factor works for a house and prices routes and berths, and the docket is the page that house is willing to show a stranger. The Hinge Shore has no current written form of that older kind. *Eolstrand* is the retired name of that slot, replaced because the compound had crowded three names that already had jobs: *Eoloren*, the witnessed hinge and the clerical year-count; *Eolthael*, a month; and *Eolvaeth*, a city. A few old dockets still carry the crossed-out form. Current speech and current writing use the Hinge Shore.

Kumbaan, which the maps also call the Sundering Isle, is not a sixteenth power. It is an isolate with a table, a moon, and a storm-wall, a people at home on their own island, and it is not a flag on the Seat's list. A mile-shrine is a stone along a road, about a day's walk, often with a ribbon and a name, and the Isle has none.

Beneath the fifteen the world still swarms with marches, free towns, petty Tree-seats, and wild that no polity has claimed. A Tree-seat is a town that answers to its own Turning Tree, the tree grown from a graft, a cutting of living wood, so a child can stand under it. Fifteen names the powers worth naming at this table, while the marches, the free towns, and the wild continue under that count.

The name a polity gives itself shows the stance it has taken. The root sits in the old tongue, and the sound then drifts with that polity's devotion and with how far its Trees reach. On this page, **CAPS** mark the stress, `ae` is "ay", and `th` is the *th* of *thin*.

On Maiethorn the speech stays conservative. Pious talk keeps the *th* and the long vowels, which is why the Mother-names cluster in that country, why Threnmaieth counts her, and why Maiethvael gives in her name and will not count.

On Heskoren the speech is worn. Ordinary talk takes a *th* that follows a vowel and wears it down to *t*, and careful prayer puts the *th* back when the prayer is said. The frontier coinage *hesk-*, the far edge, is younger than the liturgy that still keeps the older sounds. The name written Vaethorn is spoken *Wetorn*. Ornled lets the old ending go. On this continent, dropping that sound is heard as a neglect, so the fuller reconstruction *Ornledd* goes unused.

On Strandoren the speech is eroded. A *th* goes to *t* or drops out, and the ends of words get clipped. Lestrand is the most drifted of the old three, the faith worn off the docks and the *th* worn off with it. Trenledd is what happens when *thren*, the sacred counting, itself wears down to a roll. The reconstruction *Threnledd* goes unused, because they will not say the holy word.

Every polity sets three things, and it sets them independently of one another.

Theology is how gift-devout the place is. At one end an ardent Motherfaith, the faith of the Leaf-Mother, takes the gift into ordinary life. The Leaf-Mother is the mother the hymns give the Tree, the one those hymns address. At the other end the polity is frankly secular, and it does not take the gift into the life of the place. That choice is already at work before the law speaks, because it decides how people read the Struck, who changed later and alone, the Kept, whom a Tree hugged and did not mark, and the Conditions a place has learned to fear. A Condition is the change a person comes to carry, and a Kind is the ancestry they were born with, so the two stack on the same person: what you become is a Condition, and what you were born is a Kind.

Reach is the difference between a Tree-rich core and a Tree-poor edge. Where healthy Trees stand, reach is full, and the Given are dense. The Given are the people a Tree marked at the Leaf-Fall, the tenth-year festival, when a leaf's colour names what the child will carry. Where the Trees are few, young, or sickening, more children come away Kept, and more people are Struck later, at some other threshold, with fewer witnesses standing by.

Governance is how far the state reaches onto the person. It runs from light guild self-rule, the warm default of this world, all the way to a crown that writes its subjects down.

Three more corners sit on the map beside the three drawn first: Maiethvael, devout, rich, and lightly governed; Trenledd, a cold surveillance with no faith to sanctify it; and Ornled, a secular frontier with the same thin Trees as the Waiting Lands and no net of congregations underneath.

The fifteen are hearth-countries, towns and households, and none of them is one born people under a crown. The Sown Tree is sand-country, not a kingdom of the fox-born. The Thaw-Land is a watershed, not a country of the Tengu. The Hinge Shore and the Night Shore are shores, not sea-realms of the Selkie. The Far Yield keeps the old guest-meal. It is not a nation of the Taken-In.

The Watching stays a heartland heresy inside Maiethorn's districts. It is an older Tree-faith the Motherfaith grew over. They keep the Tree, they refuse the Mother-name, and they say the colours fall because the Tree sees, not because a mother chooses. Leddvael is the one power that made a rival faith civic. Even there the Book-Hands, the clerks who stamp a Fair Hand signing and recite its terms, have no seat of their own.

### How they sit

**Maiethorn** holds six of the fifteen, on the old heart, the country of the grove. The six inherit one grove-gate that fell, not six ancient kingdoms, and they are still competing for that grove and for the Old Crossing, the oldest trade route in the world.

The Tallied Crown, written Threnmaieth, sits coiled near the sacred centre because it wants everyone written onto the Threnhael, the roll of persons it keeps and calls care. Its seat is Maiethlir. The Hand there, the town's own Turning Tree, stands on the Core-thaw, the meltwater river Maiethlir sits on, and that Hand is not the grove. Pilgrims still walk on from the town to Thaeloren, the Awakening Tree, and the crown does not own it. The First Seat, the college of tree-wardens, stays in the Motherwood beside the town.

The Gift-Realm, written Maiethvael, shares the Mother the hymns address and refuses the list, so it sings the same hymns Threnmaieth sings and keeps the opposite paper. Its capital is Seinbrun.

The Witness-Hearths, written Orenbren, are the lodging-country of the Near Mile, the approach road to the grove, and the rule there is civic rather than clerical. The First Seat sits in the Motherwood beside them and is not their crown. Their capital is Rothallo, still called the Inner Close, standing inside Orenbren and not a power of its own. The Third Hearth is one lodging-hearth on that road, three days out from the grove. The Mill-hold, two days past Brenthael, is a Hands-town whose Hand is unsound this year, so its children walk in toward Brenthael, the neighbour town whose own Tree can still do the week's work. Nelath is a spur that leaves the Mile a day short of the Third Hearth and then stops.

The Sown Tree, written Saelthael, is the leeward east, still walking west after the core stopped, with its city at Larbril and the smaller well-town, Ornsael, farther into the dry.

The Hinge Shore faces Strandoren across the Old Crossing and classifies the hull at Votaer, deciding what a ship is before anyone across the water prices it.

The Thaw-Land, written Lirorn, holds the Rain-Wall, which the people of the range call the Thaw-Wall and lowlanders call the Rain-Wall, and its town is Tasain, in a sheltered valley below the Shelf-gate. Fox-of-the-Snows hearths, the white-coated fox-born of the high snow, sit densest on that snow, and so do Tengu hearths, though neither of those peoples has the snow as their country.

**Strandoren** holds five, along coasts and compacts, with no single crown over them. They inherit a Salt Walk that outlived the grove-gate, the old sea-then-land road to a Turning, and a fever that made people write new paper.

Lestrand is the premier coast, seated at Orentel on the Salt Quay, where the White Note keeps a house on the third quay that writes terms and does not wear the crown, while Brenledd, the Hearth-League, sits its council at Raitin and stays a league of hearths rather than becoming one house. Along the same shore, Leddvael keeps the signing-watch at Naenor, Trenledd keeps a roll of persons at Lunbra on the Chart-run and leaves its ruler unnamed, and Netstrand quotes the far crossing from Braetu while it watches for hulls that do not arrive.

**Heskoren** holds four, with a long wild between the Trees. They came into the clocks late, host-rights were already on the ground when the wood arrived, and waiting, sowing, and slate are three answers to the same late cart.

Vaethorn preaches the waiting-gospel, the hot faith of a country where the gift is scarce, from its seat at Eolvaeth on the pilgrim-edge, which is not Saelvaeth's march, not Harrow's Green, and not the three hamlets past the ford.

Saelvaeth, the Sown Waiting, stands its march-voice at Natai. Harrow's Green sits in the orbit of that voice, a grove-town whose Tree grew up enough to do the week's work, and it stays out of the seat.

Vaelhesk, the Far Yield, keeps the land itself as the seat, so the First Bowl, which folk call *Lonasir*, stays a guest-grove, and a traveler who asks for the seat is pointed at a door.

Ornled, the Outer Ledger, keeps the secular frontier's slate at Sanbreo.

The Three Hamlets Past the Ford sit on the seam where those four can all be heard. From the hamlets you can see Harrow's canopy, which is counted as Saelvaeth's luck, and you can still hear Vaethorn's guilt and Vaelhesk's refusal. Folk call the three *Brenod*, *Vaelun*, and *Ornath*. The Seat still does not use those names.

What the fifteen inherited is younger than a row of ancient kingdoms, and two clocks still organize a life beside that inheritance: how a person Turned, by the old walk to the grove or under a town Tree, and how far the wood has reached. The clerical count, the Cut-years, runs from the First Cut, the grafting's arrival, and the present year of that count is Cut-year 387, which is why a border, a rate, or a grudge can be pointed at the year that left it. In the Closing, from Cut-year 19 to 38, the old gate on the one Tree tried to pull the copied Trees up and could not. In Cut-year 171 the Grey Summer, which the docks call the Salt-Grey, was a quay-fever in which people died who already had a local Tree, and no colour fell with the fever, so the summer never became a Tithe. When a household fails for want of what keeps it whole, that failing is a fade. In Cut-year 304 the Hinge Hush ended a fight between the Hinge Shore and the Ledger Coast over who set the toll, and the rate it left still covers hulls without covering the origin-winter, the prestige year a household books at the grove.

### Two lists of the same fifteen

A clerk of the Seat and a Lestrand factor do not hand you the same map, and the shared practice is what keeps the peace between them. You belong by the rite you actually keep, so a Watcher who brings a child, a Settler of the Fair Hand who stands witness to the colour, and a speaker of the Old Ways who also walks the old doors can all stand at the same Leaf-Fall. Doctrine fights, and the festival usually does not. The list each speaker is willing to write is what gives that speaker away.

> **From a Cut-year minute, First Seat (Cut-year 387).** *Threnmaieth names itself the Reckoning and would name us. Maiethvael will not be named to them and still gives the extra mile. Orenbren lodges what we do not house. Saelthael still walks, which the origin finds inconvenient. The Hinge Shore takes the Old Crossing. Lirorn takes the thaw. Across the water the factor's five: Lestrand, the Brenledd compact, Leddvael which files a Bound as a civic virtue, Trenledd which keeps a roll and will not call it a Threnhael, Netstrand which sells the west. On the far reach: Vaethorn waiting, Saelvaeth planting, Vaelhesk feeding a first meal we did not authorize, Ornled which will not say her name and will not keep a list either. Kumbaan is not a land we graft. We do not write it.*
>
> **From a house-year docket, a Lestrand factor.** *Price the Hinge Shore and do not argue hymns with the Gift-Realm. The Witness-Hearths' beds are a market that calls itself a duty. Avoid the Worn Count's roll unless you like being written. The Reckoned Gift will sign; that is cheaper than faith. The Hearth-League is many tickets. The Night Shore quotes the far crossing like a pilgrimage. Heskoren: the Waiting pays in blessing, the Outer Ledger in slate, the Far Yield in a meal you had better eat, the Sown Waiting in whatever the queue did this year. The Isle is a tale. We do not book it.*

Neither list is complete, and both are true of the speaker who wrote them.

## The three corners

Say the Waiting Lands, the Ledger Coast, and the Tallied Crown. Charters, rites, and learned maps write *Vaethorn*, *Lestrand*, and *Threnmaieth*. The same law, the same Tithe, and the same family life run through all three, and the three settings turn them into different places. Each pair shares exactly one setting and differs on the other two.

The Waiting Lands and the Ledger Coast share a light state, and that is the only setting they share, one of them warm, poor, and faithful, the other rich, cool, and secular. The Waiting Lands and the Tallied Crown share the same ardent faith and live it in different circumstances, devotion at a thin edge beside devotion in a sanctified core that watches its people. The Ledger Coast and the Tallied Crown share the same wealth of Trees and spend it in opposite directions, free markets on the Coast and a census of persons held as holy under the Crown.

Theology is not a measure of good or bad. High theology produces both the kindest of these corners to a whole neighbour and the most inescapable control in the world, because devotion multiplies whatever else the polity already is, and each corner owns the harm that multiplication makes. Warm faith, free markets, and sanctified order produce different harms, through different institutions. A polity's name for a shared thing shows the stance, which is why a devout land's gift-hall, where a Far-Voiced vents feeling at worship, is a surveillance-crown's counted hall.

### Threnmaieth: the Tallied Crown

*(THREN-mai-eth. The name sets thren, sacred counting, beside maieth, the Mother, and it means the Mother's Reckoning. People say the Tallied Crown. The crown writes Threnmaieth. Theology high, reach high, governance high.)*

Threnmaieth is a pious, Tree-rich core kingdom that is also Watchful, the far end of governance, a crown that writes its subjects down. It keeps a census of persons and of the Conditions they carry, and it does this in the wealthiest, most devout, most Tree-dense land, so the roster is kept as sanctified duty. The Mother's gifts are precious, and the crown's answer is that they must be accounted for. Trenledd keeps a roll of persons too. The difference is that the Tallied Crown calls its roll holy, and Trenledd does not. The other two corners have injustices you can name and resent. This one dresses its injustice in the two things the world most trusts, faith and plenty.

**Law.** The record is held on people, every subject and the gift they carry written down. Guild rolls and tax ledgers exist everywhere. What is unusual here is a census of persons, framed at home as reverent stewardship of the Mother's blessings and lived, from outside, as surveillance. Other cities nest the witness as a hearth-stand, a quay-gang or a courtyard small enough that someone actually saw you Turn, and then they stop. Threnmaieth copies that stand onto the Threnhael, and the copying is the whole difference. Reach is high, so there is a great deal to count: dense Given, dense guilds, and abundant Long-Lived, the ageless keepers of memory, whose memory the crown distrusts precisely because it is not the crown's. That distrust is the Tallied Crown's deepest tell. It will not rely on the three pillars the rest of the world runs on: Long-Lived memory, which keeps what a person witnessed even a century later; a Bound's sworn word, which closes a bargain and belongs to someone who can hear a lie as it is told; and Far-Voiced feeling, which comes through in the voice and is hard to fake on a stand. Living memory, word, and feeling answer to themselves, and a ledger answers to the crown. The unvouched are hunted, and the Struck are pursued so they can be catalogued. The Bound are watched far past their real hazard, stigma dressed as safety, because the Inviolate Will, the maxim every child is taught, says the will is one's own. The Whisper that comes with a Bound debt can suggest, and it cannot command another person's will, which the roster's own clerks know. Devotion supplies the justification a bare Watchful crown lacks. You would not hide a gift from the Mother, so why hide it from her stewards?

**Economy.** Tithe-need, the upkeep a person requires in order to carry what they have become, has been turned into leverage, and then consecrated. The crown provides that upkeep efficiently, even generously, because a pious, rich state can afford to, and it holds what it provides. The green a Taken-In needs, time on living earth, the novelty a Long-Lived needs, new books and new things to learn so the years do not go stale, the outlets where a Far-Voiced can vent, and the medicine of the Stilled, whose gaze can hold a wound or a patient still, all come through channels the crown controls and can withhold. A population whose staying-whole runs through the state cannot afford to refuse it. That is the most complete control anywhere, and here it wears a halo. To receive your provision is to receive the Mother's bounty through her appointed hands. To be cut off is framed as your lapse, not the crown's lever. Its deathless-house finance, the credit of houses that do not die when their founders do, is excellent and centuries-deep. The Long-Lived here are courtiers and crown-archivists as much as bankers, their memory bent to the census rather than left free to check it.

**Daily life.** Sacrament and census run together. A child's Turning, the hour they stand at the Tree, is a joyous holy day that is also a state registration. The colour that falls is celebrated with a hymn and recorded by a clerk. Marriages of heritable Conditions, Two-Bodied bloodlines especially, the families who carry a second animal self and pass that animal down, are noticed, encouraged or discouraged, and filed. The medicine that furnishes a Tithe is excellent, and it runs through the crown's channels. A household whose bloodline and upkeep are both on file cannot easily refuse the crown anything. The Kept are pitied here in the warm way, and recorded in the Watchful way, passed over by the Mother, and noted as such.

**Its injustice.** This is the worst of both high axes, each excusing the other. The devotion sanctifies the surveillance, because it is called reverence to be counted, and the surveillance enforces the devotion, because to refuse the count is called impiety. There is no clean villain. A wealthy, faithful, orderly people believe that accounting for the Mother's gifts is the highest form of tending them, and that belief makes the machinery of control almost impossible to resist. Elsewhere the roll reads as chilling, because only two crowns keep one. Here the faith is what lets it feel like grace.

**The seat.** Maiethlir *(MAI-eth-lir)* is a Core-thaw Hand in the Sacred Core, one or two days' walk from the wood, on a different road from Orenbren's Near Mile, a thaw-river town whose own Tree Turns the town's children. The older job of this ground is the extra mile, and devotion still sends people on that walk, on from the town toward the grove. The Threnhael is the roll laid over that older walk. Thaeloren stands outside this crown. The First Seat stays in the wood beside Maiethlir, and the seat does not own the grove.

**What they remember.** The Two Papers, in Cut-year 67, are what this crown calls the year the reckoning stood. Limrae opened a desk and wrote persons down, because a gift you cannot find again, as they said it, is a gift you have already failed to tend. The crown-count starts in that year. A devout clerk may write Cut-year 387 and Crown-year 320 on the same slate, and the difference is the split. Maiethvael is the cousin who would not be named. The Inner Close keeps another list, of rank and not of care, and will not share ink.

### Lestrand: the Ledger Coast

*(LES-trand. Ledd, to reckon, joined to strand, the shore, and then eroded smooth, so the name means the Reckoning Coast. People say the Ledger Coast. Formal records keep Lestrand. Theology low, reach high, governance low.)*

Lestrand is a dense, Tree-rich merchant power. The Trees are mature and many, the Given are dense, and the faith is worn lightly. Deep markets, thin welfare, and a cool, transactional read on becoming are the character of the place. It shares the Waiting Lands' light-touch governance, in that no crown writes anyone down. Where the Lands' light state leaves a gap the faith fills, the Coast's light state leaves a space the guilds fill.

**Law.** This is the world's warm default carried as far as it goes, guild self-rule at full development. The state is a charter-granting council of merchant houses and craft-guilds. It charters the Stilled's guild and the element-guilds, and it watches the guild, never the individual. Citizenship-by-witness runs smoothly here because reach is high. Long-Lived notaries are abundant, a Bound's sworn word closes a contract, and a Far-Voiced stands on every dock and in every court. A city this size nests that witness as hearths, a quay-gang or a courtyard, rather than asking twenty thousand people to share one memory. The three pillars are not reverence here. They are infrastructure, the machinery that lets a huge commercial city run on trust without a register. Travelers clear on a road-word or a guild-mark. The gate asks three questions, and it does not ask your colour. The Struck are vouched in briskly, as efficiency rather than grace, because an unvouched person is a transaction that cannot clear. The Inviolate Will is cited less as sacred bedrock than as sound commercial law. A Bound's contract binds property, not persons, and the Coast likes that line because it makes the Bound the perfect notary.

**Economy.** This is the richest of the three corners, and the coolest. Deathless banking is most developed here. Deathless houses, whose trusted notes are the nearest thing the world has to a gold standard, run century instruments, and the endow-not-inherit loop runs at scale. They have no line of heirs waiting on a death, and their own Tithe needs a steady supply of novelty, so the great endowed libraries and universities cluster here and fund that novelty by existing. Self-paying Conditions prosper, because their labor is their upkeep and the market pays for the labor: the Answered, whose craft is an element that answers their hands, the Two-Bodied haulage and courier trades, and the Stilled surgeons. Tithe-provision is a private market, not a public good. You buy your green, your novelty, your outlet, and if you can afford it you stay whole in comfort. The injustice is the market's. The provided-for Tithe-poor, the people whose upkeep someone else has to supply, get thin welfare in a rich city, the starkest have-and-have-not gap anywhere. The shadow economy runs biggest here because the legitimate market is biggest here: smuggled green, black-market novelty, sold vouching, and off-book contracts that feed the Given-Over, a Bound who has contracted away so much of themselves that the counterparty effectively owns the person. The concentration risk the deathless pose is sharpest on this coast. A handful of houses that never die quietly steer the wealthiest polity through patronage that never disperses.

**Daily life.** Condition-medicine is excellent for those who can reach it. The great Stilled infirmaries and the deathless-endowed hospitals are here, in the rich core, and the queue outside them is the longest here too. Family life shrugs at theology. There is a marriage market that reads Conditions as assets. A Two-Bodied bloodline is a trade dynasty, a Long-Lived match is a century of credit, and the aching Long-Lived marriage, grief set against advantage, gets weighed coolly. The Turning is a civic and commercial milestone more than a sacrament. A family's Leaf-Fall is watched for what guild the child's colour opens. It is not warm. It is free, and it is prosperous.

**Its injustice.** This is the coolest of the three. Nothing is policed. Your marriage, your gift, and your faith are your own. Everything is priced, and a person who cannot pay slides while a whole rich city steps around them. The Coast will never file you, and it will never conscript you. It will let you fade if you run out of coin, and it will feel that it owed you nothing.

**The seat.** Orentel *(OREN-tel)* is a salt-quay city on the Old Crossing. The written name is a witnessing-Tree with the *th* worn off, and coast-folk say the Salt Quay and mean the city. The White Note House sits on the third quay, north side, and it is not the crown.

**What they remember.** Salt Walk paper outlived the gate of the Closed Seat, and the notes outlived the Grey Summer. The rate the first quay still charges is the Hinge Hush, the peace of Cut-year 304, after five summers in which the Hinge Shore and this coast fought over who set the toll. Sirtol spoke for a pile of charters that was not the White Note and was not a crown. Brenledd piled a compact rather than be priced one hearth at a time. The White Note is one desk-house. It is not the polity.

### Vaethorn: the Waiting Lands

*(VAETH-orn, spoken Wetorn. Vaeth, the waiting, joined to orn, land, so the name means the Waiting Land. Careful writing and prayer keep the sacred th. People say the Waiting Lands, or simply the Waiting. Theology high, reach low, governance low.)*

Vaethorn is a broad, thinly peopled land at the world's Tree-poor margin, where the faith burns hottest because the Trees are scarce, and where the ruling mood is waiting: for a graft, young wood set in this ground, to take, for a distant Tree to be reached, for a gift that may never fall. Where the Trees are few and young, more children come away Kept, more people are Struck later and alone, and the Motherfaith answers that scarcity with more devotion, not less. Pilgrimage, longing, and a theology built around a gift that may not come are the answer the land actually makes.

**Law.** Governance is almost absent. No crown reaches the individual, and custom outruns written law the way it does on any frontier. Citizenship is witnessed in the oldest, most literal way, a settlement small enough that everyone was there when you Turned. Thin reach makes that fragile. With few Long-Lived to remember and few Bound to vouch, the three pillars that make proof-by-people work are stretched thin. The unvouched Struck, a person who changed alone, with no guild to catch them and few witnesses to stand for them, is a constant, aching problem, and the Waiting Lands meet it with faith rather than machinery. A feared Condition here is more likely to be met with a rope or a blessing than a licence, because there is no guild to issue one.

**Economy.** There is little formal Tithe-infrastructure and there are few specialists, and the theology reframes that poverty as charity. Where a secular frontier improvises provision as barter and favor, the Waiting Lands do it as alms and congregation. The venting a Far-Voiced needs happens at worship. The green a Taken-In needs is furnished by neighbours who count it a sacred duty. The Taken-In, whom rich cities keep at arm's length and still need on the lots, are here the honoured backbone of the land. Working the living earth is the holy economy. It is commons-minded generosity with almost nothing to fund it: real warmth, real want, and a thin edge where the provided-for Tithe-poor slide with only prayer for a net.

**Daily life.** Ceremony is thin, and devotion makes what there is of it enormous. A child's Turning at a small, hard-won grafted Tree is the event of a decade, held as a joyous, anxious sacrament. The Kept child is felt most sharply here, because in a land starved of the gift, to be passed over reads, wrongly and cruelly, as being passed over by the Mother herself. Medicine is a travelling stiller if the settlement is lucky, and a rope and a prayer if it is not. The gap between core and edge is at its widest here. Families are warm, mixed, and self-reliant. A lapsing Condition with no infirmary to reach is a frontier tragedy the faith consoles and cannot cure.

**Its injustice.** The harm is not a boot. It is a guilt. The scarcity of the gift becomes a theology of unworthiness. The Kept wonder what they lacked. The Struck wonder why the Mother came to them at the wrong door and alone. A hard, quiet piety can shade into treating want as a spiritual failing. The Waiting Lands are the kindest place in the world to a whole neighbour and the loneliest to an unvouched one.

**The seat.** Eolvaeth *(EOL-vaeth)* is a pilgrim-edge vale behind the Strandoren-facing coast. Everyday worn speech shortens the name to Elvaeth *(EL-vaeth)*. People walk here to wait, and the Tree at the end of that wait is not a certainty. The seat is not Harrow's Green, not the three hamlets, and not Saelvaeth's march.

**What they remember.** The wave arrived late, and the First Cut is the name of that arrival. The Hinge Hush says they may bless a Heskoren landing and may not levy that landing as a gospel-due, which leaves Ornled with no hymn to charge and lets Saelvaeth take a pot, soil and living wood for a graft, without Vaethorn's waiting-price. The guilt in the gospel wants both of those neighbours unworthy. The history of the planting does not agree.

## The twelve

The twelve that are not the three corners each have a seat, a capital or a city or a town, except in the Far Yield, where the land itself is the seat and no city was built to hold it. Maiethorn's five are Seinbrun, Rothallo, Larbril, Votaer, and Tasain; Strandoren's four are Raitin, Naenor, Lunbra, and Braetu; and Heskoren's are Sanbreo, the land of Vaelhesk, and Natai, with the corner capitals standing apart from all of those at Eolvaeth, Orentel, and Maiethlir. What stays unnamed is part of the count: six charter-towns around Raitin, the person who governs Trenledd, and Nidtol, which the Hearth-League still recites as a hearth rather than as a city you can walk into. The Book-Hands have no seat. A square a party can visit does not become a capital by being visitable, and the First Seat stays in the Motherwood, a college of tree-wardens, outside this list of twelve.

### Maiethvael: the Gift-Realm

*(MAI-eth-vael. Maieth, the Mother, kept and joined to vael, gift, so the name means the Mother's Gift. The old sounds stay. People say the Gift-Realm and Gift-folk. Charters write Maiethvael. Theology high, reach high, governance low.)*

Maiethvael is an old, Tree-dense realm of the Motherland that treats Tithe-provision as public tending. Congregation and guild furnish the greens, the halls, and the medicine, rather than a channel a crown can shut. You belong because living people saw you Turn, and there is no Threnhael here. They will tell you that counting persons is a failure of trust in the three pillars. The hymns are the same ones Threnmaieth sings. The paper is not, because Threnmaieth writes people down and Maiethvael will not.

**Site.** This is full-reach Maiethorn country, in sight of the pilgrimage weather and not built around the grove. The older job of the ground is an origin pilgrimage-country that furnishes. The extra mile, the walk someone still makes toward the grove when a local Tree would have done, is hospitality here. It is not a line on a roll.

**Seat.** Seinbrun *(SAYN-brun)* is the capital, a large city off the Core-thaw, down that river and north of Maiethlir on the atlas, more than a day from Maiethlir and past the first day below the Down Gate. That first day is the Down-Bank, the road below Maiethlir's gate, and Gift-folk live along it, so a traveler can spend the day among them and still not have arrived. Seinbrun is neither Maiethlir nor the Down-Bank. The smaller towns and villages around it stay unnamed. The hall furnishes what people need, and the city does not keep a list of them.

**How it formed.** The realm is a gift-country that never accepted a census. In Cut-year 67, the year of the Two Papers, Manril would not be named to Limrae's roll. Maiethvael kept the older proof. You belong because you were seen to Turn, and you stay whole because your neighbours tend what you were Given. The state is light on purpose, because faith and guild do the work a clerk would do elsewhere.

**What they remember.** They remember the same hymn and the opposite paper. Threnmaieth's crown-count is a river-habit they will not convert to. The Close's Book is someone else's rank.

**Structure.** Guilds rule themselves under an ardent practice. The realm recruits by office, by faith, and by the Condition a person carries, and not by Kind. The Watching persists in old districts here as it does elsewhere on Maiethorn. It is not a second flag.

**Goals.** They keep the gift hale without writing the gifted down, and they furnish generously. They pity the Kept in a land that teaches that a hug at the Tree is rare. The land is wrong about that, and it teaches it anyway. The pity is a slight, and it has no thin-Tree excuse, because the Trees here are not scarce.

**Its injustice.** What hurts here does not arrive as a boot. It is a social tax of piety, real warmth with a real bill attached. Fall out of the congregation and the net goes with it, and there is no crown to appeal to. The Kept here are pitied in the middle of plenty. Threnmaieth at least files them. Maiethvael loves them and cannot stop measuring what they missed.

### Orenbren: the Witness-Hearths

*(OREN-bren. Oren, to witness, beside bren, hearth, so the name means the witnessing-hearth. People say the Witness-Hearths and Hearth-folk. Charters write Orenbren. It began as a string of inns and became a country, not a single house. Theology high, reach high, governance mid.)*

Orenbren is the civic mass of the Near Mile: lodging-squares, upper rooms, and old road-houses that stayed hearths after the walk stopped being required in the core. Pilgrims still come, some of them on the extra mile and some buying the First-Hand year, the prestige season spent near the grove, and Orenbren houses them. The power in that housing is a hold on the beds within a week's walk of the grove, not a crown set on the grove itself. Orenbren does not sell the Coast's product. The berth is Orentel's to hold, the paper is the White Note's to write, the hull may be Netstrand's to charter, and what Orenbren takes is the sleep.

The First Seat sits in the Motherwood beside this power and remains a college, not a throne. Threnmaieth would like the lodging-rolls. Orenbren witnesses who slept, and it does not witness what colour they are. It does not own Thaeloren.

Inside the lodging-country, and not as a sixteenth flag, sits Rothallo *(roh-THAL-oh)*, the walled capital, one day's walk from the wood, still called the Inner Close, and the Close. The walls answer to those names. The Closed Heirs, the households that still claim rank inside those walls, rank the Conditioned by a Book of Tithes. Orenbren calls the arrangement a guest-compact. Its inns lodge anyone outside the wall, and the Book rules inside it. The Close records who owes, Orenbren records who slept, and neither copies the other's records. The guest-compact starts at Hildal's Retreat. In Cut-year 38 Hildal, the last person of the Closed Seat that anyone will still name, ordered that withdrawal, saved a book rather than the grove, and put the beds outside the wall. Smaller towns and villages around the walls stay unnamed. The First Seat stays in the wood, the wood is not crowned, and Rothallo is not the college.

A Hand stands inside the walls, and the city has no speaker of its own for the Leaf-Fall. A warden of the First Seat may come in for Turning-Week and Speak the fall there. If a colour is copied afterward, the copy is the Close's. The Low Wall is a different run of stone in a fold of the same lodging-country, outside the Close, found already in the ground. The Close will tell you that wall is theirs. The stone is a different colour, and the quarry is not the Close's.

**Site.** This is Maiethorn's Sacred Core approach-country, an origin pilgrimage-town repeated along the road. A Tree stands at the centre of each square. The road used to go further, and now it ends in a festival season.

**How it formed.** These were walking-era hearths that federated around the one-week door, the single week in which a child had to be present or miss being Given. In the Closing they would not starve a pilgrim-season in order to save the grove, and that refusal is the lodging-country's first weather. When the Years of Hands made the origin-walk optional, the years in which a town Tree is the ordinary way to Turn, the beds did not empty. The work those beds did became a different job. The lodging-network learned it could levy, charter, and speak as one without becoming clergy.

**What they remember.** They housed the war by keeping the extra beds open. They did not sit the grove then, and they do not sit it now. Threnmaieth wants the lodging-rolls. The Close ranks who owes.

**Structure.** Orenbren is many hearth-towns, not a single inn, run by town-wardens and lodging-guilds, and it does not recruit by Kind. Deathless houses exist here as patrons of beds, paying to keep rooms. The Third Hearth is one lodging square of that type, three days out from the grove. Brenthael is the neighbour Hands-town, and its children Turn at home while the Near Mile goes on past its Tree. The Mill-hold, two days past Brenthael, is a Hands-town whose Hand is unsound this year, so its children walk in toward Brenthael. Nelath is a road-end on a spur that leaves the Mile a day short of the Third Hearth and then stops. Rothallo remains the capital, and the seat.

**Goals.** They keep the extra mile possible, and they charge for it in coin, in duty, or in hymn. Which of the three a given town uses is the local argument. They refuse to be named as the First Seat's civic arm, and they refuse to be named as Threnmaieth's counted inn.

**Its injustice.** The harm is hospitality as rent, devotion priced as board. A pilgrim who cannot pay the First-Hand year still needs the hug, the moment under the Tree they came for, and the upper room has learned what a bed is worth in Eolthael, the month when the children stand. The kindness comes with a slate.

### Saelthael: the Sown Tree

*(SAEL-thael. Sael, sowing, beside thael, Tree, so the name means the sown Tree. People say the Sown Tree and Sown-folk. Charters write Saelthael. The shared sael with Saelvaeth, the Sown Waiting, stays visible in the writing, and speech keeps the two powers apart. They are still the Motherland. Theology high, reach mid, a country of wells, governance low.)*

Saelthael is the Rain-Shadow, the country leeward and east of the Rain-Wall, dry hills and sand, with towns set at wells and at whatever graft will take. The seasonal water is the Well-wash, on a continent that is otherwise full-reach, though this side of it is thinner wood. The core stopped walking once its own Trees were enough. Saelthael did not. Children still go west in the neighbour's week, the walk to a Tree that can do the work when theirs cannot, or they go further than a week, because a well-Tree is not a certainty.

Fox of the Sands hearths are densest here because the ground is dry, and that density is the terrain, so a town with no fox-born would be the thing worth asking about.

**Site.** This is Maiethorn's dry east, a Rain-Shadow walk-hold on the west-road the core stopped walking. The hold is the proof that Ornthael, the liturgical name of the present years, meaning Tree-land, the years in which a town Tree is supposed to be enough, is unfinished even on the origin continent. Ornsael *(ORN-sael)*, the sown-ground, stays the smaller well-town, farther into the dry, and it is still walking west. Its drink is a well.

**Seat.** Larbril *(LAR-bril)* is the city, a medium one, where the west road meets the Well-wash, short of the pass. Walking west, the Shelf-gate is still ahead. Walking east, the dry is in front of you. The Dry Stair is another rise, on its own ground, and it is the local dare that is not a Tree. They climb it so they know the difference, and then they walk west. The Stair is not the reason for the city. The city is here because the road meets the wash.

**How it formed.** Tree-seats formed around wells and around grafts that took slowly. From Cut-year 0 to 80 the work was slower here, and the cause was dirt, not a different gospel. When the origin-squares became Hands-country, places where a local Tree could do the week's work, the sand-country kept the habit of going to a healthier canopy. That habit grew into a polity of the people who still sow. The Thaw-Break, the one-week melt of Cut-year 233, cut the west-road for two summers, so some children went later and some missed the door. The Given-door, the week in which a child can still be Given, did not loosen.

**What they remember.** The core calls their waiting a matter of soil. They still walk west.

**Structure.** Custom is light. Town-wardens keep it, and the wells are a civic fact, something the town has to manage whether or not anyone is devout that week. The realm does not recruit by Kind. The Taken-In are honoured here as they are on any thirsty land, the backbone of the work and not a nation of their own.

**Goals.** They keep grafts alive in thin soil, they keep the west-road open, and they refuse the core's story that waiting here is a spiritual failing.

**Its injustice.** The motherland treats their waiting as a local soil-problem, which is true as far as it goes, and also as a slight against the origin. Plenty sits next door. The pilgrimage has become a leftover they cannot retire. Vaethorn's guilt, the teaching that want is a failing of the soul, has been imported onto sand that never asked for it.

### The Hinge Shore

*(The Hinge Shore is the name people say and the name the maps write, and its people are Hinge-folk. Some old dockets write Eolstrand, a form no current authority uses. Theology mid, reach high, governance low.)*

These are Maiethorn's western ports on the Old Crossing. The shore is ancient and rich, and less frantic than the Ledger Coast. The Trees are still healthy, because this is still the Motherland. Faith is worn thinner here than in Maiethvael, because a dock's work is to sort one arrival from another.

Lestrand prices the crossing, and the Hinge Shore sorts it, which is why a child, a pot of living earth, a Given-Over, a recanter, and a necessity-family on a neighbour's week can all make the quay-bell ring the same note. A recanter is someone taking back a standing, the claim that a witness watched them. A necessity-family is walking because the Tree at home cannot do the work. The bell does not tell those arrivals apart. A clerk writes which docket they belong on, and that writing is the job. The Standing Trade, the people who sell a standing, works this face more often than Orentel, because a standing has to travel and this is the water it has to cross.

Selkie families are common on these quays, as they are on any old water. That is a hearth of families, not a sea-realm with the Selkie as its flag.

**Site.** The shore is the Old Crossing's other face, salt-adjacent quays on the side of the Salt Walk where people landed. People left Strandoren and came ashore here. The Hush-rate, the charge the Hinge Hush left behind, is collected as old custom. They will not call it a victory.

**Seat.** Votaer *(VOH-tayr)* is the port, one of the largest on this coast, because the crossing is why the people are there. Hulls are classified on those quays, and the Hush-rate is charged there. The port faces Orentel without becoming a second Orentel. The White Note writes its terms on Orentel's third quay, not on this one. Towns and villages along the rest of the shore stay unnamed.

**How it formed.** Port-kings and charter-harbours grew up on the crossing. The tongue stayed conservative because the grove is at their backs, and the devotion stayed middling because Lestrand is in front of them. A hinge has two sides. The Grey Summer taught this pious shore how to step around a fade without putting the blessing down. The cooler way of doing things that Lestrand is known for began here as the weather of that summer, not as a conversion.

**What they remember.** Taerso spoke the Hush for the hinge-houses. The name is a desk, not a throne. They charge the rate, and they say the blessing if a customer wants one.

**Structure.** The shore is a guild-coast with a light state and old houses. It does not recruit by Kind. The Far-Voiced stand on every quay as infrastructure, the same as on Strandoren, with more hymn left in the voice.

**Goals.** They keep the Old Crossing as the world's highway by classifying a hull before anyone prices it, and they broker pilgrimage-traffic and cargo without becoming Threnmaieth's counted port or Lestrand's priced one.

**Its injustice.** The harm is category as fate. Lestrand will let you fade, and it will feel that it owed you nothing. The Hinge Shore will bless you in the same hour that it files you, and a Tithe-poor household fails in sight of the pilgrimage-road with a hymn still in the air. They learned that step-around by looking across the water in a grey summer. They kept the hymn so they would not have to call the step a conversion.

### Lirorn: the Thaw-Land

*(LIR-orn. Lir, thaw, water that runs again, beside orn, land. People say the Thaw-Land and Thaw-folk. Charters write Lirorn. They call the range the Thaw-Wall. Lowlanders say the Rain-Wall. Theology mid, reach high, governance mid.)*

Lirorn is Maiethorn's highland spine: high snow, towns you reach by rope or by wing, and rivers that begin as thaw. Reach is still full, which here means the grafts take in the valleys and on the sheltered shelves, but the weather is the bigger fact, and the state is a watershed holding, organized around the water rather than around a cathedral square. The lowlands drink Lirorn's spring, and the ridges have noticed. Maiethlir drinks the Core-thaw, the melt that comes off this wall, and that thirst is a lever Lirorn can use.

Tengu ridge-towns and Fox-of-the-Snows hearths sit densest here. Every shelf is mixed Kinds, people of more than one birth. A lowlander arriving on foot is a guest who climbed. A wing-post is a job a person holds, not an order closed to every other birth. Sky-couriers hear first, and the hearing has a price.

**Site.** The holding is the Rain-Wall, where ridge-roads run along the old walking-paths to the grove, stones stand in the high squares, and some of those roads now end at a local Tree. Crossing the range takes two passes. The Noon Pass is the water-line over the old ribbon-notch, the mark where travelers used to tie a ribbon, and it stays a pass, higher and older than the road below. The Shelf-gate is the road Narol took after the Thaw-Break.

**Seat.** Tasain *(tah-SAYN)* is the walled town in a sheltered valley below the Shelf-gate, where the west road comes down and the levy is taken, reckoned on how much snow fell the winter before. The town gate is Tasain's own wall, and the Shelf-gate is the pass above it, while the villages and hamlets stay up on the shelves and Larbril sits on the dry side of that pass. Narol of the Pass is dead. The household that inherited the toll still levies Break-rate, the toll named for the Thaw-Break, even in a kind year, and will not explain it to a lowlander. No new person is named beside Narol.

**How it formed.** The holding grew as march-holds on the snowmelt. Governance sits in the middle because water is a fact you can levy without taking a census of souls. You tax the thaw, you tax the pass, and you tax the high road. Theology cooled because of the height and the distance from the grove's daily pressure, not because anyone here stopped believing. In Cut-year 233 the levy became visible as a water-line, and that year gave the older injustice its name.

**What they remember.** After 233 they named the Shelf-gate, and Lirorn charged for the water anyway. Threnmaieth still wants the high road counted. The people stay off the Threnhael, and the keepers of that roll will name a pass when they can.

**Structure.** Ridge-compacts, pass-tolls, and valley charters sit under the one holding. The holding does not recruit by Kind.

**Goals.** They keep the passes and the spring, and they mean to be paid for water the core calls a gift.

**Its injustice.** The ridge pays in isolation for water the valley files as a gift. When a shelf-town's graft sickens, that town is also a long way from a stiller. The state is large enough to levy, and not large enough to furnish every Tithe on a cliff. A toll that feels like geography feels like 233 if you were on the shelf when the water-line was drawn. The Core-thaw runs louder the week before Leaf-Fall. That week belongs to Maiethlir, and Maiethlir does not explain it. It is not this holding's sermon.

### Brenledd: the Hearth-League

*(BREN-ledd. Bren, hearth, beside ledd, to reckon, so the name means hearths of the ledger. The older sounds have worn down the way names wear on this coast. People say the Hearth-League, the compact, and League-folk. Charters write Brenledd. Theology low, reach high, governance low.)*

On Strandoren, Brenledd is a league of charter-towns, river-ports, lesser harbours, and inland markets, that compacted together so they could face Lestrand's houses without becoming one house themselves. A charter is the writing a town actually lives by. Each town remains a hearth, with its own Tree in the square and its own vouching, which is simply someone of that square standing up to say a person belongs there. The compact is the arithmetic that makes the separate hearths worth joining. They share tickets, they share notes of credit, and they share a refusal to be priced one town at a time.

Faith is worn lightly here, the same as along the rest of this coast, and the Trees are mature. The league polices nothing. What it means to keep, it writes into a charter.

**Site.** The towns sit behind and between the premier coast: salt towns, river towns, and some road-end Tree-towns that federated rather than fade into the hinterland of a deathless house.

**Seat.** Raitin *(RAY-tin)* is the throne-city, a river-city and one of the largest on this shore, which is where the council sits and where the shared notes clear. The shared list is seven hearths, Raitin among them, with six charter-towns sitting alongside that still have no names. Villages and hamlets stay off the list. People still recite Nidtol when Lestrand offers to deal with one ruler instead of seven hearths. Nidtol is that recited hearth, not the throne-city, and not a city of its own. Raitin is not a second Orentel. The league means to stay a league of hearths rather than become one house.

**How it formed.** The towns got tired of being the other party on a Lestrand note. After the Grey Summer, in Cut-year 174, Nidtol's hearth piled the first shared refusal: berth-fees charged in a summer when there were no berths, and notes called in early because a house was frightened. No king founded the league, because a set of charters began acting together, and that shared acting is what the league is. The Hinge Hush added a side-paper later, so hinge-tolls will not price them one hearth at a time either.

**What they remember.** Lestrand still offers to simplify them, which would turn the many hearths into one party at the table. They are still reciting the refusal.

**Structure.** A council of hearths runs the compact, and you are of your town first. The league does not recruit by Kind. Someone vouched in one Brenled square may still be a stranger in the next, because the compact moves coin better than it moves standing.

**Goals.** They want many tickets to stay cheaper than dealing with one house. The towns underwrite each other, and they stay a league in the seasons when Lestrand would rather negotiate with a single spokesman.

**Its injustice.** The harm is falling between charters. You can be vouched in one town and unknown in the next, and Tithe-provision stays a town matter the compact will not touch. Welfare stays thin inside a rich network, and a person fades in the gaps the league calls local, because no single charter has been written to catch them.

### Leddvael: the Reckoned Gift

*(LEDD-vael. Ledd, to reckon, beside vael, gift, so the name means the gift reckoned. People say the Reckoned Gift and Gift-clerks. Charters write Leddvael. Motherfaith devotion is low. The Fair Hand is worn as civic law. Reach high, governance mid. They do not rewrite Bound Terms.)*

Leddvael is a signing-coast on Strandoren that took the Fair Hand off the dock, where it had been a theology of terms, and made it a civic rite, so Leaf-Fall is a festival and a signing-watch, with clerks there to see the term opened. The Tithe is treated as a term to be serviced, and the Bound are model citizens here, employed as the shape of honesty rather than kept at a distance. Governance sits in the middle, where the book works as a kind of statute and stops short of a census of souls. They will file a contract, and they will not copy Threnmaieth's list.

**Site.** This is a different stretch of indented coast from Lestrand, with enough water to have grown its own houses. It is a salt quay with a signing-watch, and Fair Hand clerks and Motherfaith tree-tenders work in the same wind. The held prestige berth stays on the Ledger Coast.

**Seat.** Naenor *(NAY-nor)* is the port where the signing-watch sits, slightly smaller than Orentel. It competes with the Ledger Coast, and it wins the term and loses the berth. The signing happens here, and the prestigious dock does not. Towns and villages along the rest of that coast stay unnamed. The Book-Hands stamp the signings and recite the Terms in this wind. They have no seat of their own, and they do not rewrite Terms. Naenor is not a second Orentel.

**How it formed.** Fortune-and-contract practice thickened until a charter-coast found it cheaper to establish the Settling, the Fair Hand's signed close, than to argue hymns at every dock. After the Grey Summer the witnesses had died, and a Bound's word still closed a bargain, so the civic rite is that year made into a seal. A habit acquired a seal and became the statute, and no prophet stood at that founding.

**What they remember.** A Given child is a term opened. In the Grey Summer, honesty was in short supply, and the Bound were the people who kept it.

**Structure.** A charter-council sits over the book-tables. The coast does not recruit by Kind. Gift-reading families live here, the households that still hear a colour as a gift rather than a price, and they are the minority that sounds devout and looks like a credit risk.

**Goals.** They keep the signings clear, and they vouch a Struck person briskly, reading that later change as a later contract. Motherfaith talk of gifts is allowed through the town. It is unpriced language, and unpriced language is not the language they prefer.

**Its injustice.** The harm is bargain-theology as statute. A Given child is a term opened, and a household that insists the colour was a gift is a bad debtor in a signing-town. The Bound are safe here, and they are over-read. The person the world already uses as a contract-pillar is asked to be the world's whole person. There is no villain-faith in that, only a sincere ledger laid over real Conditions.

### Trenledd: the Worn Count

*(TREN-ledd. Eroded thren, sacred counting, beside ledd, to reckon. People say the Worn Count and Count-folk. Official records write Trenledd. They call the list the roll. On the Ledger Coast that word means a tax roll, and here it means persons. Theology low, reach high, governance high.)*

Trenledd is the wealthy interior of Strandoren, with mature Trees, a dense population of the Given, and guilds thick on the ground. Over that wealth sits a state that writes people down. The list began as the machinery of tickets and taxes, safety-licences and house-notes and charter-rolls, and it forgot to stop when it reached the edge of property. No hymn calls the list stewardship, and there is no Mother to be accounted to. You are on the roll because commerce and hazard-control wanted a name they could find again.

Threnmaieth at least claims to keep you hale, whole and looked after. Trenledd claims to keep you findable. Cutting a person off from what they need is the default here, and the state does not treat that cut as impiety, because it keeps no piety the cut could offend.

**Site.** The country is Tree-rich interior, and the reach here is high. Road-end Tree-towns got filed, and the stones in the squares have numbers on the back.

**Seat.** Lunbra *(LUN-bra)* is the large, wealthy city on the Chart-run, four to eight days upriver of Orentel, where the roll is kept and the money is shown in the square, because people come for a fortune a family already has, or for one they want. Towns and villages through the filed country stay unnamed, and whoever governs Trenledd stays unnamed. Vamar keeps a column of the roll, who owes, who Turned, and who can be found. The file reads Melro, Vamar, because a clerk in this list-land says the house first, while a neighbour says Vamar. Vamar is not the ruler.

**How it formed.** The roll grew from guild-tickets, from deathless memory, and from a council that distrusted a living witness the way Threnmaieth does, without Threnmaieth's scripture to bless the distrust. The Grey Summer added the column that would not stop, and Dirrol wrote down who is still alive to be found. The three pillars work here, and the state treats them as insufficient, because feeling, word, and memory answer to themselves while a ledger answers to the desk. The growth was a pile of crises, each one adding a column, rather than a single coup.

**What they remember.** They wanted the Hinge Hush written as persons, and both shores refused. The roll they kept is a fever's child, not a hymn's. It is colder than the Tallied Crown.

**Structure.** A crown or a council writes the roll and runs the state, and the person who sits at the head of it is not named. Licence-guilds work as organs of that state. The state does not recruit by Kind. The Struck are pursued so they can be catalogued, because an unwitnessed change is a row that appeared on the page without a clerk.

**Goals.** The state wants to know who is dangerous, who owes, and who Turned. It furnishes the Tithe through channels that can be shut. When someone objects, its clerks answer with procedure, and they never answer with theology.

**Its injustice.** The harm is a census with the halo worn off. You cannot appeal to piety when you want off the list, and you cannot shame them by saying the Mother's gifts should not be listed. They agree with the sentence, and they list you anyway, as cargo and as hazard.

### Netstrand: the Night Shore

*(NET-strand. Eroded neth, night, the long dark, beside strand, shore. The reconstruction Nethstrand goes unused in daily speech, and people there will not say why the name means night. People say the Night Shore and Night-folk. Charters write Netstrand. Theology mid, reach high, governance low.)*

Strandoren's west and south face is the West Water, the open ocean toward Heskoren and, far beyond that, the storm-wall. The bold houses run the frontier trade. What they sell is hulls and west-water berths: crews, capacity, and a crossing quoted the way other shores quote a pilgrimage. They do not sell the First-Hand year, the origin-winter, as a finished product. That year is assembled on the Old Crossing, and the work is split. Netstrand charters the ship, the White Note writes the terms, and Orentel holds the berth. Almost none of the people of this shore have crossed to the Sundering Isle. The people who claim they have are the tellers of Yumboe stories. The Yumboe are the hill-folk of Kumbaan, pearly and silver-haired, and off that isle they are rare enough that most mainlanders know them as a story.

Faith on this shore is mixed. Motherfaith is worn light, the Fair Hand is ordinary on a signing-quay, and there are quay-charms nobody writes down. The Trees along this wealthy edge are still mature.

Vaethorn waits for wood the way a devout shore waits on a gift, and the Night Shore waits for hulls the way a house waits on a fare. From the quay the two waits can look alike. One of them is faith. The other is money.

**Site.** The coast faces the open ocean. It holds empty berths in Eolthael for the Heskoren run and for hulls chartered in to Orentel's first quay. The holds smell of earth because graft-pots are going out. A neighbour's week gets billed here as a crossing. The far crossing is this shore's prestige, and the origin-winter is not.

**Seat.** Braetu *(BRAY-too)* is the harbour city on this face, smaller than Orentel because the far run is thinner than the Old Crossing. A far crossing is quoted there, and a lamp can be kept on a name. They keep a dark book of quoted crossings that never returned, and a Held bed for hulls, a berth held back and not let onward. Houses pay to keep a lamp on a name. The book is not a Threnhael, and it is not a White Note. It is the only record the shore keeps of its lost ships. One berth stays unlit, with no house attached, and the city leaves that berth that way. Towns and villages along the rest of the shore stay unnamed. Selkie stay common on the quays and off the flag. Braetu is not a second Orentel.

**How it formed.** The west-houses grew out of Salt Walk traffic and the later carrying to Heskoren, by sea, the coasts first and then the far water. This is a shore that faces the wave's supply-line, not the grove. After the Grey Summer the west trade thinned for a decade. After the Hinge Hush they kept the west-water clause, under which Netstrand quotes the far crossing, Saelvaeth may take a pot without a waiting-due, Vaethorn may bless a landing and may not levy it, and Ornled is a beach-fee.

**What they remember.** They quote the far crossing, and the clause they inherited covers hulls. It does not cover the origin-winter. A lamp kept on a name that will not come back is charged as watch, not as grief.

**Structure.** Merchant houses, berth-guilds, and a light charter-council run the shore. It does not recruit by Kind. Sky-couriers come from Lirorn, and Tengu families keep the clock-towers, all of it as jobs rather than as a district of their own.

**Goals.** They quote the far crossing, keep the Heskoren run profitable, and charter hulls to the Old Crossing when inland coin wants an origin-winter. They sell tales of the Isle without having to survive the crossing those tales describe. They do not let Trenledd write the berths, and they do not claim the First-Hand year is theirs to crown.

**Its injustice.** The harm is the crossing as inventory. Prestige is priced, necessity is billed, and a neighbour's week owed to a waiting cluster becomes a line on a docket. Kindness is for the people who can book. The origin-year they do not own still uses their wood.

### Ornled: the Outer Ledger

*(ORN-led. Orn, land, beside ledd, to reckon, clipped. The reconstruction Ornledd goes unused. People say the Outer Ledger and Outer folk. Slates and learned maps write Ornled. Theology low, reach low, governance low.)*

Ornled is thin-reach Heskoren country that never took the waiting-gospel. The grafts are as young as they are in Vaethorn, and the people run the same way, heavy with the Kept and the Struck. What Ornled does not have is a congregation to fill the gap those young Trees leave. Tithe-provision is barter, favor, and a slate on the wall. An unvouched Struck is a problem met with a rope, a deal, or a neighbour, not with a blessing. Custom outruns law, because there is almost no law here and almost no hymn.

They do not hate the Mother, and they do not call on her either. Vaethorn hears the missing *th* in Ornled's name as godlessness. Ornled hears the *th* Vaethorn still pronounces as a luxury. The name is worn down, and they let the old ending go.

**Site.** The country is frontier pockets off the hottest pilgrim-roads. It looks like a waiting cluster, and it has no gospel to wait with. The same stones are here, and the same upper rooms where a walk used to pass. The work in those rooms is necessity rather than devotion.

**Seat.** Sanbreo *(san-BREE-oh)* is the town on the brink of a small city where the slate is kept, and a beach-fee can be paid there when a hull arrives. It stands on Heskoren's west coast, the slate-shore. The beach faces west, toward the last capes and the storm-wall beyond them. The frontier coast that faces the West Water is on the east side of Heskoren, so a hull that wants this beach comes round the capes. The town remains a town rather than the city it is nearly large enough to be. Villages and hamlets in the pockets around it stay unnamed. Eolvaeth is a vale in the waiting-gospel, and it is not this gate.

**How it formed.** Districts heavy with the Kept and the Struck watched the waiting-theology arrive with the grafts, and they declined to make scarcity into a problem of the soul. They make things up as they go, and that improvising has become the culture. The government is light because nobody has spare people enough to be a state. The Fair Hand is a rumour that comes off ships, not a civic rite, because they have few Bound to make a book of. The Thaw-Break gave one cluster a year they can point at. Vathne's slate is the first line on the wall, cut after a waited pot drowned in someone else's thaw. The line says the town will not wait with a hymn for a stick that drowned. Someone recopied the words onto newer wood. Vathne is that line, not a person you meet on the gate. Whoever is on the shore gate that day cuts the beach-fee, and that person is not named. The Hush writes them as slate-shore. What they owe a hull is a beach-fee, not a hymn. They were not asked.

**What they remember.** Vaethorn hears godlessness in the missing sound. Ornled hears Vaethorn's waiting-gospel as a luxury the well-supplied can afford. They keep no net of congregations.

**Structure.** Town custom, slates, and vouching stretched thin are what pass for membership. The pockets do not recruit by Kind. The Intake, the catching of a raw Struck before the change has anywhere to go, is an acute problem here and an unofficial one. A raw Struck gets a shed if they are lucky, and silence if the silence works.

**Goals.** They try to stay whole with what is already in the pot. They owe no hymn in exchange for green. They trade with Netstrand when a ship comes, and they try not to become the neighbour that ship can bill.

**Its injustice.** Where Vaethorn's cruelty is a guilt laid on the person, Ornled's cruelty is no net at all. A person who cannot barter their Tithe slides, with no prayer under them and no guild beside them. The loneliest unvouched in the world are not in the waiting-lands. They are next door, where no one is waiting with them.

### Vaelhesk: the Far Yield

*(VAEL-hesk. Vael in the older sense, what the land yields, beside hesk-, the far edge. People say the Far Yield and the Yield. Charters write Vaelhesk, and saying it that way keeps the spoken name clear of Vaethorn. The fight with the Waiting Lands is in the first syllable, vael against vaeth. Motherfaith devotion is mid. Old Ways practice is high. Reach low, governance low.)*

Vaelhesk is the pre-graft host-lands on Heskoren: old greens, doors kept for ancestors, and a first meal before anyone is allowed to leave with something of the place. The Mother's wood, the grafted Tree of the Motherfaith, is a guest on that older ground. A Cutting-leave, the Seat's permission to take living wood, is a claiming if it happens without the meal. The Door-Keepers walk here as Kin-speakers, people who speak for the kin the door is kept for. The land is the seat, not a college, and it does not need a college in order to be owed a meal.

The Struck and the Taken-In are read here as the land's own children. A neighbour's week to a Motherfaith Tree can be refused on purpose, and what the refusal says is that the child is already the hill's. Vaethorn reads those same children as Kept-for-want, passed over because a gift was scarce. The disagreement is what decides whether a child travels. Both families are telling the truth they have.

**Site.** The country is Heskoren's old greens, and not only the live front where new grafts are still arriving. Its guest-grove is the First Bowl, which folk call *Lonasir*, and the bowl is not a capital, because the land itself is the seat. There is no new city and no new name. Villages and hamlets stay on the old greens. A traveler who asks for the seat is pointed at a door. The First Bowl stays the guest-grove it already is. Harrow's Green is the grove-town on Saelvaeth's march, next door to this ground and not this seat.

**How it formed.** These were animist and ancestor polities, answering to the land and to the dead, and they predate the wave of grafts. When the grafts arrived, across Cut-year 200 to Cut-year 387 and still arriving, some districts became Vaethorn, and these districts kept the door. The state stays light because the land does not need a clerk in order to be owed a meal. The Thaw-Break moved guests off flooded ground and up to high doors. The Hinge Hush does not write Vaelhesk into its clauses, because a first meal is not a landing-due. Netstrand crews who skip the meal learn that, and they learn it without a treaty.

**What they remember.** The host-right is older than any Cut-year. A waiting-gospel that came later called the refusal a deprivation.

**Structure.** Membership is the hearth-cluster, the door-keeper, and the custom of the host-right, and the land does not recruit by Kind. The Taken-In are densest here as frontier labor and as kin, and that density is still not a flag.

**Goals.** They feed the first meal, and they name a Struck person at the door that made them. A warden who arrives with a pot is a guest if they sat down for the meal, and a thief if they took the wood without sitting.

**Its injustice.** The harm is host-rights used as a claim that can shun. Refuse the meal, take the wood, and you are not a neighbour. A Road-hand, one of the hands who carry pots of living wood for the Seat, can become a trespasser for following a sentence the Seat already wrote. The kindness has a threshold. The wrong side of that threshold is real, and the injustice stays local.

### Saelvaeth: the Sown Waiting

*(SAEL-vaeth. Sael, sowing, joined to vaeth, the waiting, so the name means sowing into the waiting. People say the Sown Waiting and March-folk. Charters write Saelvaeth. The shared sael with Saelthael stays visible in the writing, and speech keeps the two powers apart. Harrow's Green sits in the march's orbit, and it does not sit as the capital. Theology mid, reach low, governance mid on the table and mid-low in the march itself.)*

This is the live front of the grafting on Heskoren, the still-moving planting of Trees, running from Cut-year 200 to Cut-year 387 with the front still incomplete, and the wave of planting reached this edge in this decade. Towns form where a graft has taken, and between them are waiting clusters who can see a neighbour's canopy and do not yet have one of their own. Harrow's Green is the luckiest kind of square in this march, a Hand that grew up, and the Three Hamlets Past the Ford can see that green. They were written as remainder, the part left over, under the folk names *Brenod*, *Vaelun*, and *Ornath*, which the Seat still does not use. New towns everywhere still say the Harrow-sentence, *we Turned at Harrow's Tree before we had our own*, and on this front that sentence is the decade, not an old story. The queue is visible from the ground, because people can see who is waiting. Ornsael, on Saelthael, is the same sowing on the origin continent, with the city of that sowing at Larbril and the well at Ornsael.

Governance sits mid-low because someone has to stand and answer for the sentence the Seat already passed on Harrow's Green. Wardens do that standing, Road-hands carry pots of living wood through on the way to a town that was not chosen, and a march-voice speaks that did not exist when the first pots came. The state is large enough to be blamed, and no larger.

**Site.** The march is Heskoren's grafted pockets on the moving edge. It hosts the neighbour's week out of necessity rather than devotion. Natai sits west of Harrow's Green, between Sanbreo and that grove, on ground of its own, and not on the ford.

**Seat.** Natai *(NAH-tye)* is the town where the march-voice stands, and the town can be blamed for a queue it did not write. Other towns where a graft has taken stay unnamed, and so do the hamlets and waiting clusters between them. Harrow's Green stays as it is and stays out of this seat. The Three Hamlets Past the Ford stay the three hamlets. Eolvaeth is Vaethorn's seat, on a different road.

**How it formed.** Who is next for a scion, a cutting of living wood, depends on who can pay, whose soil will take it, and who is next in the chain on a list written in the Motherwood. The polity is that luck and that queue. The Grey Summer thinned the supply-line for a decade, and the Thaw-Break turned the neighbour's week into worse mud. The Hinge Hush wrote one mercy into the peace. This march may take a pot without paying a Vaethorn waiting-due. Vaethorn will preach that the march is unworthy of what it received. The history of the planting does not agree.

**What they remember.** The queue is the border. Harrow's luck is politics, not a reward for having waited more faithfully than the hamlets. The mercy in the clause does not move them up the scion-queue.

**Structure.** Town-wardens keep the squares, and Turnings are borrowed, a child's standing done where a Tree exists because the waiting cluster does not have one yet. The march does not recruit by Kind. The Old Ways are neighbours, which is Vaelhesk, and they are not a museum of a dead practice. Vaethorn's heat sits on many hearths as gospel, and it does not always sit there as a flag. The march they stand in is Saelvaeth.

**Goals.** They keep the grafts that have taken alive, they host the neighbour's week, they argue for the next pot, and they survive being the sentence the Seat has already written.

**Its injustice.** The harm is the queue as a border. The hamlets can see the canopy and send their children anyway, and spreading Trees did not make the march kind.
