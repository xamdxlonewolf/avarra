*From* The Keeping of the Grove, *a history of the Closed Seat. The copy kept in the Book's house at Rothallo. The writer's name is not on the copy.*

---

**Of the Seat before the theft**

Before anyone counted years, our houses sat at the grove and kept the gate. Somebody had to. There was one Tree and one week, and the whole world wanted to stand under it at the same time. Without a gate, the strong would have pushed to the front and the small would have been trodden at the roots. Grove-leave was the paper that turned a crowd into a line.

Children who came without leave were sent home whole. Nobody was hurt by it. A child who was sent home was a child kept as they were, and many of them were glad of it in later years.

The Tree was standing before our houses came to the grove. We do not put a year on it, and we do not trust anyone who does.

The Seat never ruled the coasts across the water, and it never claimed the far lands. It kept one gate, and it kept it well.

**Of the theft**

In the first year of the copy, someone cut a piece of living wood from the Tree, carried it to another town, and planted it there. We do not write the name of the hand that cut it. Nobody ever brought that person to us, and a house that writes down a name it does not know is telling a story, not writing a history.

For nineteen years the stolen wood stood in that town and did nothing a Tree does. Then, in the nineteenth year, it held a Turning-Week of its own, and a child of that town was Turned under it.

**Of the gathering**

The Seat did what any steward does with stolen goods. It sent riders to bring the wood home. Where a cutting could not be carried, the riders burned it in its pot, so that it could not be stolen a second time. The riders were not soldiers. They carried sacks and spades.

The towns hid the cuttings in wells and watered them at night. The lodging-hearths would not shut their doors to travellers. Even the college at the Tree, which had called the cutting a theft, would not help burn a cutting that had already Turned a child. For nineteen years the Seat tried to keep the one gate whole, with fewer friends each summer.

**Of Hildal**

In the thirty-eighth year, Hildal, a clerk of the Seat, wrote the order that ended the gathering. Hildal was not a lord and never pretended to be one. Hildal saw that the grove could only be held by riding into every town within a day's walk, and that the Seat would have to burn more than wood to do it. So Hildal chose the Book over the grove.

Our houses left the grove to the college and left the travellers to the lodging-hearths. They went a day's walk away, into the walled city we now call Rothallo, in the lodging-country that became Orenbren. They took the Book with them.

**Of what was kept**

Some say the Seat lost everything in the Retreat. It did not. It lost a gate. It kept its houses, its manners, and its Book, and it kept them without fighting in any street. The families who once kept the gate for the whole world now keep a wall for themselves, and they keep it as carefully as they kept the gate.
