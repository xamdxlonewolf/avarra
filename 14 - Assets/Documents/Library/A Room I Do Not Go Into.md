**A Room I Do Not Go Into**

I have a room I do not go into.
The door is not locked. I know where the key is kept.
In there are five summers and the people in them:
the bread we burned, the river in the flood year,
a man who laughed with his whole back.

I am told the room is looked after.
I am told someone sweeps it.
I am told that if I asked, they would show me
a drawing of the room, thinner than the room.

I walk past it on the way to my lessons.
I learn a new script, a new road,
the name of a neighbour's new child.
The learning keeps me alive. Everyone says so.

When a neighbour asks me what is in there,
I tell the truth. I do not remember.
When they ask me whether I miss it,
I tell them I would have to remember it to miss it.
Then they are sorry they asked,
and I am sorry that they are sorry,
and the door stays where it is.
