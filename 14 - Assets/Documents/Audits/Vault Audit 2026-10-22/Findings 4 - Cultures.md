# Findings: 03 - Cultures

Notes read: 16 (all read in full: the index, What the Year Feels Like, How a Place Speaks, The Old Tongue, Naming in the Turning, Naming People in the Turning, Kinds of the Turning, Kitsune, Selkie, Tengu, Yumboe, Daily Life, Economy and the Tithe, Law and Citizenship, Polity Archetypes, How the Week Is Kept)

## Patterns
- **Production scaffolding in player bodies (10 notes).** 🔒/🟡 tags, Story and Epic numbers, "Canon status" blocks, name-generator seeds and commands, "Built with `economic-systems`", and "Good working canon, open to a later polish pass" all sit above the GM wall. Examples: `Languages/The Old Tongue.md` (seven "Story x.x — 🟡 Proposed" headers), `Naming/Naming People in the Turning.md` (Canon status block, seeds, generator path), `Peoples/Kinds of the Turning.md` (Canon status block, "What this does to later work").
- **The four Epic-2 notes read as design essays, not as a setting (4 notes).** They describe "grammars", "touchstones", and "knobs". They give revision history ("the old 28–33% scare", "the same note used to say two things at once") and say things "come later in Epic 5/7" when those epics are done. Examples: `Social Structures/Law and Citizenship.md`, `Social Structures/Economy and the Tithe.md`, `Social Structures/Polity Archetypes.md`, `Social Structures/Daily Life.md`.
- **"Only one crown keeps a list of persons" (3 notes).** This claim contradicts Trenledd, which keeps a roll of persons, and the house-name lock, which names four list-lands. Examples: `Law and Citizenship.md`, `Polity Archetypes.md`, `Naming in the Turning.md`. `How a Place Speaks.md` and `Naming People in the Turning.md` already have it right.
- **Population figures outside The Premise (2 notes with problems, 3 that are fine).** `Economy and the Tithe.md` keeps a full second roster: all nine shares, plus bucket totals it works out itself (35 / 19 / 20). That table breaks the lock. `Law and Citizenship.md` quotes three shares from The Premise, which is allowed, but then works out its own totals (~29% of people, ~39% of the Conditioned, "two in three", "half of it"). Economy also makes up two figures of its own ("a seventh", "a twentieth to a twelfth"). These are correct today but will drift if The Premise changes. "3 of 4" and "~75%" in `Kinds of the Turning.md`, `Daily Life.md`, and the index are plain quotes and are fine.
- **Custom ancestries: the mechanics hold, the framing leaks design talk (4 notes).** All four keep three features. Their Top/Bottom features match Kind Heritage, "cannot be caught off guard" appears only on Tengu, and the Yumboe needs GM leave and never mixes. Each overview still opens with a designer's "**The fresh angle:**" paragraph. Two of the notes give the second name as a byname only, which the house-name amendment made out of date. There are also two factual slips: Yumboe calls the Kind "a nation", and Selkie gets the drowning door wrong.
- **People called mouths and throats, and insider shorthand (7 notes).** Examples: "a sold mouth" and "a second sitting" (`Law and Citizenship.md`), "The White Note is a mouth" and "other throats" (`Polity Archetypes.md`), "the thorn" and "while the room is mixed" (`How a Place Speaks.md`), "the mainland lintel" (`How the Week Is Kept.md`).

## Strong — protect
- The rule blocks on `Kitsune.md`, `Selkie.md`, `Tengu.md`, and `Yumboe.md`. Each Kind has three features, the Top/Bottom order matches `13 - Game/Kind Heritage.md`, the "cannot be caught off guard" phrase is fenced to Tengu, and Yumboe needs GM leave and is always the full Kind. Do not trim or move these.
- The stock-ancestry section of `Kinds of the Turning.md` (nine groups). It uses plain sentences, says who lives where, gives one kitchen habit per group, and says what each group does not own. It creates no Kind-nations, and the Fungril spoonful is correctly left unexplained.
- `What the Year Feels Like.md` and `How the Week Is Kept.md` handle the four unexplained habits exactly as the rule asks: they say what people do, then that they will not explain it. Neither note adds a power or a rite.
- The "What they ask you" table in `How a Place Speaks.md` and the house-name table in `Naming People in the Turning.md`. Both are clear and playable, and both match the locked second-name rule.
