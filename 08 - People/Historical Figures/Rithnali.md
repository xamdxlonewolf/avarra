# Rithnali

> Rithnali was Speaker of the First Seat, and the minute from the summer the nameless green held its first Turning-Week is theirs. They are dead. They are not the cutter.

*(rith-NAH-lee.)* The minute says Rithnali. A stranger who asks *of where?* gets the First Seat, and is not given a house, and the memory of that minute is **still argued**.

## Summary

The minute is on [[The First Cut]]. Later clerks file it as Cut-year 19, the summer the nameless green, the canopy that has never been given a town's name, held a Turning-Week, the week a child can be Turned. Rithnali wrote that the wood had been cut without leave, and that the Seat would not write who. They would write how. The how on that page is to carry the wood in living earth, and not to cut a second time without the Seat. A cut, from that summer, was a leave, or it was a theft the Seat had decided not to chase.

They wanted the next pot, a young tree in the container it was carried in, not to die of a dry mile. The verso of the minute, the back of the leaf, is a list of pots that did die, which no one read aloud. Folk who keep the nameless knife think the recto, the front of that same page, is a confession. The Seat thinks it is competence. Both can hold the same copy. The name is how you ask for that page, and it is not a grave you can point at.

Speakers who will not say *first* will still not add Rithnali's hand to the story of the knife. The minute says *Tree*, and later Cutting-leave, the paper that says who may cut next, says *Mother*. The five ways of telling the Cut stay five jobs, and this Speaker is the minute those jobs argue over. They are not the hand any of the five is protecting.

## Appearance & Demeanor

Rithnali is remembered as a person who would not point, and no tomb-portrait has been allowed to settle. Where the Seat says the name, it is an attribution, said the way you say a filing word. In a Near-Mile kitchen it is said more slowly, as if the page might answer.

## Role & Relationships

- **[[The First Cut]]** is the event. Rithnali is the minute, filed as Cut-year 19, and not an attribution of the knife.
- **[[The Tree-Wardens|The First Seat]]** is the college that kept the how. The living Seat is not this Speaker.
- **[[The Closing]]** is the war over the copy. The riders never learned a face, and this minute is why a college could say it had not chased the theft.
- **[[The Walking Years]]** are the road the copy began to shorten. Rithnali did not preach the stones.
- **[[Monseoth]]** is a sermon at a mile-shrine, and it is not this page.
- **[[Sedrad]]** is a blame on the salt, and it is not this page.

The five attributions stay jobs: a stolen branch, a warden ahead of a college, a branch that came away, a pear-graft and an overlay, a guest the land received. None of them is Rithnali's name on a handle.

## GM Notes

- **Memory: still argued.** This is a past layer only. Rithnali is dead, not a present antagonist. They are the minute, not the cutter. Do not collapse the five attributions into this Speaker, or into anyone.
- **Positional.** They were the threshold guardian of a category. Their job was to say whether a taking was blight, theft, or a thing the college could write down. The technical page, how and not who, became the copy-right. They were the person at the desk when the first local Turning-Week made the argument undeniable. They could not uncut the wood, and they would not name a hand.
- **Arc, if you need one.** They wanted the next scion to live. The lie was that "we will not write who" is restraint, and that "the soil is thin" is only horticulture. What they needed was to see that the refusal to write who is the capture, and that the verso of dead pots is the argument the recto pretends to have closed. They did not take it. The queue did.
- **Says *Tree*, not *Mother*.** The early paper has not finished the overlay. Do not correct the minute in play.
- **Do not** build a tomb that puts a name on the knife. Do not date the Tree from Cut-year 19. Do not give Rithnali a house-name. The Seat wrote how, not a line. Do not make them the warden who cut.
