# Monseoth

> The sermon at the mile-shrine. Of the Near Mile. Dead. Praised because the road still makes a child say the name before the climb. Not the cutter.

*(mon-SAY-ohth.)* A neighbour said Monseoth. A stranger who asks *of where?* gets Monseoth of the Near Mile. There is no house to give a clerk. **Memory: praised.**

## Summary

They spoke in the [[The Walking Years]], while the door was still the walk. The road does not give them a Cut-year. A mile-shrine was a grave and a waymark in the same stone. Someone had to say which side the water was on, and whose name was already there, or the next column would use the stone only as a distance.

Monseoth said the name first, then the water, then the week. The Near Mile still does it in that order, and still says Monseoth when a child asks why they may not climb on the way past. That use is the praise. There is no feast and no chapel with their figure in it. The road is the memorial.

Some mouths say the sermon made a hymn out of a ford, and that children now climb what ought to have stayed a grave. [[The Watching]] will not take the saying as a rite. Later devout squares sometimes put the Mother's Hand onto a sentence that was a road. That fold is theirs. It was not this mouth. The words the Near Mile still repeats are the road's:

> *Say the name before you climb. The water is on the shade side. The week will not wait while you finish. If you are a child, leave the ribbon. If you are a Speaker, this stone is not a colour.*

They did not name a hand. The thief-songs on the same road still say *we*. [[The First Cut]]'s five tellings stay five. This sermon is about a stone that was already a person. It is not about who married the first slip to new ground.

## Appearance & Demeanor

Remembered outdoors, at a stone, not in a chair. A voice that stops a column. No vestment has stuck. Adults who loved them sound like someone making a child wait. Adults who did not sound like someone who would not let the dead be only a mile.

## Role & Relationships

- **[[The Walking Years]]** — the road they spoke on. The three walks stay logistics. This is the Near Mile's saying, not a fourth road.
- **[[The First Cut]]** — they are not in the minute, and they are not the knife. The sermon does not choose among the five.
- **[[Rithnali]]** — the Seat's page, later, about how. A different mouth. The minute does not quote this stone.
- **[[The Third Hearth]]** — does not file this name. [[Thilim]]'s slate is a different sentence, and a living house.
- **[[The Child Who Counted Stones]]** — the road's fable. Children hear a rhyme. Monseoth is the adult who still makes them say a name first.

A list-land that wants a house will not find one. The Near Mile witnessed them. It did not enroll them.
