# Taerso

> Taerso spoke for the Hinge Shore's houses, and could stop a berth, in [[The Hinge Hush]] of Cut-year 304. They are dead. They are one shore's remembered speaker. The other shore stayed a rate.

*(TARE-so.)* A neighbour said Taerso, and a stranger who asks *of where?* gets Taerso of the Hinge Shore. The memory of that hush is **still argued**.

## Summary

From Cut-year 299 to 304 the [[The Hinge Shore]] and the Ledger Coast tied each other's hulls over who set the hinge-toll, the price of the old crossing. Taerso spoke for the Shore's houses. They did not sit a throne, but they could stop a berth, which was enough to make a war of logistics.

In Cut-year 304 they hushed. The Shore kept the blessing, the Coast kept the paper, and the crossing kept moving. The hush says the Shore will say the blessing if someone wants one. Taerso had wanted the blessing and the tariff to stay the same fact, one crossing in which the blessing was said aloud, the tariff sat in the book, and pilgrims and pots moved in the same wind. The rate split them. People still argue whether that sentence saved a Motherland crossing or taught a pious shore how to step around a fade, a [[Long-Lived]] who, starved of anything new to learn, begins to forget and empty out. Stepping around, in that argument, means offering the blessing only when someone asks.

A younger clerk writes **Hush** on a berth-slate and will not add a hero. Older hands on the quay, in the fifth-summer pause in Hale-month, still say Taerso before the bells return to cargo. [[Orentel]]'s first quay holds, and will not call the name a founder of the city.

Sirtol's name sits on the Coast copy, over a rate-table, one name among a charter-pile. Orentel will not found a city on that name either. The other shore stayed a paper, and the bells do not know it as a person.

## Appearance & Demeanor

People remember a berth that did not cast off, and no admiral's coat has stuck to the telling. The Shore, when it is being devout, gives the name two sentences and puts the blessing back in the second. The quay, when it is working, says the name once and rings the bell.

## Role & Relationships

- **[[The Hinge Hush]]** is the peace the crossing still charges. Taerso is the Shore's speaker on it, and not the winner the hush refused to write.
- **[[The Hinge Shore]]** is the houses they could speak for. The living shore is not this voice, returned.
- **[[The Grey Summer]]** is the quiet sea both shores had already learned to fear. Taerso did not cause it.
- **[[Orentel]]** will not put the name on the city. [[The White Note House]] did not sign the hush.
- **Sirtol** is the other shore: a desk on a rate-table, not a second note, and not an admiral.

There is no house-name, because both shores refused to let the hush be written as persons.

## GM Notes

- **Memory: still argued.** This is a past layer only. Taerso is dead, not a present antagonist. They were a desk that could stop a berth, not a first admiral. Do not seat them.
- **Positional.** They were the bridge. They stood where blessing and tariff still had to be said by one speaker, and neither shore fully released the berth to the other. Their say-so was the lock. The hush kept the crossing and split the fact they had wanted whole.
- **Arc, if you need one.** They wanted one Motherland crossing. The lie was that saying the blessing, even optionally, keeps the tariff inside the hymn. What they needed was to see that "if someone wants one" is how a shore learns to step around a fade. They did not take it. The rate still collects.
- **Sirtol stays the other shore.** Do not write them a life, a flag, or a matching hero's note. There is one remembered person. The Coast's paper is already on [[The Hinge Hush]].
- **Do not** restart the war by bringing Taerso back. Do not crown Orentel. Do not book Kumbaan onto the clause. The west-water clause can still be a live wire without Taerso in the room.
