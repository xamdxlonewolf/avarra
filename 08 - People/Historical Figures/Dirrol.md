# Dirrol

> Dirrol kept the desk that added a column for who was still alive, in [[The Grey Summer]] of Cut-year 171. They are dead, and they were not a founder.

*(DEER-rol.)* The name on the back of the stones is Dirrol, and the memory of that desk is **still argued**.

## Summary

[[Trenledd]] already had columns for hazard and for debt, which is what a coast knew how to write down before it knew how to write a person. In the Salt-Grey, the name the docks use for [[The Grey Summer]], Dirrol added a column for the person who could still be found. There was no hymn in it. A desk had lost too many people and did not trust the square to restock itself.

The Worn Count is that summer's child, the living list that grew from the column and is not this clerk returned to the desk. [[Polity Archetypes|Threnmaieth]] at least claims to keep you hale, and Trenledd learned, in a plague-year, that it wanted you findable. Dirrol is the hand on that first person-column. The stiller on the quay had asked for water on an hour-slate that had no box for a street, while inland this desk invented the box.

[[Trenledd]] will try to make the name a first clerk-king. The stones do not, and what they keep is a row. People who will not be listed call the column the year a fever taught a coast to hunt names. People who live by the row call it the reason a grandchild can still be found. Both can point at the same worn number.

Nidtol is the hearth-name [[Brenledd]] recites when someone offers to simplify the compact. That recital is three summers later, and what it recites is a pile. It is not this column, and it is not a second face beside this desk.

## Appearance & Demeanor

Dirrol is remembered as a column, not a body, and no portrait has held. When the Worn Count speaks the name, it sounds like a heading, and when a debtor speaks it, it sounds like being found.

## Role & Relationships

- **[[The Grey Summer]]** is the fever of that summer, and it was not a colour. Dirrol did not send it and did not cure it.
- **[[Trenledd]]** is the count that grew from the column. The living list is not this clerk, returned.
- **[[Brenledd]]** is the compact, and the name that compact recites is Nidtol's. That recital is a later overlay, not Dirrol's house.
- **[[The Stillers]]** kept the hour-slate that had no box for a street. This desk built a different box, and it was too late for that quay.
- **[[The White Note House]]** is the other fortune of the same summer, in which the notes outlived the people who had signed them. Ledan is not Dirrol's heir.

## GM Notes

- **Memory: still argued.** This is a past layer only. Dirrol is dead, not a present antagonist, and a desk rather than a founder. Do not seat them as a clerk-king.
- **Positional.** They were an accidental historian. Routine columns became the only way a coast could find the living, and their competence at the desk made them essential to a count that did not exist when they sat down. They could not unwrite the row.
- **Arc, if you need one.** They wanted to find the living. The lie was that a name in a column is as good as a witness standing in a square. What they needed was the water the stiller asked for, which a row cannot pour. They did not take it. The count took the column.
- **Nidtol stays the later overlay** already on [[The Grey Summer]]. Do not give Nidtol a note, a biography, or a founding-day.
- **Do not** give Dirrol a house-name. A house would be the founder the stones refused. The name the year uses is Dirrol. The fever is not a Condition and not a punishment.
