# Limrae

> Limrae is the clerk a crown-count still starts from, and the one remembered person of [[The Two Papers]]. They are dead. The other paper stayed a refusal.

*(lim-RAY.)* The name on the roll-preface is Limrae, and the memory of that preface is **still argued**.

## Summary

In Cut-year 67 a reckoning-desk opened in the river-town that would be counted as [[Maiethlir]]'s country. Limrae wrote the colour, the mark of what a person had been given, and the person, in one breath. They said they were keeping. The Closed Seat had ranked, and this desk wanted the opposite kindness, if you believe the preface. The want under that kindness was a gift found again, because a gift you cannot find is a gift you have already failed to tend.

[[Polity Archetypes|Threnmaieth]] still dates a crown-count from that summer, which is why a devout clerk in that country writes Crown-year beside Cut-year. Present Crown-year 320 is sixty-seven behind the Cut-year, and that gap is the split. A devout clerk may write both numbers. [[Maiethvael]] will convert neither. They will tell you the year is a river-habit, and that Limrae was a clerk.

A later crown that wants a founding-ancestor tries to make this name a first king. The river-town knows the hand was a desk. Both tellings can hold the same hymn. The injustice of a list that can find you, and the care of a list that will not lose you, are the argument. Limrae does not settle it by being dead.

Manril would not be named to the roll. The guild-minute from that summer still says so, and still will not say who they went home to. The country that refused the list kept the refusal, and it did not keep a second life beside this one.

## Appearance & Demeanor

People remember tablets and a preface, not a tomb. Anyone who wants a dynasty is shown a clerk's hand and a river. The voice, where anyone imitates it, finishes its sentence and puts the tending back in.

## Role & Relationships

- **[[The Two Papers]]** is the split. Limrae is the paper that writes, and the year of that paper is Cut-year 67.
- **[[Maiethlir]]** is the river-town's country, and that country will not crown the preface.
- **[[The Reckoned Offices]]** are the living desks. They inherited a count, and they did not inherit this clerk in the room.
- **[[The Walled Book]]** filed both papers as other people's ink. It was not Limrae's employer.
- **Manril** is the argument. The name is on no roll, and it is repeated as the one who would not be named. There is not a second note.

## GM Notes

- **Memory: still argued.** This is a past layer only. Limrae is dead, not a present antagonist, and not a first king. They were a clerk whose column became a year.
- **Positional.** They were an accidental historian. The job was to find a gift again, and the system made that finding into a crown-count. They were out of their depth the moment a country dated itself from the preface.
- **Arc, if you need one.** They wanted to tend by finding. The lie was that a person written down is a person kept, and that finding is not a cage. What they needed was the square that had already seen the colour, which is the proof they would not leave unlisted. They did not take it. Do not redeem the list by liking the clerk.
- **Manril stays the argument.** Do not write them a childhood, a house, or a hero's note. There is one remembered person. The refusal is already on [[The Two Papers]].
- **Do not** give Limrae a house-name. A line would be the dynasty the river-town refused. The name the year uses is Limrae. Do not capture the First Seat. Do not make Maiethvael the kind foil.
