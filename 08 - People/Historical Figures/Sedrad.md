# Sedrad

> Sedrad is the name the salt-songs use when a hull sits past the last fair tide. They are of the Salt Walk, and they are dead. The songs condemn them for one hold. They are not the cutter.

*(sed-RAD.)* A neighbour said Sedrad, and a stranger who asks *of where?* gets Sedrad of the Salt Walk. The songs will not say the place, and what they say is the hold. There is no house to put first, and the memory of that hold is **condemned**.

## Summary

They worked a berth on the [[The Walking Years|Salt Walk]], the road that went by ship and then inland, in a summer the quay does not file as a Cut-year. The last fair tide of a season was the one that still made [[The Reckoning of the Year|Turning-Week]], the week a child can still stand under the canopy and be Turned. The note on the hull was not a clear, and Sedrad held the hull.

The people on that hull missed the week, and a late ship does not get that week reopened. The songs kept the hold and dropped the reason. On the salt, Sedrad means a clerk who would not sign, and a tenth summer that missed the canopy because the paper was unfinished. That is the blame. It has not softened into a joke the way a quay-joke does. People say the name the way they say a berth that failed.

The quay's own telling is shorter, and it does not acquit them where the songs can hear. Hale-month weather has missed more weeks than one clerk. A squall, a late crossing, a note that was never going to be good: the salt already knew those. The songs do not want the weather. They want a person to blame, and Sedrad is the person they kept.

What they said, as the quay still copies it when it is training someone not to be them:

> *Hold. Note's not clear. Last tide that makes the week. I won't sign a child onto a hull I can't clear.*

The songs use the first word, and they do not use the last.

It was one hull and one tide. They are not why the far lands waited, and they are not the hand that cut living wood. [[Orentel]] does not put them on a charter-wall, [[The White Note House]] does not keep this hull in the founding-book, and a living factor on the first quay is not their heir.

## Appearance & Demeanor

People remember a delay: quay-ink, a hull still tied, and no coat of office. Dockhands say the name in one breath with the berth, and they do not put a hymn after it. If someone starts a speech, the quay lets it go and says the name again, shorter.

## Role & Relationships

- **[[The Walking Years]]** are the Salt Walk's sort of road: cash, weather, and a week that shuts. Sedrad is one missed last crossing, not the whole road.
- **[[The First Cut]]** is not their story. This note does not add to the five tellings of the Cut.
- **[[Named Ground|the Old Crossing]]** is the water the hull sat on. It is not a city they founded.
- **[[Rithnali]]** is a minute about wood, inland and later, and that minute does not mention this berth.
- **[[Monseoth]]** is a sermon on the Near Mile, after a ship has already made the salt. That is a different mercy, and a different mark.
- **[[Sorim]]** is a living factor on the first quay, in a later century. The title is not this clerk's, inherited.

[[Trenledd]]'s person-column is a fever's child, long after this tide. It did not file Sedrad, and it does not get to invent a house for them now.
