# Marsu of the Low Wharfs

> The reluctant one — the delta commoner in whom the whole tragedy sits at kitchen-table scale. A lock-keeper who lost an ancestral water-share to the reconciliation and a brother to the siege, who shelters [[The Sworn|the Sworn]] out of loyalty and grief, and who is quietly terrified the movement will destroy the one thing the Empire actually gave her: her son's posting in imperial service.

## The person

Weathered, careful, tired; keeps a canal lock the Empire made her family employees of on water they used to *own*. She is not political. She feeds the Sworn who pass through and hides what they ask her to hide because they are her people and her dead demand it — and because the water-share was real and the theft was real. But she has done the sums the poets won't: under the old bench her family drowned in lean years while the water-judge houses adjudicated; under the Empire, nobody drowns, and her clever son can *rise*. She wants her water back and she wants her son safe and she cannot have both, and she knows it.

## The arc (positive, torn)

- **Lie:** *if I keep my head down and feed them quietly, I don't have to choose.* The belief that she can shelter the movement and protect her son and stay innocent of both outcomes.
- **Want:** for it all to stop — the grief, the recruiting, the sums — without her having to decide.
- **Need:** to *choose*, openly, and own the cost of the choice — to stop letting grief and fear make the decision by default.
- **Catalyst:** the day sheltering the Sworn and protecting her son become the same night — a cell she hid is hunted, and her son (or the [[The Assize|Assize]]) is the one who comes asking. Her transformation is small and total: she says, at last, what she actually wants, to someone's face, knowing it costs her.

## Table use

- **The human ground truth.** Where Nemara argues and Davan burns, Marsu just *lives in the gap* — the single scene that makes the whole moral parallax land at kitchen-table scale. The PCs' verdict, or their questioning, lands on her hearth.
- The commoner who proves the Restorationists aren't simply "her side": she remembers the bench let her family drown. Use her when the table needs the oligarchy twist to have a face.

## GM Notes

`visibility: gm` — player-safe above.

- Knows **nothing** of [[The Cost]]; her grievance is water, a dead brother, and a son she's afraid for — the mundane-political core, exactly as locked ([[Kessad]]).
- She is the design-contract's *"could a decent person refuse the rebellion?"* made flesh: she has every reason to join and she keeps not-quite-joining, because she can count, and the counting says the Empire fed her children. Keep both truths alive in her.
