# Njunda

> Njunda opens the hill-hall at [[Ndenjoo]], the hall the valley already comes to, and the work sits so close to supper that it is almost not clergy. They have one job, which is to keep the table.

*(NJOON-dah.)* They are [[Yumboe]], one of the small moon-waked hill-folk of Kumbaan, a birth rather than a Condition a leaf or a later threshold can give, and the name is Njunda. A neighbour says Njunda. If a witness-town on the mainland will not let the place go, they may hear of the Feeding Hill, which is the place and not a house. A list gets Njunda, of the Feeding Hill, with nothing to put first. On the hill the name is Njunda. They have not crossed the sea off the isle.

## Summary

Njunda inherited the hall because it was their grandmother's and because it still fits the crowd. The standing is reputation, not an office anyone appointed. They lay the extra place slightly back from the board. On the second night they say *no one is a stranger twice*, and they mean it.

Nolas, of a Netstrand hull, sat twice, and is kin inside the room. The valley is tired of it, and Njunda will not omit the place to make Nolas leave, because omitting the place is the one impiety. They also cannot host a ship that is not there.

## Appearance & Demeanor

They are small, pearl-pale, with the silver hair tied for work and not for a rite, and wide dark eyes. They sleep when the moon is down, if the hall will let them. They cook and they pour, and when a mainlander sets a plate at the foot of the board "for a servant," they move the plate, and they do not sermon.

They find noon-faith, the mainland's daylight worship, *loud*. They will still feed a warden.

## The job

**Leverage:** who is seated, who is still a first-night guest, and whether the unseen hands, the hands and feet with no body between them that fetch and pour for a Yumboe table, have a place to serve. A living wreck that Soonke, the shore-sitter on this stretch, walks uphill arrives at *this* board. That is the path up from the sand, and it is not destiny.

They do not Speak a colour, and they do not vouch a Struck person in the mainland sense, which would mean standing for what that person has become so a town will keep them. They do not send a mission, and they do not swim the wall.

## Role & Relationships

- **Soonke** sits the shore on this stretch. The living are walked uphill, and Soonke will not seat anyone on the sand. Soonke wants this wreck off the hill before the next loud night. It is the same faith and a different job.
- **Saalo** is of the valley, and wants a crossing out to find Waandi. Saalo will ask Njunda to treat a hull-rumour as a supper. Njunda will feed Saalo and will not promise a sea.
- **Nolas** sat twice, is of the table, and will not go back to a ship. That is the want that will not fit in the room.
- **Mbela** keeps an attic on a Lestrand salt-stair. It is the same house on other water, and they have not met.

## GM Notes

- **Pattern:** Inherited Network and a Bridge Position at the same time. The hall assumes the grandmother's continuity. The wrecks assume the table can hold them. The dual belonging is isle-kin set against a guest who has become kin and will not leave.
- **Arc (forkable, default positive).** *Lie:* if I lay the place correctly, the hall can hold anyone. *Ghost:* a grandmother who never omitted the place, and a bright night when the hall almost did not fit. *Need:* kin-making has a size, and refusing a third month is not the one impiety. *Catalyst:* Nolas awake at noon, or the hands stopping, or Saalo walking down to a sail. *Negative fork:* they omit the place to make room for one more person, and the valley learns a new impiety.
- **Lock-in:** the position itself. Wrecks walk here, the valley comes here, and there is no other hall that still fits the crowd.
- **Non-Tree want is load-bearing.** If a session only asks them what the Trees are, you are running the appendix. Play the dishes.
- **Do not** make them a pope of the moon. Do not give them a Tree to refuse as their personality. Do not send them to plant a hall in Vaethorn.
