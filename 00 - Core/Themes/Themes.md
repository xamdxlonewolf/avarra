# Themes

What Avarra is *about*. These are the ideas every note, NPC, and session should ultimately serve.

## The thematic spine

> Every civilization thinks it is living in the real world. There is no "real" world — only people afraid of losing their home. **Can two civilizations share a reality that was never built to hold both of them?**

## The campaign's arc of realization

> "We are exploring the ruins of the past." → "The past is happening somewhere else." → "The past is us." → "We have been trying to become whole by destroying ourselves." → "What if we choose to recognize ourselves instead?"

And the movement of the title's meaning:

> "We are the world." → "We are Avarra — **both**."

## Supporting motifs

- **Curiosity as the doomsday mechanism.** Recognition accelerates reunification; the party's own heroic investigation is the danger. Wonder and dread share one engine.
- **The past is not sacred.** The guild wants to understand what came before; the Wardens want to preserve what has worked before; both halves treat historical continuity as holy. The players' discovery refutes this — they are the first people to *make* history instead of excavating it.
- **The monster is a mirror.** "People can be monsters too" — not from evil, but because the structure of reality forces two decent civilizations to erase each other and call it fate.
- **Wholeness without erasure.** *You don't have to become me, and I don't have to become you.*

## Tone

Default **wonder** (early-Elder-Scrolls / Zelda) → **uncanny mystery** when it matters → **grimdark spikes** at the pressure points. Wonder first, so there is always something to lose.

## Authoring rule

Teach through *"something is weird,"* never an encyclopedia dump. Questions first; answers much later. Every reveal should feel inevitable in retrospect, not arbitrary.
