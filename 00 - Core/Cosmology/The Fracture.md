# The Fracture

> **GM ONLY — core secret.** This is the true cosmology of Avarra. It is never told to players as exposition; it is revealed only through the campaign's iceberg structure. See [[11 – Secrets]] for the reveal ordering.

## The truth

There was one reality: **Avarra**. It **fractured** — not a fall from paradise, but because it could not reconcile **two incompatible ways of existing**. Neither resulting half is "the original":

- **The Green Reach** (`world: green-reach`) — organic, growing, mutable, imperfect, warm, materially abundant. The familiar, player-facing side.
- **The Kiln** (`world: kiln`) — structured, geometric, crystalline, ordered, stable, thermally transformed. A world of glass, ash, and standing light; street-for-street the *same* places, vitrified. Beautiful and alien, not hellish.

They are **two solutions to the same existential problem**. *Same souls, alien clothes.*

## The soul-pool

Souls belong to the underlying reality, not to either world, so **the same souls recur on both sides** — not identical copies but **diverged possibilities**: lovers on one side, enemies on the other; a child here, an elder there; sometimes no counterpart at all.

## The alignments

The alignments are **reality trying to remember it was one** — attempting to reunify. Because the halves grew apart for millennia, reunification is catastrophic: matter cannot decide which version to obey; geographies, bodies, and histories try to occupy the same state; reality chooses neither and **breaks**. Each failed reunification's wreckage becomes an archaeological **stratum**.

## The strata

The "ancient civilizations" the Green Reach reveres and excavates are **not predecessors — they are previous collisions**. The impossible discovery: two civilizations in the *exact same chronological layer*, then another, then another. The layers are not `civ → civ → civ` but `world → world → world`. The players aren't digging up the past; they're digging up previous attempts to become whole.

## The Wardens

Not secret villains; **no redemption arc**. The most morally uncomfortable people the party meets. They discovered that **recognition accelerates reunification** — if enough people recognize the other half is *alive*, reality starts trying to reconcile. So their doctrine is *forget*, and their great lie is a single verb tense: **"there *was* another civilization,"** never **"there *is*."** They have made horrible choices for generations because they have never had another option, and every prior cycle proved them right. Fingerprint: investigative magic (divination, speaking to the dead, scrying the deep past) is culturally taboo — it is the fast path to recognition.

## Why this cycle is different

For the first time in recorded history, **both halves become aware of each other simultaneously**. Every prior cycle ran *discover → collide → destroy → forget → diverge.* This one can run *discover → recognize → **communicate**.* Mutual awareness is the anomaly — it is why the cycle is accelerating and the Warden calculations are failing. **Never give players a clean cycle number**: "73 years" → "61" → "19" → "there isn't a cycle anymore."

## The name "Avarra"

Avarra is the true name of the unified reality — known to almost no one. Neither half thinks "we live in half of Avarra"; each has its own internal name and is certain *"we are the world."* The word survived because the fracture was imperfect: both halves carry **unrecognized linguistic descendants** of it (working forms: Green Reach *Avara* ≈ "the world before"; Kiln *Av'r* ≈ "the joining / the completed form"). Players decode this by pattern recognition, not exposition. Reveal ladder: *"Avarra was whole" → "When Avarra divided, we remained" → "We are Avarra."*

## The ending (do not violate)

The goal is **not to restore old Avarra.** Pre-fracture unity was merely the state before the split, not an ideal. The players create **the first Avarra that has learned to contain difference** — the first version that can hold both expressions without one dying. The real final question is **"can reality become one without one half of itself dying?"** — not "can we stop the alignment?" (the Wardens' failed thousand-year project). All prior cycles failed on the false premise that wholeness = replacement. The third possibility: become *something neither has ever been* — *"you don't have to become me, and I don't have to become you."* The climax is **relational, not martial**: make each side see there is a person on the other side. The solution is **created by the players, not excavated.**

## Never explained

*Why* reality originally split, and *why* it wants to be whole at all. Players get a complete answer to "what is happening?", a sufficient answer to "why has it been happening?", but never to "why does reality itself want to be one?" This is the *origin* left unknowable — not the conflict.
