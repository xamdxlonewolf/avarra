# The Premise

> **Design hub.** The core concept of the setting: a world where "monsters" (vampires, werewolves, etc.) are **not hated or hidden — they are integrated members of society**, given a *fresh* take rather than the standard tropes. The system is unnamed so far; this note fixes the load-bearing design. Status & next steps: [[Build Plan]].

## The one-line pitch

Vampires, werewolves, and their kin exist and are **part of society** — accepted, employed, and *needed.* They are not a persecuted underclass and not secret. The world is built so that this is genuinely the case, and each creature is a **fresh reinvention**, not the horror-movie cliché.

## The two-layer model (locked)

The central mechanic that makes the world cohere. Every person has **two layers**, and they **stack**:

1. **Kind — what you are *born* (Ancestry).** Mundane. Nobody blinks at it. This is the Daggerheart ancestry layer (Human, Elf, Drakona, …) **plus a small set of custom born-ancestries** unique to this world. Being a Kind is just your birth-shape.
2. **Condition — what you *become* (acquired).** The interesting layer. Vampire, werewolf, etc. are **not species — they are becomings**: something happens to a person and they transform. Roughly **75% of people acquire a Condition** over a lifetime (so ~1 in 4 never do). A Condition carries an ongoing cost (the **Tithe** — see below).

> **The design test that sorts the two:** *a Condition is a **becoming** (something happens → you change); an Ancestry is a **Kind** (born that way).* This test is why the roster shook out the way it did (see below).

This fuses three ideas into one: Conditions-not-curses (they're managed states with law and medicine), everyone-is-prone (~75%, so there's no clean "human vs. them" line to police), and society-needs-them (each Condition grants a capability the world is built around).

## The engine (proposed — confirm on resume)

- **Conditions arise by crossing a *threshold*** — an experience intense enough to change what you are (near-death, drowning, grief, a sworn oath, a great deed). Most people cross *some* threshold eventually → ~75%. **The door you go through decides what you become.** Rare Conditions come from rare thresholds. *(This is the systemic root; not yet locked — revisit on resume.)*
- **Every Condition has a Tithe** — an upkeep you must meet to keep living well with it. Meet it → valued citizen. Fall behind → the small "scary" edge shows (the tone dial is set at **~5% scary**: mostly accepted and mundane, with a real, dangerous obstacle underneath). The infrastructure that helps people pay their Tithes is *part of why society organizes around the Conditioned.*
- **Standing varies:** some Conditions are common and normal, some rare and looked-up-to, some rare and looked-down-on. Not a monolith.

## Tone

Integrated, lived-in, **~5% scary** — creatures are neighbours, colleagues, and family, accepted for the most part; the fresh-ness is in taking the standard tropes and turning them into *roles society needs*, each with an obstacle that must be met to keep living with the Condition.

## Daggerheart fit

We are effectively authoring **custom ancestries + a Condition layer** for Daggerheart. Baseline roster to design *against* (so ours earn their uniqueness) is recorded in [[Build Plan]]: 18 stock ancestries, 9 communities. Note the trap: Daggerheart already has **Infernis** (born demon-blooded) and **Faerie** etc. — so our "Bound/demon" Condition must be clearly *acquired-via-contract*, distinct from born-Infernis. Contrast is the point.
