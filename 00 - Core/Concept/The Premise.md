# The Premise

> **GM design hub.** The operational overview of the setting: the surface players see, the question that drives everything, the design contract that keeps it from collapsing into cliché, and where the players sit. The buried truth is [[The Cost]] (`11 - Secrets`); what it's *about* is [[Themes]].

## Logline

There is an enormous, centuries-old empire — and it is **genuinely good at governing.** There is also a rebellion, and the rebels are **not obviously wrong.** The campaign is the slow discovery of *why anyone would want to destroy a government that works* — and the players cannot end it by picking the good guys, because there aren't any. They have to decide what kind of world they actually want.

## The familiar surface (player-facing)

Players arrive expecting the standard fantasy furniture — imperial bureaucracy, military occupation, conquered peoples, noble houses, court intrigue, rebellion — and all of it is *there.* But the assumption underneath it is wrong. **The Empire works, and people know it:**

- Roads are safe and maintained; trade moves; merchants are protected.
- Crime is low. Courts are relatively fair — even to the conquered.
- Famines are rare; the granaries function.
- Conquered cultures keep their languages, gods, and customs.
- Slavery was abolished centuries ago.
- The average citizen genuinely *likes* the Empire — not from propaganda, but because their life is demonstrably better under it.

And yet there is a rebellion — and the rebels are decent people with a real grievance. **The wrongness the players feel is not "the Empire is secretly cruel." It's "why would anyone burn this down?"** That question is the hook, and its answer is the iceberg.

> **On the name.** The hegemon is simply **"the Empire"** — proposed as intentional, not a placeholder. From inside, it is not *an* empire among rivals; it is *the* order of the world, the water everyone swims in. Citizens no more need a proper name for it than we need one for "society." (Revisit only if a rival power or an outside perspective demands contrast. See Open questions.)

## The central question

**Why destroy a government that works?** → and beneath it → **is a government that *works* worth what it costs to work?** The players are never handed this question as exposition; they back into it by doing their jobs and noticing that the peace has a seam in it.

## The design contract (do not violate)

This is the single rule the whole setting rests on, and every future decision must pass its test:

> **Both the Empire's defenders and the rebels must stay *right* for the entire campaign.**

The moment the secret makes the Empire simply *evil*, the story collapses back into Evil Empire / Plucky Rebels — the exact cliché this premise exists to transcend. The test for any version of the secret, any faction, any reveal:

> *Could a thoughtful, decent person, knowing the full truth, still choose to keep it running?*

If **yes**, we have the story we want. If **no**, we've broken it. Keep the benefit vast and real; keep the cost real and never hand-waved; keep the choice genuinely hard. (This is a `moral-parallax` build — use that skill when constructing the secret, the rebellion, and the factions.)

## The players' vantage — a commission of inquiry

The players are a **mixed commission in imperial service** — the working frame is a **circuit court / commission of inquiry** that rides province to province dispensing the Empire's famously fair justice. Why this frame:

- **Job, not identity.** The GM imposes exactly *one* thing — *you all serve this body* — no heavier than a normal session-zero "why are you together?" **Who each character is, and whether they privately love or despise the Empire, is entirely player-authored.** A working bureaucracy staffs itself with whoever's useful: a translator from a conquered people, an ex-rebel who took the amnesty, a disgraced noble's child, a true-believer careerist, a foreign contractor in it for the pay.
- **The central argument happens *between the PCs.*** Believers and skeptics at the same table means the moral parallax is live in the party, not just PC-vs-NPC.
- **Complicity is built in.** The players personally dispense the Empire's justice — the horror is something they are *part of,* not something they observe from outside.
- **Episodic content is free.** Each new province/case is a self-contained arc (a dispute, a crime, an unrest) that resolves satisfyingly *and* seeds one more piece of the iceberg. Fun and complete session-to-session; deep underneath.
- Feeds `positional-revelation` (ordinary-job people sitting on the world's fault line) and `underdog-unit` (a small mixed unit inside a vast institution).

## Tone & authoring rule

**Comfort and competence first** (a world that genuinely works, that you'd want to live in) → **uncanny** when the seam shows → **grimdark spikes** at the pressure points. Wonder/comfort must come first, so there is always something real to lose.

Teach through *"something is weird,"* never an encyclopedia dump. The peace is real; the cost is real; everyone is grateful. Let players poke at the seam for a long time before it opens.

## Open questions (the build roadmap)

1. **Names** — the setting/campaign title; provinces and peoples; the rebellion's name. (The Empire itself stays "the Empire" unless contrast demands otherwise.)
2. **The secret's mechanism** — see [[The Cost]]: who/what is sacrificed, by what body, how chosen; what exactly is being held back; whether magic is involved.
3. **The rebellion** — who they are, what they know, why *now*; must stay sympathetic.
4. **Geography & the imperial map** — heartland vs. frontier vs. recently-pacified provinces; where the commission rides.
5. **Session/arc structure** — the case-of-the-arc engine and how each one seeds the iceberg.
6. **Magic & the Daggerheart fit** — how bounded magic sits in a world this orderly.
