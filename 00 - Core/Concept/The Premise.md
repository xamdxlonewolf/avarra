# The Premise

> **Design hub.** This note is the core concept of **The Turning** (the setting's name, locked 2026-08-20): a world where "monsters" (vampires, werewolves, etc.) are **not hated or hidden — they are integrated members of society**, given a *fresh* take rather than the standard tropes. The world is named for its defining act — the tenth-year **Turning** at the [[Turning Tree]]. This note fixes the load-bearing design. Status and the next step are on [[Build Plan]].

## The one-line pitch

Vampires, werewolves, and their kin exist and are **part of society** — accepted, employed, and *needed.* They are not a persecuted underclass and not secret. The world is built so that this is genuinely the case, and each creature is a **fresh reinvention**, not the horror-movie cliché.

## The two-layer model (locked)

The two layers are the central mechanic that makes the world cohere. Every person has **two layers**, and they **stack**:

1. **Kind is what you are born**, the ancestry, and it is mundane, and nobody blinks at it. This is the Daggerheart ancestry layer (Human, Elf, Drakona, and the rest) **plus a small set of custom born-ancestries** unique to this world — [[Kitsune]], [[Selkie]], and [[Tengu]], native to the mainland, and the rare, far-isle [[Yumboe]] added in Epic 3 — and being a Kind is just your birth-shape.
2. **Condition is what you become**, acquired rather than born, and it is the interesting layer. A vampire, a werewolf, and the rest are **not species — they are becomings**: something happens to a person and they transform. Roughly **75% of people acquire a Condition** over a lifetime, so about 1 in 4 never do. A Condition carries an ongoing cost, the **Tithe**, the upkeep you have to meet if you are going to keep living well with it, as the section below sets out.

> **The design test that sorts the two,** and the reason the roster shook out the way it did, is the one to keep. A Condition is a **becoming**, which means something happens and you change, and an ancestry is a **Kind**, which means it is the shape you were born with (see below).

This fuses three ideas into one: Conditions-not-curses (they're managed states with law and medicine), everyone-is-prone (~75%, so there's no clean "human vs. them" line to police), and society-needs-them (each Condition grants a capability the world is built around).

## The engine — how Conditions arise (LOCKED)

There are **two paths** to a Condition, and the split is the central social fault line of the world:

### Given — at the Awakening (the Leaf-Fall)
In their **10th year, on the solstice**, children gather with their families around a **Turning Tree** (most towns have a scion, the town's own Tree; a town that is too new, too remote, or Tree-bereaved sends its children to a neighbour's Tree). Leaves fall and wrap around each 10-year-old.

When a colour falls, a Condition is *Given*, and the colour of the leaf names which one. The day is ceremonial, it is expected, and people call it proper, so you grow up already knowing what you will become, and you are trained and guilded for it. When no colour falls and the leaves *hug* the child, that child is one of the Kept. The Tree keeps them as they are, a blessing in its own right, explicitly *wanted and special*, not a failure and not a lack.

### Struck — a threshold later in life
Some Conditions **cannot** be Given to a child (you can't die at 10; a child can't swear a contract). These arrive only when a normal person crosses a **threshold** later — the door you go through decides what you become. There is no leaf and no ceremony, and people view that arrival as tragic, miraculous, or a little suspect. So **the Kept are not a closed club** — a slice of them still turn.

> **The clean rule that sorts Given, Struck, and Both:**
> **Given only** are the innate fates, the ones that are not a death, an oath, or a door: [[Long-Lived]] (the vampire), [[Two-Bodied]] (the werewolf), [[The Answered]] (the djinn, also called jinni), and [[Phoenix]].
> **Struck only** are the things a 10-year-old physically cannot have done, so a person who has died is [[Returned]] (the zombie), and a person who has sworn a pact is [[Bound]] (the demon: a sworn contract, not born-Infernis).
> **Both** are the survivable near-death doors, the ones the Tree can foresee or a person can walk through later by accident: [[Far-Voiced]] (the siren), [[The Stilled]] (the gorgon), and [[The Taken-In]] (the leshy). A Given Far-Voiced (blue leaf at 10, guild-trained) and a Struck Far-Voiced (actually drowned at 30) feel very different at the table.

### The population math (LOCKED — canonical figures)

**This section is the only source for population shares.** Other notes link here or give a derived summary (the Two-Bodied plurality, the Kept quarter). Do not keep a second roster.

The lifetime paths are unchanged, and they are shares of the whole population: **~60% Given** at the Tree, **~15% Struck** later, and **~25%** who stay Kept for life. The Given and the Struck together are the 75% who carry a Condition. At the ceremony itself about 40% of children receive no colour (the hug). A little over a third of those hugged children will still be Struck someday.

How that 75% is composed was settled on 2026-08-30, in Epic R Story R.1. The path totals above are load-bearing. The per-Condition shares below are the arithmetic that makes them add, and the percents are of *all people*, not of the Conditioned.

| Condition | Path | Share | Given | Struck |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [[Long-Lived]] | Given only | ~2.5% | ~2.5% | — |
| [[Two-Bodied]] | Given only | ~18% | ~18% | — |
| [[The Answered]] | Given only | ~10% | ~10% | — |
| [[Phoenix]] | Given only | <0.1% | <0.1% | — |
| [[Returned]] | Struck only | ~7% | — | ~7% |
| [[Bound]] (contract-lane) | Struck only | ~5% | — | ~5% |
| *…of whom currently Bound* | | *~3.5%* | | |
| *…of whom [[The Unbound]]* | | *~1.5%* | | |
| [[Far-Voiced]] | Both | ~12% | ~11% | ~1% |
| [[The Stilled]] | Both | ~7% | ~6% | ~1% |
| [[The Taken-In]] | Both | ~13% | ~12% | ~1% |
| **Conditioned** | | **~75%** | **~60%** | **~15%** |
| The Kept | hug, no colour | ~25% | — | — |

The Given column comes to ~2.5 + 18 + 10 + 11 + 6 + 12 = 59.5, plus the Phoenix trace, and the Struck column comes to 7 + 5 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 15. The half-point of slack is the "~" on every share.

Unbound sits inside Bound's share, because [[The Unbound]] is what a term-limited Bound becomes when the contract ends, and the ~1.5% is not a tenth Condition stacked on top of Bound's ~5%. Bound's row is the *contract-lane*: people currently Bound (~3.5%) plus people already released and Hollow (~1.5%). A table that lists both as parallel percentages double-counts.

Returned moved because the Struck-only pair used to be Returned ~9 + Bound ~5, and some notes added Unbound ~1.5 on top. That filled (or overfilled) the whole 15% Struck budget before a single Struck Far-Voiced, Stilled, or Taken-In. Returned is now ~7% so the three Both-path doors can carry a real Struck share (~1% of all people each). Most Both-path people are still Given: the Tree usually sees the door coming. A Struck Far-Voiced who actually drowned at thirty stays uncommon, not vanishing.

These figures are world averages. Country that is poor in Trees, with few of them, or only young or sickening ones, runs higher on the Kept and the Struck, and the Tree-dense core runs higher on the Given, while the lifetime paths stay 60 / 15 / 25 as the *world* mix.

### The Tithe
Every Condition has a **Tithe**, an upkeep you must meet to keep living well with it. Meet it, and you are a valued citizen; fall behind, and the small "scary" edge shows, on a tone dial of **~5% scary**: mostly accepted and mundane, with a real, dangerous obstacle underneath. The design rule is that a Tithe is manageable in normal play, satisfied by rest and by routine, that the scary edge is opt-in risk or narrative colour, and that a Tithe must never seize control of the player's character. The infrastructure that helps people pay their Tithes is part of why society organizes around the Conditioned.

Standing varies from Condition to Condition: some are common and normal, some are rare and looked up to, some are rare and looked down on, and the Conditioned are not a monolith.

## The Leaf-Mother (a religion — belief, not confirmed cosmology)

Some hold that the **Leaf-Mother** blessed the world with the Conditions, and the **Turning Tree** is how she reaches down — the town scions are her hands, and the Leaf-Fall is her *choosing*. This is **a belief held by some**, not locked cosmology, and it hands us built-in friction: believers hold that the Conditioned are *chosen*, skeptics hold that it is just what the Trees do, biology rather than a benediction, and the schism between them is live. If she blesses everyone, why does the Tree hug about two children in five instead of giving them a colour, and why do about a quarter of people stay Kept for life? Are the Kept unblessed, or spared? And what does it mean that the Struck receive a Condition she never handed out?

> **Built out (Epic 1, 2026-08-19; Story 1.4, 2026-08-23; clergy Story 5.1, 2026-08-23):** the faith is concrete now. [[The Leaf-Mother]] holds the doctrine, the practice, the three-branch schism, and the in-world texts. [[Turning Tree]] holds the object and the Leaf-Fall rite: there is **one Awakening Tree**, the town Trees are **living grafts** of it, and a town therefore has a Tree nearby *and* a pilgrimage. [[The Tree-Wardens]] are the working clergy, and their names are 🟡. The Motherfaith is *one faith among several*. The others are mapped in [[Faiths of the Turning]]: [[The Watching]], [[The Fair Hand]], [[The Old Ways]], and [[The Open Table]].
>
> **GM household (2026-08-23):** she is first of a real pantheon: she **Gives** and she does not Strike, and the Struck are the Other Hands under her allowance. That account is on [[The Other Hands]], and player-facing notes do not confirm it.
>
> **Keystone locked (GM):** underneath the belief, [[Is the Leaf-Mother Real|she is real and benevolent, limited, and giving costs her]] — the gifts are real gifts, and the skeptics are sincere and wrong. Player-facing notes keep her unproven; the confirmation is the GM's to place, and it lands as *warmth*, not horror.

## Tone

The tone is integrated, lived-in, **~5% scary** — creatures are neighbours, colleagues, and family, accepted for the most part; the fresh-ness is in taking the standard tropes and turning them into *roles society needs*, each with an obstacle that must be met to keep living with the Condition.

## World scale & register (LOCKED 2026-08-20)

Two dials were set before Epic 2, so that law, economy, and settlements would be built against a fixed frame:

### Scale — a wide world of rival powers
**~15 great powers** stand across 3 large continents + 1 small one, and that count is the top tier, not the whole count. Beneath them the world swarms the way a late-medieval one does: smaller kingdoms, free cities, marches, petty principalities, and un-polity'd frontier (ground that no polity claims) fill the space between the great powers. "~15" names the powers worth naming, not the number of places that exist. The shape is not one empire and not a fog of city-states, but a handful-plus of real powers that trade, compete, and *differ in how they treat the Conditioned*. This gives enough uniformity to generalize the social rules and enough contrast for border-, migration-, and law-clash stories.

**One continent is the motherland**, the origin, where the **[[Turning Tree|Awakening Tree]]** still stands. The town Trees of the whole world are grafts carried out from it, so *today* the Leaf-Mother's reach travels with the grafts, and full reach stands wherever a healthy Tree does. Thin reach is a matter of places that are genuinely **Tree-poor** (few Trees, or young ones, or Trees that are sickening), not of raw distance from the motherland. The old distance-gradient, when there was *one* Tree and the far-flung stayed UnTurned for want of the pilgrimage (folk called them Kept), is **history**, and [[The Ages of the Turning]] tells it. The keystone's edge is that the Leaf-Mother's reach *thins* where the Trees are thin, so those places run higher on the Kept and the Struck, and the edge wants a big map with a real frontier and a real origin. Scale and cosmology point the same way.

> **Build discipline (progressive elaboration).** 15 fully-built polities is pass-two work, not Epic 2 work. What Epic 2 built was the *universal social physics* — how law, economy, and daily life bend around a Tithe-carrying majority — and that physics is largely portable, because it flows from the Trees and the Conditions, which are locked and universal. Epic 2 also built 2–3 contrasting polity *archetypes*, enough to prove the rules can flex. The remaining ~12 are named-stubs as of [[Roadmap|Story 7.1]], and [[Powers of the Turning]] holds them.

### Register — late-medieval, advanced by *Conditions* not by generic magic-tech
The surface is a **late-medieval burgher world** (~1400s Bruges/Florence): chartered towns, guild halls, cathedrals, and markets, early banking in houses, letters of credit, and ledgers, mechanical clocks, water mills and windmills, and universities. There is **no printing press and no gunpowder**, and what sits on top of that life, in the hand, is swords and lanterns.

**The one advancement *beyond* our 1400s comes from Condition-labor — and only there.** This world's "magic" isn't wandering wizards; it's the Conditions, and 3 of 4 people carry one. So its selective leaps are *lawful and legible*, concentrated exactly where the Conditions live. The Long-Lived are deathless, and from that come banking houses, universities, archives, and courts with *centuries* of unbroken institutional memory, a depth our history needed the 1700s to reach, because the first Long-Lived people never died. The Stilled work at preservation and medicine, the Returned take the deadly jobs and tend the dead, and the Taken-In make frontier land productive, and together they are why public works and medicine exist here that should not be possible without industry. The Answered and the Far-Voiced are why a polity can coordinate and signal at a distance in a way no medieval polity should be able to.

> **The rule that keeps it honest:** every advance is *paid for* — by a Tithe, by someone's becoming. No free-floating "magic makes everything better" that erases scarcity. The advancement is legible (you can see *whose* labor buys it), concentrated (present where those Conditions are common, absent where they aren't), and costly. This isn't decoration — it **is** Epic 2's economy story.

## The Condition roster (9 selectable + 1 downstream — LOCKED)

Each is a recognizable monster given a fresh, integrated take. Standing spans the range from revered, through normal, through looked-down, through feared, to legendary. There are **9 Conditions a person can be Given or Struck with** (below); a **10th, [[The Unbound]] (colloq. the Hollow), is a *downstream* state** — what a [[Bound]] becomes when a term-limited contract ends — so it can't be picked at creation, only reached through Bound. All ten have **full, locked mechanics** in `09 - Creatures/Conditions/` (see [[Conditions]]), the shares and the Given / Struck splits live in the population table above, and this section is the roster's fiction, not a second census.

**The core four:**
| Condition | Monster | Path | Role society needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Long-Lived** | vampire | Given only | archivists, judges, notaries |
| **Two-Bodied** | werewolf | Given only | rescue, wild-work, labor, war |
| **Returned** | zombie | Struck only (requires death) | the deadly jobs, tend the dead |
| **Bound** | demon | Struck only (a sworn contract — not born-Infernis) | enforcers, oath-keepers |

**The added five:**
| Condition | Monster | Path | Standing / door |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Far-Voiced** | siren | Both | Revered for the signal network, the law, and performance, and the door is drowning. |
| **The Stilled** | gorgon | Both | Feared and guild-regulated, in peacekeeping and medicine, and the door is having survived being struck to stone. |
| **The Answered** | djinn/jinni | Given only | A wildcard, working as artisans, in elemental craft, and on watch and rescue crews. |
| **The Taken-In** | leshy | Both | Respected and looked down on, in agriculture, on the frontier, and in forestry, and the door is being lost and dying in the deep wild. |
| **Phoenix** | phoenix | Given only | Legendary, almost never seen, known through stories, and the hook is cyclical rebirth. |

**The downstream 10th — [[The Unbound]]** *(colloq. the Hollow).* They are the price of a contract: the state is Struck, it chains off Bound only, and it is counted *inside* Bound's contract-lane, not added to it. People pity them and feel uneasy around them, because they were released and they are missing the Pledge they paid, and they are not selectable at creation.

**The Kept** are the children the Tree hugged and left as they are. The Tree hugs about two children in five; about a quarter of people stay Kept for life, because some hugged children are Struck later. They are wanted and special in their own right, not an unconditioned lack.

The design notes that carry forward begin with the vampire sink, which is emotional and demographic at once. Long-Lived *can* have children, but **none are guaranteed** to inherit the Condition, and a Long-Lived outlives spouses and children and then either stops or begins a new family and outlives that one too, which is their deepest Tithe, under the vitality one. They die by violence, by accident, or by a failed Tithe: deathless, and not unkillable. What makes Phoenix distinct from the Returned and from the Long-Lived is **cyclical rebirth**, not a one-time return and not agelessness.

## Daggerheart fit

We are effectively authoring **custom ancestries + a Condition layer** for Daggerheart. The baseline roster to design *against*, so ours earn their uniqueness, is recorded in [[Build Plan]]: 18 stock ancestries, 9 communities. The trap is that Daggerheart already has **Infernis** (born demon-blooded) and **Faerie** and others of that kind, so our "Bound/demon" Condition must be clearly *acquired-via-contract*, distinct from born-Infernis, and the contrast is the point. Mechanics should stay reasonably system-portable (avoid making a Condition read as a stock D&D player race) in case the setting crosses to other TTRPGs.

The four custom Kinds keep three features: stock ancestries take a [[Kind Heritage|Hearth-Mark]] so they are not trap options, Mixed Ancestry is allowed as the SRD allows it (mainland Kinds), and a [[Yumboe]] player character needs the GM's leave and is always the full Kind, never a mix. Those rulings live on [[Kind Heritage]] and [[Kinds of the Turning]].
